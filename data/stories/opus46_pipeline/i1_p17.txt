# Court of the Dreaming: Territorial Dream Dispute

---

**Case No. 11,407,223-B.** *In the matter of First Love v. Mora (Unregistered), regarding unauthorized modification of a protected romantic dream sequence, Dreamer: Max Ellison (age 16, Portland, Oregon), filed under Section 9 of the Oneiric Territorial Compact, with prior related filings 11,407,221 and 11,407,222, both dismissed on procedural grounds.* The clerk's voice carried the particular deadness of someone who had read ten thousand such entries and would read ten thousand more. *Presiding: Lord Morpheus, Prince of Stories, Sovereign of the Dreaming, et cetera, et cetera.*

The "et cetera" hung in the vaulted dark of the courtroom like a small blasphemy. No one reacted. The walls shifted between obsidian and remembrance, columns rising into distances that shouldn't have existed indoors, but the gallery benches were scratched and ordinary, and someone had left a quill leaking onto the recording secretary's desk.

Dream sat the throne. His fingers, long and pale, moved once across the armrest — the barest drag of nail on stone — and were still.

"Proceed," he said.

---

First Love took the stand the way a sparrow lands on a wire: a flutter, a tremor, then an earnest attempt at composure. They were slight and shifting, all half-formed features and warm light, and they clutched a glass orb in both hands like a child presenting a school project they'd been up all night perfecting.

"I'd been — I'd been working on this one for *weeks*," First Love said, turning the orb toward the court. Inside, a field materialized: tall grass, wildflowers going gold in late-afternoon light, the kind of sky that only exists in the memories of summers you're not sure you actually had. A girl stood in the middle of it. She was nobody in particular and everybody at once — dark hair, a mouth that almost smiled, a way of tilting her head as though Max had just said something worth hearing.

"The approach was from the south," First Love continued, warming to it. "He'd come over the hill. There would be wind — I spent three days on the wind, getting the way it moves her hair without messing it up, you know, because he *notices* that kind of thing, he's very — anyway. They'd sit. She'd lean close. And then —"

First Love's luminance pulsed faintly pink.

"The almost-kiss. You don't land it. You *never* land it. That's the whole — it's the *yearning* that does the work. He'd wake up and feel like the world was full of something he couldn't name yet, and he'd carry that feeling into his Tuesday, and — and it was *good work*."

The orb trembled slightly in their hands.

"It was my best one this quarter."

---

The corrupted version played next, projected above the court in light that flinched.

It began the same. The field. The girl. The wind (three days of work). But as she leaned in — closer than the template allowed, the gallery noted — her weight shifted, and she was on top of him, and the sky cracked, and then the face was wrong. The mouth kept going, too wide and too many, and Max couldn't move because his limbs were stone now, and something was drawing out of him through the space between his ribs, something warm and essential, and his heart was hammering with terror that was — the recording secretary paused, checked the dreamer's biometrics — "terror cross-indexed with... acute arousal, Your Lordship."

A sound rolled through the courtroom: low, wet, delighted. Cackling. It bounced off the vaulted ceiling and nested in the corners and would not leave.

Three entities in the gallery shifted their seats. A minor nightmare crossed its arms and looked at its shoes.

Dream's jaw tightened by a degree that only the throne would have felt.

"We will hear from the respondent."

---

Mora unfolded herself from the defendant's chair like smoke learning to walk. She was dense and pale and ancient, and her eyes were the absolute black of the space behind a closet door at 3 a.m. She smiled, and it was the kind of smile that made you check your locks.

"Look," she said. "I understand the kid put a lot of effort in." A nod toward First Love, not unkind. "Nice field. Really. The wind was a good touch."

First Love flickered, unsure whether to be flattered.

"But here's the thing." Mora spread her hands, reasonable as gravity. "A girl's gotta eat. You know what it's like out there? The Waking world's pumping these kids full of melatonin gummies and white noise machines. Half of them don't even drop below REM-2 anymore. I haven't had a clean feed in *months*. And then this boy — this sweet, open, helplessly hormonal boy — drops into a deep-cycle romantic sequence with a girl *already on top of him*?"

She looked at the court the way a plumber looks at a leak.

"I didn't destroy the dream. I *pivoted*. Everything was already there. The vulnerability. The surrender. The quickened heartbeat. I just... adjusted the context." She shrugged. "Efficient resource utilization. If anything, First Love should be flattered. The scaffolding held beautifully."

"That is NOT what scaffolding is FOR—" First Love's voice cracked into a register only dogs and minor deities could hear.

A cackle rolled through the dark above them.

"Enough," said Dream.

The word did not echo. It simply was, and then there was silence, and the silence was his.

---

He regarded them both for a span that might have been a moment or might have been the length of a held breath in a dream about falling.

"The territorial claim is valid," Dream said. "The modification was unauthorized."

First Love brightened. Mora's expression didn't change.

"However." The word settled like frost. "The dreamer's response was... hybrid. The biometrics do not lie. Max Ellison experienced the corrupted sequence as both violation *and* desire. The territories have already merged in the only place that matters — the dreamer's mind."

He paused. His fingers moved once more across the armrest.

"The dream will run again. As written — the field, the girl, the wind, the almost-kiss. But the dreamer will now always feel, in the moment before the kiss does not arrive, a flicker of something cold behind her face. Something waiting. He will not know why. He will not be able to stop wanting to lean closer."

First Love made a small, wounded sound.

Mora grinned.

"This displeases you both," Dream observed. "Good."

He rose. The courtroom began to disassemble itself around the edges.

"Next case."

Somewhere in Portland, Oregon, a sixteen-year-old boy rolled over in his sleep and reached for something he would spend years not being able to name, and the reaching was sweet, and the sweetness had teeth, and the teeth were patient.
