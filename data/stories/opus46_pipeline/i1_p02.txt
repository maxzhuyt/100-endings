# The Lighthouse Keeper

---

**14th September 1972**

Dear Mam,

Well I'm here and in one piece which is more than the supply crate can say — they've a method of winching things up from the boat that would put the fear in you. The tower is sound though. Walls three foot thick, everything where it should be. There's a wee kitchen on the second level and a bunk above that and above that the light itself, which is a grand piece of engineering, a clockwork mechanism you've to wind every six hours so she keeps turning.

The Bass Rock sits out to the east, white with gannets. Thousands of them. You can hear them from here even over the water which is

Anyway there's coal enough and the prior man left tins I've not bothered counting. The light is what matters and the light is simple enough work. Honest work. Tell Rab I said the NB Loco is a certainty for Saturday.

Your loving son,
Callum

---

**23rd September 1972**

Dear Callum,

I'm glad you're settled. Mrs Fairley's hip has her in the Royal again and she's asked me to water the tomatoes which I do though they're past saving if you ask me. Your brother says to tell you the Loco lost 3–1 and you owe him a pint when you're home.

The weather here has been grey but mild. I've put the good curtains up in your room so it'll be ready.

You didn't say whether there's another keeper with you or is it just yourself out there. I know you said they sometimes have two.

Look after yourself and eat properly and not just tins.

With love,
Mam

P.S. I've put a tablet in with this — it'll be stale by the time it reaches you but you'll manage.

---

**12th October 1972**

Mam,

It's just myself. That's the job. One man, one light.

Storm last week took the aerial off the roof so that's the radio gone. Reported it when the supply boat came Tuesday. They said they'd send a part. The boat comes fortnightly so that's that. Not much use for a radio anyway. There's only the coastguard and they've nothing to say to me nor I to them.

The Bass Rock in a storm is something. The sea comes over it and the gannets lift all at once, thousands, like the rock itself is coming apart.

C.

---

**3rd November 1972**

Dear Callum,

I've not heard from you in some time so I hope this finds you well and the radio is fixed. Rab says you'll not write because you're a thrawn so-and-so and always were. I said leave him be.

There's talk they'll close the brewery which would put 40 men out. Your father would have had something to say about that. I saw Jean Leckie in the Co-op and she asked after you which I thought was kind.

The curtains are still up in your room.

Mrs Fairley is home now and walking with a frame and in foul temper about it which is a good sign I think.

Are you sleeping all right? I only ask because you were never a good sleeper even as a boy and I wonder about the noise of the light turning.

With love,
Mam

---

**19th January 1973**

Mam,

The mechanism needs winding at twelve and six, twelve and six. You can set your life by it. I go up the stairs and I wind it and the light turns and the ships pass safe and I come back down.

The rock is still there every morning. I check. Some days it looks closer than it should. The gannets are gone now — they go in winter, I don't know where. Without them the rock is just rock. White with their droppings. Like bone.

I've been thinking about the house on Inchkeith Terrace, the one before Dad died. The front step was red, wasn't it? Someone painted the step red. I can see it but I can't remember if that's real or something I've made up.

The supply boat was late this month. Only two days but you feel it.

I'm fine. I wind the light.

C.

---

**2nd February 1973**

Dear Callum,

The step was red. I painted it myself, every spring, with that floor paint from the ironmonger's on Duke Street. You used to sit on it before it was dry and I'd skelp you and you'd greet and do it again the next year. Your father said just let the boy sit where he wants. So I did and you'd come in with the seat of your trousers red and I never minded really.

Now you listen to me. I have spoken to the Board and there is a relief due at the end of March and you are owed leave and I have told them you will be taking it. I did not ask, Callum. I told them. You can be angry with me when you get home.

The curtains are up. The room is aired. Rab will come for you at the harbour.

I know why you went. You don't have to explain it and you don't have to have fixed it by the time you come back. You just have to come back.

The step wants painting again if you're looking for a job.

All my love,
Mam
