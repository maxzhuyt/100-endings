My phone says 6%.

Six is not a number; it’s a countdown. It’s the sound of a door latch clicking somewhere I can’t see. It’s the tiny sliver of time between “hey, be there soon” and “actually, sorry, something came up,” and then nothing, no proof I exist except the heat in my own face.

I tilt the screen, like the angle will change reality. Low Power Mode. The little yellow battery icon looks sick. I consider texting *I’m leaving now* just to create a record, a breadcrumb trail so if he disappears I can point to something and say, *See? I tried. I was here. I was good.* But then I picture him reading it and rolling his eyes.

*She’s so intense.*

I can hear his voice as if he’s rehearsing it too, like we’re both in different rooms preparing lines for the same play.

“Why do you have to narrate everything?” Imagined him says, smiling in that careful, patronizing way.

“I’m not narrating, I’m communicating.” Me, defensive, already sharp. “Some people like to know what’s happening.”

“Some people,” he repeats, meaning *you*. Meaning *your anxiety is the main character and I’m tired of auditioning as supporting cast.*

I swallow. My throat feels thick, like my body has already started crying without my permission.

Stop. That’s not him. That’s not what he’s said.

But he *has* been distant.

Distant is a word that sounds polite. Like *space* and *busy* and *just a lot going on.* Distant is “I’m so sorry, I fell asleep” sent at 11:34 the next morning. Distant is leaving my last message on read for an hour and my brain using that hour to build a courtroom.

In the courtroom, my messages are Exhibit A: “Do you still want to do tonight?” Exhibit B: “No worries if not.” Exhibit C: “Just checking.” Exhibit D: “Hope your day’s okay.” In the courtroom, the jury is everyone I’ve ever needed. They look disappointed.

I force my fingers away from the keyboard. If I send one more “just checking,” it’ll become evidence for *clingy*, for *too much*, for *why can’t you be normal*. My phone buzzes anyway, like it heard the thought and decided to punish me.

A notification—him.

My heart jumps so hard I feel nauseous. I tap it like it’s fragile.

“On my way. Sorry I’ve been quiet today—work was a mess. Can’t wait to see you.”

Can’t wait.

My eyes snag on that phrase like it’s a hook. For half a second, warmth floods me. He wants to see me. He misses me. He’s thinking about me moving through his day. The craving in my chest—being wanted, being chosen—sits up like a starving animal catching the smell of food.

Then the second half of me arrives, always late and always armed.

*He’s saying that because he knows you’re sensitive.*  
*He’s saying that because he doesn’t want a fight.*  
*He’s saying that because he’s decent, not because he feels it.*

I stare at the message until the letters start to blur.

If I reply too enthusiastically, he’ll feel trapped. If I reply too coolly, he’ll feel rejected and—good, maybe. Maybe I should be the one who’s distant, for once. Preemptive. Protective.

“Okay, see you soon :)” I type.

My thumb hovers over the smiley. Too much? Too needy? Too… eager. I erase it, put it back, erase it again. My battery ticks down to 5% like an impatient metronome.

I send: “Yay. Be safe.”

Yay feels like I’m begging. Be safe feels like I’m pretending I’m calm.

The mirror catches me mid-spiral: mascara wand frozen in the air, mouth slightly open like I’ve been interrupted. My own face looks accusing, like it knows I’m doing it again.

*You’re making a problem out of nothing.*

Yes. And also no. Because the feeling is not nothing. The feeling is a tidal wave and I’m standing in it pretending it’s a gentle rain.

I choose a shirt he likes. I hate myself for that sentence. I hate that my brain thinks in terms of earning. Like love is a punch card: look cute ten times, get one unprompted “I adore you.”

He *has* said that. He said it last week, in my kitchen, after we’d made pasta and ended up kissing against the counter like we were teenagers. He cupped my face like it was precious and said, “I adore you,” and I felt it in my bones, like a bell being rung.

Then, immediately: *He said it too soon.*  
*He didn’t mean it.*  
*He’ll regret it.*  
*You’ll ruin it.*

I check my phone again. 5%. I grab my charger, then stop, then grab it anyway, as if I can plug it into the air and charge myself. I shove it in my bag like a talisman.

I practice in my head what I’ll say when he arrives, because arrivals are dangerous. Arrivals are moments where you can see in someone’s eyes if they’re happy to see you. If his smile is smaller tonight, my body will treat it like a verdict.

Scenario one: He walks in and he’s distracted, looking over my shoulder, not touching me right away.

Me: “Are you okay?” casual, light.  
Him: “Yeah, just tired.”  
Me: “You’ve been… kinda distant.” Not accusatory. Honest.  
Him: “I’m not distant. I’m just busy.”  
Me: “Okay. It just feels like…”  
Him: “Here we go.”  
Me: “No, I’m not trying to start something.”  
Him: “You always think I’m abandoning you.”  
Me: “I don’t always—”  
Him: “It’s exhausting.”

My chest tightens as if this is already happening. My palms sweat. I’m so good at hurting myself with imaginary words. I’m so fluent.

Scenario two: He says it cleanly. He’s not ready for this. He wants to be friends. He met someone else. He wants to “take it slow,” which is the same as “take it away.”

In that version, I get cold. I become sharp enough to cut him before he cuts me.

Me: “It’s fine. I knew this would happen.”  
Him: “What? No—”  
Me: “Don’t pretend. You were never actually into me.”  
Him: “That’s not fair.”  
Me: “Fair? You’re the one who—”  
Him: “I didn’t promise anything.”  
Me: “You said you adored me.”  
Him: “I did. I do. But—”  
Me: “But I’m too much.”

I can feel the righteous anger rising, already rehearsed, already waiting behind my ribs like a guard dog. Anger is safer than begging. Anger gives me shape. Anger says: *I didn’t need you anyway.*

Except I do. I do. I do.

My phone buzzes again. I flinch like I’ve been caught stealing.

“Parking now,” he texts. “Do you want me to pick up ice cream? I remember you said you like the mint one.”

He remembers.

It’s such a small thing, ice cream. A detail. A line thrown across water.

My whole body lunges for it. *He knows me.* He listened. He’s thinking about my likes in the grocery store aisle. He’s offering something sweet.

Then: *He’s buying ice cream to soften the blow.*  
*He’s buying ice cream because he knows you’ll cry.*  
*He’s buying ice cream so he can feel like a good guy when he leaves.*

I hate that I can’t accept anything without checking it for poison.

“Mint would be amazing,” I type fast, before my brain can sabotage it. Battery: 4%. Of course. Of course the world would choose tonight to make me unreachable. Like even the phone is leaving.

I stand by the window and watch the street. Every car’s headlights feel personal. I imagine him outside in his car, looking at his own phone, reconsidering. I imagine him reading our thread and thinking *Why did I get involved with her?*

I remember what he actually did yesterday: he sent me a photo of something stupid—two pigeons on a ledge that looked like they were having a serious meeting—and wrote, “This is us planning dinner.” There was no cruelty in it. There was intimacy. There was *us*.

My brain replies: *He sends that to everyone.*  
My chest replies: *He sent it to you.*

Both feel true. That’s the torture. The facts come in one door and my fear rushes to interpret them before they even take their coat off.

A knock. Soft, then another.

My heart is loud. My phone is at 3%. I consider plugging it in, like I’ll need a lifeline if this goes bad, like I’ll need to call someone, anyone, to prove I’m not alone. The idea of my phone dying while he’s here makes me panic irrationally—like if I can’t text after, then the night will evaporate and I’ll be left with only my thoughts, which are already sharpening their knives.

I open the door.

He’s there. Not imagined him. Real him. Hair slightly windblown, cheeks pink from the cold, holding a paper bag that smells like sugar and mint. His eyes lift and—God—his face changes. He smiles. A real smile, not polite. His shoulders drop like he’s relieved to be here.

“Hi,” he says, and it’s soft, like the word is meant for me specifically.

My body floods with something so intense it hurts. Relief, hunger, love, whatever chemical it is that makes me want to crawl into his chest and live there.

He steps forward and kisses me, not rushed, not hesitant. His hand lands at my waist like it belongs there. Like I belong.

For a moment, the courtroom empties. The jury goes silent.

Then the bailiff in my brain clears their throat.

*He kissed you because that’s what you do.*  
*He could still leave after.*  
*Don’t get comfortable.*

“I got your mint,” he says, lifting the bag. “Also… I’m sorry I’ve been in my head lately. It’s not you.”

Not you.

My throat tightens. I want to grab that sentence and staple it to my skin. I want to believe it so badly it feels like a physical ache. I nod too quickly, like if I move wrong the moment will shatter.

“It’s okay,” I say, which is what I always say. The rehearsed line for when I’m not okay. The line that keeps me from being “too much.”

He looks at me like he’s reading the part I didn’t say. “Hey,” he says again, quieter. “I’m really happy to be here.”

Happy to be here.

The craving in me surges—validation like oxygen. I breathe it in and immediately cough, because my fear wants to label it contaminated. My brain scrambles for the hidden meaning, the trap door.

I can almost see the pattern like a diagram: distance → panic → imagined rejection → anger armor → test → relief → doubt → repeat.

I know it’s happening. I know I’m doing the thing where I rehearse pain so I can pretend it won’t surprise me. I know resentment feels like control, and control feels like safety, and safety is the only thing my body understands as love.

Knowing doesn’t stop it. Knowing just adds a second voice:

*This is your disorder talking.*

And the first voice snaps back:

*Or it’s your intuition. You’ve been abandoned before. You’re not stupid.*

He brushes his thumb along my cheek. “You’re quiet,” he says, not accusing. Curious. Concerned.

My mind launches into another internal script: *Say something normal. Don’t confess the cyclone. Don’t ask him to promise anything. Don’t make him responsible for your fear.*

But my chest feels like a held breath. My phone in my pocket is probably at 2% now, ticking toward dark, and the symbolism makes me want to laugh and cry at the same time.

“I’m just…” I start. *Don’t.* “I’m really glad you’re here.”

He smiles again, like that matters. Like it lands. Like my words have weight.

“Me too,” he says. “I missed you.”

Missed you.

The sentence hits me like sunlight and I flinch, waiting for it to burn. I let myself feel it anyway, just for a second, because I’m so tired of living like every good thing is a trick.

Then, right on schedule, my brain whispers: *Enjoy it while it lasts.*

I swallow it down, because I don’t know how to turn off the rehearsal. I only know how to keep acting, keep listening for the moment the script changes.

But for now, his hand is warm in mine. The ice cream bag rustles. The room smells like mint and him. And I stand in the unbearable space between being loved and not trusting it, holding both truths like they’re both glass.
