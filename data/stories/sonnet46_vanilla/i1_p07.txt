# Lost & Found in Osaka

The song stopped her mid-step.

Hana had been heading toward the used electronics shop on the corner — the one with the dusty Korg MS-20 she'd been visiting like a sick relative for three weeks now — when the opening riff cut through the ambient noise of Den Den Town and planted her feet on the pavement. She knew the melody immediately. *Cruel Angel's Thesis.* But not the way she'd heard it before. Whoever was playing it had bent the thing sideways, stretched it into something rawer, the notes bleeding at the edges.

She followed the sound.

The busker was maybe seventeen, eighteen, stationed near the arcade entrance where the crowd was thickest. She had short hair tucked behind one ear and a battered Telecaster slung low, and she played with her eyes half-closed like she was listening to something happening slightly ahead of the music. A open guitar case sat at her feet with a modest scatter of coins and a few folded bills.

Hana stopped at what she calculated to be a non-intrusive distance — approximately four meters — and listened.

The girl finished with a flourish, bending the final note until it almost broke, then grinning at the small cluster of people who'd gathered. A few clapped. Most drifted away. The busker crouched to check her case and that's when she looked up and caught Hana still standing there, oversized headphones pushed back on her head like a crooked halo.

"You sticking around for the next one?"

Hana's headphones slid forward. She pushed them back. "I — sorry. I wasn't — I mean, I was just passing."

"You've been standing there for like four minutes."

"Three," Hana said, then immediately wished she hadn't.

The busker laughed, but not unkindly. "Okay, three. I'm Reina." She straightened up and slung the guitar strap off her shoulder with the casual ease of someone who'd done it ten thousand times. "You know that song?"

"*Zankoku na Tenshi no These.*" Hana's headphones slid again. She caught them this time. "From *Neon Genesis Evangelion.* 1995. Composed by Ohtaka Hidetoshi, lyrics by Oikawa Neko. The original arrangement is in D major but you were playing it in—" She stopped. "Sorry. That was a lot."

Reina tilted her head. "No, keep going. What key?"

"B minor. Which is — it makes it sound more — I don't know. Unresolved. Like it doesn't want to finish."

"*Yes.*" Reina pointed at her with one finger, delighted. "That's exactly it. I've been trying to explain that to people all day and nobody—" She laughed again. "Okay, what's your name?"

"Hana."

"Hana. What are you doing in Den Den Town?"

"There's a synthesizer." She hesitated. "At a shop. A Korg MS-20. It's used but the patch bay is intact and the filter still — it doesn't matter."

"It clearly matters. You lit up when you said *patch bay.*"

Hana's headphones slid. She grabbed them. "I did not."

"Little bit." Reina crouched again and began tucking her earnings into a small zip pouch. "You play?"

"Synthesizers. Mostly. I don't — it's not like performing. I just make things at home. Textures, mostly. Layered stuff." She was aware she was describing it badly. "It's not interesting."

"You literally just described my favorite kind of music." Reina stood, slinging her guitar case over her shoulder. "Do you know Midori Takada?"

Hana blinked. "*Through the Looking Glass?* 1983?"

"I've been sampling the rain section for six months trying to figure out what makes it—"

"The space," Hana said immediately. "It's what she doesn't play. The intervals between the hits are doing more work than the hits themselves."

Reina stared at her for a moment. "Okay. I'm buying you sushi."

"What?"

"There's a kaitenzushi place, like two minutes from here. You're going to keep talking and I'm going to keep listening and we're going to eat conveyor belt sushi." She started walking, apparently assuming Hana would follow. "You like salmon?"

Hana stood still for a second. Then she followed. "I like tuna."

"Tuna's fine. Classic. Respectable."

"You don't have to buy me—"

"I made forty-two hundred yen today playing anime songs to tourists. I think I can cover tuna." Reina glanced back at her. "Do you always wear headphones outside?"

"They help with — it's loud. In Den Den Town. There's a lot of—" She searched for the word. "Input."

"Input." Reina seemed to taste the word. "I like that. I'm the opposite. I get weird if it's too quiet." She held the door of the restaurant open. The warm smell of rice vinegar and dashi rolled out to meet them. "Like my brain starts eating itself."

They found seats at the counter. The conveyor belt moved past them in a slow, meditative loop — small plates of nigiri, maki, tamago, the occasional ambitious dessert.

"So the MS-20," Reina said, grabbing a plate of salmon without hesitation. "How much are they asking?"

"Thirty-eight thousand yen."

Reina winced. "Ouch."

"I have twenty-six." Hana watched a plate of tuna pass and took it carefully. "I've been going back to check it's still there. Which is—" She set the plate down. "A little pathetic."

"It's not pathetic. It's devotion." Reina said it simply, without performance. "There's a difference."

Hana looked at her. Reina was already reaching for another plate, completely unbothered, and there was something about the easiness of it — the way she said true things like they cost nothing — that made Hana's shoulders drop approximately two centimeters.

Her headphones slid forward.

This time she didn't fix them.

"The rain section," she said. "In the Takada record. I think I know what she's doing with the tuning."

Reina set down her chopsticks and turned to face her fully. "Tell me everything."
