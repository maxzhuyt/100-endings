# **The Watch That Ate Tomorrow**
## Chapter Seven: Tick, Tick, Older

The watch showed up in Marcus's locker on a Monday.

No note. No box. Just a sleek black smartwatch sitting on top of his math textbook like it had always belonged there, its face glowing a soft, pulsing blue.

Marcus Chen, twelve years old and perpetually late for everything, strapped it on immediately.

The display was strange — no fitness tracker, no notification alerts. Just a single button on the side and two words on the screen:

**REWIND AVAILABLE**

He figured it was some kind of game app. He'd deal with it later.

Later came faster than expected.

---

Third period. Mr. Donovan's science class. Marcus had spent six nights building his model of the solar system, and he'd left it on the kitchen counter.

He watched Mr. Donovan write a fat red zero in his gradebook and felt his stomach drop through the floor.

*This is the worst day of my life*, Marcus thought.

Then the watch buzzed.

**REWIND AVAILABLE — 3 HOURS**

Almost without thinking, Marcus pressed the button.

The world *lurched*.

Like someone had grabbed the morning by the collar and yanked it backward. Colors smeared. Voices ran in reverse — a garbled, underwater sound. Marcus felt a strange warmth crawl up his arms, his neck, behind his ears.

Then everything snapped back into place.

He was standing at his kitchen counter. The solar system model sat right in front of him, Saturn's rings slightly crooked but gloriously *present*. His backpack was open. His mom was humming in the other room.

Marcus grabbed the model, shoved it carefully into a paper bag, and grinned so wide his face hurt.

Third period: A-plus.

---

He used it again on Wednesday.

He'd tripped in the cafeteria — full lunch tray, chocolate milk, the whole catastrophe — directly in front of Priya Sharma, who he'd been trying to impress since September. The entire cafeteria had erupted.

**REWIND AVAILABLE — 45 MINUTES**

*Press.*

*Lurch. Warmth. Snap.*

He walked around the wet floor this time. Sat down smoothly. Priya smiled at him.

Perfect.

He didn't notice, when he looked in the bathroom mirror that afternoon, that his jaw looked slightly sharper than it had that morning. Didn't notice his sneakers felt a little tighter.

---

By Friday, he'd used it four more times.

Avoided a pop quiz he hadn't studied for. Unsaid something cruel to his best friend Devon that he'd immediately regretted. Caught a fly ball in gym that would have smacked him in the face.

Each rewind felt warmer than the last. Each one left him with a strange hollow feeling, like something had been scooped out of him — something small, but something real.

Friday afternoon, Devon stopped walking and stared at him.

"Dude," Devon said. "Did you... grow?"

Marcus laughed. "Since *Tuesday*?"

"I'm just saying." Devon squinted. "You look different."

Marcus checked his reflection in a store window as they walked home.

His face looked back at him — but older. The soft roundness was gone. His features were harder, more defined. There was a shadow along his jaw that definitely hadn't been there Monday. He was taller. He was *sure* he was taller.

His voice, when he said "what is happening," came out deeper than he expected.

The watch pulsed blue.

---

He barely slept. By Saturday morning, his mom had knocked on his door twice asking if he was feeling okay. His clothes from last week were too small. His reflection showed someone who looked fifteen, maybe sixteen — not twelve.

*Each use is aging me*, he realized, sitting on his bed with his heart hammering. *It's not giving me time back. It's taking mine.*

He should have stopped there.

He would have stopped there, except that Saturday afternoon, while he was trying to figure out what to do, Devon called him, panicked.

"Marcus, something's wrong. My little sister — she was supposed to be at soccer practice, but she never came home, and now nobody can find her, and the last thing I said to her was—" Devon's voice cracked. "I told her I wished she'd never been born. I was so *mean* to her and now she's just *gone* and—"

Marcus looked at the watch.

**REWIND AVAILABLE — 6 HOURS**

Six hours. That would take them back to before Devon's sister had left for practice. Before whatever had happened, happened.

He pressed the button.

---

The lurch this time was violent.

Not a smooth snap-back. The world tore sideways. Colors didn't just smear — they *shattered*, like a windshield hit by a stone, and Marcus felt the warmth become a roaring heat, felt something wrenching in his chest, felt years peeling off him like—

*No. Not off him. Off everything.*

He landed hard on his knees in the middle of his street.

The street was wrong.

The Pattersons' house on the corner was gone — replaced by an empty lot with a rusted fence. The oak tree Devon had carved their initials into was a sapling, barely four feet tall. A car rolled past that Marcus recognized from old movies.

He'd gone back too far. Way too far.

And Devon's sister — he didn't know if she existed yet. He didn't know if *he* existed yet.

The watch screen flickered.

**REWIND AVAILABLE — WARNING: CRITICAL INSTABILITY**

Marcus stood up on shaking legs. His hands were a twelve-year-old's hands again — soft, small. Whatever years he'd burned through, the big rewind had consumed them.

He didn't know whether to feel relieved or terrified.

A sound made him turn.

A stray orange cat sat on the rusted fence, watching him with amber eyes that were entirely too calm, entirely too *knowing* for an animal. It had been outside his school every single time he'd rewound. He'd noticed it without noticing it.

It blinked slowly.

Like it had seen this before.

Like it had seen *him* before.

Marcus looked down at the watch. The blue pulse was faster now, almost frantic.

He had one rewind left.

Maybe.
