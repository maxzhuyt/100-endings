# Blood Debt

The torch had burned for an hour before either man moved.

It stood jammed into a crack in the altar stone, its flame bending and straightening with the drafts that crept through the temple's broken walls. The light it threw was unreliable—orange and amber washing across the faces of thirty spectators pressed shoulder to shoulder along the crumbling colonnade, their breath rising in pale columns into the cold dark above.

Kenji Muro stood at the northern end of the cleared floor, twelve meters of cracked flagstone between him and his opponent. He wore his school's deep indigo, sleeves bound tight at the wrist. His right hand rested on the hilt of the short wooden practice blade tucked through his belt—not because he intended to draw it, but because stillness was a performance, and Jigen-ryū men understood performance. His feet were set at shoulder width, weight slightly forward. Ready to explode. That was the art's essential truth: you did not wait for the fight to come to you. You arrived before it did.

Across the flagstones, Wei Longshan rolled his neck once, twice, and the joints popped audibly. Someone in the crowd laughed nervously. Wei wore no school colors—a rough grey training jacket, trousers tucked into worn boots. He looked like a man who had walked in off a road somewhere and decided to stop here. He stood with his feet wider than his shoulders, arms hanging loose and slightly forward, palms open. His weight shifted fractionally side to side, a barely perceptible sway, as though the ground beneath him were a boat deck.

Neither man had agreed to rules. Neither man had asked.

The torch guttered. A coin hit the stone floor somewhere in the crowd.

Kenji moved.

He covered the first four meters in a single driving burst, right foot launching off the flagstone with a crack that echoed off the walls. His wooden blade came free of his belt in the same motion—Jigen-ryū's foundational draw, the kesagiri diagonal cut aimed at Wei's left collarbone, executed with the full torque of his hips and shoulders behind it. The speed of it was extraordinary. Spectators on the colonnade flinched backward.

Wei pivoted left on his rear foot, letting his upper body tilt away. The blade passed six inches from his shoulder. Before Kenji's arm had completed its arc, Wei's right hand was already moving—a low, whipping backfist that came from below his own hip in a rising circle, catching Kenji's extended forearm just above the wrist. Not a block. A redirect. The impact knocked Kenji's blade-arm further across his own centerline, spinning his momentum sideways.

Kenji absorbed it, pivoted hard on his left foot, and drove a straight elbow back toward Wei's face.

Wei slipped under it, ducking right, and the elbow sheared past his ear. He was already behind Kenji's right shoulder.

Kenji spun to face him.

They stood four feet apart now, breathing.

The crowd had gone completely silent.

Kenji reset his stance, weight forward again, blade held low and angled. His jaw was tight. The redirect had told him something—Wei's hands were faster than they looked, and the angle had been wrong, unexpected, coming from below when every trained reflex said the counter would arrive laterally. He filed it.

Wei circled left. His footwork was small and constant, weight never fully committed to either side, arms drifting in loose arcs at his flanks. He looked relaxed in a way that was deeply deliberate.

Kenji feinted high and drove forward with a two-handed overhead strike, the wooden blade aimed at the crown of Wei's skull—pure Jigen-ryū aggression, designed to force a collision of force against force, where Kenji's explosive drive would win.

Wei stepped inside it.

He moved forward and left simultaneously, slipping past the blade's path entirely, and his left arm came up in a long whipping arc—elbow leading, forearm following like a chain—that wrapped around the outside of Kenji's right arm at the moment of full extension. The impact was enormous. Kenji felt his shoulder wrench, felt the blade leave his hand and clatter across the flagstones into the dark. Wei's momentum carried him through, and his right palm drove hard into Kenji's chest, open-handed, the force of it concentrated through the heel.

Kenji went back three steps and hit a stone pillar.

Dust fell from the ceiling. The torch flame bent sideways.

He pushed off the pillar immediately, left hand coming up to cover his face, and drove forward again—no blade now, just fists and elbows, the close-quarters ferocity that Jigen-ryū permitted when the sword was lost. A right hammer fist toward Wei's temple, then a left elbow cutting horizontally at his jaw.

Wei caught the hammer fist on his forearm, deflecting it outward, and the elbow grazed his cheekbone—the first clean contact Kenji had landed. Wei's head snapped sideways. He stepped back once, twice, circling away, and touched his cheek with two fingers. He looked at them. No blood. He lowered his hand.

Kenji pressed forward, throwing a left jab to close the distance.

Wei let the jab come, slipping his head outside it, and his right arm swung in a massive descending arc—a piguaquan chopping strike, the whole arm rotating from the shoulder like a flail—that came down across the back of Kenji's extended left arm and shoulder simultaneously. The impact drove Kenji's arm down and folded him forward at the waist.

Wei stepped around him and swept his rear leg.

Kenji hit the flagstones face-first. The impact drove the air from his lungs. He lay still, cheek against cold stone, staring at the torch flame from floor level. It wavered. Straightened.

Wei stood over him, breathing hard, arms still loose at his sides.

Neither man spoke.

The crowd did not cheer. They watched the torch burn.
