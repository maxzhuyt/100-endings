# Overthinking It

The projector squeaks again and I count it as a reason not to look up.

That's four squeaks since Mr. Hendricks announced the partners. Four opportunities I've used to study the grain of my lab table instead of turning to acknowledge that Jordan Vasquez is now sitting fourteen inches to my left and has been for approximately three minutes and I have said exactly nothing.

*Say something.*

Okay. Yes. I will say something. I've been saying things to people my entire life, I know how it works, there's a mouth and there are words and you release them into the air in a sequence that approximates meaning. Simple. Fine. I'll do that.

*What do you say to someone you've been mentally rehearsing conversations with for six weeks?*

Not that. Definitely not that.

Here's what I've prepared. I've had since Monday, when Priya told me the partners list was already posted outside the bio room, to construct an opening. I spent Monday night drafting it. I spent Tuesday refining it. The version I landed on is casual but not aggressively casual, which is a real distinction that I believe in. Something like: *Oh, ecosystems unit—I actually read ahead a little, so I have some ideas if you want to start with the food web diagram.* 

That's good. That's a real thing a normal person says. It establishes that I'm prepared without being weird about it. It opens a door for him to walk through conversationally. It's not a compliment, which would be too much, but it's not cold either. I've said it to myself in the mirror and it sounds like a sentence.

The projector squeaks.

*Say it. Right now. Ecosystems unit, I read ahead—*

"So," Jordan says.

I look up. This is a mistake because he's already looking at me and now we're making eye contact, which I was not ready for, and my prepared sentence has evacuated my brain completely. There's nothing in there. Just the word *so*, reverberating.

"So," I say back.

Great. Excellent. I've achieved a perfect echo. I've contributed *so* to this relationship.

He's smiling a little, which could mean anything. It could mean he thinks I'm funny, or it could mean he's noticed that I said *so* back to him like a broken toy and he's smiling the way you smile at something sad. I cannot determine which from available data.

"You want to do the food web or the energy pyramid first?" he asks.

*The food web. I read ahead. I have ideas.* "Either one is fine with me," I say.

I want to reach into my own mouth and take it back. *Either one is fine with me* is the verbal equivalent of a white wall. It is the least interesting thing a person can contribute to a decision. I had a preference. I had a whole sentence about my preference. I surrendered it because the moment arrived and I couldn't tell if expressing a preference would seem like I was being controlling, and now he has to make the decision, which means I've made him do more work, which means—

"Food web, then," he says, pulling out his notebook. His notebook has a sticker of a mountain range on the cover. I've noticed this before, from across the room, which sounds more alarming than it is. I notice things. It's just how I'm built.

I open my own notebook. I've already labeled a page *Ecosystems Project* in my neatest handwriting and I immediately regret this because what if he sees it and thinks I'm the kind of person who pre-labels pages, which I am, but I don't necessarily want that to be the first thing he learns about me.

I angle the notebook slightly away.

*Stop. You're angling your notebook away from your project partner. That's insane.*

I angle it back.

The projector squeaks through a slide about trophic levels. Mr. Hendricks is explaining producers and primary consumers and I'm absorbing maybe forty percent of it because the other sixty percent of my brain is running a continuous audit of my posture, my expression, the exact position of my hands, whether I've been quiet for too long, whether the last thing I said was too agreeable, whether agreeable is actually fine and I'm pathologizing a totally normal interaction, whether—

"You're in accelerated English, right?" Jordan asks.

I look up again. He's not looking at his notebook. He's looking at me, and there's something genuinely curious in it, and I have about one second to respond before the pause becomes a thing.

"Yeah," I say.

*Say more. He asked you a question, that means he wants information, give him information.*

"With Ms. Okafor," I add.

He nods. "She's supposed to be really good."

"She is." *She's actually extraordinary. She made us read Octavia Butler in September and I wrote twelve pages on it when the assignment was four and she didn't penalize me, she just wrote 'you have a lot to say' in the margin and I've thought about that almost every day since.* "We've been doing a lot of science fiction," I say.

"Cool." He seems like he means it.

There's a silence. Not a terrible one. A regular one, the kind that exists between people who are about to do something, like start a project. But I spend it cataloguing everything I didn't say. The twelve pages. The margin note. The part of me that is apparently enormous and loud and completely inaccessible the moment another person enters the room.

I look down at my pre-labeled notebook page.

*Ecosystems Project.*

Jordan's pen clicks open beside me. "Okay," he says, "so producers. Plants, algae—what else?"

"Phytoplankton," I say, and I say it immediately, without checking it first, and it's the right answer.

He writes it down.

The projector squeaks.

I keep going.
