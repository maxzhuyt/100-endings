# The Lighthouse Keeper

---

Stoer Head Lighthouse, Sutherland
12th October

Dear Mum,

Arrived safe Tuesday. The ferry was fine and there was a man from the board who met me at Lochinver with the van. The accommodation is solid — two rooms, own kitchen, electric that runs off the generator which I am now responsible for. The light itself I won't attempt to describe till I understand it better, only that it is every inch the job I was trained for and then some.

You can stop worrying. I know you will not, but you can.

The view from the lantern room goes on longer than seems right. There is a stack out in the water to the north — they call it the Old Man of Stoer. Sixty feet of red sandstone standing straight up out of the sea like it was put there on purpose. I can see it from the window of my room. Good to have a fixed point.

Wages first Friday. I will send something home then.

Your loving son,
Callum

---

Stoer Head
9th November

Dear Mum,

The fog horn ran fourteen straight hours last Monday, which is a thing you get used to apparently. Tuesday clear. Wednesday fog again. The log wants entering every four hours; I have been doing it on the sixes: six, ten, two, six, ten, two. Makes the day into a shape you can follow.

You asked about Christmas. I would need to look into what the relief schedule allows. There is a process for it. I will look into it.

The work is honest and the pay is honest and there are worse postings. Kinnaird Head is known to be grim. This is not grim.

The Old Man looks different in November. Goes dark earlier than the surrounding water, if that makes sense. I watched it a while last night after the six o'clock log. I don't know why I am telling you that.

Your loving son,
Callum

---

Stoer Head
3rd December

Dear Mum,

Shorter one tonight. Tired.

The supply boat came Tuesday — a man called Morrison from Lochinver, doesn't say much, drops the cans and the post and the stores and that's him gone inside forty minutes, weather depending. He was away by quarter past ten. The afternoon was very long after that.

I want to ask you something and it is a strange thing to ask. Do you still hang the washing on the pulley in the kitchen when it's too wet outside? I have been thinking about the smell of that. The damp wool coming off the radiator. I don't know why that is in my head.

I am eating fine. Sleeping fine.

The Christmas relief has a waiting list. I am on it.

Your loving son,
Callum

---

Stoer Head
21st January

Dear Mum,

I am not going to write what you think I am going to write, which is that everything is fine and not to worry. Instead I will say it is hard in January and I knew it would be hard in January and I am getting on with it. Which is not the same as fine but is closer to the truth.

The thing I didn't tell you: there was a week in December, the week before Christmas, where I did not sleep more than three or four hours a night. Not because of the work. Just because the dark comes in under the door somehow, even when the light is turning. I should have told you then. I'm telling you now. It passed.

The Old Man is almost invisible in the winter haar. Some mornings I go to the window expecting it and cannot find it and there is something in that I don't have the vocabulary for.

I am sleeping now. That is true.

Your loving son,
Callum

---

Stoer Head
14th February

Mum,

I don't have much.

Last week the sea came up over the rocks and through the night I could feel the weight of it in the floor of this room, through the bed, the way you feel a lorry passing when you're lying down. It ran on for hours. The light does what the light does regardless and I was doing what I do regardless and somewhere around four in the morning I sat on the floor of the lantern room and watched the beam go out over the water into nothing and I thought: this is as far as I can go with this.

I am not in any danger. I want to be clear about that.
