# Prison Strings

---

The light in corridor D has been dying for three weeks. Nobody fixes it. It stutters in four-second intervals — on, off, on — and the shadows it throws are wrong in ways that don't have names yet. Jolyne Cujoh walks the corridor like she owns it, which she doesn't, but ownership was never really the point. She reads the walls. She reads the air. She reads the particular quality of silence that means something ahead of her isn't behaving the way silence should.

She's already unwinding the first thread before she sees the woman.

---

Soft footsteps. That's the first thing. No prison employee walks softly here; they stomp, announce themselves, perform authority with their shoes. These footsteps barely register. Jolyne stops.

The woman coming around the corner is dressed in black, unhurried, and smiling in the way that people smile when they genuinely cannot see a reason not to. She looks completely at ease. That's the wrongness. Nobody is at ease here. The facility holds three inmates currently manifesting abilities that the guards have been instructed to classify as medical episodes. The woman in black doesn't look like she works here. She doesn't look like she's afraid of anything that does.

Jolyne's jaw sets. Her eyes go flat and still.

"Jolyne," the woman says. Not *hey you.* Not *inmate.* Her name, said warmly, like they've met before.

---

"The hell are you smiling at?"

The woman tilts her head. Her eyes are a color that isn't quite right — ringed, the pupils sitting at the center of something concentric and too deliberate to be natural. "You," she says simply. "I've been curious about you for some time. Stone Free is remarkable. The integration alone—"

"You want something." Jolyne doesn't move. Her left hand hangs loose at her side, and the string moving from her fingertip along the baseboard is invisible to anyone not looking for it. She keeps the woman's eyes on her face. "So stop complimenting my Stand and say it."

The woman — she hasn't offered a name, which is information — pauses, but the smile doesn't. She examines Jolyne the way someone examines a coin they've pulled from a jar. Not hostile. Cataloguing. "I wanted to see you in your environment. You adapt extraordinarily well to constraint." A beat. "Most people, under these conditions, become small."

"Good for them," Jolyne says. "And?"

---

The string moves along the wall, finds the corner, continues. Jolyne talks.

The woman talks back. She asks questions about Stone Free framed as admiration and they're still questions, still probing, and Jolyne answers none of them directly, redirects with profanity where necessary, keeps her own eyes steady. The light flickers. On, off, on.

Then: "You shouldn't be here, you know." The woman says it gently. "The conviction — the circumstances. I've looked into it. There are people who could review your case. The right word in the right place and you walk out of here."

And there it is.

Jolyne feels it — the pull. Not toward the woman, but toward the thing she's dangling. Freedom. Her mother. Sunlight on pavement that isn't being watched. She feels it the way you feel a tide pulling at your ankles and she recognizes it for exactly what it is, which is why she shuts it down hard, and the shutting down feels like closing a fist.

"No," she says.

"I only—"

"You're not offering me anything." Jolyne's voice goes up, clear and sharp and carrying. "You want me to owe you something. That's what you're actually doing. And I'm telling you right now, in this corridor, that I won't." She points. "And stop looking at me like I'm a dog you're deciding whether to keep."

For a moment something behind the woman's eyes does something. Not hurt. Not anger. Something that files a record.

---

The smile holds. But the register drops, just slightly, just enough. Just enough to let the underneath show.

It's vast and cold in there. Jolyne has looked at dangerous things before. She has stood in front of monsters who announced their monstrousness with sound and light. This woman stands in front of her and says nothing more and the nothing is a door opening onto something that doesn't have a floor.

She isn't afraid of getting hit. That registers in Jolyne's chest as tactical information: landing a punch on this woman won't mean what landing a punch is supposed to mean. The usual math doesn't apply.

But the strings are in position.

---

Jolyne moves.

The strings snap taut through the corridor at eleven angles, and what they catch isn't the woman — it's what the woman brought with her without announcing it: a presence coiled near the security door, wearing the shape of the guard badly, the way a coat gets worn when there's no body inside it. The strings pull it into visibility, force it into the light, hold it there.

The woman's smile stays exactly where it is. But her eyes change. The concentric rings orient toward Jolyne with something that is, in its way, the most genuine expression she's offered since entering the corridor.

Jolyne stands in the net of her own strings, breathing.

"People aren't assets," she says. "Figure that out."

---

The woman looks at the revealed thing without urgency, and then she looks back at Jolyne.

"You're much more interesting than the file suggested," she says. The soft voice is fully restored, pleasant, unhurried. She turns to leave. At the door she pauses. "You'll start noticing, soon, which of the people around you made choices. And which of them were chosen."

The door closes behind her. The footsteps don't make enough sound.

---

Jolyne stands alone.

The string retracts. The presence by the door loses its shape and disperses without theater, leaving nothing but the smell of something electrical and the particular quality of silence that has a name now.

The light flickers.

Then it holds.

So does she.
