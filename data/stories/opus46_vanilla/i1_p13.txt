# Blood Debt

The torch guttered in its rusted sconce, throwing a cone of trembling amber across the flagstones. Beyond that cone, the abandoned temple dissolved into darkness—broken rafters overhead like the ribs of some dead leviathan, walls scabbed with moss and time. The air tasted of wet stone and old incense, and beneath that, the copper-bright tension of two dozen bodies pressed shoulder to shoulder along the perimeter, their breath visible in the autumn chill.

Katō Sōma stood at the northern edge of the clearing, feet planted in the narrow stance of Jigen-ryū, his weight loaded over his rear leg like a drawn bowstring. He wore no armor—just a sweat-darkened training gi, sleeves torn away at the shoulders to free the deltoids. His bokken, a red-oak replica weighted heavier than a live blade, rested in a high guard above his right ear, tip angled forward at forty-five degrees, edge aligned with the invisible center line between himself and the man twelve feet south. His jaw was a slab of clenched bone. His eyes did not blink.

Fang Yucheng occupied the southern margin barefoot, bouncing on the balls of his feet with the restless rhythm of a man who had learned to fight in alleyways before he ever saw the inside of a proper school. His arms hung long at his sides, palms open, fingers loose—deceptively slack. He wore a thin cotton changshan with the sleeves rolled past the elbows, the fabric already darkened at the sternum with sweat. His shoulders rolled in small, oiled circles, loading and unloading the fascial chains that ran from scapula to fingertip. Piguaquan lived in those chains. Every strike would come from the back, whip through the shoulder, and arrive at the hand like the crack at the end of a towel—faster than the eye could parse.

Between them, a ceramic tile had been placed on the flagstones. When it broke, the duel began.

A boy from the Katō faction knelt, raised a stone, and smashed it down.

The tile split with a sound like a knuckle cracking.

Sōma exploded forward.

There was no feint, no probing jab, no cautious circling. Jigen-ryū did not believe in preamble. He closed the twelve feet in three lunging strides, rear foot driving off the stone with a scrape that echoed through the ruin, and brought the bokken screaming down in a diagonal cut aimed at the junction of Yucheng's left neck and shoulder—*kesa-giri*, the monk's robe cut, designed to cleave collarbone and continue through the ribcage. The oak displaced air with an audible hiss.

Yucheng was already not there.

His rear foot swept behind him in a curving backstep that carried his torso just outside the arc of the descending blade. The bokken's tip kissed the air three inches from his chest and cracked against the flagstone where he'd stood, sending a chip of granite spinning into the dark. In the same motion—and this was the art's genius, the seamless welding of evasion to offense—Yucheng's right arm rose behind him, shoulder hyperextending, and then whipped forward in a looping overhand that traveled not in a straight line but in a vicious crescent. His open palm connected with the side of Sōma's jaw just below the ear.

The sound was flat and dense, like a plank dropped on concrete.

Sōma's head snapped sideways. His vision stuttered. But his body had been forged in ten thousand repetitions of *uchikomi*—the obsessive, monastic striking drills that defined Jigen-ryū—and his hands did not release the bokken. He let the momentum of the slap rotate his torso, channeled that rotation into a rising backhand cut aimed at Yucheng's lead knee from below.

Yucheng read the hip rotation and leaped, tucking his knees, and the bokken swept beneath his shins with a whistle. He landed two feet to Sōma's right, now flanking him, and immediately launched a left pigu chop—the signature falling axe-hand—targeting the trapezius muscle between neck and shoulder. His body folded at the waist to generate the whipcrack, his left heel stamping the stone for grounding force.

The chop landed. Sōma's left shoulder dropped as the nerve cluster beneath the trapezius screamed. His left hand momentarily loosened on the bokken's haft. A gasp rippled through the Japanese contingent pressed against the eastern wall. Money rustled. Someone swore.

But Sōma surged inward rather than retreating—*irimi*, entering, the samurai's answer to pain: more proximity, more violence. He rammed his forehead into Yucheng's nose. Cartilage flattened. Blood sprayed in a black mist across the torchlight. Yucheng staggered backward, heels catching on a broken flagstone, and for one critical half-second his weight sat over his heels with nowhere to go.

Sōma raised the bokken overhead in *jōdan-no-kamae*, the high fire guard, both hands locked, and brought it straight down the center line—a vertical splitting cut aimed at the crown of Yucheng's skull. This was the killing stroke of Jigen-ryū. The one they drilled against wooden posts until the posts splintered. The one that had ended duels for three hundred years.

Yucheng did the only thing his body knew to do when cornered: he twisted sideways and dropped his weight, converting his stumble into a low spinning crouch. The bokken grazed his scalp—he felt the oak part his hair, felt the wind of it on his skin—and hammered into the flagstone behind him with a crack that numbed Sōma's wrists to the elbows.

From the crouch, Yucheng uncoiled.

His right arm swept upward from below in a rising backfist that traveled along the same whipping circular path as every piguaquan strike—shoulder, elbow, wrist, knuckles arriving last and fastest. The back of his fist connected with Sōma's chin from underneath, snapping his teeth together with a porcelain click and lifting him onto his toes.

Sōma's eyes rolled white. His grip failed. The bokken clattered to the stone.

He swayed—one second, two—then folded sideways and hit the flagstones with the boneless finality of a man whose consciousness had left before his body understood.

Silence filled the temple like floodwater.

Then the Chinese contingent erupted, and Fang Yucheng stood bleeding over his fallen enemy, chest heaving, and felt nothing at all like victory.
