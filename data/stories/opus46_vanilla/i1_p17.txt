# Court of the Dreaming: Territorial Dream Dispute

# *In the Matter of First Love v. Mora*

# *Case No. 7,841,229,003 — Contested Dreamscape (Minor, Age 14)*

---

The Throne of Dream is carved from a single unfinished thought. Its occupant sat upon it now with the particular stillness of someone who had been listening to arguments for what felt like an eternity but had, in fact, been only eleven minutes.

Eleven very long minutes.

"Let me be certain I understand the complaint," said Dream. His voice carried the weight of every night since the first night. "You are alleging trespass."

First Love stood at the petitioner's lectern, which was made of petrified sighs. He was a small entity, round-faced, perpetually flushed, dressed in what appeared to be a school uniform from no particular era. He clutched his case notes to his chest like a shield.

"Trespass, sabotage, and—and *creative vandalism*, my Lord." His voice cracked on the last word. It always cracked. That was rather the point of him. "I spent *weeks* on that dreamscape. The wildflower field alone took me four nights to seed. Do you know how difficult it is to get the exact ratio of buttercups to forget-me-nots that evokes first romantic longing without tipping into hay fever anxiety?"

"I do not," said Dream.

"Very difficult. And the girl—the *rendering* of the girl. I modeled her from seventeen different fragments of the boy's waking impressions. The way she tucks her hair behind her ear. The way she laughs at things that aren't funny. The specific confusion of wanting to hold someone's hand but not knowing which hand or how hands even work anymore—"

"We are familiar with your craft," Dream said. "Proceed to the grievance."

First Love pointed a trembling finger at the respondent's lectern.

Mora sat there—or rather, *perched* there, because Mora did not sit on things so much as settle upon them with intent. She was old. Older than First Love by several geological epochs. She had the shape of a woman the way a bruise has the shape of a fist—you could see the outline of what caused it. Her hair was long and dark and moved independently of any breeze. She was smiling. She had been smiling since the proceedings began. It was not a reassuring smile.

She emitted a faint cackle. It echoed uncomfortably through the vaulted ceilings, bouncing between obsidian columns before settling somewhere in the back of everyone's teeth.

"She *ate* my almost-kiss," First Love said, with the devastation of someone reporting a war crime. "She took the moment—the *pinnacle* moment, my Lord, where the girl leans in and the boy can smell her shampoo and his heart is doing that thing where it forgets it's a heart—and she *corrupted* it."

"I improved it," Mora said.

"You turned the girl into a *chest-crushing sleep paralysis demon*."

"I turned the girl into *me*. There is a difference." Another cackle. The ceiling flinched. "The boy didn't seem to mind. Not entirely."

Dream's left eye twitched. It was a microscopic movement, invisible to most beings in the Dreaming, but his raven Matthew, perched on the back of the throne, saw it and quietly shuffled three inches further away.

"Elaborate," Dream said, in a tone that suggested he very much did not want her to.

Mora leaned forward. "The dreamscape was already about a girl climbing on top of him. I simply... *redirected the energy*. The boy is fourteen. He doesn't know what he wants. One moment it's buttercups and shy glances, the next it's—" she gestured vaguely at herself, "—something with more *weight* to it. I provide weight. I provide *gravity*. I sit on his chest and I take a little breath, and he wakes up terrified and confused and—" she paused, selecting her next word with care, "—*alive*. Very, very alive."

"She's feeding on a minor!" First Love shrieked.

"A girl's gotta eat too, you know?" Mora said, examining a nail that appeared to be made of condensed dread. "You think I *enjoy* squatting in some teenager's wildflower fantasy? The ambiance is terrible. Everything smells like body spray and desperation. But the pickings are slim these days. Adults take pills. They drink. They stare at their little glowing rectangles until they fall into dreamless nothing. Children are all that's left for honest working nightmares."

She looked directly at Dream.

"I am *old*, my Lord. I was old when the Slavs still left milk out for me. I was old when they knew my name. Now I am old and hungry and I take what I can get."

The courtroom was silent.

Dream regarded both parties. First Love, trembling with righteous indignation, a smear of pollen on his cheek. Mora, ancient and unapologetic, trailing shadow like a bridal veil.

"I have reached a decision," Dream said.

Both entities straightened.

"The dreamscape shall be restored to its original state."

First Love exhaled.

"With one amendment."

First Love stopped exhaling.

"The almost-kiss," Dream said, and something flickered behind his eyes that might, in a lesser being, have been called amusement, "shall remain an almost-kiss. *Permanently*. The boy will never reach it. He will approach it endlessly, and it will recede. This is, after all, the nature of first love."

First Love opened his mouth. Closed it. Opened it again. "That's... that's *cruel*, my Lord."

"It is *formative*," Dream corrected. "As for Mora."

The nightmare spirit tilted her head.

"You may visit the boy on the third Thursday of each month. You will not borrow another's dreamscape. You will craft your own. And you will leave the girl out of it entirely."

Mora considered this. "The third Thursday."

"The third Thursday."

"And if I get... *hungry* between Thursdays?"

"Then I suggest," said Dream, rising from the throne of unfinished thought, "you learn to cook something lighter."

He swept from the chamber. Behind him, very faintly, Mora cackled.

The ceiling said nothing, but it didn't care for it one bit.
