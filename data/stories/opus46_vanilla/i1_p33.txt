# The Last Outpost

## Chapter One: The Stranger's Tab

The thing about pouring drinks at the edge of nowhere is that you learn to read people before they open their mouths. Body language is universal — doesn't matter if someone's got two arms or six, mandibles or a mouth full of teeth like cathedral glass. Tension lives in the same places across every species I've encountered so far.

So when this stranger slides onto the stool at the far end of my bar — the stool nobody ever picks because it wobbles and sits right under the vent that blows cold — I notice.

"Vega whisky, neat. Better make that a double."

I reach for the bottle without breaking eye contact. Her tentacled dreads hang limp around her face, and two of them are knotted together in a way that looks accidental and probably painful. Keth-born, I think. Or at least Keth-adjacent. The iridescent skin at her temples is dull, which for their kind is like showing up somewhere with mascara streaked down both cheeks.

"Twelve credits," I say, setting the glass in front of her.

She taps her wrist chip against the counter sensor without flinching at the markup. That tells me something too.

"Running a tab?" I ask.

"Depends on how long I'm staying."

"Well, that depends on the next shuttle out. Which isn't until Thursday."

She looks up at me for the first time. Her eyes are that deep Keth violet, shot through with silver — striking, even exhausted as she is. "Thursday."

"Thursday."

"What day is it now?"

"Monday. Station-time, anyway. Doesn't mean much out here." I lean against the back counter and fold my arms. "You came in on the Drift Runner, didn't you? Goss's freighter?"

"You know everyone who docks here?"

"I know every *ship* that docks here. There are four berths. It's not exactly a feat of intelligence."

That gets the ghost of a smile. She takes a long pull of the whisky and closes her eyes. Behind me, I hear Pell laughing too loud at his own joke over at the corner table where he's losing at Hex — again — to Marta, who cheats with a serenity I genuinely admire.

"I'm Nets," I offer.

She opens her eyes. Hesitates. "Sable."

"That your real name?"

"Does it matter?"

"Not even slightly. Welcome to the Outpost, Sable."

She nods and goes back to her drink. I give her space, drifting down the bar to top off Ruuq's seltzer — he's been nursing the same glass for two hours while he reads something on his data pad, and I love him for his undemanding presence — and then I swing back around to check on the stranger because something is nagging at me. Something about the way she keeps glancing at the door.

"Expecting someone?" I ask.

"Hoping not to be."

I process that. "Okay. Should I be worried?"

"Probably not."

"That's not a no."

She sets her glass down. "Do you always interrogate your customers?"

"Only the ones who look like they haven't slept in three cycles and keep watching the door like it owes them money." I pour her a water without being asked and set it beside the whisky. "The Outpost is quiet. I'd like to keep it that way."

Sable wraps both hands around the water glass. Her fingers are long, the nails bitten ragged. "I'm not here to cause trouble."

"Nobody ever is. Trouble just packs itself in the luggage sometimes."

From across the room, there's a sharp crash — Pell's knocked his drink off the table gesticulating about some hand he swears he should've won. Glass shatters across the floor and Marta swears at him in two languages.

"Pell! That's the third one this month!" I shout without looking.

"I'll pay for it!" he shouts back, which we both know is a lie.

I grab the broom from behind the bar and toss it in his general direction. It clatters to the floor near his feet. He takes the hint.

When I turn back, Sable is watching me with something that might be amusement, or might be assessment.

"You run this place?" she asks.

"I tend bar. Harlan runs it, technically, but he's been planetside on Voss for a month getting his lungs recalibrated. So." I shrug. "I run this place."

She's quiet for a moment. Then: "I need to ask you something, and I need you to be honest."

"I'm always honest. It's one of my many flaws."

"Is there anywhere on this station to hide?"

The word drops between us like a stone into still water. I keep my face neutral — another thing you learn behind a bar at the edge of nowhere.

"Hide from what?"

"From whom." She corrects me softly, and the silver in her eyes catches the low light. "There's a ship that may come looking for me. A survey vessel called the *Meridian*. If they dock here and ask about a Keth woman traveling alone—"

"You were never here," I finish.

She searches my face. "Just like that?"

"I don't know your story, Sable. But I know that look. I wore it when I first showed up at this station three years ago." I rest my hands flat on the bar. "The Outpost has a long tradition of not asking questions and having very poor memories when strangers come knocking."

Something shifts in her expression. Relief, maybe. Or the dangerous beginning of trust.

"Thank you," she says.

"Don't thank me yet. If whoever's on that ship brings trouble through my door, you and I are going to have a much longer conversation." I nod at her glass. "Another?"

"Please."

I pour. She drinks. The Outpost hums its familiar hum around us — Pell sweeping glass, Marta shuffling cards, Ruuq turning a page, the ventilation system wheezing its eternal wheeze.

And somewhere out in the black, a ship called the *Meridian* is heading this way. I can feel it the way you feel a storm before the sky changes — just pressure, just instinct, just the quiet certainty that Thursday is going to be very interesting.

I really hate interesting.
