# The Watch That Steals Tomorrow

## Chapter 7: Borrowed Time

The watch appeared in Milo Huang's locker on a Monday, nestled between his pre-algebra textbook and a crumpled permission slip he'd forgotten to turn in three weeks ago.

It was matte black with a face that seemed to swallow light rather than reflect it. No brand name. No charging port. Just a single button on the side, shaped like an hourglass, and a screen that displayed the time in numbers that pulsed like a heartbeat.

Milo put it on because he was twelve and it was free.

---

The first rewind happened by accident.

He was standing at the front of Mrs. Dietrich's class, halfway through an oral report on the Louisiana Purchase when the word *acquisition* turned to cement in his mouth. He stammered. Someone giggled — Brianna Walsh, third row, the laugh she saved specifically for other people's humiliation. Milo's face went nuclear. His thumb found the hourglass button and pressed.

The classroom *lurched*.

Not physically. More like reality hiccupped. The fluorescent lights buzzed backward. Mrs. Dietrich's hand un-wrote a note on the whiteboard. Brianna's laugh sucked itself back between her teeth. And then Milo was sitting at his desk again, five minutes earlier, heart hammering, the report still ahead of him.

He gave the report again. Nailed it. Even remembered *acquisition*.

Walking out of class, he felt a strange itch along his jawline. He scratched it absently and didn't think about it again.

---

Outside the school's side entrance, a stray cat sat on the recycling bin. It was an ugly thing — orange and white, one ear torn, eyes the pale green of old copper. Milo had seen it around for weeks, skittering away whenever anyone got close.

Today it didn't skitter.

It stared at him with an expression that cats should not be capable of making.

*Recognition.*

"What?" Milo said.

The cat blinked slowly, then looked away, as if disappointed.

---

By Wednesday, Milo had used the watch four more times. He rewound a failed pop quiz. He rewound tripping up the stairs in front of the eighth graders. He rewound an argument with his best friend Dev where he'd said something cruel about Dev's parents' divorce — words he wanted to stuff back into his mouth the moment they escaped.

Each rewind felt easier. A press, a lurch, a second chance.

But Thursday morning, Milo looked in the bathroom mirror and froze.

There was hair on his upper lip. Not peach fuzz — actual, visible, *dark* hair. His jaw looked different too, sharper somehow, the soft roundness of his face reorganizing itself into angles that didn't belong to a seventh grader. He'd grown at least an inch overnight. His favorite jeans stopped above his ankles.

"Growth spurt," his mom said at breakfast, beaming. "Your father was the same way. Seemingly overnight!"

But his father hadn't grown overnight. Nobody did. Not like this.

At school, things felt different in ways Milo couldn't immediately name. Teachers spoke to him more directly, less gently. In the hallway, a sixth grader flinched out of his path. In the bathroom mirror under those merciless fluorescent lights, the face staring back looked fourteen. Maybe fifteen.

Dev squinted at him during lunch. "Dude, are you... taller?"

"I don't know."

"Your *voice*. Are you sick?"

Milo hadn't noticed his voice. He noticed it now — thicker, rougher, like someone had sanded the boyhood out of it while he slept.

The cat was waiting by the dumpsters when he stumbled outside for air. Same stare. Same awful knowingness. This time, Milo noticed something that made his stomach drop: a collar he hadn't seen before, half-hidden in matted fur. Hanging from it was a tiny hourglass charm.

Identical to the button on his watch.

---

Friday, Milo made the big mistake.

He was crossing Maple Street after school when Dev, walking beside him, stepped off the curb without looking. The car came from nowhere — a white SUV running the stop sign, moving too fast, and Dev was *right there* —

Milo slammed the button.

The world lurched. Harder this time. The sky flickered like a dying bulb. Milo felt something *tear* inside his chest, not pain exactly but loss, as though pages were being ripped from a book he hadn't finished reading.

He was back on the sidewalk. Thirty seconds earlier. He grabbed Dev's arm before he could step off the curb. The white SUV blasted through the intersection.

"Whoa," Dev breathed. "That guy almost —"

But Milo wasn't listening. He was staring across the street.

The school was *gone*.

Not destroyed. Just... absent. Where Westbrook Middle School had stood for forty years, there was an empty lot filled with gravel and weeds. The crossing guard was gone. The flag was gone. A faded sign read: **FUTURE SITE OF WESTBROOK MIDDLE SCHOOL — GROUNDBREAKING 2019.**

2019 was four years ago. The school had been built. Milo had *attended* it this morning.

His phone buzzed. A text from Mom: *Who is this? I think you have the wrong number.*

Milo's hands were shaking. He looked down at them and saw knuckles that were too large, veins that were too visible, and — oh God — a single grey hair curling on his wrist.

He looked up. Dev was gone. The sidewalk was empty.

The cat sat on a fire hydrant across the street, watching him with those ancient copper eyes. It opened its mouth, and for one horrifying second, Milo was certain it was going to speak.

It didn't.

It didn't have to. Its expression said everything: *You can undo this. You know the cost.*

Milo looked at the watch. The pulsing numbers on its face had changed. They weren't counting forward anymore.

They were counting down.

He put his thumb on the hourglass button and tried to calculate how much of himself he had left to spend.
