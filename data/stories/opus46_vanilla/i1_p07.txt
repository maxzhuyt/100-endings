# Lost & Found in Osaka

## First Connection

The riff hit her somewhere between the stomach and the sternum.

Miku stopped mid-step, one foot hovering above the pavement outside Super Potato, a bag of secondhand patch cables dangling from her wrist. She knew that melody. Everyone knew that melody — the *Cowboy Bebop* opening, "Tank!" — but she'd never heard it screamed through a single electric guitar like that, all the brass parts collapsed into one furious, distorted line that bounced off the narrow walls of Den Den Town and rattled the capsule-toy machines outside the arcade.

She followed the sound the way water follows gravity.

The girl playing was maybe seventeen, eighteen. She had short hair dyed the color of a copper penny, a beat-up Fender Jazzmaster covered in stickers, and a tiny battery amp at her feet that had no business being that loud. An open guitar case held a scattering of coins and a few crumpled bills. She played with her eyes closed, chin tilted up, grinning like the music was a private joke between her and God.

Miku stood at the edge of the small crowd, clutching the strap of her bag. Her oversized headphones — Sony MDR-7506s, studio monitors, way too big for her head — sat around her neck like a padded collar.

The song ended. The crowd clapped politely and began to dissolve. The busker opened her eyes, swept a quick bow, and then looked directly at Miku.

"You're still here."

Miku flinched. "S-sorry?"

"Everyone else walked off. You look like you just saw a ghost." The girl crouched to adjust a knob on her amp, glancing up. "Good ghost or bad ghost?"

"Good. Definitely good." Miku swallowed. "You, um. You voiced the trumpet line in the bridge using parallel fifths instead of the original harmony. That was — that was a choice."

The busker's hand froze on the knob. She stood up slowly.

"Okay. *Okay.* Who are you?"

"I'm — nobody. I'm Miku."

"Nobody Miku. I'm Rei." She thrust out a hand. Miku shook it. Her headphones slid forward over her ears. She pushed them back, face burning.

"Those are 7506s, right?" Rei pointed. "Studio cans. You produce?"

"I — just — I have a little setup. At home. It's nothing. A MicroKorg and a — and a Volca Keys and some free DAW stuff, it's really not—"

"A MicroKorg." Rei's eyes went wide, delighted. "You're a synth kid."

"I guess."

"You *guess*. She *guesses*." Rei said this to no one, to the arcade entrance, to the blinking UFO catcher machines beyond the glass. She turned back. "So tell me about the parallel fifths. Because you're right, I did that on purpose, and literally no one has ever noticed."

Miku's headphones slipped again. She caught them. "Well — the original arrangement voices the trumpet section in thirds, right? Very standard big-band harmony. But on a single guitar you can't really do that and keep the rhythm going, so you went with fifths because they're easier to fret as a shape, but they also make it sound more like — like a rock thing? More aggressive. It changes the whole feel."

She realized she'd been talking for an unbroken thirty seconds, which might have been a personal record with a stranger. Her mouth snapped shut.

Rei stared at her. Then she laughed — a real, full laugh, head thrown back.

"Where have you *been*?"

"...Den Den Town. Buying cables."

"No, I mean — where have you been all my life? I've been busking this strip for three months and the most feedback I've ever gotten is some salaryman telling me I was blocking the vending machine." She started unplugging her guitar, winding the cable in quick loops. "Are you hungry?"

"What?"

"Hungry. Food. The thing humans need." Rei snapped her guitar case shut. "There's a kaitenzushi place around the corner. The one with the little shinkansen that delivers your order? It's cheap and the salmon is actually decent."

"I — you don't have to—"

"I know I don't *have* to. I want to. You just gave me the best musical analysis I've gotten in my entire busking career, and I want to hear more while eating tamago nigiri. Come on."

Miku opened her mouth. Closed it. Her headphones slid down again. She didn't fix them this time.

"...Okay."

---

The restaurant was narrow and bright, every seat facing the conveyor belt. They sat on adjacent stools. Rei propped her guitar case against the wall and immediately grabbed a plate of ika.

"So the MicroKorg," she said, already chewing. "What do you use it for? Like, what kind of stuff do you make?"

"Um. Ambient, mostly. Pads. Textures." Miku watched a plate of salmon ride past and didn't reach for it. "Sometimes I try to — this is going to sound weird."

"Weird is my whole brand. Go."

"I try to make sounds that feel like specific places. Like, I have one patch that's supposed to be the sound of the Midōsuji Line at night. The hum of the train and the silence between stations."

Rei put down her chopsticks.

"That's the coolest thing anyone has ever said to me in a sushi restaurant."

"You're making fun of me."

"I am absolutely not making fun of you. Miku. Hey. Look at me."

Miku looked. Rei's expression was completely serious for the first time since they'd met.

"I play other people's songs on a street corner. You're building entire worlds out of sound. That's not weird. That's art."

Miku's throat tightened. She reached out and took the salmon plate off the belt.

"The — the Midōsuji Line one isn't finished yet. It needs something. A melodic line, maybe. Something human on top of all the texture."

"Something like a guitar?"

Their eyes met. Miku's headphones slipped. She let them fall.

"...Maybe."

Rei grinned and stole a piece of her salmon.

"Then I think we've got a lot to talk about."
