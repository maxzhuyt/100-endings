# Blood Moon

The harvest moon bleeds crimson over the rooftops of the abandoned village, painting every cobblestone in shades of rust and ruin. Somewhere in the distance, a shutter bangs against its frame in a wind that carries the stench of death — bodies left unburied in doorways, throats opened like second mouths. Whatever passed through here did so with enthusiasm.

Yennefer of Vengerberg steps over a corpse without looking down. Her boots click against the stone with deliberate rhythm, each footfall a declaration that she refuses to creep through shadows like prey. The chaos magic thrumming beneath her skin reads the residue of violence here, and it is *old* violence — not in hours, but in nature. Something ancient did this. Something that enjoyed it.

She smells the blood before she sees him. Then lilac and gooseberries meet the copper tang of a slaughterhouse, and her violet eyes narrow.

He sits on the edge of the village fountain like a king on a stolen throne. Red coat pooling around him, wide-brimmed hat casting his face in shadow beneath the bleeding moon. The water behind him has gone black with blood. He holds a massive silver pistol across one knee, polishing it with a gloved thumb as though he's been waiting — as though this entire massacre was merely a way to pass the time before the main event.

"You're the one they sent?" His voice comes from everywhere and nowhere, reverberating off the dead village walls. A grin splits the shadow beneath his hat — too wide, too many teeth. "A *woman*?"

"You're the one who did this?" Yennefer gestures at the carnage without breaking stride. "How disappointingly unoriginal."

Alucard's grin doesn't falter. If anything, it sharpens. He tips his hat back with the barrel of his gun, and the moonlight catches his face — angular, pale as bleached bone, eyes hidden behind orange-tinted lenses that glow like embers. He looks at her the way a cat looks at something it hasn't decided to kill yet.

"No one *sent* me," she clarifies, stopping twenty paces from him. Close enough to strike. Far enough to run, if she were the running type, which she is not. "I was passing through. You've made rather a mess of my route."

"My sincerest apologies." He doesn't sound sincere. He sounds *delighted*. He rises from the fountain's edge in a motion that defies physics — one moment seated, the next standing at full height, coat settling around him like folded wings. He's enormous. He shouldn't be able to move that quietly. "And what manner of creature are you, that walks through a killing field in heels and doesn't flinch?"

"A sorceress." She lets chaos energy crackle between her fingers, violet light matching her eyes. "What manner of creature are you, that I should care?"

He laughs. The sound bounces off the buildings and comes back distorted, layered, as though multiple voices are laughing from inside him. "I am the No-Life King. I am the monster that other monsters fear." He takes a step forward, and the shadows at his feet move wrong — stretching, reaching, alive. "I am Alucard."

"Alucard." She tastes the name. Rearranges it. Her expression doesn't change, but something cold and calculating flickers behind her eyes. "How very dramatic. Do you rehearse these introductions, or does the melodrama come naturally?"

The shadows stop reaching. His grin twitches — not with anger, but with something far more dangerous. Interest.

"You have a mouth on you, sorceress."

"Yennefer. And you have blood on yours."

He touches his lip with one gloved finger, examines the smear of red, and licks it clean without breaking eye contact. "So I do."

The air between them thickens. Yennefer feels the pull of his presence like a gravitational anomaly — dark energy folding around him in layers she can almost read. He's not a vampire as she knows them. He's something else. Something *worse*. The magical signature rolling off him is a void, an absence that devours light and sense and reason.

She wants to study it. She wants to tear it apart and understand what makes it tick.

He can smell her curiosity. It mingles with the lilac and gooseberries, and beneath that — beneath the perfume and the magic and the carefully constructed exterior — he smells something broken. Something *remade*. His nostrils flare.

"You've been altered," he says softly, and the playfulness drains from his voice. "Someone took you apart and put you back together differently. I can taste the scars."

Chaos energy *erupts*. A bolt of violet force slams into the fountain where he stood, shattering stone and sending black water skyward. But he's already behind her — impossible, *impossible* speed — his breath cold against her neck, his voice a whisper.

"Touched a nerve?"

She doesn't flinch. Her hand comes up trailing fire, and she presses it backward against his chest. The flames eat through his coat, through his skin, and he *lets* them — lets himself burn for three full seconds before the shadows knit him back together.

"Magnificent," he breathes.

"Don't patronize me."

"I'm not." And for one fractured moment, his voice holds no mockery. "I've walked this world for centuries looking for something that could hurt me. You smell like lilac and you *burn* like hellfire." He steps back. Tips his hat. "I think I'll keep you interesting, Yennefer of Vengerberg."

"You'll *keep* nothing of mine." She turns to face him, violet eyes blazing, chin raised. "But I'll admit — you're the first corpse I've met worth talking to."

They stand beneath the blood moon, predator facing predator, and neither bows. Neither blinks.

The village of the dead holds its breath around them.

"Same road north?" he asks.

"Unfortunately."

His grin returns. Her lips almost curve.

They walk.
