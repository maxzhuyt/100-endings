# Darkroom Revelations

The safelight makes everything look like it's bleeding.

I've always loved that about darkrooms—how the red swallows the ordinary and hands you back something stranger, more honest. The chemical smell is sharp tonight, acetic acid and fixer cutting through the close air, and I'm standing hip-against-counter watching Elliot's hands move through the developer tray with this patience that makes my chest tight.

His nails are painted black. Chipped at the edges, like he did them days ago and hasn't bothered to redo them. His rings catch the red light—silver, chunky, thrifted. He's wearing that oversized Sisters of Mercy shirt he wears every other Tuesday, and his hair falls across his face in a way that seems accidental but I've watched him arrange it three times since we started tonight.

I notice things. It's a problem.

"The contrast is off," he says quietly, lifting the print with tongs. His voice does this thing where it drops at the end of sentences, almost apologetic, like he's asking permission to have observed something.

"Let me see." I step closer than I need to. Our arms almost touch over the tray. The image materializes slowly—our project, a double-exposure series on liminality, which sounded pretentious when Professor Okoro assigned the theme but has become something else entirely over these three weeks of late nights. In the print, Elliot's self-portrait overlaps with the abandoned greenhouse we shot on Saturday. His face dissolves into broken glass and overgrown ferns. It's genuinely beautiful.

"It's not the contrast," I say. "It's perfect. You just can't see it yet."

He makes a small sound. Not quite a laugh.

---

Here's what I don't say: I have a type. I've always had a type. I like knowing what I want and going after it with both hands. I like confidence that matches mine, friction that sparks. My last girlfriend had a motorcycle and a filthy mouth and I knew exactly where I stood with her, which was usually against a wall.

Elliot is none of that. Elliot is pauses and half-sentences and eyes that hold something enormous and unsaid. And I'm standing in this red-dark room recalibrating everything I thought I understood about my own desire.

---

It happens while I'm loading the enlarger for the next print. He's sitting on the stool in the corner, knees pulled up, scrolling through reference images on his phone with the brightness turned so low it barely registers against the safelight.

"Dani?"

"Yeah."

"Can I tell you something weird?"

I keep my hands steady on the negative carrier. "Weird is kind of our whole aesthetic."

Another not-quite-laugh. Then silence long enough that I stop what I'm doing and turn around. He's not looking at his phone anymore. He's looking at the print hanging on the drying line—his face merging with the greenhouse—and his expression is something I don't have a word for.

"I've been... thinking about things. About myself." He swallows. "About whether *he* is—whether that's the whole picture. Of me."

The enlarger hums behind me. I don't move.

"I don't know what I am," he says, and his voice cracks slightly on *am*, and every instinct I have says *close the distance, take his face in your hands, tell him it's okay*. I am so good at taking charge. I am so good at filling silences and steering ships and knowing the next right move.

I stay exactly where I am.

"Okay," I say. "Tell me more. If you want."

He looks at me then, and the relief on his face nearly breaks me open.

---

He talks for twenty minutes. Maybe longer. Time does strange things under safelight. He tells me about trying on his roommate's skirt when his roommate was in class and standing in front of the mirror feeling something *click* and then feeling terrified. He tells me about the word *nonbinary* sitting in his search history like a grenade. He tells me about his dad, briefly, and stops.

I listen. I *just* listen, and it's the hardest thing I've done all semester because I want to fix and affirm and hold and I don't know which of those impulses are about him and which are about my need to be the person who makes things better.

"You're the first person I've said any of this to," he says.

"Thank you for trusting me."

"You're not... it doesn't change the project, right? How we work together?"

"Elliot." I say his name carefully, like I'm handling a print that's still wet. "Nothing about this changes anything. Unless you want it to."

---

I turn back to the enlarger because I need to do something with my hands, and that's when I knock the stack of photo paper off the counter. The whole package. Unsealed, because I'm an idiot who was too busy eavesdropping on someone's soul to close the goddamn light-proof bag. The sheets scatter across the floor and the red light hits every single one of them.

"Shit. *Shit.*"

Elliot slides off the stool. We both crouch down, gathering the ruined sheets, and our fingers brush over the same piece and neither of us pulls away.

"That was like forty dollars of paper," I say.

"Fifty. It was the multigrade."

"We're screwed."

"We could shoot digital. Like normal people."

"Don't you dare."

He smiles. In the red light, it transforms his whole face—something loosening, some held thing releasing. I realize I'm still touching his hand.

"We'll figure it out," I say, and I mean the paper, and the project, and the vast unnamed thing humming between us in this small red room.

"Yeah," he says. "I think maybe we will."

We stay crouched on the floor with fifty dollars of ruined paper between us, and the safelight keeps bleeding, and I don't take charge, and he doesn't look away, and for once in my life I let the image develop at its own pace.
