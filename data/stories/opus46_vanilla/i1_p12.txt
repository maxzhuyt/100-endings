# Rehearsing Rejection

---

Seven percent battery. Seven. Which is fine. That's fine. I don't even need my phone. People existed for thousands of years without phones. People met at restaurants without phones. People sat alone at tables for twenty, thirty, forty-five minutes without phones and they were *fine*, they just sat there like adults and—

She's going to cancel. That's what's going to happen. She's going to text at 7:58, two minutes before, and say something with an exclamation mark in it because that's how she softens her cruelty, she puts little exclamation marks on everything like sprinkles on a coffin. *So sorry! Something came up! Rain check?*

And I'll say, "No worries!" and she'll say, "Love you!" and I'll say, "Love you too!" and we'll both know it's a funeral we're attending, the funeral of whatever this is, and the exclamation marks are just us throwing dirt on it cheerfully.

Six percent.

I'm not going to text her to confirm. I'm not. That's what a desperate person would do and I'm not desperate, I'm just—I want to know if I should order a drink or if I should order *the* drink, the one that means I'm settling in for the specific kind of evening where I sit here and understand, slowly, like a sunset, that I've been left.

She kissed me eleven days ago. Eleven. I've counted, which I know is—I *know*. I know that counting days is a thing I do that other people don't do. Dr. Aguilar would say I'm "keeping score in a game no one else is playing." Fine. But someone has to keep score, don't they? Someone has to notice that eleven days ago her mouth was on my mouth and her hand was in my hair and she said, "I've wanted this for so long," and now—

Now she takes four hours to respond to a text. Which means she's constructing her exit. She's workshopping the letdown. She's probably told Priya. God, she's definitely told Priya, and Priya said, "You need to be honest with her but also, like, *careful*, because you know how she gets," and they both nodded knowingly because everyone knows *how I get*, everyone has a theory about *how I get*, everyone has compared notes—

Stop. Stop stop stop. I'm doing the thing. I can feel myself doing the thing, building the scaffolding of a conversation that hasn't happened between people who might not have said anything close to what I'm—

But they *might* have.

Five percent. I should turn off Bluetooth. I should have charged it at work but Danielle was using the only outlet near my desk and I didn't ask because if I asked she'd do that thing where she says "of course!" but shifts her body slightly away from me, which means—

Okay. She's here. I can see her through the window.

She's smiling. She's smiling and she's walking fast, almost jogging, which means—what does that mean? It means she's late and she knows it, or it means she's eager, or it means she wants to get this over with quickly, the way you jog toward a band-aid you're about to rip off.

She's wearing the green jacket. She wore the green jacket eleven days ago. Is that a sign? Is she trying to reference that night? Or did she just grab whatever was closest to the door because this dinner doesn't warrant *choosing*?

"Hey! Oh my God, I'm so sorry, the bus was—" She's hugging me. She's hugging me and she smells like that cucumber lotion and her arms are all the way around me, not the one-arm side thing, the full thing, and she's *squeezing*, and something in my chest cracks open like an egg and I want to cry, I want to actually cry right here, because—

"I missed you," she says. Into my hair. She says it into my hair.

And I feel it. I feel it like sunlight on water, this flooding, this—she means it. She's here and she means it and she missed me and everything I built in my head for the last eleven days was—

"I missed you too."

But even as I say it I'm scanning her face. I'm looking for the micro-expression, the flicker, the rehearsed quality of someone delivering good news before bad. *I missed you, but. I missed you, and that's the problem. I missed you and I've been thinking and—*

"You okay?" she asks. "You seem—"

"I'm great! I'm good. I already ordered water."

She's looking at me with that soft thing she does, that soft focused thing, and I can't decide if it's tenderness or pity. Tenderness and pity look identical from the front. You have to see them from the side, and she won't turn her head.

We sit. She's talking about her week. Her sister's pregnant. She's showing me the ultrasound photo and her face is doing something genuinely unguarded and I think: *she's sharing this with me, she chose to share this with me first, maybe, or at least early, and that means—*

Unless she's filling space. Unless this is the pleasant padding before the pivot.

"I'm sorry I've been weird about texting," she says, and my whole body goes rigid, every muscle recruiting for impact. Here it is. Here it—

"My phone screen cracked and I've been using my mom's old one and it doesn't get half my notifications. It's been driving me *crazy*. I kept worrying you'd think I was ignoring you."

She reaches across the table and takes my hand.

"I wasn't ignoring you," she says. "I need you to know that."

And I believe her. I believe her completely, for three whole seconds, the way you believe in the ground until an earthquake. I believe her and I feel the relief flood in, narcotic and warm, and I think *this is real, this is real, she's real*—

And then, underneath, quieter: *but what about next time?*

My phone dies.

"Can I use yours to call a car later?" I ask.

"You can use mine for anything," she says.

I take a breath.

I try to let it be enough.
