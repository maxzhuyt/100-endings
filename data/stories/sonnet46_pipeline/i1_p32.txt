# The Morska Conspiracy
## The Underhold

The door opened without resistance.

That was the first wrong thing.

Aya had expected the ward—a cold flash behind the sternum, the taste of iron, the particular nausea that came with crossing spelled thresholds. She'd picked the lock herself three weeks prior, had learned the shape of the immateria curled around its hinges like sleeping eels. She'd expected to feel it uncoil.

Instead: nothing. A door swinging inward on oiled hinges, as though it had been waiting.

Katra's hand found her arm in the dark. Simeon, behind them both, went very still.

The underhold was lit.

Lanterns—six, eight, she counted without meaning to—cast the vaulted chamber in amber and shadow. The ceiling dripped with condensation. The walls were the belly of the city itself, old stone sweating brine, and somewhere beyond them the harbor breathed its slow cold breath. The artifact's case stood on its plinth at the chamber's far end, exactly where guild records said it would be. Exactly where Aya had studied it on stolen maps by stolen candlelight.

Maren Voss stood before it with her arms crossed.

She was a short woman, which Aya had not expected. The guild's spoken history had made her large—had made her a figure of fog and reputation, of threats delivered through intermediaries and payments collected in advance of the deed. But here she stood: compact, iron-haired, her oilskin coat still wet from the harbor. Not a prison coat. Not a prisoner's posture.

Behind her, four men. Two with boathooks. Two with something longer, something that caught the lamplight with the particular dullness of well-maintained steel.

*Incarcerated,* Aya thought, with a detachment she recognized as the first stage of terror. *She was decidedly incarcerated.*

"Close the door," Voss said.

Simeon closed it.

There was a moment—the pause before the mechanism engages, before the clockwork commits—and then the men moved, and it was over with a professional economy that insulted all three of them. Katra went sideways into a pillar. Simeon lost his footing on the wet stone. Aya found her wrists pinned behind her at an angle that suggested the man holding them had done this before, and would do it again, and felt no particular way about it.

Her mask was pulled free.

The cold hit her face. The lamplight did the rest.

Voss looked at her for a long moment. Then she looked at the mask in her man's hand. Then back at Aya—at her jaw, her brow, the architecture of her cheekbones that had been her mother's before hers.

"Oh," Voss said. "How extraordinary."

---

Katra was bleeding from somewhere above her ear. Simeon had gone the careful white of a man calculating whether his odds improved or worsened with the seconds. Aya stood between them, wrists bound with cord, and did the only thing she could think to do.

She lifted her chin.

"You weren't arrested," she said.

"Observant girl." Voss moved toward her with the unhurried patience of a woman who had already won. "I was not. A useful fiction, as it happens. Shake the tree, see what falls." She paused. "You fell rather faster than anticipated."

*She shook us loose,* Aya thought. *She set the tree shaking herself to find who'd reach for the fruit.* The elegance of it was almost admirable. The implications were not.

"You know who I am." It wasn't a question.

"I know whose face that is." Voss stopped two feet away. She smelled of tobacco and saltwater. Her eyes were the particular grey of overcast harbor mornings. "I knew your mother, girl. Not well. Well enough."

Aya held very still. The foghorn sounded in the harbor beyond the walls—once, twice, three slow pulses that traveled through stone and settled in her chest like a hand pressed flat.

"Then you know," Aya said carefully, "that I am worth considerably more to you breathing than otherwise."

Katra made a sound. Simeon's head turned a fraction.

Voss tilted her head. "Elaborate."

*Yes,* Aya thought. *Elaborate. Speak clearly and quickly and do not let your voice shake.* "Lord Arndt Solberg is my father. Whatever you think of me—whatever you've deduced about the nature of my particular skills—he does not know I am here. He does not know I have been *anywhere* near this guild, or any other. He believes me safely abed in Morska's most respectable quarter, entertaining no ambitions more transgressive than an interest in natural philosophy." She let that settle. "I am, in short, a secret. And secrets have value proportional to who would pay to keep them."

"Ransom," Voss said, as though tasting the word.

"Leverage," Aya corrected. "Ransom implies you release me after. I'm suggesting an arrangement more—durable."

In her periphery, Katra had gone very quiet in the way that meant she was paying close attention. Simeon's expression had shifted fractionally toward something that might, in better light, have resembled hope.

Voss regarded her.

"Your mother," she said at last, "climbed her own gallows without assistance and thanked the warden for the courtesy of a clean rope." She said it without cruelty. Merely as information. "I expect you understand what it means that I am telling you this."

*It means she knew her. It means she respected her. It means she knows exactly what I am and what I can do, and she is deciding whether that is an asset or a liability.*

"I understand," Aya said.

The lantern nearest to her guttered. Just once.

No draft. No cause.

Voss looked at it. Then at Aya's bound hands. Then at Aya's face.

"Sit," she said finally. "All three of you. We are going to have a conversation, and you are going to tell me everything—" she turned back to the artifact's case, resting one palm against its dark glass "—and then I am going to decide what you're worth."

Simeon sat. Katra sat.

Aya sat last, and thought of her mother's hands, and did not let herself shake.
