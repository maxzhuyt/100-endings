Eli ran her fingers through her freshly buzzed hair. She felt a subtle tingle on her scalp and a refreshing coolness all over her body. 
        For years, she labored over her thick, wavy, chestnut hair. She’d spent thousands of dollars on gels, conditioners, serums, volumizers, and so many other products. She had a whole drawer under her sink full of hair ties, curling irons, driers, and pins. Pulling the full drawer out of its cabinet, she walked out of the bathroom and down the hall to her kitchen garbage. She threw all the contents into a trash bag.
        Her mother had always told Eli that her hair was her best quality. It was the first thing people noticed about her. When Abigail, her little 5-year-old niece, drew pictures of her, she depicted a tiny stick figure fully surrounded by brown swirls. For the first 30 years of her life, Eli had never cut her hair above her shoulders. She’d gotten into the habit of pulling her long locks over her face when she was feeling insecure and twirling strands around her fingers when she needed to think.
        “Okay. Time to face the music,” she said out loud to herself as she grabbed her car keys and headed out the door.
        Before backing out of the driveway, she looked at herself one more time in the rearview mirror. She smiled at her unfamiliar reflection, taking in the contours of her skull and studying the shape of her face. It was an intensely intimate moment. She felt light, alert, and somehow more alive than she could ever remember.  
        When she pulled up to her childhood home, she noticed that her sister’s car was already in the driveway. She took a deep breath. She grabbed the plate of cookies she had baked for the party and rang the doorbell.
        Abigail, dressed in a princess costume, came barreling through the door. As soon as the little girl registered Eli’s face, she gasped. “Auntie E! You’re bald,” she declared.
        “What are you talking about, sweetie?” Eli heard her mom ask from the other room.
        Abigail grabbed Eli’s hand and led her into the overcrowded kitchen. Eli’s parents, sister, brother-in-law, cousin, and aunt all sat around the table. Her mom was fussing over a pot of sauce on the stove while drinking a glass of wine. Her father and brother-in-law were in the middle of some kind of debate about stock options.
        “Auntie E is bald,” Abigail calmly stated again.
        Everyone turned to look at Eli in the doorway, and a hush fell over the busy room. Her mom dropped her glass of wine. It spilled all over the linoleum floor.
        “Here, let me get that,” Eli said, grabbing a towel. At the same moment, her mom ducked down to dab at the spill, and the two bumped heads.
        “Ouch!” both said at the same time, reaching to touch their foreheads. They looked at each other and began to laugh.
        “Your beautiful hair!” Eli’s mom said. She was still laughing, but tears were gathering in the corners of her eyes. 
        “I know mom. What do you think?” Eli said, rubbing the red spot where she had collided with her mom.
        “I don’t know what to think,” her mom said.
        Abigail was at Eli’s side again and began rubbing the top of her head. “It is so soft. You feel like a duckling,” she said.
        “You look like a rock star,” said her sister.
        “Can I feel?” asked her aunt, bending down to rub Eli’s head.
        “Why would you do this?” her mom asked as she grabbed another towel.
        “I needed a change,” Eli said. 
        “What did your boss say?” her dad asked.
        “I quit last week,” Eli said, registering his look of shock.
        “What does Uncle Joey think about your haircut?” Abigail asked.
        There was a long pause. Eli’s parents made eye contact, and her aunt squeezed her hand.
        “Uncle Joey doesn’t like it, sweetie, but that doesn’t really matter. I like it,” Eli said, looking into her niece's eyes. Abigail looked up at her with a big smile.
        There was a lot more Eli needed to say. Eventually, she would have to tell her family about dropping out of her residency program. She’d need to rehash the details of her sudden and dramatic breakup with Joe. She’d have to cry with her mom as she explained the details of her plans to take a full year away from the family to travel.
        But first, there was wine to be cleaned, sauce to be seasoned, and a meal to eat. Looking around that bright little kitchen at the surprised faces of her family, Eli knew that it would take them a long time to understand the choices she was making. Yet, she also knew that, no matter what, there would still be a seat for her at the table.
