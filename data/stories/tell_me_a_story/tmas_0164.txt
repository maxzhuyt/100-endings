There I was with the boys, taking care of business on a Friday night. Hitting the cha-cha slide, drinking fruit punch and soda; we lived for nights like these. This was the freshman homecoming dance. What a night it was shaping up to be.
        It was after I inhaled my third slice of pepperoni that Penelope approached me. I found it unexpected because I never talked to Penelope in class, but when in Rome, I suppose. We shared a slow dance at arm’s length but just before I was going to twirl her around, we were untimely interrupted by a tap on my shoulder.
        I turned around and blindly said, “There was room for Jesus, I swear!” I was surprised to find that I was not talking to a teacher on chaperoning duty. Instead, I looked into my mother’s eyes. An overwhelming fear took hold of me. What on Earth was Mom doing here?
        As she spoke, my heart sank. “Hi sugarplum. You left a couple of things at home, so I figured I’d bring them by.” With every item she listed, my forehead grew sweatier. “Your inhaler, your lucky suspenders, your new bow tie from Grandma, your glasses, and most importantly, a kiss from your mommy!”
        She leaned in for her signature Mom-smooch. I recoiled, all too aware of the growing, unwanted attention from everyone on the dance floor. Despite arching my back and craning my neck uncomfortably, Mom was persistent. I managed to turn my face sideways and get away with a quick cheek peck, but I could already imagine the never-ending jabs from my friends for this.
        In the middle of the basketball-court-turned-dance-floor, I stood holding my inhaler, my lucky suspenders, my new bow tie from Grandma and my glasses, watching Mom finally walk away. I turned back to Penelope, who I could assume no longer wanted to dance.
        She chuckled lightly, but not in a mean way. Then she said, “That was kind of cute.” I could barely believe I heard the words correctly. 
        Over my shoulder, a blood-curdling word pierced through the air, “Sugarplum!” What did I do to deserve such torture? Just when I thought I had escaped, she returned! 
        For some unknown reason, she spoke louder this time. Or, maybe the music was quieter. Either way, more people were listening. “I almost forgot,” Mom said, “You left a love letter that you wrote!” My mind went numb. She continued, “I’m guessing you’re going to want to give it to this lovely young lady you were dancing with.”
        I tried to save face as best I could. “Love letter? Nope! Not mine! Couldn’t be!”
        She was not going to be convinced that easily. “Then why is it in your handwriting? And it’s talking about the freshman homecoming dance and how you want to be with Kelsey.” She spoke to Penelope directly for this next line, “I’m guessing you are Kelsey. He’s got a real crush on you. Here, read the letter.”
        Mom extended the ripped-out paper from my diary toward Penelope. I lunged forward and intercepted the handoff. Without a second thought, I wadded the paper up, stuffed it in my mouth, chewed as fast as I could manage, and by some miracle, I swallowed it. 
        At this point, I realized everyone at the dance, chaperones included, had paused what they were doing to watch and see how this all played out. The whole freshman class saw the spectacle. Penelope looked at me like I was an alien. My friends howled at my misfortune. I even caught a glimpse of Kelsey ducking for cover in the crowd.
        t was going to be a rough few weeks for me after this debacle. I sighed, then looked at Mom. “Drive me home. I’m done.”
        She stayed behind me for a moment and called out, “What’s the matter, sugarplum? Did I say something?” Then she followed me in tow, “Everything alright?” 
        Everything was not alright at that very moment. I was the laughingstock of the school.
        Sure, there will be another homecoming dance. Not another freshman one, but who cares? That was going to be the lamest one out of them all anyway. On the bright side, I had my first slow dance with someone that was not Mom or Grandma. And the school ordered stuffed-crust pizza, which is my favorite. I grabbed a fourth slice on my way out. At times like these, you just have to take the wins with the losses. Plus, I needed something to wash that disgusting inky paper taste out of my mouth.
        Fingers crossed that Mom feels bad for me and lets me skip school on Monday.
