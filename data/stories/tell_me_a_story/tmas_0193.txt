Bonnie stood at the bathroom mirror, carefully applying her signature cherry-red lipstick. She pressed her lips together, blotted them with a tissue, and then assessed her reflection. 
        Happy with what she saw, Bonnie put the lid back on her lipstick and grabbed her backpack from her bedroom floor. As she headed down the hallway to the kitchen, her little brother, Benji, emerged from his room, sleepily rubbing his eyes. 
        “Bon Bon!” he shouted gleefully, clapping his hands. Bonnie smiled down at him, ruffling his chestnut-brown hair. Benji reached for her, and Bonnie picked him up, grinning and kissing his cheeks until he was breathless with giggles. 
        Bonnie carried Benji to the kitchen and set him down in his booster seat, strapping him in and kissing his forehead. Bonnie’s mom set a bowl of dry cereal and a plate of orange slices in front of him. 
        Kissing Bonnie on the cheek, she said, “Thank you for bringing Benji in. What’s on the agenda for today?” 
        Bonnie grabbed an apple from the bowl on the counter, biting into it. “I have a student council leadership meeting during homeroom and then cheer practice after school. If it’s okay with you, the rest of the cheer squad wanted to grab dinner at Burrito Express after practice.” Bonnie looked at her mom, hopefully. 
        “I’m sorry, hun, I picked up an extra shift tonight. I need you home to watch Benji.”
        Bonnie nodded. “Of course.” 
        She kissed her mom on the cheek, ruffled her baby brother’s hair again, and headed outside. Her boyfriend, Rick, was at the curb waiting to drive her to school. She threw her bag in the backseat and climbed into the front seat, kissing Rick quickly before buckling her seatbelt. 
        When they arrived at Clarksville High School, they headed inside in search of their friends. As the head cheerleader and captain of the football team, Bonnie and Rick were #couplegoals and the envy of most of their classmates. Everyone said Bonnie and Rick would be together forever, but Bonnie had her doubts. Rick had his sights set on a major university on the other side of the country, but Bonnie had to stay local so she could help her mom with Benji and live at home. They were only juniors, however, so Bonnie tried not to dwell on the future too much.
        “Hey, girl!” Her fellow cheerleader, Cherice, called out to her, waving. Bonnie waved back, pulling Rick over to the cafeteria table where their friends were gathered. 
        “Hey, y’all,” Bonnie said, taking a seat next to Cherice. She reached into the bag of donut holes sitting on the table and popped one into her mouth. 
        “I’m sorry, but I can’t go to Burrito Express tonight,” she told them. 
        The girls groaned, whining. 
        “Why not?” Cindy, Bonnie’s least favorite on the squad, asked. 
        “My mom signed me up to volunteer at a homeless shelter in Cobey,” she lied. “It’ll look good on my college applications.” 
        Cindy looked at her suspiciously, but didn’t say anything. 
        “Okay, well what about this weekend? Maybe we could all go get pedicures,” another cheerleader, Samantha, suggested. 
        “Oh, I can’t this weekend. I have SAT prep,” Bonnie replied. In reality, she had a double shift at the Big Burger Diner in Cobey. It was a bit of a drive, but Bonnie couldn’t risk running into any of her friends or classmates. She had been working at Big Burger since she turned sixteen last fall and had been using all of her earnings to pay for cheerleading, dinners with her friends, trendy clothes, and everything else she needed to fit in. She knew her mom would buy her these things if she could afford it, but even working as a full-time bookkeeper every weekday and picking up shifts at the Main Street Diner a couple of nights a week, she was still barely able to pay their bills. Bonnie couldn’t imagine asking her mom for a cent of her hard-earned money to cover the frivolous trappings of high school popularity. However, Bonnie also couldn’t imagine going through high school as a loser and an outcast. So, she worked at Big Burger every weekend and tutored a couple of kids in her AP Chemistry class twice a week.
        “You have SAT prep every weekend,” Rick whined. “You’re one of the smartest girls in our class, why do you need so much help?”
        Bonnie just shrugged, popping another donut hole into her mouth to avoid answering. She had been having this fight with Rick at least once a week since they started dating last fall, and she was exhausted from it. She wished she could just tell Rick about her financial situation, but she knew that he would dump her if he knew she was poor.
        Bonnie didn’t particularly care about losing Rick, but she did care about keeping up appearances, and dating the captain of the football team was on-brand for the head cheerleader. Not to mention, if Rick knew about her finances, everyone else at school would know, too. Bonnie had sworn to herself when they had moved to Clarksville at the beginning of her eighth grade year that she would not be “Poor Fatherless Bonnie” here. She had done a great job of reinventing herself and creating a world where she was envied, sought after, praised. So, if she had to keep dating Rick and lying to her friends, that is exactly what she would do. 
        The following weekend, as Bonnie was getting ready for her shift at Big Burger, her phone rang. Incoming video call from Rick flashed across the screen. She groaned and checked her watch. She didn’t have time for this. She tossed a hoodie on to cover her uniform polo and answered the call. 
        “Babe, blow off SAT class and come to the mall with us!” Rick shouted into the phone. It was noisy on his end. From the looks of it, he was in the backseat of a car. Bonnie could hear girls’ voices in the background. 
        “Who is ‘us’?” she asked. 
        “Me, Bobby, Joey, Cindy, and Krystal!” he replied. 
        Ah, so the girl voices belonged to Bonnie’s cheer squad frenemy and her bestie. Cool.
        “No, I’m sorry, I can’t. Have fun. I’ll see you tonight.” Bonnie hung up the phone and tossed it into her satchel, along with her history textbook and a copy of Jane Eyre. If it was a slow night at the diner, she wanted to catch up on her homework so she didn’t have to listen to Rick’s complaints about her doing schoolwork during their date again. 
        She said goodbye to her mom, kissed Benji, and headed out the door. She checked her watch again: 11:35. Crap. Her shift was scheduled for noon, and it was at least a thirty-minute drive, and that was if there wasn’t any traffic. She threw the car into gear and reversed out of the driveway. 
        Bonnie arrived at the diner a few minutes after twelve. She rushed in, tossing her bag into her locker. 
        “I’m sorry, I’m sorry,” she said to her manager as she tied an apron around her waist. Her manager frowned, looking down at his watch. 
        “Bonnie…” he warned. 
        “I know, it won’t happen again!” 
        Bonnie grabbed a pitcher of ice water and rushed over to serve her first table. 
        “Oh!” she shrieked, dropping the pitcher of water to the floor. Cold water splashed her pants, chilling her legs and soaking her socks. The group of teens sitting at the table looked up at her in alarm. 
        “Oh. My. God. Bonnie?!” Cindy and Krystal grinned wickedly at her, nudging each other under the table. Bonnie’s face flushed, her cheeks matching her cherry-red lips. She avoided eye contact with Rick as she bent to wipe the spilled water from the floor. 
        “What are you doing here? I thought you were prepping for the SATs! Do you work here?” Rick was standing over her, his expression unreadable. 
        Bonnie left the wet rags and ice cubes on the floor, and rushed to the employee bathroom, tears rolling down her face. She had worked so hard for the past four years to hide this part of her life from everyone and now here it was, splayed out in front of them. And of all the people to see her working at a diner, it had to be Cindy. Bonnie knew the entire school would know about her situation before the day was over. 
        She convinced one of the other waitresses to take Rick’s table, and she hid in the employee bathroom until her boyfriend and classmates left the restaurant. When Rick had invited her to go to the mall with them, she had assumed he meant the mall in West Clairemont, not the rinky-dink rundown mall in Cobey.
        When Bonnie got off work, she drove home in silence, fretting about how she was supposed to face the rest of the cheer squad and her other friends on Monday. Maybe she could just skip school next week. Or be homeschooled. Or move.
        When she pulled into the driveway at home, she was surprised to see several of the other girls’ cars parked at the curb in front of her house. Apparently, she wouldn’t have to wait until Monday to hear everyone’s snide remarks about her deceitful behavior and cutting insults about her financial situation. She took a deep breath and opened the car door. 
        When she walked into the house, she was surprised to see the girls crowded around her dining room table with her mom and Benji. Everyone was laughing, sipping sodas, and nibbling on cookies from the half-full plate in the middle of the table.
        “Um, hey,” Bonnie said timidly. 
        The girls all looked up at her, and, it seemed to Bonnie, they were happy to see her. Maybe Cindy hadn’t ratted her out after all. But then, why were they all at her house? She had never invited the squad over, for fear that they would see the generic groceries in the pantry or the discount box of laundry detergent in the cupboard. 
        Cherice was the first one to speak. “Bonnie, how could you keep your situation from us? You know we all love you! We don’t care if you have five dollars or five thousand dollars. We love you, not your things!” She stood up and embraced Bonnie. The rest of the girls followed suit, and soon, they were all hugging tightly in the middle of the kitchen, tears streaming down Bonnie’s face. All this time, she had kept this secret and hidden this part of herself. Now, she was realizing that it had been completely unnecessary. These girls were her true and actual friends, and they loved her, even if she wasn’t perfect. 
        “So, I’m guessing Cindy told all of you?” Bonnie finally asked. 
        Samantha snorted. “She couldn’t create a group chat without you fast enough. Unluckily for her, none of us gave a rat’s patootie and her little scheme to ruin you was a failure.”
        “Yeah,” Katie agreed. “Anyone who actually knows you loves you, Bon, and anyone who cares about your money isn’t a real friend anyway.” 
        “Well, I think Rick cares,” Bonnie said. The girls laughed. 
        “And who cares about Rick?” they all shouted in unison.
