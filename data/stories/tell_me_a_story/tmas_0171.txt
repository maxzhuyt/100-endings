“I can’t believe you lied right to my face.” Anita typed the text into her phone and sent it to her brother, Anoop.
        “I am so sorry,” he typed back moments later.
        “Come home right now,” she typed.
        Blue dots appeared, indicating that he was writing. Then, they disappeared. 
        “NOW!” Anita was vibrating with rage as she typed.
        She held the phone, waiting for a response. Nothing. Anita screamed and threw her phone across her kitchen, knocking over a half-full coffee mug. It cracked against the wall, and cold coffee sprayed all over the small kitchen.
        “Okay. Okay,” she said out loud to herself, and she began taking deep breaths. Her hand was still shaking with rage. She put her hand over her heart and counted to three. 
        Across the room, her phone vibrated. She picked a shard of broken ceramic off the screen and noticed a new crack on the screen. Anoop had written, “on my way.”
        “I can handle this,” she said as she grabbed a broom and started to sweep up the broken pieces of the mug.
        She continued to take deep breaths as she began to wipe the coffee off the floor. When the kitchen was tidied, she looked at her phone and clicked on the “Find My Friends” app. Anoop always shared his location with her. She could see the little blue dot on her screen moving. He had just left campus, only twenty minutes away by bike.
        The apartment was eerily quiet now. 
        “Anoop will be back soon. This will all get sorted out,” Anita said to herself. Her hands stopped shaking. She was just about to brew herself a new pot of coffee when she heard a sudden crash. The unmistakable sound of breaking glass came from Anoop’s room. Anita jumped up and nervously walked over to the door.
        “I’m going to come in now, buddy. Don’t freak out,” she said timidly as she slowly opened the door. There, standing on Anoop’s dresser with shards of broken mirror all around him, was Jasper, a 15-pound macaque monkey. 
        “Alright Jasper. Anoop will be back any minute,” she said, looking at the monkey. He stared back at her with beady eyes and an indifferent expression on his scrunched-up face. She backed out of the doorway and closed the door behind her. 
        Would he be okay in there with the broken glass? She began to worry. But the idea of being in a confined space with a wild animal did not appeal to her. She couldn’t sit still, and she couldn’t open the bedroom door, so she began pacing around her little kitchen.
        Exactly seventeen minutes later, Anoop came hurtling into the apartment, out of breath.
        “Jasper broke your mirror,” Anita said as her brother approached her.
        “Did he get hurt?” Jasper asked.
        “I don’t think so,” Anita returned.
        “I’ll get him out of here tomorrow, sis. I promise,” Anoop said casually. He was pulling his old “nothing to see here” move, and Anita wasn’t going to let him get away with it.
        “You told me you found a place for him. You swore to me, he’d be out of here by the time I woke up,” she said, her tone flat.
        “I know. I know,” he said, guilt creeping into his voice.
        “Anoop, I woke up to a monkey in my bathroom. I had to chase him into your room before he climbed out the window,” she said. Anoop knew she was using her trademark “serenity now” voice. 
        “I’ll find someplace for him to go. Please don’t freak out,” Anoop said, as he cautiously sat down at the table across from his sister.
        “I am not freaking out. I am calmly and intentionally setting a boundary. You may not harbor a stolen lab monkey in my two-bedroom apartment. Anoop, we’ve had this conversation. Jasper has to go now, and you have to move out.” Now, she was using her no-nonsense, therapy-approved, queen of calm voice. Anoop could see right through it.
        “Come on, sis. You know you don’t want to do that to us,” Anoop said. Just the hint of his “but I am so cute; you can’t stay mad at me” smile began to appear on his face. He moved his chair a bit closer to his sister's. If he was taking her demand even a little bit seriously, he certainly wasn’t showing it.
        “That monkey was in my bathroom,” Anita repeated. This time, Anoop couldn’t read her tone.
        “I can’t bring him back to the lab," he said flatly.
        "You can. You just won't," she responded. 
        "They killed his sister,” Anoop said, leaning his head on her shoulder.
        “They killed his sister?” she said, turning to look her brother in the eyes for the first time that day.
        “His sister’s name was Jenny. She was a year younger than him. They were both born in the lab. It was terrible, Anita. I saw the whole thing. They gave her too much anesthesia before the experiment. Jasper was right there in the room with her when they did it. He was going to be next. I couldn’t just do nothing,” he said. Anita knew that the sadness in his voice was genuine.
        “I didn’t know they killed his sister,” she said.
        “I can’t imagine anything worse. Can you?” Anoop had tears in his eyes.
        “One more day,” Anita said.
        “He’ll be out tomorrow. I’ll find somewhere for him to go. I swear,” he said.
        “OK,” she said. She knew that he knew that she did not believe him.
        “I love you, sis,” Anoop said.
        That much, at least, she knew was true.
