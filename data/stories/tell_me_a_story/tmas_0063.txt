“Will you marry me?”
        Jack was on one knee, smiling up at Ash. He held an open black box displaying a sparkling diamond ring. Both of Ash’s parents, Jack’s mom, Ash’s sibling Rae, Jack’s best friend, and the rest of the dozens of people in the crowded bar were staring at Ash. 
        “Of course,” Ash said, beaming.
        A cheer rose up over the crowd as Jack stood to kiss her. Jack’s dad raised a toast, and Ash’s mom brushed back tears. Jack placed the ring on her finger and she held it up to the light.
        “It is beautiful,” Ash said. Jack took her hand and kissed her fingertips.
        “You’re beautiful,” he said.
        The rest of the night, Ash felt like she was floating. Jack and her dad came to the table with a bottle of champagne. Her grandmother called and tearfully told her how much everyone loved Jack. Rae gave her a big bear hug.
        “I am happy for you, sis,” they said.
        “I thought you didn’t approve of marriage,” Ash teased Rae.
        “Well, if you love each other so much that you want to get the government involved, who am I to stop you?” they lovingly snarked back.
        “You do realize this means you have to be my person of honor,” Ash said.
        “I am obviously always your person of honor,” Rae said, with more sincerity than Ash expected. She found herself tearing up.
        Late that night, the whole party hugged and parted. Ash was tipsy from the champagne and the excitement of the evening. She and Jack walked home, hand in hand. She kept running her thumb over her ring finger, fiddling with the band.
        As they lay in bed that night, holding each other, Ash told Jack, “The ring is so beautiful. Where did you get it?”
        “It was my mother’s. She gave it to me before she passed last year. I told her that I was going to give it to you, and it made her really happy,” 
        “You knew that you were going to propose a year ago?” she asked.
        “I knew I was going to propose the day I met you,” he said, and he kissed her again.
        The next morning, she woke up early. Jack was still lightly snoring beside her. She felt a rush of joy, remembering the night before. She used the bathroom and smiled at her reflection as she washed her hands. Then, looking down, she was suddenly hit with a sensation of horror. Where was the ring?
        She knew immediately that the duende was back. Her grandmother told Ash and Rae that the duende, a chaotic spirit creature, had followed her to America from Portugal. Ever since, strange things were constantly going missing in the family home. One of Ash’s earliest memories was searching her house for a missing teddy bear. In third grade, as she was about to hand in her big book report project that she had painstakingly written by hand in cursive, she looked in her folder, and it was gone. In middle school, her baseball glove disappeared the day of her first playoff game. On her 16th birthday, when her grandmother had let her borrow her car to take to the driver’s test, she spent the whole morning panicking as she searched for the keys that she had left on the front table. Cell phones had vanished from their chargers. Favorite novels had gone missing from her bookshelf. Multiple diaries, left on her bedside table at night, were simply not there the next morning. These precious missing items always reappeared, but she would need to trade with the duende.
        She looked back in the bedroom at her sleeping fiancé. She quietly went into her closet and got dressed. She then reached into her jewelry box and grabbed a full handful of plastic trinkets and shiny beads that she kept for just this occasion. She snuck out of her apartment, quietly closing the door behind her.
        As soon as she was on the street, she called Rae.
        They picked up with a groggy voice and said, “Ash, it is too early.”
        “The duende is back,” she said in a shaky voice.
        “No! What did that little creep steal this time?”
        “The ring! Jack’s dead mother’s ring!”
        “Calm down. Meet me by the treehouse and get everything ready for the ceremony. I’ll be right there.”
        Ash jumped in her car and stopped by the convenience store, grabbing a lighter and a box of cookies. She then drove as fast as she could down the familiar streets to her childhood home. Rather than parking in her parents’ driveway, she left her car on the street and began walking through the dense wooded path on the border of her family home where she and Rae used to play as children. She saw Rae standing in front of the treehouse their parents had helped them build decades earlier. 
        “Do you have everything we need?” Rae asked as Ash ran up to them.
        “Yup,” she said as she began to lay the cookies on the ground in a circle. Rae then helped her start up a little fire with some dry twigs and leaves in the middle of the cookie circle. The siblings held hands and recited the words they had learned so long ago.
        Sugar sweet and fire burn
        The things we lost, now return
        Then, out of the ground near the miniature bonfire, a creature began to dig out of the earth.
        Rae and Ash were never sure how their duende would appear. When they had first encountered the duende as children, it had appeared as a white rabbit. Years ago, after Rae’s piggy bank had gone missing, the siblings told their grandmother. She had told them all about the duende, a mischievous spirit that could take on the form of any animal and steal precious items. Apparently, their family had been cursed with a duende for generations. If one family member envied the possession of another family member and said the duende’s name, then the duende would appear and steal the object that caused the tension. However, their grandmother had explained that a duende could be convinced to return a stolen object if the owner of the object was willing to make a trade. She had taught her grandchildren how to bribe the duende out of the earth with fire, food, and incantations.
        This time, the duende appeared as a large chipmunk, pushing the dirt away from its puffy little face as it emerged from the hole in the ground.
        “Duende, you must return the ring you stole,” Ash harshly commanded. The creature looked up at her with intelligent, beady eyes.
        “What do you have to trade?” The duende’s voice was really quite adorable. When Ash and Rae had first heard it as children, they burst out laughing. However, the duende had been offended, and they had to wait another week before it would appear again to make a trade.
        “We have these offerings,” Ash said as she emptied her pockets.
        The duende picked up the beads and trinkets. It inspected them, then put one shiny bead in its cheek for safekeeping.
        “Where is my ring, duende?” Ash asked harshly.
        The duende put another paw to its mouth and pulled out the gold band and the sparkling stone. Ash reached down, and it dropped the ring in her open palm.
        “How did you get into my house, duende? I never summoned you.”
        The duende looked up at Ash with a mischievous squint. “Rae summoned me,” it squeaked.
        “I did no such thing,” Rae declared.
        “You called my name,” it squeaked. 
        Ash looked at Rae with confusion. Rae looked back at her. She could see the realization come over Rae’s face.
        “Oh, Ash. I am so sorry. I didn’t mean it,” Rae said with an exasperated voice. They then turned their gaze at the duende and said, “Get out of here you demon. Leave my sister alone!”
        The duende stood up on its hind legs, gathered the cookies from around the circle, squeaked at them joyfully, and then burrowed back into the hole.
        “You summoned the duende?” Ash asked.
        “I didn’t mean to,” Rae explained. “It was when we were leaving the party. I left my wallet at the bar. It was my own fault. But I cursed the duende.”
        “Rae, you know that it has to be more than that. The duende only appears when the person who summons it is feeling envy. What’s up?”
        “Ash, I swear. It’s not really envy. It’s just kind of hard. I see you moving on. A new partner. A new life.”
        “I know. It is a lot of change. But it is still me. Still my same life.”
        “I’m happy for you. I really am,” Rae said. 
        “Well, I guess that as long as you’ll keep coming back here to help me deal with the duende, I can’t really be mad at you.”
        Rae squeezed Ash’s hand and looked at the ring on her finger.
        “It’s a beautiful ring,” they said.
        “It’s just a ring,” she replied.
        “It’s not just a ring, but that’s okay. I love you, sis.”
        The siblings hugged, and returned to their separate, but always connected, lives.
