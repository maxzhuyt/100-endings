Drake slammed his entire body into the wall. It was an impressive feat, the kind of thing one only ever sees from an unexpected obstacle appearing in the way. Drake staggered back, hand going to his nose to ensure it wasn’t bleeding. It wasn’t, thankfully. 
	“Where the heck did the door go?!” Drake called into the meeting room behind him. 
	The other scientists in the room turned to regard the notably blank surface of the doorway, which now looked like the rest of the wall. 
	“Is it just closed?” Leon, a well-dressed man, asked. 
	“Obviously not, if it was closed the door would still be there, just closed. That’s just a blank wall right there,” Drake said as he ran his hand across the smooth surface. 
	Susanne, who’s hair perfectly matched her lab coat, moved up next to him and started examining the wall as well. 
	“What’s weird is that it seems to be a different wall compared to the bulkhead that the rest of the room’s walls are made of. Is that drywall?” she asked.
	She brushed Drake’s hand away, pulled her own fist back, and punched the wall. A solid thunk resounded, but the wall appeared unscathed.
	“Okay, breaking through it isn’t an option. Are we still in contact with the rest of the base?” Susanne said as she rubbed her now slightly bruised hand. 
	Leon slid his office chair over to a computer console, then began pecking at the keys in a shockingly rapid fashion, despite the inefficient typing method.
	“Looks like it. We’ve got readings from the rest of the base, can still access data outside this room, and even have instant messaging. Oh, Jen wants to know what you want for lunch, Drake,” Leon said. 
	“Just a salad for me. On a diet right about now. Also, can she even get in here? We certainly can’t get out, so it only stands to reason that no one else would be able to enter,” Drake said. 
	Leon typed away at the keyboard. 
	“Well, I just asked her to try and come in the door. Hopefully we’ll get an answer soon, but you know how busy they are in R&D, it could be an hour before they manage to send someone up here,” Leon said. 
	Susanne sighed and went back to her desk. She pulled up her own monitor and continued her work. The job of the Experimental Requisitions Assistance Department couldn’t stop, not even for a moment. Stuff like this was just par for the course here. 
	“Do you think our recent acquisition has anything to do with why the door just up and vanished?” Susanne asked as she sipped from her coffee mug. 
	The three scientists looked out of the one-way window that adorned the wall on the far side of the room from the door. Through it, they could see a twisting, morphing object that their eyes couldn’t quite focus on no matter how hard they tried. Doing so was liable to induce headaches, so they just avoided looking at it.
	Drake sat back down as well. 
	“Honestly, I couldn’t tell you what the heck that thing even is. But it's far from the most alien thing we’ve gotten our hands on. It could certainly just have the unique property to get rid of means of ingress in a specific vicinity at irregular intervals. At least this’ll make our job easier,” he said. 
	Jessica shrugged, then went to take a sip of her coffee. The cup was empty.
	“Hey, Leon. Mind filling up my cup? We’re gonna need the energy if we’re gonna get through this report on time,” she said.
	“Oh. The coffee machine is in the other room,” he said.
	Shortly after this statement, all three scientists were hammering at the wall where the door used to be, begging for release. Unfortunately for them, it took the security team an entire hour to cut through the unusually durable drywall. By that point, all the scientists were tragically decaffeinated.
