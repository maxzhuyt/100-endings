“Footloose, footloose, kick off your Sunday shoes.”
        I love this song! I would like to say that I remember the movie, but I’m a bit fuzzy on the details. I mean, I remember there was some sort of dance off, and the new guy wins, thus liberating the town from boredom. 
        If Colin Firth or Tom Hanks came to town and was like, “Yo! I’m here to shake things up,” I would be all for it, because I mean it’s Colin and Tom. Moreover, they’re not likely to bring bad tidings. However, that isn’t how things roll in real life. Events don’t happen with a great actor, good intentions, and a brilliant soundtrack. Instead, they are usually brought about by really annoying people like Marcy.
        Maaaaaaaaarcy, as my group of friends at the pub pronounce her name. Before Marcy, life was good. It’s peaceful here in Leafton, a small town located in the Northeast Corridor. Granted, it can get boring at times. I work remotely for a large company in the city just south of us. Most of my week is taken up with work. On the weekends, I stroll into town and enjoy getting coffee at Jennie’s during the day, or sometimes come instead during the evening for a beer at our local pub, Timber. If I want to, I can hop the train and go into the city, but usually I just opt to take my dog for a walk and read. Every couple of weeks, I go to Sasha’s beauty salon for a mani pedi, and massage.
        Into this tranquil routine enters Marcy from stage right. Completely unwelcome. Boo. Marcy is, how do I say it nicely? Well, if I ever see her flying around on a broom, I just won’t be surprised. I can hear her cackle-exclaiming in her high pitched voice “I’ll get you my pub dweller, and your rather large dog too!” A wicked witch to be sure. 
        Marcy’s husband is the head of the new timber industry just outside of town. He’s gone all the time, and so she has a lot of money, no imagination, and time to kill. A lethal combination. Marcy decided that our town is too forward-thinking, and that we need to go back to the “good ol’ days.” 
        I’m never sure for whom these days were supposedly good, but the changes Marcy wants are not good for anyone in the town, save for herself and the ladies of the clique who are just Marcy clones. They even dress alike, as if they buy everything from the same catalog. Which, now that I think about it, they probably do. Ever since Marcy arrived, they are all about acting like her, and getting into her good graces. It’s like high school when everyone wants to be friends with the lead cheerleader, just because of her position. Marcy’s husband is rich, and clone-like people want to be her friend.
        The first thing she did was to get a spot on the town council. The council has no legal power. However, it does influence what happens socially in town, especially amongst the Marcy clones. I never go to any of those meetings anyway. They put out trivial proclamations in the town newspaper, post them around common areas, and leave them on your car windshield.  These edicts are not legally binding (so far), but nonetheless irksome. Until they are officially enforced, they are ignored by my group of friends and me. My favorite one so far is dogs can no longer be walked in the town park after 9:00am. 
        Alas, many trees die for these decrees to be posted about the town. But, whatever, the sheriff can give me a ticket when he is not walking his dog there. I often have a hot dog with him from the local stand, and we both give our dogs a little piece. Life goes on in spite of Marcy, the clones, and their flyers from on high.
        One day, Marcy decides to take her little power trip too far. She and her clones went to the town clerk with a proposition that all families need to donate a baked good, homemade, at all town events to be used as a fundraiser for their council. Nice try, oh flying one, I don’t bake. It’s my good deed to the world to forgo baking. If I submit a baked good, people will pay not to eat it. Though, that itself is a great fundraising idea. The idea of enforced baking might sound funny, however, they have enough signatures to get it on the ballot. I shudder at the thought. 
        As it turns out, Marcy and her clones win the battle, enforced baking is up for vote in our next election, which is only a few weeks away. In my head I shout to myself, they shall not win this war!
        Dance off, it is. Because, I’ll be… well, since I’m in public, let me just say that I’m not going to let this proposal win. We are not back in the 1950s, and I’m not baking to fund the activities of Marcy and her clones. I mean, I’m not baking full stop, but I’m also not going to buy a cake at the store and try and pass that off as my own.
        I need to mobilize some like-minded people. The best place for anti-1950s feelings here is good old Timber’s. As I walk in, I see my group already talking to the bartender, Sam. Most of what’s being said should not be repeated, but the bottom line is no one wants to bake to support Marcy and clones’ proclamation posting.
        I order a beer and join in the fray. It’s lit! 
        Doug is yelling, “If I get one more note on my truck to wash it before parking in town, I’m going to lose it! I work for your husband Maaaaaarcy, my truck gets dirty running supplies to the various work areas. Get over it!”
        “Yesterday, I was getting my haircut, and one of the clones told me I should be ashamed of myself, coming to the salon dressed in jeans and a t-shirt! Sorry, clones, I am not wearing dress clothes to get my hair done!” said Terri.
        Chrissy chimed in, “One of them saw me buying some frozen dinners at the store. I’m surprised they didn’t have a heart attack. Sorry ladies, I don’t always have time to cook for myself.” 
        “Well, I illicitly buy hot dogs for my dog at the park after 9am. So many trees die for these proclamations we ruthlessly ignore!” I say. We all laugh as we make a mock toast in memory of the trees.
        I mean, it’s all fun and games until you are about to be forced to bake to fund these annoyances. We agree that there is only one thing that we can do to fix this problem. We need to get out the vote! Our rally cry will be, “Vote no to enforced baking!” 
        Sam has agreed to print flyers for us to put around the town. We all feel sorry for the poor trees, again called to sacrifice for Marcy and the clones. Doug volunteered to drop them off at the various work locations he goes to during the week. Chrissy and I will post the flyers around the town, and Terri agreed to take some when she goes to the parents’ meeting at the school tomorrow. But for tonight, we are having another round together to enjoy the company of good friends and excellent battle plans.
        We’re not going to bake without a fight! We start to sing together “we’re not going to bake it! No, we ain’t gonna bake it!” Don’t worry, we’re all taking cabs home.
        Election day is finally here, my friends. All of us are at the pub with Sam. The polls have just closed. Results will be announced any minute on the news, which we are all attentively watching. Commercials. Ugh. Then we have a good laugh, because one of them is about a cake that tastes “just like grandma used to make.” 
        The newscaster begins to announce the results. We wait with bated breath. Silence falls over the pub. Then it is finally proclaimed by the newscaster, it is a landslide no for the enforced baking proposal. 
        A resounding cheer echoes through the pub! We’re all so happy. To celebrate, Sam gives us a round of champagne on the house. Victory is ours! We raise a glass, no one will be baking tonight.
