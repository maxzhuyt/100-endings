On a foggy morning around noon, the Minotaur appeared on a New York street corner. The only people present were a hot dog vendor, and a group of businessmen who were completely unperturbed by the mythological creature’s appearance as they ate hotdogs on their lunch break. 
        “Holy cannoli, my guy,” the vendor sputtered, “where did you come from?” 
        A towering beast over seven feet tall, the Minotaur blinked his bovine eyes, looking around at his environment. His human body was chiseled, muscular and nearly nude as it glistened in the midday sun. From the neck up, tanned skin swapped for rough brown hide as the Minotaur’s strong, human-like body transitioned into a bull’s head.  For as much as a bull’s facial features could express bewilderment, the Minotaur was doing so.
        He turned to the hotdog vendor. “The underworld,” he stated simply. “Where am I now?” His voice, formed by the mind of a man and the vocal cords of a bull, was deep and gravely, with an inhuman quality to its tone and cadence.
        The vendor gestured with his tongs at the world around him. “I dunno about no underworld or whatever, but we’re in New York City, buddy.” He clamped the tongs together, pointing them at the Minotaur. “You don’t got hardly any clothes on.”
        The Minotaur looked down at himself, then back at the vendor. “Is this an issue?”
        “I mean, yeah. Cow face aside, you’re a handsome guy, but I don’t wanna see that.”
        “Hm.”
        The vendor grabbed a spare apron and tossed it at the Minotaur. “Here, put this thing on.”
        After taking a moment to study how the vendor was wearing his own apron, the Minotaur began to tie the garment around his own neck and waist. As he did, the vendor came around the back, tying another apron onto the Minotaur backwards to better cover his rear.
        “This tunic has a strange design, but it is quite breathable,” the Minotaur stated.
        The vendor just nodded. “Yeah. Sure, buddy.”
        Once dressed, the Minotaur turned to face the vendor. “What is your name?”
        “Anthony,” the vendor said, “what about you?”
        “Asterius.”
        With a scoff, Anthony replied, “Fancy name for a fancy schmuck. So, uh, where’d you come from? You just kinda… appeared, buddy.”
        Asterius hummed, pondering the question. “Of that, I cannot say. The last thing I remember is trading words with that blithering fool, Virgil, in the sixth circle.”
        “The sixth circle?” Anthony frowned. “Is that on Staten Island or something?”
        “Possibly.”
        Anthony shook his head. “Well, whatever. We live in a weird world. Sometimes things just kinda happen. Speaking of which…” He gestured towards Asterius’s face with his tongs, clapping them together. “What’s with the mask? Is that ugly mug real or something?”
        Asterius took a step closer. Anthony took a step back.
        “You have my thanks for clothing me,” Asterius said, “so I will allow you this impudence once. Do not presume to taunt my appearance again, Anthony.”
        “Okay, okay, I’m sorry,” Anthony said. He reached down, quickly assembling a hotdog with all the speed of a streetwise professional. “Here, take it easy. Eat this thing, yeah?”
        Asterius paused as he looked at the hotdog. He looked back at Anthony, and then looked back to the hotdog once more. He picked it up gingerly, sniffed it with his enormous bull nose, then stuffed it into his mouth in a single bite. One chew, another, and then Asterius swallowed. A moment of thought passed before Asterius turned back to Anthony.
        “You clothed me, and now you have fed me,” he said. “I will allow you another pardon, then.”
        “I, uh… great. Does that mean it was good?”
        “It was delicious.”
        “Right?” Anthony asked, grinning a prideful grin. “Old family recipe.”
        Asterius let out a huff of air. “I would not know of such things. My family locked me in a maze that they used to kill sacrifices from the city of Athens. I was used as a tool of death and destruction.”
        “I… huh.” Anthony just nodded. “That sounds rough, my guy.”
        “Indeed.”
        Anthony looked around, seeing that there was still no one nearby. “So, uh, what’re you gonna do now, Mister Asterius? Now that you’ve escaped from Staten Island, I mean.”
        “Of that, I know not,” Asterius stated. “This world is unknown to me.”
        Anthony took a moment of consideration, then asked, “Wanna stick around with me? You’d make a great advertisement for the stand.”
        “I will not be used as another man’s tool again,” Asterius growled.
        “Whoa, whoa, whoa,” Anthony said, shaking his head.  “I wouldn’t be using you or anything. You have rights. You’d get paid. You’d be a proper employee of the greatest hotdog stand in the world.”
        “Hm.” Asterius bored into Anthony with deep, unblinking eyes before replying, “This sounds agreeable.”
        “Great! Welcome to the team, Asterius!”
        Anthony extended his hand, and the two new business partners shook on their freshly fastened deal.
