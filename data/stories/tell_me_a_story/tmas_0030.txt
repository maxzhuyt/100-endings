No one knew where the metallic sphere came from. It hovered ominously over the group of scientists that scrambled underneath it. Their footsteps echoed through the massive cavern, causing a cacophony of sound to redouble upon itself. Not the best conditions for research.
	John added his own steps to the noise as he rushed over to answer a blinking light on a computer. His eyes scanned over the screen as it spat out line after line of data. He nodded along with the information and turned to his coworker.
	“I have no idea what any of that says. Is this even in English?”
	Jessica rolled over on her office chair to check the screen.
	“I believe it’s some kind of ancient Sumerian,” she said.
	“Okay, so why is this computer now spitting out ancient friggin Sumerian instead of a language I can read? Also, how in the world can you just identify ancient Sumerian on sight?” John asked.
	Jessica shrugged.
	“We all need hobbies. If you think that’s bad, Reggie’s computer spat out a sequence of flashing lights. Poor guy has been drooling in the medical tent for a few hours, but it seems like he’s getting better,” she said.
	The cables that ran up to the sphere from the computer banks writhed as another pulse of power pounded through them. Immediately, the banks of computers lit up again. John groaned. 
	“God, so soon after the last one? We’ve barely had time to analyze the data from a second ago. What does this sphere even want from us?” John said.
	“It seems like it wants us to work harder. That or it just doesn’t want us to get lunch breaks. Maybe some evil interdimensional middle manager is inside this thing. Maybe they thrive on giving us work like this. Oh God, maybe it’s even worse than that and this thing is using our hard work to generate enough energy to open a portal to its home dimension! Then its armies will flood through, conquering our world with their cool technology. But the portal will release a burst of energy that unlocks latent power in certain members of the-”
	“Jess, this isn’t some sci-fi novel, this is real life,” John interrupted.
	“Oh, forgive me, I forgot that an ominous hovering sphere in the middle of a recently uncovered cavern in the depths of Antarctica was part of everyone’s usual life experience. I’ll make sure to regard that when I consider what is possible in real life,” Jessica says.
	“Ugh, fine. Let’s just get through this batch so we can move on,” John says. 

	A few hours later and both of them slammed their trays of mediocre food on the cheap lunch tables. The cafeteria was located only a few levels up in the facility, which meant that the heat from the generators still reached that deep. It was one of the few places where they didn’t have to wear cold weather clothes, even while indoors. Which meant that it was by far the most crowded place in the facility. The pair had thankfully found a spot at a two-seater table, so they had some degree of privacy, but only after waiting a solid five minutes for it to be emptied of coworkers. 
	John picked through his pile of watery beans. 
	“So, wild sci-fi theories aside, what DO you think is up with the sphere?”
	“Well, it seems like it keeps sending out messages in a variety of ways. I think it's trying to find a specific format that works for us to understand what it’s saying. That, or maybe it’s trying to learn? I dunno, all I’m getting paid for is recording the output,” Jessica says through a crumbly dessert.
	“We’re on the verge of one of the most significant scientific discoveries in the history of our species, and you’re worried about your pay?” John asked.
	“Hey, we’ve all gotta pay rent,” Jessica said.
	“You quite literally don’t, your lodgings are all covered as part of the contract,” John said.
	“Well, I’m gonna have to pay rent AFTER this is all over,” Jessica said.
	“I’m getting enough money to buy my own place outright after this. It sounds like you needed to negotiate your price a bit better,” John said.
	“Yeah, thanks for the heads-up, I’ll get right on that. Anyway, what makes you think this is going to be such a huge scientific breakthrough? It’s just a sphere,” Jessica said. 
	“That’s extremely reductive. It’s a sphere that’s been sending magnetic fluctuations out across the entire globe every few weeks. The governments can only keep this a secret for so long before things start getting crazy, what will all the tech going haywire. It’s also a sphere we can’t directly interact with aside from the cables that it stuck into our computers. We have no idea what the heck this thing is, so I’d say figuring that out would be a pretty massive breakthrough,” John said.
	“Well, if anything could be in there, that means there’s a chance it’s basically nothing as well,” Jessica said.
	“In that case, I’ll have earned a lot of money from a wild goose chase, and you will have earned a middling amount of money from that same chase,” John said.
	Jessica stared at John for a beat before knocking his coffee into his lap. He leapt up with a shout, then ran to grab napkins. 
	“Teach you to be smug about pay differences…” Jessica mumbled.
	
	In the sphere’s chamber, the enigmatic orb had fallen mysteriously silent. John slapped his monitor in frustration.
	“Of course, for absolutely no reason, the thing goes silent. Any readings on your end, Jess?” John asked. 
	“Most of my monitors have nothing, but this one in the corner seems to be slowly loading something,” Jessica said. 
	John rode his office chair over to the monitor Jessica indicated. On it a loading bar, much like one he recently saw on a website, slowly filled. 
	“Uh… have we seen this one before?” John asked.
	“Nope. Do we… go on break until it’s done?” Jessica asked. 
	“For once, I think we hold off. If this is unheard of, there’s a good chance this could be a breakthrough,” John said. 
	As the bar filled, the two became aware of a growing sound in the room. A droning that built until they could feel it in their bones. 
	“Hey, I’m gonna go ahead and call security. Just, uh, just in case,” Jessica said.
	Suddenly, the bar jumped to full.
	Both scientists tensed up as the screen went black. Then, an image slowly faded into being. It was their lunch table. With them at it. The point of view from the image appeared to be from the head level of someone standing next to their table. The image moved. Jessica’s arm reached over and knocked John’s coffee into his lap. But the movements were wrong, stilted, like they were being animated by a puppeteer. Once the slap stopped, the image twitched and started again. As the second repetition played, a single question mark faded onto the screen. 
	The two scientists shared a look. It was the first time the sphere had reached out to communicate with them. Any response could be interpreted in a variety of ways by the alien entity. Jessica decided it was best to simply say something.
	“I did that because John here was being insufferable. It seemed funny at the time,” Jessica said. 
	The screen went black. With a slithering noise, the cables detached from the computers and retracted into the sphere. Then, it simply vanished.
