“I don’t know why you even hang out with her,” Luke said, rinsing the toothpaste out of his mouth. “She’s so toxic.” 
	Carrie shrugged helplessly. “We’ve been friends since the fourth grade, I don’t know. Plus, she’s Suzie’s cousin. And I’m not going to uninvite Suzie, she’s my literal best friend, Luke.” 
	Luke wiped his hands on the hanging towel and shook his head. “Whatever, Care, I just think she sucks, and I think we would all have a better time without her.” 
	"Oh, come on, Luke, she isn’t that bad. She’s fun at a party. Just steer clear of her tonight if she bothers you.” 
	“Steer clear? There’s only going to be like seven of us. Not exactly an ‘avoid her’ situation.” 
	Carrie walked over and kissed Luke on the cheek. “Listen, I know Janet annoys you the most, but it’s because she has a crush on you.” She took Luke’s face in both of her hands and grinned up at him. “And why wouldn’t she? You’re absolutely adorable.” 
	Luke laughed, sighing. “Okay, okay. Fine. I’ll play nice. But I’m doing it under protest.” 
	When they arrived at Mack’s house, Suzie and Janet were already there, helping Mack put out snacks on the coffee table. Suzie ran over, hugging Carrie and Luke tightly. Janet followed her, wholly ignoring Carrie as she narrowed her eyes at Luke. 
	“Well, Luke, don’t you look nice?” she purred, reaching out and stroking the sleeve of his shirt. Luke yanked his arm away as though he’d been burned. 
	“Hi, Janet,” he said tersely. Janet ignored his tone, turning to Carrie. 
	"Hey, Carrie Beary,” Janet said, reaching out to hug her. Carrie accepted the hug and stifled her laugh as Luke mimed himself vomiting over Janet’s shoulder. 
	Just then, Mack came down the stairs. “Carrie! Luke! Welcome to my place!” 
	“Where are your parents?” Carrie asked, looking around. 
	“Ah, they went to Bermuda for my dad’s work conference. It’s just me and my sister ‘til Wednesday.”
	“And you didn’t throw a wild rager?” Suzie asked teasingly. They all knew that Mack was an introvert and could barely handle the seven members of their current friend group; he most certainly would not be hosting any ‘wild ragers’ in his lifetime.  He high-fived each of them before excusing himself to finish getting the snacks and drinks ready. 
	“Hey man, let me give you a hand,” Luke said, following Mack into the kitchen. 
	A few minutes later, their friends, Carter and Douglas, had arrived and everyone was hanging out in Mack's living room, eating chips and dip and sipping sodas. 
	“I have an idea!” Janet said. Luke groaned, and Carrie smacked his arm. 
	“Cut it out!” she whispered. 
	“Let’s play Truth or Dare!” Janet squealed, looking around at everyone.
	The group all exchanged looks, each nodding and shrugging. 
	“Sure, why not?” Mack agreed. 
	“Yay! Okay, I’ll go first!” Janet said, looking at Carrie with a mischievous grin. 
	“Carrie, truth or dare?” she asked. 
	“Truth.” 
	Janet’s grin widened. “Okay, is it true that you wet the bed until you were twelve?”
	Carrie’s face fell, and blood rushed to her cheeks. Without answering, Carrie burst into tears and ran from the room. 
	“Carrie, wait!” Suzie yelled, hurrying after her. She shot Janet a glare as she passed her. Janet smiled innocently. “What?” she said. 
	“You know what! She told us that in confidence, Janet!” Suzie said, shaking her head in disgust. 
	Suzie found Carrie in the guest bathroom, sitting on the edge of the bathtub, sobbing. Suzie sat down next to her and rubbed her back affectionately. 
	“Care, honey, it’s okay. Really. You were a kid, and you didn’t have any control over it. And it’s been years. Everyone has embarrassing moments in their past.” 
	Carrie dropped her head into her hands, tears still streaming down her face. “I can’t believe she would tell everyone that,” she whispered. 
	“Stay right here. I have an idea,” Suzie said, getting up and leaving the bathroom. 
	She returned a few minutes later. “Carrie, come with me, please.” 
	Carrie shook her head. “No way. I can’t face any of them,” she protested. 
	Suzie grabbed her hand, pulling her up. “Please, just trust me,” she said. 
	Carrie wiped the tears from her eyes and unwillingly followed her best friend back into the living room. She sat down on the couch next to Luke, who put his arm around her and pulled her close.
	“Babe,” he whispered into her ear. “It’s okay. No one cares.” She peeked at him through tear-soaked eyelashes and saw how earnestly he was looking back at her. She relaxed a little, leaning into Luke’s side. 
	“Carrie,” Suzie said, taking her hand. “Everyone has embarrassing things from their childhood. Hell, some even from adulthood,” she said, looking pointedly at Mack. 
	“It’s true, Carrie,” Carter said. “I sucked my thumb until I was fourteen!” 
	“And I wrote a really detailed love note to my crush in middle school, and she read it out loud in front of the entire class,” Douglas said. “I skipped school for a week after that.” 
	Luke cleared his throat. “I had a crush on my mom’s best friend growing up. I was like ten, and she was like forty. But I was so in love with her, I used to secretly plan marriage proposals for her.” 
	Carrie laughed but then covered her mouth. “I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to laugh. Thank you all for sharing these. It does make me feel better.” 
	“And you already know this, but I’ll share mine, too,” Suzie said, pulling her tablet out of her bag. “I write Galaxy Voyager fan fiction and post it on my blog.” She opened the website and held it up as proof. 
	Mack cleared his throat and pulled something from behind the couch. It was a ratty old blanket. From the looks of it, it used to be a soft blue color, but was now more gray than anything. 
	“This was my baby blanket that my mom brought me home from the hospital in. I have slept with it every night of my life. I still do,” Mack said sheepishly. 
	Carter reached over and clapped Mack on the shoulder. “Nothing to be ashamed of, bud,” he said.
	Carrie looked at Janet, who had been sitting quietly. 
	“Janet,” Carrie said. “Anything to share?” 
	Janet looked around at everyone, looking back at her expectantly. 
	“I can’t ride a bike,” she said finally. The group erupted in anger. 
	“That isn’t an embarrassment!”
	“Come on, you can do better than that!”
	“Tell us something actually embarrassing!”
	Janet held her hands up in protest. “Okay, okay, geez. I wasn’t trying to hurt anyone, y’all are being too sensitive.” 
	Suzie glared at her. “Janet, we all know you were absolutely trying to embarrass Carrie with your little game. So, share something or leave.” 
	Janet sighed. “Okay, fine. I couldn’t read until I was in high school, and my parents finally got me a tutor.” 
	“Thank you for sharing that, Janet,” Carrie said. 
	“Okay, can we all go back to being friends again?” Carter asked. He couldn’t stand confrontation of any kind, and this evening had been enough to last him a lifetime. 
	“Yeah,” Douglas agreed. “Besides, I want to read Suzie’s fanfic!” he yelled, grabbing the tablet from the coffee table and standing up to read to the group. 
	“Oh no!” Suzie groaned, reaching for the tablet. “I shared that I write it—we are not reading it!” 
	Everyone laughed, watching as Suzie chased Douglas through the house, Douglas shouting lines from Suzie’s latest story.
