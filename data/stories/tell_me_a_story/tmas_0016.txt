“No way!” Jessica said as she and Megan parked in a line of cars next to a startlingly gorgeous mansion.
        “This place looks like a fancy ski lodge,” Megan said as she fixed her eyeliner.
        Megan had convinced Jessica to come with her to a fancy house party hosted by her boss, Robert Orchman. He was the CEO of an enormous oil company headquartered in Anchorage, Alaska. Megan had recently moved to Anchorage for the new job, and Jessica had flown in from San Francisco to visit her friend in this beautiful city.
        Jessica hadn’t packed any formal clothes. She had planned for a chill weekend of sightseeing, dive bars, and crashing on Megan’s couch, so had just thrown her warmest clothes in an overnight bag. But when Megan received a last minute invitation to a dinner party at her boss’s mansion, Megan said, “When your billionaire boss asks you to go to a house party, you say yes.”
        Jessica borrowed one of Megan’s little black dresses, which had looked surprisingly good on her. Megan loved dressing Jessica up. They’d lived together in a tiny, one-bedroom apartment when they were in graduate school, and they’d often shared clothes. Megan had always wanted a sister, and loved picking out outfits for Jessica. Megan’s style was always more flashy than Jessica’s, but Jessica was a good sport about letting Megan dress her up.
        he women confidently walked into the mansion, marveling at the views from the huge floor to ceiling windows. Caterers were walking around with colorful appetizers. Megan immediately ran into a few coworkers and started chatting while Jessica made her way to the bar.
        Jessica had just ordered a dirty gin martini when she felt a bump at her elbow.
        “Give her the goat cheese olives” a handsome blonde man said to the bartender. “They are really good,” he smiled at her.
        Jessica thanked him and he cracked a joke. She was somewhat taken aback by this arrogant, tall, smiling man. When she got her drink, she took a sip and exclaimed, “That is the best martini I’ve ever tasted.”
        “I know how to order,” he said.
        Jessica returned to Megan, who was perched next to a high top table, staring at her. “Do you know who that guy is?” she asked. Megan explained that Jessica has been flirting with Steve Orchman, her boss’s oldest son. Apparently, Steve had a reputation for being a quintessential millionaire party boy.
        Soon, Steve joined Jessica and Megan at their table and kept returning with drinks. Later in the night, Steve brought Jessica into a private library and gave her a glass of the best whiskey she’d ever tasted. She was giddy from the drinks and overwhelmed by the lavish beauty of the house. He leaned in and kissed her.
        As the party was winding down, Steve and Jessica were still feeling flushed and flirty. He asked her if she wanted to stay the night.
        “I have to catch an early flight back to San Francisco tomorrow,” she said with the slightest hint of hesitation.
        He said, “No way! I’m leaving for San Francisco tomorrow too. I can fly you back into the city.”
        "Fly me back?”
        “I’m a pilot. I have my own plane.” He said this with a big, cocky smile.
        “Of course you do,” she said, leaning into him for another kiss.
        Before giving Steve an answer, Jessica checked in with Megan.
        “I don’t know,” Jessica said. “I’ve got a weird feeling about getting into a plane with a stranger.”
        “Jess! If a handsome millionaire playboy asks you to fly into the sunset with him, you say yes,” Megan said enthusiastically.
        Megan was always trying to talk Jessica into “saying yes to the moment.” Jessica was usually the more conservative of the two. However, she had a few flings with some wealthy men before and was usually pretty confident in situations like these. 
        She couldn’t quite pinpoint why, but Jessica felt a slight pang of anxiety as Megan was leaving with the rest of the group.
        The rest of the night with the charming Steve Orchman was a fun and wild blur. Jessica awoke the next morning in Steve’s bed with a dry mouth and a raging headache. Steve lay next to her, loudly snoring.
        She wanted to get dressed, but only had the little black dress she borrowed from Megan. She grabbed a shirt off of Steve's floor, and put that on. Then, she heard her phone buzz in her bag. Megan had texted her a series of eggplant emojis.
        It was already 10:00 am. She should be boarding her flight right now, and she hoped that this party boy would make good on his promise to fly her back home. She’d never been on a private plane. 
        Finally, Steve groggily rolled over and saw Jessica in bed next to him. He leaned over to kiss her and then got up to use the bathroom. She noticed that he didn’t bother to close the door. He came back in, looking a big ragged. He then looked at his phone and cursed. 
        "I'm running late," he told her. He asked her if she still wanted a ride to San Fran. She said yes and asked him if he’d drive her to her friend’s place to get her bag. He told her that he didn’t have time. Megan lived all the way on the opposite side of the city, and he was already late. They’d have to go now.
        Jessica was a bit shocked with his lack of consideration, but he said it in such a charming way, that it didn’t really matter. She told him that she didn't have anything to wear, and he told her to hang on. He returned a minute later with women’s designer leggings and a soft sweater. He said that his ex never picked up some of her stuff. Jessica found the whole moment creepy, but she was still trying to play along. The clothes were a little small for her, but she squeezed herself into them.
        He asked her, “What size shoe are you,” and brought her into a mudroom full of coats and boots. It freaked her out a little that there were 4 or 5 different sized women’s boots lying around. 
        “Why do you have so many women’s shoes?” she asked. 
        He shrugged it off. “People come here on ski weekends and leave stuff all the time,” he said casually.
        She shoved Megan’s dress and her heels from the night before into a tote bag, and they headed off to his plane. She texted Megan, “I’m leaving all of my stuff to fly off with Steve. Can you bring it back when you visit next month?” 
        Megan responded with a GIF of Minnie Mouse blowing kisses from an airplane, which Jessica took as a yes.
        They arrived at the Merrill Field Airstrip. They passed by a large row of helicopters, then stopped by a single engine, two-seater plane. She was surprised by how small it was.
        They jumped in, and he told her to buckle up. Then, he turned on the engine, as if he were just in a car backing down his driveway.
        “Aren’t you supposed to do a checklist or something?” she asked.
        “Trust me,” he said. “I know what I am doing.” There it was again: that cocky twist in his smile.
        The takeoff was somewhat exhilarating. He laughed at her as she gasped when the ground rapidly fell away from them. It was exciting to be so high up in such a small craft. 
        She looked out her window and took pictures of the view. They flew over the white peaks of mountains. The peaks seemed far too close, but Steve looked so relaxed. 
        He laughed at her when she said, “Steve, that mountain looks so close!” 
        “Trust me, baby,” he said and put a hand on her knee.
        “That’s a pet peeve. I don’t really like being called ‘baby’.” She said this in a casual tone, but Steve stiffened a little.
        “Oh no?” he said, with a grin. He started rubbing her knee. Another giant white slope appeared before them.
        “Hey, Steve. Look ahead. Don’t we need to pull up higher or something?”
        He was now moving his hand further up her leg.
        “Dude! Both hands on the wheel!” She said it as if making a joke, but she could feel her body start to shake. Something was wrong.
        “Nervous, baby?” he said. His smug voice had a new ring to it. It was less charming. Underneath his calm demeanor, she could sense a hint of threat in his voice. The hair on her arms stood up. The realization came to her so suddenly that she could hardly register it. I am in danger. Gently, she tried to push his hand off of her leg.
        “Steve. For real. Watch where you are going!”
        He tightened his grip on her leg and his hand kept moving up further to her thigh. He was fully facing her now.  With only one hand on the controls, he was looking down at her chest and away from the increasingly horrifying view of the impending slope.
        “Steve!” She screamed. “Pull up! We are not going to make it.”
        He looked ahead, and cursed as he suddenly pulled at the control wheel, trying to gain altitude, but it was too late. 
        It all happened so fast. Jessica heard herself screaming. Steve began to scream too. She saw the white slope of the mountain growing before her. There was a loud beeping. Suddenly, tree tops appeared right outside of her window. She held onto her seat in a panic. Then, everything went black.
        When she regained consciousness, Jessica was in more pain than she had ever felt before. There was shattered glass on her lap. She turned her head and saw Steve. He was groaning.
        The plane had crashed into a clearing. 
        “Are you OK?” She asked.
        Steve glared at her with a fury in his eyes that terrified her. He pounded his fists down on the control panel and yelled out a long string of profanities.
        Jessica slowly began moving her body. She reached for her phone in her pocket. It was still intact and charged, but she had no service.
        She wriggled her toes. She could move both of her legs. Reaching to unbuckle her belt, she felt a sharp pain in her right shoulder, but she could move her left arm.
        With effort, she pulled the latch at her door. It opened, and she was able to step out of the wreckage. A gash on her forehead dripped down the side of her face and into the snow.
        As far as she could see, mountains stretched away from her. It was breathtaking. The sheer natural beauty would have astounded her in any other circumstance, but now she was full of terror.
        “Steve! Look!” She yelled. From her vantage, she could see a gray line of smoke and a mountain lake far below her. The smoke was clearly coming from a campsite near the lake.
        “Come on!” she yelled back at Steve, who was also pulling himself away from the wreck of the plane. “There are people down there. Let’s go get help.”
        “We are not going anywhere,” the anger in his voice made her take a step back.
        “What do you mean?” she asked.
        “You always stay near the wreck, you idiot! My dad will send a crew out for us. If we leave the plane, they won’t know where to look.”
        “But, Steve, there are people right down there,” Jessica said, frantically pointing down at the source of the smoke.
        “I know what I am doing,” he snarled.
        Jessica looked at him. Contempt was boiling in her chest.
        “No, Steve. You don’t.”
        She took a few steps. The pain in her shoulder was excruciating, but she could walk. Grabbing her little tote bag, she found Megan’s pretty black dress and wrapped it around her face. She ripped the stockings she wore the night before with her teeth and used them to bandage her arm. Then, slowly, she began to walk away from the wreck in slow, labored steps, heading down the steep slope of the mountain towards that distant smoke.
        “What are you doing?” Steve screamed these words out at her. “We have to wait here!”
        “If we wait here, we will freeze,” Jessica said. She was surprised how calm her voice sounded.
        Her panic had been so overwhelming that she now felt hollow.
        “Don’t you dare take another step. You stay right here!” Steve commanded.
        Without another word, Jessica turned her back on him. She took a step. Then another. He continued to scream at her until she was out of earshot.
        It took her two hours of painfully hauling herself through the frigid snow to make her way down the slope towards the smoke. Finally, she found the small campsite occupied by a group of hikers. She nearly lost consciousness by the time she was near enough to call out for help.
        Soon, she found herself in a tent with two older women. One woman forced her to drink something warm, and another wrapped blankets around her.
        The next day, Megan came into Jessica’s hospital room with a tear stained face. Jessica squeezed her friend’s hand. She had broken her shoulder and sustained some other minor injuries, but she’d be ready to go home the next day.
        Megan pulled up her phone to show Jessica a story about the crash. Steve Orchman had been found next to the wrecked plane hours after Jessica had made it to the camp. He was stable, but had lost 3 toes from frostbite.
        “So, if a handsome millionaire playboy asks you to fly into the sunset with him, you say yes, huh?” Jessica laughed at her friend
        “From now on, let’s just trust our guts. OK?” Megan said, squeezing Jessica’s hand.
        “Deal,” Jessica said with a smile.
