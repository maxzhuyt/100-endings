I sat at a table near the window and waited for Jim to show up. I’d been away at my first semester in college, and we decided to get together to go Christmas shopping for our families. Jim went to State U a couple of hours’ drive from here. I ended up at Wentzen, a small, four-year college on the East Coast, a three-hour flight away. We had messaged each other pretty regularly for the first week or two, but things got busy as a first-year college student, so the frequency really dropped off once we got past orientation and into our classes. Jim wanted to study engineering, so he took a heavy load of science and math in his first semester. I was just working on getting requirements out of the way before deciding, but I was leaning towards history.
        Jim and I were inseparable in high school. We ran guys’ track and played in the band together. We both played trombone and mostly ended up sitting next to each other because it was always neck and neck between us for the first-chair position. Ours was a friendly competition. I didn’t really care if I won all the time, and neither did he. We had most of our classes together, too.
        Not seeing each other every day was an adjustment at first, but I got used to it. They made it easy to make friends at Wentzen with orientation week, plus all kinds of organizations and activities. I didn’t do band or sports in college, but I joined the newspaper. Since we hadn’t really talked much in the past couple of months, I didn’t know what Jim had gotten into at State. I had some pretty big news to tell him, but I didn’t really know exactly how to work it into the conversation. Sometimes friends grow apart when they go away to school. I really hoped we hadn’t. I started to get antsy as the clock above the register ticked away.
        When Jim finally showed up, I stood to say “hi” and gave him a quick pat on the back before we took our seats together.
        “Sorry I’m a few minutes late. Parking, you know?”
        “Yeah, the Saturday after Thanksgiving. Who would’ve thought?” We had decided to meet on the second-biggest shopping day of the year. Probably not ideal, but we both had to return to our respective campuses for finals the following week.  
        “I ordered already. I got you that cinnamon latte thing you like.”
        “Perfect. Thanks.” Jim looked nervous. He shifted in his seat.
        “Buddy, are you okay? It’s no big deal that you’re a little late”
        “What? Oh. Uh, no. It’s not that. I wanted to talk to you about something before we get going with the shopping.”
        “Yeah? What is it? Sounds serious.” My heart speeded up a bit. Did he know already? Was he mad at me?
        “It is… I guess, kinda.” He cleared his throat. “So I met somebody at school.”
        “Dude! That’s great! What’s her name? What’s she like?”
        We had both done a little dating in high school. Very little in my case. I went to prom with my friend Amanda, but I suppose that counted. He’d briefly gone out with Kaylee Austin our junior year. She was big in Student Council and fairly popular, so that ended up giving him a bit of a boost in his social status. Not that he needed it. The track trophies had already sealed that for him.
        “Umm. Well, uh. That’s the thing I wanted to talk about.” Jim looked grave.
        “What, is she terminal or something? What’s wrong?” I began to wonder whether I should bother to get to my news after this.
        “No, no. Nothing like that. It’s just…” He looked down at the table before going on. “I… it’s not a girl. His name is Del.”
        I swallowed hard. This was a bit of a shocker. “Um, well, uh… does this mean you…”
        “Yeah. If you’re asking if this means I like guys, yeah, I do.” He cast his eyes downward again.
        I didn’t know how to respond, so I sat there, trying to gather my thoughts. This was not where I thought this conversation was going at all.
        He tried again. “I… I’m… I wasn’t lying to you all this time. I just wasn’t, you know, sure. Please don’t be mad at me. I…”
        I moved to stop him, but just then they called my name for the drinks.
        “Hold that thought,” I said as I got up to grab the coffee beverages.
        Back at the table, I handed Jim his cinnamon latte and sat back down across from him. His face was red, I guessed with embarrassment or just intense emotions of some kind. He obviously needed some reassurance, so I looked him in the eye and let out a breath I didn’t realize I’d been holding.
        “Dude, it’s okay. I actually have some news I need to tell you, too.”
        “Oh, well, sorry to steal your thunder, then.” He laughed. It sounded hopeful.
        I took a breath and steadied myself. “You’re not the only one.”
        Jim raised an eyebrow. “Sorry, what? Not the only what?”
        “You’re not the only one who likes guys. Me too. I’m not going out with anybody, but yeah. I’ve known for a while, too, but I didn’t want to jeopardize our friendship.” There. It was done. I said it. “I like Nick, a guy I’m working on the newspaper with, and we’re talking a lot, but I haven’t had the guts to tell him yet.”
        “What? Are you kidding me?” He looked like he was about to explode with joy. “That’s great! What? No way!”
        “I’m glad you’re so happy for me. That was a lot easier than I thought.”
        Jim burst out laughing. “Yeah, well, I'm happy I could make it so easy for you.” His sarcasm had made a return, so I could tell he was relieved.
        I laughed too. In fact, I couldn’t stop for a while. Finally, I breathed out, “Why?... How did we never talk about this before?”
        “I don’t know, man. Cheers.” He held his latte aloft and waited for me to tap his paper cup with mine.
