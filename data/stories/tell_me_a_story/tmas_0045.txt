Romeo and Mercutio were supposed to be finishing a coding project for their junior seminar. It was not a particularly challenging assignment. All they had to do was replicate the biological genome of an organic matter of their choice, and they were allowed to use their collab-AI. However, the boys had been procrastinating for weeks. They were still debating whether to replicate a cannabis plant, Mercutio’s idea, or a rose, Romeo’s.
        Romeo had an image of the flower pulled up on his projection screen.
        “Coz, what do you even need the rose for? At least cannabis has a purpose,” Mercutio asked him through bites of his protein bar.
        “You know why,” Romeo said, rolling his eyes at his friend.
        “You want to give it to Rosaline.”
        “Yup.”
        “Because of her name.”
        “Exactly.”
        “You are such a dork.” Mercutio laughed as he unwrapped another protein packet. “You’ve been spending too much time in the archives. Giving a girl a flower to tell her that you like her is old-order.”
        Romeo smiled at him. “I have an old-order soul,” he said.
        “You know, even if we code the rose, and even if we get it right, and even if Roselyn likes it, and even if she decides that she likes it so much that she is willing to overlook your extraordinary dorkiness and actually go out with you, it still won’t work.”
        “And why is that?” Romeo said, for the first time actually paying attention.
        “Because Rosaline’s family just got demoted to working class D. She got kicked out of university this morning.”
        “Shut up! For real?” Romeo said, suddenly distraught.
        “For real.”
        Romeo groaned and slammed his projection screen on the table, shutting it off. Mercutio snickered, rolled the protein wrapper into a ball. He threw it at Romeo, hitting him in the head. 
        “I’m sorry coz. It’s a known fact. All the hot girls are in D or lower.”
        “It’s so ridiculous. Do you think Mom would be so weird about me dating outside of my rank if she weren’t running for leadership promotion?”
        “Coz, your Mom is weird about everything. Didn’t you see her new post?”
        “Oh no. How bad?”
        Mercutio handed his screen to Romeo, with an apologetic shrug. He had clicked open on a new press post which projected an image of Romeo’s stern looking mother at a podium. When Romeo swiped up, his mother’s familiar, booming voice filled the room. “To answer your question, yes. My team and I fully support proposition HB8,000 to remove legal protections for those in inter-status marriages.”
        Romeo slammed Mercutio’s screen on the table next to his own.
        “Hey!” Mercutio protested, but he changed his tone when he saw Romeo’s face. It was an expression that looked more frightened than angry.
        “I can’t believe it,” Romeo said this quietly.
        Mercutio playfully punched Romeo’s arm and casually said, “Coz, don’t sweat it. It’s just politics.”
        “It is people’s lives. This is going to rip apart families. My own mother! I can’t believe she is siding with those culture war, statusist, fear mongering hypocrites!” He was yelling now.
        “Coz, you’ve got to keep your voice down.”
        There were tears in Romeo’s eyes.
        “Mercutio, this doesn’t end here. Those people Mom is working for aren’t going to stop until they control everything. Where we live! Who we are! What we do! Who we love!” He was breathing heavily, pacing the floor.
        “Romeo. Stop. Coz. Look at me.” Mercutio grabbed Romeo by the shoulder and shook him.        Romeo took a deep breath and let it out.
        “Hey. I have something that might cheer you up,” Mercutio said, a little glint appearing in his eyes.
        “What?” Romeo asked in a low voice.
        “You really want to freak out your mom?” he asked, teasing.
        “Yup,” Romeo said as he raised an eyebrow.
        “There's going to be a huge party in The Foundry tonight.”
        “The Foundry? That’s rebel territory.” Romeo tried to suppress his nervousness.
        “I know. We can reprogram the face scanners by the university exit. No one will even know that we are gone. If you want to really know “the enemy” that your mom is so scared of, this is your chance. Come on, coz. You in?” 
        Romeo looked at his goofy, best friend who was willing to risk everything without a thought. He was young, careless, and joyful. If anyone could survive in a world governed by these fearful and oppressive adults who made such absurd and cruel choices, it was Mercutio. “Yeah, bud,” he said. “I’m in.”
