“Hello darkness, my old friend” ~ The Sound of Silence
        I’m awake again at 3:00 am. Because the thoughts in my head are creeping. My mind racing instead of sleeping. And the words keep repeating in my brain, all the same. That’s the sound of, “Oh. My. God. If I fall asleep at this very moment, I can get three hours of sleep.”
        How likely is that to happen? I mean not likely since I’m thinking about it at this very moment. Nice. I have less than three hours to sleep now, and the amount of time will keep shrinking  as I continue to talk to myself. Of course, all my brain will do presently is think up alternative lyrics to Simon and Garfunkel songs. 
        Tomorrow is going to be a beautiful day as I struggle to stay awake, only to have slumber once again elude me at night. I’m going to be so grumpy. Grumpy is the nice spin I put on my mood. Trust me, it’s very dark and foul without sleep. I’ll have to do my best to avoid people. It’s better not to inflict myself on the world any more than necessary after a sleepless night. 
        Meanwhile, my dog Buttercup is softly snoring next to me, her little silver head is resting on my pillows. Insomnia is not her issue. She is under the blanket with just one front paw curled over the covers. In about an hour, she will need to get up to go outside. Ugh. The amount of sleep I may get keeps decreasing at a seemingly exponential rate.
        This brings me to my next sleep-time-reducing thought: I have to decide, should I just stay up until Buttercup needs to go out or risk just barely falling asleep only to be awakened by “the paw?” The second choice seems worse because when I wake up just as I’m falling asleep I get an awful dizzy nauseous feeling.
        Spoiler alert, I start to fall asleep and get “the paw.” Buttercup stands up in bed and hits my shoulder with her paw until I respond.  As the Borg say, “resistance is futile.” As predicted, I feel positively awful at the moment. I’m at the door, waiting for Buttercup to come back inside. She is taking forever. I flick the outside lights on and off in a desperate attempt to get her inside while I’m feeling a little sleepy. There may be hope for some sleep yet, if the dog ever comes back inside.
        I end up opening the door and pleading with Buttercup to come back inside. My pleas go unanswered. So, I tell her if she doesn’t come in, I’m going back to bed and will just leave her out there. 
        It’s during these moments that I’m not the best dog mommy. Now I feel like the worst person ever, and I have to apologize to Buttercup. I tell her that I would never leave her outside. To make it up to her, I give her some peanut butter on her bone. That’s another ten minutes stricken from my remaining sleep time. As an added bonus, I feel awful that I got upset with my dog. It’s not her fault that I can’t sleep. 
        After Buttercup finishes her peanut butter, we return to the bedroom.  One would think we could simply slip back into bed and try to sleep. However, getting settled is a project. I don’t even want to think of how much time I’m losing during this process, so I just try to suspend my belief for a bit.
        To settle in, Buttercup must first decide which side of the bed she wants to sleep on. Once that is determined, I have to hold the covers up so she can go underneath and walk in small, tight little circles until she plops. Often she plops and realizes that things did not turn out the way she expected, so we have to repeat the process. 
        After plopping, she needs to decide if she will have her head covered or not covered. Sometimes she just pokes her nose out. If she sticks her whole head out, I cross my fingers in hopes that she is content to steal my pillow. If it turns out she is not happy for some reason, we have to start at the beginning. I hear the ticktock of the time I have left to sleep playing in my mind like a metronome. 
        At this point, I probably have an hour left to sleep. This leaves me with the eternal dilemma: do I stay awake or try to sleep for a bit? Do I check the time, or is ignorance bliss? 
         I ponder these questions, and check the time in spite of myself. I have about forty-five minutes left to sleep. Ugh! Less time than I thought. Why did I look? 
        Perhaps the thirty-minute power nap can be my thing today. After all, that gives me fifteen minutes to fall asleep and thirty minutes to nap. I can do this!
        No, I can’t. I might as well log into the game I play on my phone because sleep is not happening for me tonight. I pick up my phone and the glow from the screen is blinding. I’ve the worst headache now.  Well, at least my castle wasn’t burned to the ground whileI was busy not sleeping. 
        My alarm goes off on my phone. Blah. I get up to start the day, and leave the room alone because Buttercup is happily snoring under the covers. Lucky dog, I envy her sometimes. 
        As for me, it’s time for coffee.
