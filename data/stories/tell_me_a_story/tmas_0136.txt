Many good myths about human creation involve knowledge and an apple. Sometimes, a bad apple. Other times, there is a desire for knowledge that is forbidden. They say an apple a day keeps the doctor away, and as legend would have it, such knowledge saved the liver of Prometheus. Or so the story goes. However, I think it is important to separate the myth from the god, even if he is a Titan.  
        Let’s get this story started! Cue the music! It is the most wonderful time of the year! 
        As we enjoy the season, I stand here talking to you, my friends, in front of the golden statue of Prometheus. I had a part in making this creation, which now sits in the hustle and bustle of the Rockefeller Center. But, I’ll tell you more about that later. {1} 
        It’s amazing. Festive people are willing to brave the cold and wait over an hour in line to rent dirty, used skates, just to be on the ice there in front of him, Prometheus. A golden god in the Big Apple. Oh, the irony. The music, the lights, and a tale as old as time— or older. After all, it predates the Julian and Gregorian calendars.
        You folks know the basic story of Prometheus, right? He is the god that brought you fire. You’ve done quite well for yourselves, by the way. Zeus was really upset about the situation and sentenced Prometheus to an eternal recurrence of having his liver eaten by an eagle each day. Nietzsche’s evil demon would have a field day with this myth — I mean the worst day of your life on eternal repeat. But, I digress.  
        So, as the myths go concerning Prometheus, much has been said. However, for us,  it begins with Heracles, a son of Zeus, who needed information about how to get golden apples. Prometheus had this knowledge, but he was chained to a rock, as I have mentioned, and having his liver eaten by an eagle on orders from Zeus. All because he gave fire to you humans. 
        Normally, Zeus would not want his orders second guessed and Prometheus would be eternally damned. However, Heracles found himself on a mission that required the “second questioning of Zeus” because Prometheus is the only one who can answer his question. Heracles needs to know how to get the golden apples, and Prometheus knows the answer. 
        Zeus was not happy about this turn of events, but it was his argument with Hera that put his son in this situation. Heracles shot the eagle eating the liver with an arrow, Prometheus was freed from the rock, and they all lived happily ever after. 
        Not really, that is not how Greek Mythology actually rolls, but you get the idea. 
        Sometimes a story is just that, a tale.The truth is something different from this myth you have been told. Let me tell you the real legend. 
        As you might have guessed, I, my dear friends, am Athena, the goddess of wisdom, war, and justice. I was also a mentor of sorts to Prometheus, who got the fire from my workshop. We all love our students, and Prometheus and I go way back in terms of our affinity to humankind. We have created you folks together. Plato and Aeschylus have some theories, and who I am to argue with the great ones. However, truth is in the eye of the teller. Let me tell you how the myth actually unfolds. 
        I find out that Zeus chained Prometheus to a rock for all eternity. Of course, I am upset. To be honest, eternal incarceration didn’t seem just to me, and justice is my thing. Therefore, I sent my owl to deal with the eagle. Suffice it to say, she returned to me with most of her feathers intact. 
        Contrary to the myth, I unchained Prometheus, and he being the Loki of his time, constructed a golden statue that would come to life should anyone approach. Zeus would never know. This worked for years, until the time of the great relationship problems. 
        When Prometheus and I found out about the twelve labors of Heracles, we knew it was only a matter of time before he would show up at the rock looking for answers. The statue would not be able to supply the knowledge sought by Heracles, so we had to chain Prometheus on the rock again and find a new eagle. This eagle did not eat livers, but we were hoping Heracles would have other things on his mind and not notice this particular detail. I took the golden statue to a garden at the edge of the world, where it remained in hiding for years. Thus, the poets told the tale of Prometheus’s release from bondage by Heracles. All hail the poets and their conveyance of truth. 
        The story ends here, my friend. I’m sorry I did not have time to tell you how the statue came to be in the Big Apple. I must be gone now. More tales, at another time.
