The pounding on the cockpit door irritated him. He couldn’t think. What did they expect? A flight attendant — or maybe an Air Marshal — had just tried punching in the emergency code, and he hit the override. It would be another thirty minutes before they could try again. He shook his head sadly. 
       The Los Angeles skyline loomed ahead of him. To the right, he could see the San Gabriel Mountains, the Los Angeles River, and an endless sprawl of palm trees and neon lights.
       Bam. Bam. Bam.
       Julio ran his hands over his face, through his hair and down the back of his neck. His vision roamed across the control panel, noting the altimeter and the heading and speed indicators. Six thousand feet, two hundred and fifty knots. He chuckled. Wouldn’t want to break any speed limits.
       Checking the navigational aid, he could see that the plane was slightly to the left of the heading he needed. He reached out towards the middle of the control panel and pressed the landing system localizer. As he did so, he had to turn his body slightly to the right. This brought the co-pilot's seat into his peripheral vision, and he glimpsed Daphne, his Aussie co-pilot, slumped over, still buckled in.
       Bam. Bam. Bam.
       His right hand unconsciously went to the cupholder and felt the Glock 19 pistol there.
       Julio thought back several months to the time he had completed his Flight Deck Officer training. I just wanted to protect the souls on my flights.
       Wiping a tear from his eye, Julio glanced at Daphne again. The bullet hole in her temple had burn marks around it but very little blood.
       I can still smell the nitro.
       In the distance, he could make out Long Beach and the Queen Mary. 
       “Tower,” Julio said aloud as he stared down at SoFi Stadium lit up below, “I can see you have turned on the landing lights. Thank you for the clearance.”
       Seventy thousand people crowded into Los Angeles Stadium for a Thursday night football game. Gear down, flaps 20. “Slowing down to final approach speed,” he muttered. Glancing at the radio, he raised his eyebrows in mock surprise. “Gosh, I guess I turned it off.”
       Bam. Bam. Bam.
       “Landing Checklist,” he muttered. "Wait, I guess I don’t need that.”
       Julio reached into his shirt pocket and pulled out a picture of himself with a woman and two young boys. He thought about the voice that had spoken from his wife’s phone just before he had arrived at the airport.
       “James and little churro are fine. Your Princesa says to be home this weekend for Trivial Pursuit and pizza night.” Then the call ended.
       Little churro. Julio Junior’s nickname. His wife, Veronica, he called her Princesa. Trivial Pursuit, and pizza. They hated both of those things.
       The next phone call came after he had passed through security. He had not been able to reach his wife or his sons on their mobile phones.
       “Do not alert the authorities. Get on the plane. Fly to Los Angeles. Before you land, you need to shoot your co-pilot and crash your plane into Los Angeles SoFi Stadium. Otherwise, your wife and sons will die horribly.”
       Dazed, Julio had boarded the plane. As the flight crew moved in and out of the open cockpit in preparation, he received a series of texts. Images of a package being put into the cargo hold. An earlier image of the package open, with what looked like wires and switches inside. Then a final text:
       Everyone on that plane is dead already. Do what 
       we say, or your wife and sons will die horribly. 
       Your sons in front of your wife, then your wife last.
       Julio had flown the plane almost in a trance. When they had passed over the high desert and Los Angeles could be seen on the horizon, he had pulled his sidearm and shot Daphne point-blank. She had never known what had happened. One minute she prattled on about a boyfriend in Ontario, the next minute nothing.
       As he slowed the plane and made his descent, his mind raced.
       If I try to land the plane, it could blow up. If I crash the plane, thousands more could die. He sobbed uncontrollably as he thought of his wife and children. 
       Then he remembered Inglewood Lake Park adjacent to the stadium. Eleven million gallons of water. Stunning, serene and sustainable. At the last moment, he banked in his descent and steered toward Lake Park. As the jet passed over the game in progress, Julio thought he could see the football players on the field freeze and look up.
       One hundred and fifty miles per hour. The plane will break up. One hundred and forty. One twenty-five. The 747 hit the water, bounced and skidded across the surface.
       While the plane immediately began to take on water, all the passengers and flight crew outside the cockpit were able to don life vests and make it to shore. 
       Recovery teams later found that Julio had taken his own life after bringing the plane down safely. When they found his phone and texts and searched the hold, they discovered that the pictures Julio had received were not from his plane. No bomb had been placed aboard. Julio’s wife had had her car stolen, with her phone and purse inside. She and the boys were fine and at home.
