Amea and Taleia ran through the woods hand in hand. They were close, but they had to be quick. Their absence would be noticed as those in the house woke and began their day. Identical twins, Amea and Taleia, knew they and their family only had one chance.
	It was written that “twin flames” would fulfill the nation's destiny and return the golden goose. Twins born within the nation were submitted for testing at age twelve for magical ability. This edict was strictly enforced. Any family who opposed would have their property taken away, be flogged, and sent to the mines. 
        Most families obliged, but there were some, like Amea and Taleia’s family, who refused to bow. They would risk it all to protect their girls. Everyone knew the stories. Those who failed the test were taken and returned as empty shells. Those that passed, suffered unspeakable acts to hone their abilities in preparation.
        Amea and Taleia had decided they’d steal away before they turned twelve. They had grander plans. Today, as the sun began its descent, they’d put all their months of preparation into action. They would return the golden goose to its ancestral home. 
        Amea and Taleia ran, reciting the plan in each other's minds. They could hear each other's thoughts, even in the womb. And shortly after birth, they discovered their magic. They knew not to reveal this, not to anyone, even their family.
        Amea released the hold of her twin's hand. She stopped while Teleia continued on for several more meters. Taleia dropped to the ground and interlaced her fingers. Amea planted her foot and sped off in the direction of her twin. She stepped in her sister's hand and was propelled through the air. Amea looked down at Teleia and constructed a rope that tether the two. Teleia was tugged into the air. She felt like a bird.                         Amea smiled, this was her favorite part, flying.
        Their expressions quickly changed to stern as they approached the walls of the castle. As their tether evaporated, Telia casted a cloud-like surface for them to land on. 
        There were no guards. The neighboring kingdom didn’t think much of the twins’ people. But tonight, Amea and Taleia planned to show them their power.  Amea recast her rope and used it to scale the tower. Teleia waited, keeping a keen eye on the surroundings. Just because there were no guards now didn’t mean there would not be any.
        Amea squeezed into the window. Her feet hit the floor, but made no sound. The floor was padded. She recast her rope into a ball of light, holding it high. The light revealed golden eggs neatly stacked on shelving. A bucket with golden flakes, a brush, and a rag lay underneath the shelves.
         Amea moved the light around and found a sleeping goose sitting atop a green egg in the center of the room. Amea tilted her head in confusion. Why was it green? 
        Teleia pushed Amea’s thoughts aside and encouraged her to hurry. Refocused, Ameia reshaped the light into a cage. She lowered it over the animal, who slept through its imprisonment. Amea held the cage tight to her chest and jumped out the window. Teleia caught her sister with a spell-made cloud.
        The girls gleefully walked back to their home, their prize in hand. They had accomplished the impossible. They had proven that they were the twin flames. They could see their family home up ahead. Amea and Taleia recounted their adventure, deciding how they were going to share this with the family. Their laughter escaped their minds into the world. 
        “Amea, Taleia you’re okay, where have you been? Your sisters and father have been searching for you all day,” Mother ran up to the girls and embraced them.
        “We did it, Mother,” the Girls replied in unison as they did.
        Mother glimpsed the cage hovering behind them. “Do you two have the magic?" Mother’s face contorted as she backed away.
        “Of course, we are the twin flames, and we have returned the goose,” the girls said matter-of-factly.
        “No, no, no. It is a lie. You must return it now. You cannot use your magic. It’s why we hid you. ”
        Amea and Taleia’s thoughts, fears, and questions jumbled in each other's minds. With cluttered thoughts, they blurted out. “What, why not?”
        Mother looked around in panic. She ushered the girls into their home and sat them down. She eyed the goose and sat. Mother moved to the window, hoping to be relieved with the sight of her husband and daughters. Again, Mother took her seat, unsure of what to say to the two split souls before her.
        Amea and Taleia could no longer take the silence. The panic caused even their thoughts to fall silent. 
        “Just tell us, please,” they pleaded.
        Mother closed her eyes. “I had hoped this day would never come. The fairytale of the twin flames is a lie. It is used to track and trap girls like yourself. There was never a golden goose. There is no other kingdom.”
        Ameia thought back to the tower room. The goose nursed a green egg. The bucket had remnants of gold paint. She looked to Teleia filled with sorrow. 
        “So, why tell this lie,”  Taleia shouted.
        “Our family, as all the others in the kingdom, are cursed with spreading this and other lies. We can feel the deception of our words, but to not pass them on is a death sentence. I can only tell you this now because you fulfilled the prophecy.” Tears spilled from Mother's eyes.
        Amea and Taleia never thought feeling the other's sister's emotions was a detriment till now. Their panic, pain, and pervasive thoughts built from each other.  It was a never ending tower of despair.  
        Father and their sisters entered with their bodies sunken in fear. Hope and joy rose up in them as they laid eyes on the twins. But it just as quickly vanished, seeing the terror on Mother’s face.
        “I’m so sorry.” Father said as he wrapped his arms around the twins. Mother and their sisters joined the familial embrace. 
        Amea and Taleia sat overwhelmed with emotion, knowing they only had themselves to blame. Admittedly they looked down on others knowing they were chosen. And now, the cost of their reckless action was the pain their family would endure and their imprisonment.
