Ash couldn’t imagine anything more romantic than being curled up with John in front of a roaring fire in a remote cabin in snowy mountains. They watched the snow fall as they looked out of the enormous floor-to-ceiling windows. The sun had just set over the peaks in the distance, and a pale orange glow still illuminated the sky. John’s arms were gently draped around her. She rubbed her cheek against his soft sweater and then reached to the table to pick up a warm mug of tea.
        “I think this might be the most relaxed I’ve ever been,” John said, softly rubbing her back.
        “Me too,” Ash said, taking a sip. She curled her feet up under the blanket.
        “It’s really coming down out there. Look at the car,” John said, gesturing out the window to their little sedan. It was now almost completely covered, just a barely distinguishable bump in the enormous snow drift.
        “Good thing we don’t have anywhere to go,” Ash said, yawning. John took the mug from her hand.
        “Do you remember when I went to visit you in Baltimore when you were still at Loyola and I got stuck in that huge snowstorm?” John asked.
        “Snowpocalypse 2006!” Ash clapped her hands together.
        “Remember, you didn’t own a shovel, so we had to use lunch trays from the cafeteria to dig out the car?”
        “It took hours!”
        “And then once we got it dug out, I was still stuck because the roads were so bad.”
        “Right. I was selfishly excited to have you to myself for two whole extra days.”
        “But I was no fun to be around, remember?”
        “You were always fun to be around,” she said, and then gave him a light kiss.
        “You were the fun one. I was awful that weekend,” he said.
        “I don’t think you’ve ever been awful in your whole life, John,” she said as she gave him a teasing nudge.
        “I couldn’t drive back to campus, so I missed that big chemistry exam.” John took a sip of her tea and made a face. “Green?” he asked.
        “I have Earl Grey in the cupboard. I remember you pacing around my dorm room doing that swinging thing that you do with your arms. You were convinced that if you missed that test, you’d fail the class,” Ash said.
        “Then everything would fall apart. I’d lose my scholarship. I’d never be a doctor. It felt like the end of the world. I was such a mess,” John replied.
        “You? A mess?” Ash laughed. “You’re the least messy person I’ve ever met. I was the mess. Remember? I was drunk that whole weekend. I made you walk to the liquor store in the blizzard.”
        “You only kept me around that year because I was already 21.” John smiled at her. 
        “Nah. I had an excellent fake ID. I kept you around because you were so cute.” 
        “If you like skinny, prematurely balding dudes who look like Kermit the Frog.”
        “Kermit the Frog?” She looked at him, squinted, and laughed. “Well, maybe a little. The color is wrong but there is something Kermit-y about your face. I think it's the friendliness. But if you’re Kermit, I am most certainly Miss Piggy. Kermit is an order Muppet. Miss Piggy is pure chaos.”
        “You can’t be Miss Piggy. Miss Piggy needs to be a blonde,” he said as he took a lock of her long, dark curls in his fingers.
        “But I’m fabulous and needy,” she retorted.
        “You, my dear, are most certainly fabulous, but you’re more aloof than needy,” he said.
        “Aloof. I like that. But, for real, I don’t think I ever apologized for that weekend,” she said, her tone shifting.
        “No need,” he said, kissing her again.
        “I don’t know how I would have made it through those years without you.”
        “I should have done more,” he said, stiffening.
        “You know. I think there was a weird part of me that wanted you to fail that test. It was hard for me to be spiraling down while you were on such a straight and narrow path. You had this super-specific vision of the life you wanted. I didn’t think I fit into it.”
        "I really thought that I knew exactly what I wanted. But in order to get to this very specific life, I had to walk a tightrope. I had to ace every test, get into the exact right school, and get accepted to the exact right residency. It was exhausting. But you. You were so, I don’t know, free. Is free the right word?"
        “Maybe free is the right word. Sometimes I felt free. But I think a better word is untethered. I had no direction. I was out of control,” she replied.
        “Being around you never felt out of control. It was exciting. You had guts. I was all squirreled away behind books and you just dropped out of school with no plan and just did whatever you wanted. I thought it was amazing.”
        She took John’s hand. “Yeah, but it was scary too. You were the only stable part of my life, and I asked way too much of you during all of those years,” she said. The lighthearted humor was gone from her voice.
        “Ash, it was never too much,” he said, matching her tone.
        “It was,” she said matter-of-factly.
        Ash closed her eyes. She remembered John’s face, stricken with concern, the night he bailed her out of jail. He had the same expression the next time he had to bail her out. The late-night calls. The intervention. The time he drove all the way from New York down to Baltimore to drive through the streets at three a.m. trying to track her down after a friend had left a panicked voicemail. The hospital. The long recovery. His tears when she broke up with him for his own good. His smile when she found him again. Even when they'd been apart, he'd always been there. John, her solid rock.
        John was remembering too. Her wild, young laughter. Her pale face on the hospital bed. Those awful words she said out of kindness. Those slow, grim years when he’d lost track of her.  Lonely nights. Pressure and strain building up behind his eyes as he studied. The dry, dull, lifeless feeling that came over him during their years of separate lives. The rush of joy he felt when, after nearly a decade, he saw her beautiful face again.
        They both sat in silence.
        Inside the fireplace, a log tipped over and sent sparks out of the hearth with a loud “snap.” John and Ash jumped back. John laughed and got up from the couch to poke the log back into place. He looked over at Ash who was smiling at him.
        “Ash, without you, I keep my whole life in a solid box. Everything is organized and in place. But a hearth without a fire is a lifeless stone with no purpose.”
        “John, I can rage out of control and with no direction. An uncontained fire sizzles out or burns too hot and destroys everything.” Ash got up to stand beside him. “I need you, John. You’re my hearth.”
        “I need you, Ash. You’re my flame.”
        Together, they tended the fire. All night, it burned steadily and warmed the cozy room as the snow fell around them.
