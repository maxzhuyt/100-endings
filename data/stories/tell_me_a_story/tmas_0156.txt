“Shhh.” Maven whispered harshly to Bobby, her twin, as she sat a pan of marinated chicken on the counter. 
	“But, he needs a transplant. The whole family is getting tested, you don’t think he’ll find out?” Bobby said in a hushed tone.
	“Look, as far as we are concerned, it ain’t our business. Momma didn’t want him to know, so unless she’s digging her way out of her grave and attending this cookout to tell’em, we ain’t. Got it?” Maven raised her eyebrow. 
	Bobby raised his hands in surrender. He felt nauseous. He hated secrets and wished he’d never found out that his baby brother had been adopted. Everyone bringing food was supposed to be early. That meant James was due to arrive in the next thirty minutes.
	Bobby didn’t think he’d be able to look James in the eye, and that was dangerous. The whole family knew he was the leaky pot. If he knew something, there was no need for prying. All you had to do was give him a stern look. Almost every family secret had been spilled by Bobby. 
	“I’m gonna go, I can’t do this.” Bobby grabbed his coat and made his way to the door. Maven’s hands were deep in the sweet potato bread mix. She yelled at her twin, “When you gonna grow up and learn to keep your lips tight? People will wonder why you aren’t here.” 
	“Let’em,” Bobby shouted back. He sighed as he opened the door. Bobby jerked back at the sight of James standing before him with a box in hand. 
	“Bobby, good to see you,” James said excitedly. Bobby stepped aside, so James could enter. They moved into the house toward the kitchen.
	"I've got Mama's potato salad and ribs ready to grill. You on the grill today?" James asked.
	Bobby skirted away. The less he spoke or reacted, the better. 
	Once they were in the kitchen, he’d be in the clear. Maven was known to be a talker, and Bobby could use that as cover to run. 
	Maven pulled James into a deep embrace, as he sat the box on the table. She shook her eyes at Bobby, who was trying to gracefully exit. 
	“You look good, how are you feeling?” Maven asked as she released James.
	“Today is a good day. I feel good and look better,” James laughed. 
	Maven smiled, despite everything, James' spirits were good; this was the point of the cookout. This was the Baker family way. When anyone was going through something, the family gathered, laughed, and ate. 
	“So, who’s grill’n?” James questioned.
	“Guess I am,” Bobby offered. He grabbed the pan of chicken and the ribs. James slid the door open for Bobby and watched him add the meat to the grill, before shutting the door.
	“He okay?” James asked Maven.
	“We may be twins, but I can’t read his mind,” Maven retorted as she moved about the kitchen. James stepped in front of her. She stared at him for a moment. When James didn’t move, Maven sighed. “What is it, James?”
	“The vibe is off. What’s up?” James asked, “Y'all weirded out by the testing?”
	“No… Maybe a little, but you know, we got you,” Maven smiled.
	“I know Bobby and some of the kids don’t like needles. I don’t want people to feel like they have to,” Bobby offered.
	Maven chuckled. “Grown folks' fear of needles don't mean nothing.” 
	James, exhaled hard. “I’m a little scared no one is gonna be a match. But the doctor said since our family is so big, there is likely to be a match. But I’m still not sure if I want to put surgery on someone. It is a big ask.”
	“You are a good man. Momma raised you right.” Maven turned her attention to Bobby cooking outside. She gulped. “Bobby,” Maven yelled. 
	Bobby turned and pointed to the grill. Maven motioned for Bobby to come in. She led James to the table. Bobby joined them. The three siblings stared at each other for some time
	“Well, for once, Bobby is right,” Maven lowered her head. “I got a letter from the bank. Mama had a deposit box. Nothing really of note was in there, except adoption papers. James, Daddy wasn’t your dad. Not long after you were born, he adopted you.”
	A vigorous laugh from James shook the table. Maven and Bobby looked at each other, then James. He’d surely lost it. The stress of his illness and the news must have shook a screw loose. Maven touched James' arm. 
	James covered his mouth and calmed himself. He smiled at his siblings. “Y'all two are about five years late.” 
	Astonishment and relief swept over Maven and Booby. “You knew, why didn’t you say something?” Maven pressed.
	“Probably the same reason y'all weren’t planning on telling me,” James smiled.
	“Just for the record, I wanted to tell you,” Bobby said. 
	“Look, Ma told me when she first got sick. I was mad at first, but what you gonna do?”
	“No one's sure to be a match then,” Maven responded with sad eyes.
	“We still have a parent in common, there is still a chance,” James said. “Now, if we’re done with the heavy stuff, can we have some fun?” James asked.
	Maven slapped her hand on the table. “Well, let’s get to cook’n before the horde comes.” James and Bobby laughed. James joined Bobby at the grill and Maven watched them all smiles.
