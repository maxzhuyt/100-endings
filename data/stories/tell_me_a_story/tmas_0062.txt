Kevin Anderson adjusted his glasses and pulled back on the throttle. His lab partner, Norbert Dweedle, gave a squeal of delight as they zipped through the high plains desert of New Mexico. They sped through the night, and it seemed like they could see every star in the universe shining down as they topped out at speeds of one hundred and twenty miles per hour in one of the US military’s latest scientific breakthroughs, a flying car. 
        Kevin quickly pulled a red knob, turning on the jet engine propulsion system and creating liftoff. The rounded, silver two-seater vehicle began to rise off the desert floor, and Norbert gave a boyish shout of triumph. The rest of the guys back in the lab said it would take another three months of in-house data analysis before the car would be ready for a real test run, but the joke was on them. This baby was boogying! 
        The year was 1947. Norbert and Kevin were two young scientists working at a secret military lab in Roswell, New Mexico. For the last three years, they had worked as military chemical engineers for the Department of Defense on Air Force contracts during the war at various European bases. They had hoped to come home and land a gig at Los Alamos Labs in the nuclear science division. But instead, they were sent to this remote outpost. At least once the war ended, the Los Alamos boys could head into Santa Fe for a margarita or three on the weekends. Here in Roswell, the closest hotspot was Sally’s Diner, whose only claim to fame was if the saucer under your coffee cup got cold, your drink was on the house. 
        But this little adventure couldn’t be topped by anything going down in Los Alamos. The silver bullet of a car had risen to around 3,000 feet and was coasting beautifully above the desert. Mesas and buttes lurked in squared-off shadows in the moonlight, and Kevin and Norbert could just make out the edges of the Guadalupe Mountains in the distance. Just about the time they were going to turn the flying car around and make their way back to a decent landing spot, red lights started flashing across the dashboard. 
        “Ummmmmm. I don’t think it’s supposed to do that,” Norbert said with an air of concern in his voice.
        “I am aware, NORBERT. Give me a minute.” Kevin began to pull on various dials, but the flashing lights were now accompanied by a high-pitched alarm.
        “Nope. That’s not right at all,” Norbert said as he pulled out a large, leather-bound book he had brought along for the ride, shuffling through the pages nervously as if looking for a catchall lever or perfect singular button that would right all their troubles.
        “Seriously? You brought your dissertation?” Kevin shouted. 
        “I wrote it on jet propulsion systems! I thought it would come in handy!”
        The car began to shudder and gyrate wildly. Kevin tried to regain control, but soon found that it was rapidly gaining altitude. 
        Norbert took his dissertation and proceeded to bang it all over the dashboard keys like a hammer. 
        “You idiot! My fingers!” Kevin yelled after Norbert bashed his right hand with his far-too-long dissertation. “We need to use the emergency eject release. Now!” Kevin used his left hand to slam a red and orange-striped lever up and press a green button below it. 
        The two seats in the vehicle shattered through the glass roof of the car, with Norbert and Kevin buckled firmly in place. They plummeted several hundred feet before two red and white-striped parachutes erupted from the seats. The two men drifted down into the desert. Kevin got entangled in the branches of a mesquite tree, while Norbert landed face-first in the dirt. They watched in horror as the flying car flew solo up into the night sky before exploding into a million pieces. Red points of light, like tiny laser beams, shot across the sky for a brief moment, and then all that remained was a smoke trail curled up in the moonlight.
        Within twenty minutes, a US military convoy arrived. Six marines jumped out of a military truck, armed to the teeth. They said very little and merely motioned with their weapons at the two shaking scientists who held their hands high above their heads. Kevin and Norbert hopped into the bag of the truck and took a long and awkward ride with the armed men back to the base. 
        Upon arrival, Kevin and Norbert unceremoniously shoved into a small, windowless backroom with a very cranky general. The two engineers were surprisingly unscathed from the accident, save for Kevin’s now massively swollen smashed fingers and Norbert’s two black eyes from his crash landing. 
        General James Owens, a fifty-year-old veteran whose forehead had more creases than a bulldog, spoke in a low, growling voice. “Boys, I have a few questions for the two of you. And unless you want to be working at a military research facility in Saskatchewan for the rest of your career, I suggest that you answer me honestly here and now. So, first things first, why did you decide to take a top-secret military vehicle on a midnight joyride in the desert?”
        Kevin knew a good answer for this one. He said immediately, "Well, sir, we are the leads on this project. I know it may seem a bit hasty, and I accept full responsibility for that haste, but this vehicle was more than ready for a field run. My colleagues have been far too conservative in their estimates of its readiness. And so I decided that we should go ahead and do a test drive. After all, we must keep ahead of the Soviets at all costs.”
        Norbert looked at Kevin with a knowing and slight smile on his face. “Yes, General. I agree wholeheartedly with my colleague Kevin here. We rushed the test this evening. But before the crash, it was an absolute success!”
        Kevin winced. Norbert had said the word CRASH.
        The General had heard it too. “Yes, you mention this little crash. Not only was this crash of a highly classified vehicle seen and reported by multiple civilians in the region, but the loss of the vehicle has cost the US government an outrageous amount of money. Your going against scientific protocols is one thing. But the crash and the loss of a military vehicle are major national security issues. I could have the two of you court-martialed for this. So I need details. Who was responsible for the destruction of this highly valuable asset?"
        Norbert gulped. He began to sweat. And then a tiny bubble of a thought blew up and popped in his head.
        “Aliens.” He said it calmly and clearly. “Yep. It was aliens.”
        Kevin’s eyes bulged out of his head, but he kept the rest of his face completely still. Meanwhile, he stomped hard on Norbert’s left foot, quietly under the table.
        General Owens leaned back in his chair. One side of his very frowny mouth lifted in an incredulous expression. “Aliens? Really? So what did these aliens do?”
        Kevin decided that he might as well go all in at this point. "Well, sir, we were flying at about 3000 feet when we saw this strange vehicle hovering next to us.”
        The General looked at Kevin over his glasses. “Ok. What did the vehicle look like?
        Kevin panicked a little. All he could think about was the stupid cup of way too hot coffee at Sally’s Diner that they served on a flimsy little… “Saucer, sir! They were in a big, silver flying saucer! And they shot us down with some kind of weapon, sir!. We crashed, and here we are.”
        “Well, son. How do you know it wasn’t some kind of advanced weapon from the Russians?” The General replied.
        Kevin’s mouth opened, but his mind went blank. Then Norbert spoke with a big grin. “Because we saw them, sir! Yes, we saw the aliens! They shot at us from their ship, and just before it exploded, we ejected and crashed. Then they landed their flying saucer and stepped out of their ship. We saw them, but they left just before the army came and rescued us.” 
        Norbert looked very satisfied with himself and this answer.
        “Ok.” The General said. “So, what did the aliens look like?”
        Kevin’s eyes narrowed, and his brain reached back into the dusty recesses of his visual memory.  He recalled an old billboard they flew past as they crossed over the abandoned highway in the flying car hours earlier. It was for a yard maintenance company called “Roswell’s Green Men Lawn Care.”
        “They were little green men with big heads!” Kevin said.
        Norbert looked at Kevin’s right hand, his fingers now cartoonishly large and swollen.
        “And they had massive hands with giant fingers.” Norbert waved his hands in an awkward motion.
        Kevin looked back at Norbert, staring at his increasingly dark skin under his battered eyes. “And they had big, black eyes! HUGE!” He said.
        “Uh-huh. Yep.” Both men muttered sounds of agreement.
        The General sat back in his seat, crossing his arms. “Well, gentlemen. That is quite a story. I guess you don’t remember what kind of weapon the aliens used to shoot our very expensive flying car out of the sky, do you?"
        Kevin was about to say "no" when Norbert got a bit carried away. He leapt out of his seat and said, “Yeah, they shot us out of the sky with their red laser beams going PEW PEW PEW!” Norbert closed one eye as he blasted his finger guns randomly across the room before Kevin grabbed him by the shirt collar and yanked him roughly to his seat.
        The General stood up and pushed his chair under the table. “Thanks fellas. I have all the information I need. I don’t think you two will be needed in Saskatchewan, but I hear that Alaska is lovely this time of year.” With that, he promptly left the room. 
        Norbert’s brow furrowed into a question mark as Kevin banged his head loudly against the table.
