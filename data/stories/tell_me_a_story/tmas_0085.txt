“I don’t think I can do it…” Amara said, standing on the edge of the ocean, squeezing the warm, crumbly sand between her toes. She looked out at the expansive, glowing turquoise sea. Amara knew that there was no telling all the secrets it held, and that’s what scared her the most. It didn’t matter how many marine biologists or deep-sea divers there were out there, the unknown was what stopped her, every summer, from floating in the deep blue.
        “Sure you can, it’s fun! Just take it one step at a time. You swim in pools!” Marina said, throwing her arms up in the air as the slow, wavy water curled and sloshed around her body.
        “Yeah… I can also see what’s in pools!”
        "No, you can’t! Do you know how many people, especially children—”
        “Don’t you dare finish that sentence!” Amara yelled, trying not to think of what Marina was about to say. 
        Marina laughed and waded back over to Amara, grabbing her wrists and slowly walking her backward into the water. Amara closed her eyes, allowing herself to be led into the ocean. 
        “See, there you go. Now, do we have to do this on every vacation?” Marina asked.
        Amara opened her eyes, giving Marina an irritated look. This was the third year in a row that they had gone on a vacation to the beach together. Amara was unsure why she kept going since she had been afraid of the ocean her whole life, but she figured she could swim in pools, enjoy the scenery, and have a couple of drinks at night. This summer, after another) heinous breakup, a vacation felt exceptionally important to Amara. The insufferable ocean was worth it if it meant she got to take a few weeks off work and chill with her best friend, trying to forget the painful months that now laid behind her.
       The sun started to hide behind the ocean, casting different hues of pink and orange across the sky. Amara and Marina headed back to their beachfront hotel room, their balcony visible from the coast. The sand was much cooler now, a nice contrast to when they first arrived, hop-skipping their way to the water, the tiny rocks burning the balls and heels of their feet. Their matching beach bags (white, with baby-blue stripes) hung at their sides, Marina’s slipping off every once in a while from her soaking, salty shoulder. 
        Their hotel room was quaint and small. The two queen-sized beds were white, with blue headboards and folded blankets on the ends with some computerized print of a swordfish on them. Above the bed hung photos of generic fish of all colors—orange, blue, and yellow - all with mouths opened as if they were singing. In a small corner was a “kitchen” area, which really consisted of a miniscule refrigerator, a microwave, and a coffee pot. Their bathroom was tiny, with a sink, shower tub, and toilet. Every morning, they had to take turns getting ready. Neither one of them was sure why they did their hair and makeup if they were just going to go swimming an hour afterward, ruining the time they spent - but they did, anyway. 
        After they showered, Amara and Marina went down to the hotel restaurant and bar. Amara, never the seafood type, ordered a cheeseburger and a side salad, while Marina feasted on oysters and crab. They both drank Blue Lagoon margaritas, a concoction of alcohol and syrups that they weren’t quite sure of. The bittersweet flavor burned Amara’s chest, causing her to cringe and shiver with each passing drink. Men sauntered by their table, Amara glancing at each one, hoping to gain some attention, or at least, a summer romance. Most of them looked at Amara but then turned their attention to Marina, sometimes stopping to talk or flirt with her. Amara was unsure how Marina did it. 
        “What about him?” Marina asked Amara with a sly smile and a laugh.
        “It looks like he’s wearing a ring on his finger,” Amara responded.
        “Maybe,” Marina said, “but that’s never stopped me before.” She picked up her drink and walked over to the mystery man. 
        Marina had this intoxicating way about her—a mysterious lure that people were drawn to. On the other hand, Amara, whose failed relationships were plentiful, could hardly get anyone to look at her anymore. Marina walked back over to the table quickly.
        “He’s engaged,” Marina whispered to Amara. “We’re going for a quick walk on the beach. Want to join us?”
        “No, that’s okay, Marina. I’ll just head back to the room. I’m almost done with this, anyway,” Amara responded, holding her drink up and then sipping the last of the bright blue droplets through the straw.
        Amara fell asleep quickly, her body becoming nearly motionless right when her head hit the pillow.    It wasn’t until 3 a.m. that she was jolted awake by a light banging on the balcony door. She blinked her eyes swiftly, rubbing them nearly raw, and finally got them open enough to look at Marina’s bed. It was dark—it took a minute for her eyes to adjust, but she realized Marina was not there. She shifted under the covers, then pulled them off of her. 
        “Marina?” Amara called out. “Marina?”
         She sat up and walked over to the sliding glass door, slowly flipping the lock from down to up, and stepped out onto the porch. The wind was forceful and intense, with a heavy current gusting around her. Sand from the beach below blew in her eyes, her hair whipping all over her face and forcing its way into her mouth. Amara coughed and quickly ran back into the hotel room. She flicked all the lights on, and slammed the door to the bathroom open, still calling Marina’s name. Stinging tears started to filter out of Amara’s eyes, scared and worried about her best friend. Amara rushed out of the room and down the stairs, assuming it would be faster than the elevator. 
        The hotel lobby was nearly empty, with only a receptionist behind a desk and a few early-morning arrivals checking in. They noticed Amara, breathing heavily, calling out, “Marina! Marina!” 
       “Uhm, sometimes people have a little too much fun at the bar, I guess,” the receptionist said with an awkwardly scared smile. 
        Amara pushed the doors open and made her way outside, her bare feet grating against the sodden, concrete ground. She ran to the beach, her silk shorts nearly hanging off her hips. Once she reached the oceanfront, the sight before her eyes terrified her. 
        Marina was standing on the outskirts of the water, a cyclone swirling around her. There was a crystal-blue glow. She held her arms stiffly, her palms facing out. Amara squinted. “Marina? Is that you? You should come inside, it’s too windy out here. Something is going on.”
        “I can’t come inside, Amara.” Her voice was trance-like, the words coming out slowly and calmly. Amara, arms crossed and shivering, stepped to Marina’s side. Her eyes were closed, and her body was stiff yet harmonious. Amara reached out to touch Marina’s arm, but before she could, a blue, ghost-like figure appeared before her. Amara squinted her eyes again, trying to make out exactly what this creature was. 
       “You’re….you’re Marina?” Amara said, as both a question and a statement. 
        “Hello, Amara. I’ve been wondering when you would come to the ocean,” Marina, now a supernatural figure, said. The voice was high-pitched and echoed.
        “What…what are you? Where’s Marina?”
        “I am Marina.”
        “But, Marina is a human!”
        “No, you’re wrong. I, Marina, am a nixie.”
        “What?” Amara’s hair was still thrashing around, her arms crossed. She could feel the goosebumps welling up on her skin and the hairs on the back of her neck standing up.
        “A nixie is a water spirit.” Marina shifted around, the water following her every move. “We would like you to join us here in the ocean, Amara. You can live amongst the other nixies. We find men and lure them in. It’s truly a wonderful life.”
        “I can’t. I have a job, a home, and a life,” Amara shouted at the top of her lungs.
        “Maybe you do, but what could be better than spending the rest of your life in the water with your best friend, luring men here one by one? You may have a life back home, but every relationship you’ve had has failed. If you come to live with us, you can have any man - and as many men - as you could possibly want.” 
        “That sounds terrible!” Amara shouted back. “I do not want to do that. I’m not that desperate!”
        “Are you sure?” Marina shifted again, and all of a sudden images of Amara and her past relationships came bubbling up. The replay always started out happy: meet, fall in love, laughter, and pet adoptions. Then, it turns dark; there is fighting, screaming, and crying, and eventually, one of them leaves. Amara started crying at the thought of being alone forever. Now that she knew who Marina truly was, she really would be alone, losing her best friend. 
        “We’ve been coming to the beach for three years, Amara. And never once have you wondered or noticed that the men who flirt with me all disappear. They’re here, in the ocean. They’re mine. If you join us, you, too, can be beautiful and alluring and have any man you want.”
        Amara had tears flowing down her cheeks, the salty drops landing on her lips and chin. 
        “So if I come into the ocean, you’re telling me I’ll no longer be lonely?” Amara yelled with intense wonderment now, considering the offer.
        “Of course, and the best part is, you can turn into a human form at any time. Come, join the water spirits. We’ve been waiting for you for three years and have your contract all ready to be signed.”
        “Contract?” 
        “It’s very simple, Amara. Just an agreement that once you become a water spirit, you’re ours for all time. But the benefits far outweigh any negatives. You are free to roam the ocean, bring in any man you like, and you’ll be with me, forever. Just sign right here, on the line.” Marina held up a contract made out of crushed seashells and rock. Amara, still with tears coming down, couldn’t deny that the life of a nixie sounded alluring and seductive— two things she had always wanted to be, but never really felt.
        “O-okay,” she said, taking small steps forward. She could feel the water misting her body more and more. A broken limb of orange coral with ink on the tip appeared before her. Amara grabbed it, her fingers wrapping around the end. She stopped abruptly before signing, considering a negotiation. 
        “Take it or leave it,” Marina said. 
        Amara put the rigid coral up to the rock-hard contract and signed her name: Amara Bardot.
        A rush of water came swirling around her, and she was lifted high into a wave, taken out to sea.
