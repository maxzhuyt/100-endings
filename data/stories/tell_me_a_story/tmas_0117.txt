Joan’s landline rang and a surge of hope ran through her body. Sure, she’d get the occasional telemarketer, but there was only one other person in the world who still had this number memorized. 
        She picked up and immediately said, “Kev, please tell me it's you!”
        “Hi big sis.”
        She heard the familiar, warm voice on the other line and she felt tears leaking out her eyes, the first she’d cried all day. “It is so good to hear your voice,” she said, her throat catching.
        “Are Nina and Sammi okay? Are you all together? Where is Nabeel?” Kevin’s questions were spilling out rapidly. Joan tried to compose herself as she responded.
        “I’m with the girls. They are fine. I’ve just been trying to distract them but I think they are more upset that they can’t watch YouTube than that the whole world is in darkness. And Nabeel is still on rounds at the hospital. I mean, I hope. I can’t even imagine what it is like over there right now. I haven’t been able to reach him since the power went out.” She was shaking and speaking in a low voice. Her daughters were giggling while playing “Go-Fish” by candlelight at the kitchen table. She took a breath and tried to calm herself. She knew she had to conceal her panic from them as long as possible.
        “If anyone can keep their head on straight in all of this, it’ll be Nabeel,” Kevin said these words with his usual, confident tone, but Joan could hear the tension in his voice.
        “I know. I just… I want him here. I know it’s selfish, but..”
        “Of course you do. He’ll come back. He’ll be okay.”
        “Is that what you are telling your flock? That it will all be okay?” Joan asked.
        “No. I haven’t said that. I’m just…” his voice trailed off.  Joan could hear a low murmur of singing in the background and then organ music. For a moment, both Joan and Kevin listened. 
        “Amazing Grace,” Joan said.
        “Yeah. Sister Margie has had the whole congregation singing for hours.”
        “Sister Margie! No way! She is still alive? She was like 80 when I left the church!”
        “Oh, she is alive and kicking. You should see her. I think this whole thing has given her a new lease on life. She’s been the one running things all day.”
        “Sister Margie! I haven’t thought about her for years. I had her for 3rd grade. I used to be terrified of her.”
        “Oh, you think she was scary then? You should see her in a budgetary meeting. She’s a force!”
        “I can imagine. Is the whole church packed?”
        “It is like Christmas and Easter combined,” Kevin said with a little laugh. Joan began to laugh too. Her breathing slowed. For the first time since she had awoken to a dark sky, she felt her heart rate returning to normal.
        “So, Father Kevin. Tell me honestly, what are you telling everyone? How are you explaining this?”
        “Joan, the sun didn’t rise today. You know I can’t explain this.”
        Joan heard a shout coming from the kitchen. Nina, youngest daughter was yelling                               “You’re cheating!” at Sammi, her oldest.
        “Mom, I’m not cheating,” Sammi shouted back.
        Joan lowered the phone. “Girls, be nice,” she called into the kitchen.
        “I’m hungry,” Nina whined.
        “Grab some crackers.” Joan took a deep breath and then picked the receiver back up. She made sure the girls were out of earshot before she said, “Kev, I know you can’t explain it. But what are you telling everyone?”
        “Just the basics. Pray. Have faith. Trust in God’s love.” 
        “You sound exhausted, Father Kevin.”
        Kevin laughed, “Please, don’t call me that.”
        “I’ll call you whatever I want,” Joan laughed back. 
        “Truthfully, I don’t know what to tell anyone,” Kevin said. Joan couldn’t read Kevin’s tone.
        “That doesn’t sound like you. You’re everyone’s favorite priest. You always know what to say.” She said this with a teasing note, but she was desperate for her brother to comfort her. “Come on, Kev. Tell me what you actually think is happening.”
        Kevin took a deep breath and solemnly said, “The sun will be darkened, and the moon will not give its light.”
        “And the stars will fall from the sky, and the powers of the heavens will be shaken,” Joan continued. “Matthew, 24.”
        “You remember,” Kevin said.
        “I remember,” Joan returned.
        “My skeptical, atheist sister quoting scripture. I never thought I’d see the day,” Kevin said gently.
        “Kevin. The sun didn’t rise.”
        “I know,” he said.
        Joan felt a tug at her pant leg. Nina looked up at her with big, questioning eyes. “Can I have peanut butter?” she asked.
        “Sure, sweetheart,” Joan said, giving her a squeeze.
        “Hi munchkin,” Kevin said.
        “Uncle Kev says ‘hi’,” Joan told her daughter.
        “Hi Uncle Kev,” Nina said brightly. Then she squirmed out of her mom’s embrace to get herself a snack.
        When Nina was out of the room, Joan asked, “Kev, do you actually believe that these are the end times?” She tried to say this in her trademark flippant tone, but her brother knew that the question was in earnest.
        “I don’t know. I really don’t… But I know one thing for sure,” he said.
        “What is that?” she asked.
        “I love you,” he said.
        “I love you too, little brother.”
        They sat in silence, listening to the church ladies singing in the background.
        “Glory, Glory, hallelujah!
        Glory, glory, hallelujah!
        Glory, glory, hallelujah!
        His truth is marching on.”
