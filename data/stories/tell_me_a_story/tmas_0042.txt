“Jammer, why’re you fiddling with that thing? There won’t be anybody calling us, not now, not ever.”
        Jammer brushed their hair out of their eyes and sighed. “Just because you don’t think things’ll ever get better, Stepper, doesn't mean you get to try and snuff out my hope that we’re not alone like it seems.”
        The young man grimaced but nodded.
        “Besides, Stepper, WorldGov’s pretty confident these days. The last rebellion was, what, twenty years ago? All the dissidents stamped out, all the deviants eliminated, everybody’s one big happy family divided into their work units and affiliation groups. Everyone has a place, and everyone in their place, and no room for anybody to fall through the cracks, right?”
        Stepper snorted. “Except for us, and whomever else there is out there who doesn’t fit in. You’d think WorldGov’d have the statistics, right? You found those notes in the archive, the ones that talk about the percentage of what they called LGBTQIA people?”
        “Somewhere near one in fourteen,” Jammer responded. “There’s got to be a lot of us out there, but WorldGov makes sure they don’t know what they are. You and me, we only got out because we ran before the authorities got to us and reformatted our brains. Made us think we were two women, put us in pretty dresses, bowing and serving and the rest.”
        Stepper shuddered. “Or made Bobbisox think she liked men.”
        “Exactly. WorldGov would’ve made us fit their idea of people, never mind what we wanted. We got out, thanks to Ash. Their parents are happy little cogs in WorldGov, but Ash wasn’t ever gonna be happy as just one gender. I dunno how Ash figured the three of us out before WorldGov could, but even with the hiding and surviving, I’m still glad I’m here an’ not there.”
        The interior door to the bunker slid open, and Bobbisox bounced in, gleeful and balancing a couple of duffle bags across her shoulders. Instead of her usual habit of throwing “liberated” goods onto the floor, she called, “Hey, bit of help here you two. Delicates, and I don’t mean pretty underthings.”
        Stepper was on his feet immediately, while Jammer carefully put down the soldering iron and switched it off before they stood up. Bobbisox was fantastic at scavenging, and if she said something was delicate, there was a good chance it was technology they didn’t have access to.
        “How’d you get this stuff?” Stepper asked with a bit of teasing in his voice. 
        “WorldGov shut down a clinic, but some bureaucratic snafu meant the removal teams didn’t get to it right away.”
        Jammer whistled. “Wait, a clinic clinic? Like medical scanners, surgery beds, that kind of thing?”
        Bobbisox winked, and responded, “Exactly. I couldn’t get everything out of there, not without getting all the wrong kind of attention, but most of the internals were easy enough. Ash confirmed last year that WorldGov just tosses all of it into a crusher, so nobody can get it onto the black market. And the removal teams don’t check the weight before loading it up.”
        As Stepper was unloading the first duffle bag, he swore. “Did you disassemble one of the surgery beds? I mean, either that, or you removed a bit of internal working from every single machine.”
        “I thought about disassembling one of the beds,” Bobbisox said with a shrug, “but that’d have been obvious and we already have one. What you’re looking at is one piece of critical electronics from every single thing in that place. Nothing with trackers, before you ask. Don’t want the WorldGov to turn my friends into brainless puppets, you know?”
        The inner door slid open again, this time revealing Ash in their boy mode. “Hey all. Good news, bad news, I’m afraid. The parents are going away for a week, and I have to go with them.”
        Jammer grimaced as they continued unpacking, moving on to the second duffle. “Sorry to hear that, friend. But what’s the good news?”
        “Looks like Bobbisox got the good news. I used my dad’s passcode to delay the removal team for a clinic that WorldGov shut down. I couldn’t juggle the asset inventory, though,” Ash sighed as they went behind a screen to change clothes. “But I think maybe there’s enough there to get our surgery bed operational, maybe. Bypass the restrictions, get it working the way we want. What do you think, Jammer?”
        Jammer looked everything over, and started nodding slowly. “I think yeah. It should be enough to get the machine running, get Stepper fixed up right. A body that’s his, and not the body he grew up in.”
        “Doesn’t mean I’ll abandon you,” Stepper responded instantly. “WorldGov’s records won’t list me as a guy, and anyway, you’re my friends. I don’t abandon friends.”
        Bobbisox put a hand on Stepper’s shoulder, and squeezed gently. “Never thought you would, Step.” She was about to say more when Jammer whistled low and long in surprise.
        Ash came out in a dress and tilted their head. “What?”
        “Comms tech. Like, kind of the piece I needed for my little transmitter project,” Jammer responded, reverently. “I’d kiss you, Bobbisox, if that wouldn’t make it weird.”
        Bobbisox curtsied, the way WorldGov taught the population they identified as women to do, and grinned. “Okay, my instincts were right then. And I know you, you’re not talking about the kind of kiss I’m looking for, but heartfelt thanks go a long, long way.” She settled on the scavenged couch, and exhaled slowly. “You really think that transmitter thing’ll work?”
	Jammer leaned back a bit, and thought. “We all know the story from that archive we found. A hundred years ago, humanity had a big fight over people like us. Those like us, or who supported us, tried to make things better, but the rest became WorldGov and drove them off. They’d built starships in secret, though, and saved who they could. And said they’d come back for us in the future, on account of how humanity just keeps having children, and people like us are actually normal and not deviants like WorldGov says.”
        Stepper sighed as he began putting the electronics into their makeshift shelving units. “It sounds all right, but wouldn’t they have come back before? That’s why I don’t know if this’ll work, Jam.”
        They nodded as they finished emptying the second duffle. “I know. But that receiver I built before I ran? It was picking up something other than background radiation.” Jammer held up the precious piece of technology. “The tech should be similar. I know I can build a receiver, and the information in the archive told me how to build a protected transmitter. Now it’s just a matter of putting it together.” They looked over at Stepper. “After making the bed work, of course. The bed, then the transmitter.”
        He looked around the room, then at Jammer. Ash and Bobbisox were silent, watching the two with concern.
        Finally, Stepper said, “If they’re out there, they’ve had a hundred years to improve the technology WorldGov uses. How long will it take you to build your transmitter?”
        Jammer began to smile. “A couple of hours, maybe less.”
        Bobbisox exhaled. “I’m okay with that, as long as the bed goes next. Stepper’s needed this for years.” She looked over at Jammer. “You too, though. Ash can’t, not yet, because their parents would notice. But don’t think I haven’t seen you sighing about the parts of your body that aren’t right for you.” She looked at Ash. “Speaking of, how long do you have?”
        They shrugged, their dress shifting slightly. “My parent-lackeys leave the day after tomorrow. I’m over at a friend’s house, and we’re studying for the entrance exams. Jason’s kept my cover all this time, I trust him.”
        Stepper nodded. “Okay, transmitter first, then get the bed up and running. I should get out and scavenge some food. It’s about time for the markets to start dumping the unused stuff, and if Bobbisox is up for going out again, we can get back with a few days worth of supplies by the time you’re done, Jammer.” When the young woman nodded, Stepper grinned. “Any requests? I figure whatever happens, we’ll want a bit of a feast.”
        Ash perched on the couch after the two left, watching quietly while Jammer assembled the transmitter they’d designed. Based on the blueprints from the archive, it was slow progress since technology had advanced in the past century. But by the time Stepper and Bobbisox came back with food, the transmitter was ready, humming quietly. Ash had changed outfits a couple of times during that hour and a half, needing to accommodate their gender shifts, and was now in a pair of sporty black leggings and a sleeveless tunic salvaged from a clothing shop.
        Jammer helped put the food away, bouncing a little bit as they did. Stepper was quiet, but Bobbisox filled in the gap with all the gossip she’d collected about various people they knew before they went on the run from WorldGov. The bunker they’d found was underground, built back during the war when there’d been the threat of city-destroying weapons, then abandoned after WorldGov took over. It still got enough power for them to live, and over time Stepper had rerouted other essential utilities, making it enough of a home for the three of them to hide from WorldGov. 
        Finally, Ash, Stepper, and Bobbisox settled onto the couch as the only place big enough for them, while Jammer sat on a scavenged chair and began tuning the device into the frequency they’d last identified. Almost immediately, a voice began speaking, not quite male or female.
        “– we have monitoring stations around the planet, listening for your signal. We hope the totalitarian state hasn’t suppressed all of you, and that some of you can find this message and respond. We’re here, we’re queer, we’re free, and you can be too. All we need is you to respond. We will return to Earth, but we can’t until we know for sure there are people like us who need us. End of message, message repeats tomorrow on this frequency.”
        The four of them looked at each other in shock. 
        “WorldGov has to have been suppressing this for decades now,” Stepper whispered.
        Ash had gone sickly pale. “My dad’s pretty high up in WorldGov, and I’ve never heard even a whisper of this.”
        Bobbisox stood abruptly and started pacing. “Of course they hide it! Of course they control all the communication! They can’t risk anyone hearing that message!” She turned on Jammer, and nodded. “We’ve got to respond. Can we do that without giving away the location of the bunker?”
        It took Jammer a few moments to respond, but they nodded. “I’ve been planning to send a message for years. It’ll go out from the entire city, because I made it so there’s no way to narrow down where it’s coming from. But we only get one shot at this; if I do it again, WorldGov will figure out a way to find the bunker.”
        Stepper blinked, tears running down his face. “What do we say to them?”
        Ash took a shuddery breath, and said, “We’re here, we’re queer, we need your help.” They laughed a bit. “Use some of their words, make sure they hear us.”
        When the others nodded in agreement, Jammer coded the message, then sent it.
        For the rest of the evening, the four of them busied themselves around the bunker. Almost immediately, the newscasts went dark, replaced by more explicit propaganda from WorldGov, asking any good citizen to turn in anyone they suspected of “terrorist attacks.” Jammer decided to focus on getting the surgery bed up and running; Ash functioned as both a go-fer and a second pair of hands for some of the more intensive repairs. Bobbisox monitored their cameras, kept an eye on the public broadcasts, and fiddled with their escape bags in between. Stepper decided he’d make food for the group at one point, so they all took a break for dinner, even though none of them was particularly hungry.
        Sometime after midnight, Jammer pronounced the bed ready to go. But before they could ask Stepper if he wanted to get in now or wait until the morning, the air in the room shifted, pressure increasing and decreasing suddenly. The continuous propaganda flashing on the screen in the corner of the room went dark, and the four friends looked at each other with apprehension.
        The black screen suddenly lit with the image of a person unlike anybody the WorldGov would ever have put in front of their cameras. The person’s face looked like a mix of what the WorldGov would have labeled “assertively masculine” and “delicately feminine,” and they wore clothes that seemed to be some kind of uniform but without the obvious military aspects favored by WorldGov.
        “Greetings, people of Earth,” the person began. “A century ago, we were driven off-planet by the people you now call WorldGov. In their pursuit of a supposed ideological purity, they decided to eliminate everyone who didn’t fit in, and they have continued this practice over the decades that they have controlled the Earth. I represent the Alliance, and we have been waiting all this time for a request for help that WorldGov would never have permitted. But somewhere, someone among you has reached out to us, and we are back.” The person smiled. “We’re here, we’re queer, and we bring liberation from the totalitarian state.”
        As Jammer stared at the screen, their transmitter sprang to life, with the same voice as the person on the screen. “WorldGov is nearby. We’re sending a craft to get you, whoever sent us that brave, brave message, but we need you to be ready. Bring only what you can carry, but be sure to bring your transmitter. Head for the large open space with the lake, our craft will find you there. Be brave, freedom is coming.”
        In moments, the four of them had grabbed their escape bags, and were heading out the door, Jammer at the end with the transmitter. 
        As they ran down the tunnel for the surface, Stepper managed, “Sorry I didn’t believe you, Jam.”
        “Escape now, apologize later,” Jammer responded.
