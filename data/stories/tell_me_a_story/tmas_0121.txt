Dear Finn,
        I hope you’re handling the current situation much better than myself. I’ve been checking the clock constantly. I need to make sure I’m not going mad.
        I've always considered myself a night owl, but it’s hard to be a night owl when the sun doesn’t rise. As someone who knows me well, you would probably assume that I would relish a day like today. But it’s not the same. It’s not just that there’s no sun—there’s no glow of the moon or sparkling stars—two things that define “night” for me. It’s just black. Even my orange tabby, Atlas, who is most lively at dawn and dusk, is confused. He didn’t get to have his dawn playtime followed by his lengthy morning nap. His uptick in energy never came. I can only assume it’s because the night never faded away into a dull blue, followed by the normal pink, purple, and yellow sunrise that brightens the tops of the homes and businesses in town. Atlas is now sitting on top of the couch, staring out of the window, looking back at me every once in a while with a soft "meow." I’m not sure what he’s looking for, but I wish I could ask him.  
        I used to pity my friends who couldn’t stay up late. I was convinced that the night was for creatives such as ourselves—artists, writers, and musicians. But now, I sit here in my own self-pity, wondering what I’m supposed to do with my time during the day when there is no sun and if I’ll feel any different when it technically becomes the time that I normally consider "night”. Sleeping is out of the question; the kids are up and highly enjoying their day off of school. Yes, apparently, when the sun doesn’t rise, school is canceled.   They haven’t yet realized how catastrophic this can become, and I’m too much of a coward right now to tell them. I need to give it more time before I drop this potential bombshell. 
        I’ve been listening to the reports all day, and still, there seems to be no answer. There are a lot of conspiracy theories floating around, jumping from mouth-to-mouth and ear to ear and home to home, but there’s no solid reason or evidence for this yet. I was scrolling through social media this (dare I say, morning?) and mentions of the apocalypse, the rapture, and some type of government conspiracy were being posted. It’s hard for me to believe any of this stuff. I would rather think there’s something way more scientific behind this, hopefully that can be fixed by NASA or, perhaps, the government. But, at this point, it could be anything. No one really knows for sure. 
        Even though I always considered the sunrise beautiful, I’m sure you remember from college how much I hated waking up in the morning. When a person is  used to waiting for the quiet of the evening so they can concentrate on the things that bring them solace, early-morning wake-ups are never easy. Remember how I would mumble and groan while hitting the “snooze” button on my alarm clock over and over? Eventually rolling out of bed, my hair usually in some knotted, falling out bun or ponytail; my eyes red from the lack of moisture? None of that has changed. I always look crazy while standing with my kids at the bus stop. Other moms are well put-together— freshly showered with perfect make-up, blow-dried hair and painted nails; usually wearing some expensive blazer with slacks and heels. I wonder what they’re all doing now. I can’t imagine anyone making themselves look perfect on a day like this.
        Look at me, writing as though this has been going on for days. It’s only been a few hours, and I already feel batty. I can’t imagine what it will be like if this continues. Will my body know when it’s technically night, or will I be stuck in this strange, cosmic, disoriented state, where I don’t know when to shower or write or even go grocery shopping? What if I never write to you again? I know what you’re thinking. “But, you have a clock! You know what time it is.” That doesn’t matter, Finn. If anything, every clock in my home is laughing at me today. I think I’ll turn them all off; I can’t stand the tick-tocking of a supposed “day” passing me by. 
        I’m feeling achy, probably from a lack of vitamin D. Oh god, am I being a hypochondriac? Too dramatic about it all? I mean, the sun—the sun—didn’t rise today. What if it never rises again? What if we’re stuck like this? What if my kids never go to school again, and I am forced to homeschool them? I mean, really. Could you picture me as a teacher?
        I think I might step outside for a bit to see if that clears my mind.
        Okay, that was a mistake. A big one. 
        I stepped out onto my front lawn. The grass is full of dewdrops that never dried. The air is foggy and damp; a heavy mist stayed dancing on the street, with no signs of clearing. I looked to the sky, hoping to see some semblance of light. There still isn’t even a hint of the sun, moon, or stars. It’s a blank, onyx canvas—an empty void. If I’m being honest, looking at such a thing scares the living daylights out of me. The thought of disappearing into a vacant oblivion is terrifying. 
        I started to walk down the street, and in the distance, I could hear what sounded like car crashes and sirens. People are screaming. Dogs are barking. The sheer screeching sent piercing waves through my eardrums. If anarchy hasn’t started yet, I’m sure it will soon, unless the sun decides to make an appearance within the next four hours. Even by that time, we’ll only have two hours of daylight before it gets dark again.
        My street is eerily quiet. On a normal near-summer day, people are outside in their yards, tending to their gardens, washing their cars, or walking their dogs. Old Jane, four houses down, often sits on her porch in her robe with curlers in her hair, petting her fluffy, Russian Blue cat, watching out for potential gossip. Kids are outside on their bikes or running around, falling and scraping their knees. But today, there’s no one tending to their gardens—I can’t even see the blue tulips and white roses that adorn the Johnson’s yard. No kids are circling on their yellow and purple bikes. Presumably (and understandably), no one is allowing their kids outside. No one’s driveway is full of dripping bubbles as they finish up waxing their black and red sports cars. There is no sun to illuminate the usually lustrous world.
        It just occurred to me—what about the environment? We have to keep most of the lights on all day to be able to see in the house. I would rather not burn candles. Having candles burning constantly is dangerous, especially with the kids’ feet thumping upstairs as they hop on and off beds and have dance parties with very loud music. And my poor, confused Atlas, jumping on every surface he can, his body begging for its regular schedule back. I’m scared the candles will get knocked off and burn the place down. The energy from every house being lit up all at once for a whole day can’t be environmentally friendly, but what else are we to do? With no sun, we have no choice. 
        Finn, this “day” has made me realize how essential the sun is for writers and artists. We need the world to be luminous so that when the daylight does fall, we can write or paint, thinking back on everything we saw that day. I fear that if it stays this way, creativity will wane. I’m sure there are some visionaries out there who luxuriate in these kinds of phenomena. They can take any mysterious happening and turn it into something beautiful and amazing, and be praised for it for centuries to come. I don’t think I’m one of those people. I sincerely hope you are.
        Well, there’s a knock on my door. If you don’t hear from me again, it’s either because my town has gone into a complete anarchical state or because I’ve been driven mad by lack of creativity, screaming kids, and no sunlight. Let’s hope that’s not the case. 
        I think I may just stay up to see if the sun rises tomorrow.
        Your Friend,
        Olivia Parker
