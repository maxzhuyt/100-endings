When you live in a sleepy little community college town like Green Hills, where the most exciting thing is the local mall, and the biggest debate is whether Queequeg’s or Paul’s has the better coffee, you get excited over pretty much anything new.
        Robin, Mercy, Jonas, and I had lived in the town all our lives. We met in grade school, and formed one of those friendship groups young adult novelists tend to love. Together, we weathered things together like puberty, first loves, and coming out. 
        In our senior year, we’d all decided to go to Green Hills Community College to give ourselves some more time since we weren’t sure what we wanted to do with our lives. Green Hills wasn’t a bad college, but in October of our sophomore year, we realized none of us wanted to stay in the town for the rest of our lives — especially since the town was barely neutral on LGBTQIA matters.
        “Hey Avery!” Jonas called when he saw me come out of the library.
        “What’s up?” I headed over to him, skirt swishing as I moved. Today was more of a “girl day,” and thankfully the weather was still nice enough that an ankle length skirt wasn’t an issue. We started walking toward the parking lot, heading for Jonas’ car as the golden early afternoon light filtered through trees half bare of leaves.
        “Mercy texted me since she knew you had your head down in school stuff, and told me there’s a carnival in the lot where that developer tried to set up a second mall!” He grinned, and continued, “It’s Halloween, perfect for us to get dressed up and go, since everyone thinks we’re too old to be trick-or-treating.” 
        I nodded, at the very least intrigued by the idea. “I’m done with my midterms and caught up with homework, thank goodness. Kind of having a Something Wicked This Way Comes moment here, though. What kind of carnival sets up this late in the season? It’s not called ‘Dark’s Pandemonium Carnival,’ is it?”
        Laughing, Jonas responded, “Nah, it’s called the ‘Allston Family Carnival.’ I Googled them, they’re totally legit.”
        Grinning, I said, “Okay, but if there’s a guy named ‘Mr. Dark’ running the thing, I’m noping out of there.”
        We’d reached the edge of campus, and Mercy and Robin were waiting, holding hands and waving at us like they thought we wouldn’t see them. They were an adorable couple, and had been since 10th grade when they came out, which didn’t go over well with either of their families. Mercy’s parents were strict Catholics who’d only sort of accepted that their daughter wasn’t interested in men, while Robin lived with her aunt and uncle after being kicked out by her ultra-conservative parents. 
        “Hey you two!” Jonas called, and the two of them ran over and hugged me.
        “How was the midterm?” Mercy asked.
        “Did Jacobs get on your case about the skirt?” Robin said at nearly the same time. The two of them pulled back a bit from me and giggled.
        “Midterm went fine, I think I aced it, and Jacobs kept looking, but it’s not like I was wearing a mini or something she could get upset about.” I danced a bit in place, and added, “She’s so old school, but she’s the best public speaking teacher on the campus. I just have to get through the semester with her.”
        “So, carnival?” Robin asked, bouncing a bit in place.
        “Yes, carnival,” I answered with a grin. “Let’s get together around seven o’clock?” At their agreement, we all piled into Jonas’ car, so he could take us back to our respective houses.
        Right at seven, Jonas showed up at my door, dressed like a stereotypical pirate. I’d dithered for an hour or two before putting on the black cat costume I’d gotten in a moment of bravery. Form fitting, it required some added pieces underneath, but in the end I looked like Julie Newmar as Catwoman from the old Batman television shows. My parents were one hundred percent supportive of my gender identity. We’d had discussions lately about whether it was time to move out of Green Hills and to somewhere safer.
        Dad gave me a thumbs up as he opened the door for Jonas, who whistled at me. “I’m not into girls, but you look fantastic Avery.”
        I blushed. “Thanks! Let’s do this before I lose my nerve.”
        Mercy was dressed in a proper Greek peplos, and said that people probably wouldn’t recognize she was going as Sappho, but she didn’t care. Robin had a short brunette wig and a tennis skirt and top, declaring that she was Billie Jean King for the night. Once we were all out of the car, Mercy and Robin hugged me again, complimented me on my costume, and swore they wouldn’t leave my side for an instant. 
        We purchased admission from a man who looked like he’d seen it all several times, got our all-access wristbands, and wandered into a maze of booths and lights. I recognized a bunch of students from the college, and laughed in amazement when I saw the star quarterback and his cheerleader girlfriend dressed in each other’s clothing.
        I was about to point toward the food stalls when Jonas stopped dead and looked at me seriously. “There’s a hall of mirrors over there by the food. I don’t know if you’re up for going past it.”
        I paused. Mom had done a fantastic job with my makeup, and with my friends to support me, I thought I could probably handle going past. I got this warm feeling, and what came out of my mouth instead was, “I think I’m up for going in, actually.”
        Jonas, Mercy, and Robin all looked at me with surprise, but then the love and praise started flowing, and I felt myself buoyed along to the entrance, where a carnival barker grinned at us. “Let’s see. Sappho, Billie Jean King, Julie Newmar – fantastic choice, by the way – and young Blackbeard?”
        Mercy started giggling. “Three out of four is really good, but I don’t think Jonas, er, young Blackbeard here was going for a specific pirate.”
        The barker chuckled, and motioned us toward the entry. I was floating from the validation; at no point did he blink or frown or give any indication he thought I wasn’t really a young woman. I felt Mercy tug me in, and both Jonas and Robin hugged me once we got inside.
        In a really brave moment, I looked into the first mirror and saw a woman dressed up as Catwoman. I knew it was me, but I really did look a lot like her. I think I got distracted, though, because when I turned around to ask my friends about going further into the maze, they were all gone.
        Before I could panic, I heard their voices up ahead. It was clear there was only one way through, and I guessed they’d given me some privacy for a moment of gender euphoria. But as I went toward them, their voices began fading out, and the surrounding mirrors began reflecting different images of myself.
        I saw myself as a young woman dressed as a flapper, a young man in a tuxedo with a mask and a red rose, a figure skater (male and female versions), and other variations. They all seemed to be costumes that I could have worn, but underneath all of them, it was still me with my green eyes and reddish hair. 
        “Jonas? Mercy? Robin?” I called out, but only got silence in return.
        I kept moving, in the hope that maybe they were just further ahead, and the images got more and more unusual. I saw myself as an elf-woman, pointed ears and all. Then, I was in a far-too-futuristic form fitting jumpsuit, with what looked like a laser gun, but not quite. Next, I was some kind of superhero, a haze of shimmering light surrounding my body. 
        These weren’t reflections anymore. They were me, but alternate versions of me. I don’t know why I knew that, and I wasn’t sure why I accepted that so easily, but I was seeing how I might have been in different universes. My face mostly stayed the same, and I got the sense that no matter how I looked in those universes, I was genderfluid in all of them.
        When I turned a corner, I found myself in a completely silent dead end. Ahead of me, the mirrors were empty except for one. I resolutely didn’t look at the empty mirrors, suspecting those were universes where I wasn’t around anymore.
        They were dressed in tattered clothing, presenting mostly female, with what looked like a sword belted to one hip and a crystal rod on the other side. In their reflection, I could see the landscape behind them. It looked like every apocalyptic movie landscape I’d ever seen, except the lighting was a dim red filtered through black clouds, occasionally punctuated by flashes of violet lightning.
        My other self said, “Oh good, I got through to you. Quick, we don’t have much time.” I realized that their voice was the first thing I’d heard in a long time.
        “Do, do I call you Avery?”
        They smiled sadly. “Yeah, we picked the same name. Listen to me.” They exhaled, and looked back over their shoulder. “In my universe, magic came back, all at once, about seven years ago. Mostly, it was good at first, but then countries started using magic to fight.” They looked back again. “Some of us realized that something bad had gotten in with the magic, and was using the wars to make itself stronger. We started calling that something the Enemy, because we didn’t have any other name for it. By the time we started fighting the Enemy, it was pretty much too late.”
        “What about Robin, Mercy, Jonas?”
        “We’re still friends. We’re the last people still fighting, and the Enemy finally tracked us down.” They sighed. “Robin suggested we try and warn alternate versions of ourselves, because we’re pretty sure the Enemy goes from universe to universe, stirring up trouble and feeding off of it.”
        Instinctively, I reached out to the mirror. I wanted to pull my other self through, I wanted them to be safe.
        They managed a smile. “I wish that worked. There can only be one of us in any given universe. I still have ethics, and I’m not going to stop you from existing just because I want to keep going.” They looked around again. “The Enemy isn’t far away, but I want to ask you something.”
        “What is it?” I said quietly.
        “Let me pass on my knowledge and my experiences. I think the Enemy is coming for your universe next, and you’ll need to be ready. We’ll hold it off as long as we can, give you all enough time to prepare.” They grinned suddenly. “My Jonas, Robin, and Mercy are making the same offers to yours.” Tears started streaming down their face as they added, “Your friends won’t say yes unless you agree to it. I’m glad you have them. My versions have made my life so much better in the midst of everything else. And this isn’t a one-way thing; I’ll get a copy of your memories. There has to be an equal exchange.”
        I felt the tears rolling down my face, and nodded. I wasn’t sure I could do anything else.
        The other Avery exhaled in relief. “Okay, good. I’m sorry, this is going to be really weird, but here goes.” They took the crystal rod in their left hand, gently placed the tip against whatever surface was on their side, and said, “Move your right hand, and touch the rod from your side.”
        Sniffling, I said, “I’ve barely met you, and I’m going to miss you, Avery.”
        “Likewise, Avery,” they answered, “but at least we’ll be able to remember each other.”
        I pressed my hand up against the rod on their side, and for a moment, everything went black. All of their life, history, experiences, and knowledge flowed into me, parking itself in a neat little bundle at the back of my mind for when I was ready to start going through it.
        “This is goodbye,” they said. “Your friends accepted too, so at least you’re all in the same boat on this. We’ll give you as much time as we can. Magic came back for us on Halloween, we’ll try to make sure the Enemy can’t get through this year.” The image of my other self faded from the mirror, and what had seemed like a cul-de-sac faded away, replaced by an open space of mirrors reflecting me, here, now, the way I was dressed for Halloween. I blinked, and Robin reappeared, with Jonas and Mercy fading into view a split second later.
        “You all got that?” I asked. 
        My three friends nodded, looking shaken. 
        “Let’s get out of here,” Robin suggested. “My aunt and uncle won’t care if we go hang out at their place, as long as we leave the mother-in-law unit clean.”
        On our way out of the carnival, I saw storm clouds moving in. The memories of the other Avery assured me these were normal clouds, but the four of us looked at each other for a long moment.
        “We’ll be ready,” Mercy assured me, and I nodded.
