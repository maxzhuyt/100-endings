The market was already dusk-blue when the Jigen-ryū prodigy brushed past me, sleeve to sleeve, a nothing-bump that happened every day. But the rice-paper square he left behind smelled of gunpowder ink, and the characters read: One torch. One hour. Temple ruins. I kept walking, daikon in my basket, heartbeat already ahead of me on the mountain.

We broke curfew from opposite valleys. His sandals clacked pine needles; my cloth soles whispered. Every sentence I took, he took one, until our footfalls landed together on the same cracked flagstone inside the roofless nave. Fox spirits grinned from the gateposts, teeth chipped by centuries. I smelled rust from overturned incense bowls and the sour copper of my own anticipation.

Flint struck. A single torch lurched alive, throwing the roof tiles into nervous motion. Coins clinked into twin piles: ivory chips from samurai backers, clay tokens from street guards who had pawned their futures. “Three silver mon,” the whisper ran, “a rice-ration coupon, a grandmother’s jade hairpin, a first-born daughter promised.” Every metal kiss sounded like a throat opening.

He lunged—yokogiri, downward rivers of steel. I ducked, forearm snapping like a wet rope against a pillar. A fist-sized splinter sheared away, kissed his cheek, hung a red bead glittering in the torchlight. When it slid inside his collar it drew samurai purity down among the wood-dust commoners. First blood, scored by the temple itself.

He chained three tsuki thrusts, textbook perfect. I slipped each by a hair, looped my arm, let the elbow crack his floating rib—click like a cracked chopstick. (Were this a training hall, the sensei would applaud textbook evasion—here, only the torch hears the bone.) His smirk folded inward.

I vaulted to the broken altar, heels skidding slate. The roof coughed debris. He charged, blind, sword-wheeling, and severed the torch bracket. Flame rolled across the floor, splitting the nave into two wavering countries. We crouched, each owning half the light, half the dark, borders redrawn every second.

Torch between us guttering, we froze, backs to opposing pillars. Four beats, four beats—our heartbeats counted the silence like coins already spent. Then the rhythm tore.

I feigned low whip-hand. He committed to downward block, pride shining. Mid-air my fingertips redirected, spearing the soft notch above his breastbone. He knelt politely, spraying the clay pile with bright interest. The debt was never between schools; it was between jugular and open air.

I pried the torch from the floor. Flame thin as a needle, I walked uphill. Behind me his heels scraped twin grooves, shadows wearing him like an extra pair of legs. By dawn the mountain was empty: only two blood trails drying where moonlight polished them amber.

Masters found the ruin next morning, drew no conclusions, only assigned extra horse-stance hours. Six weeks later dusk market smelled of fresh gunpowder ink, and a new square of rice-paper circulated, unsigned, already sliding into someone’s sleeve.
