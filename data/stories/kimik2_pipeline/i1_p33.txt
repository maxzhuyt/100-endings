The glass shatters at 21:07 station time—cheap Martian crystal, sounds like winter lake ice splitting under a skater’s blade. I don’t look. Home is an ice moon I crashed into a sun; I keep polishing the tumbler and count the splinters I can’t see.

“Vega whisky, neat,” the man says. “Make it a double.”

His tentacled dreads slump over the rail like spilled udon. One strand lifts, tasting the air currents that aren’t there. I pretend the grav-plate hiccupped; everyone does.

“Double it is.” I flip the bottle; the ribbon of whisky catches violet nebula bleed from the viewport. “Name?”

“Rell.”

“Nettle. N-thumbs to people who tip.”

He slides a chit—triple the tariff. “Keep it, Nets.”

I float the glass across. “Bad day?”

“Bad decade.” He swallows half, winces. “You ever feel a pattern crawling behind your eyes? Something living in the negative space?”

I nearly laugh—John used say that before I left him in a rental sk, nav dead, lips still warm—but the laugh dries. “Station fumes. Mess with perception.”

“Sure,” Rell says. “Perception.”

The sweeper bot whirs, brushing the same tile again. Circling. I pour myself a medicinal finger of Vega.

Rell’s locks curl; one loops his glass like an anchor rope. “Heard there’s a woman out here who ran ledgers for the Andromeda Syndic. Ghosted them mid-skim. They sent collectors.”

My wrist jerks; liquor sloshes. “Rumors make good holo-scripts. Drink.”

“Thing is,” he leans, pupils wide, “collectors don’t ghost back. Unless the quarry’s worth more breathing.”

Outside, a mining barge drifts, running lights dead. My reflection is stapled across its hull: cropped red hair, apron, smile I file nightly. Looks like a woman who minds her own orbit.

“Maybe she likes being lost,” I say. (Maybe I do—what is one to do?) “Policy: don’t dig in other people’s asteroids.”

Rell clicks his tongue. “Policy doesn’t stop gravity. Or guilt.”

Corner booth erupts—Tova kisses the shy engineer. Love blooms where air is metered. I give them the bar-mom nod.

“So,” I top him off without asking, “what’s she to you?”

“Sister,” he lies smooth. “I’m the messenger bringing her home.”

All six dreads twitch now, counting heartbeats. I feel them under the bar, coiling my ankle, reeling. Skin buzzes.

I wipe a circle on the counter, clockwise. “Home’s relative.”

“Not for the Syndic. They keep receipts.”

Another glass pops—enthusiastic toast. The bot pivots, leaves a crescent of shards like micro-stars. I step over them to the taps, same arc, orbit intact.

Rell watches. “You move like you’re used to tight spaces.”

“Bar’s two meters wide.” I force grin. “Economy of motion.”

“Or practice.” He spins, tracking me. “Pattern I see: woman tending wounds she swears aren’t hers. Pouring amnesia two fingers at a time.”

I want to joke that memory’s cheaper than Vega, but the joke stalls. My face in the bottle looks layered, another woman behind it trying to peel free.

“Hypothetically,” I hear myself, “if you found this sister… then what?”

“Hypothetically?” His voice softens. “I’d ask why she left me drowning in her wake.”

Jukebox loops the same ballad. I realize I’m humming, three orbits deep. My rag moves widdershins now, undoing the shine.

I thumb the register shut. “Outpost’s a rest cure, Rell. People stop running here.”

“Some run in circles.” He downs the Vega, inverts the glass; beads cling like captive moons. “Name’s not Rell. Guess yours isn’t Nets.”

I shrug; motion feels submarine. “Names cost extra.”

He stands, leaves the chit. “Dock 12. Window 03:00. If you feel like—”

“Unlikely.”

“—saying hello.”

The dreads fan, sampling my scent, then retract. Each footfall prints new cracks in the glitter the bot keeps chasing.

I should count the till, myth the Syndic away. Instead I pour another Vega, neat. The glass trembles; for a pulse I see two hands—one steady, one shaking—before they merge.

Bot resumes, brushing absent shards. I hum, circling, stepping over John—I mean the stain—waiting for 03:00 or the next shatter, whichever comes first.

Between chorus and silence I whisper a name I haven’t used in three years, wonder whether I’m calling myself home or finally letting me go.
