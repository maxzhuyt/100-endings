I step onto the level-two treatment floor and the first thing I note is the musculature chart stapled dead-centre, a neon atlas of reds and blues. Table under it, vinyl cracked like dried lake-bed. Parallel bars to my left gleam with hospital soap, theraband rack to my right colour-coded like candy I can’t eat. I drop my crutch into the triangle of space where the foam roller waits, soft cul-de-sac in a room of hard angles.  

“Let’s see how much range we’ve lost since last week,” she says—diagnosis and verdict in one breath. I shrug, pretending the numbers don’t matter, yet my gaze skims the wasted 42 cm of my left quad, down from 49.  

(I don’t care if I walk again—parenthetical rehearsal so polished I almost believe it.) Mara’s sock slips; the scar on her ankle flashes, mirror-image railroad tracks to mine. I snort at the laminated protocol sheet she hands me, every phase memorised, every lie underlined.  

Supine, hamstrings draped over her shoulder, 90-90, ankle cocked like a relaxed gun. Her thumb’s web presses my adductor, warm palm against my cooling fascia. 6/10, I announce; the muscle answers with a rippling 8. In the corner of my eye the chart lights up, iliacus blooming amber as if ink could twitch.  

She climbs the table, knee pinning my thigh, flexing until heel grazes glute. Scar to scar we vibrate—her IT-band, my IT-band—pronouns fuse. Halogen stutters; our shadow merges on the paper corpse overhead, one quadriceps silhouette.  

“Roller,” she orders. I bridge, ASIS on the cylinder, forty percent body-weight exactly, except I lean in—sixty—until sweat salts my lips. Two fingers rest on my shaking oblique, seismograph calm inside the 30 cm forbidden ring. No words, only the tremor’s Morse.  

Single-leg squat off the edge. I fail at forty-two degrees; her forearm bars my scapula, pulse drumming through cotton. “Easy, Mara,” someone whispers—her mouth, my mind, grammar liquefied.  

Timer beeps 00:00. We repeat anyway: down forty-two, up stalling thirty-eight, knee locked in her palm. Overhead the chart keeps flashing rectus femoris like a broken marquee. I exhale—  
and the sentence never ends, breath held in the isometric pause where her heartbeat answers mine.
