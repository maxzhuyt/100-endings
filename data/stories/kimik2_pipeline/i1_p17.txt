The Bailiff’s Cough

A parchment-skinned bailiff cleared his throat; the sound ricocheted around the hollowed-out moon like a lost marble. “Calling the matter of First Love versus Mora, docket seventy-seven-B.”  
The courtroom walls glowed with fossil light, every impact crater a spectator. Upon the bench sat Dream, dusk for robes, insomnia for furniture, gloved fingers drouching across the arm-rest as if it might lull itself awake.  
Twelve lucid dreamers—jury—swayed in their seats, eyelids flickering, none yet aware they were still asleep.

Opening Pleasantries

Dream did not bother to look up. “Counselors, keep it brief. I have a plague of locusts scheduled at nine.”  
First Love bowed; peach-bloom cheeks. “My lord, the defendant converted my client’s meadow into a crime scene. I seek restoration and punitive damages: one blush, one fluttering stomach, treble costs.”  
Mora, perched on the defendant’s rail, black feathers where morals should be, snickered. “A girl’s gotta eat.”  
Her echo hatched tiny shivers in every ear.

Exhibit A—The Meadow

First Love snapped her fingers. Golden pollen erupted, drifting across the well like slow fireworks. Jurors sneezed in perfect canon.  
We saw Max on his back, daisy-chain crown slipping, his crush leaning in. The instant before contact everything softened—summer, sixteen, hope distilled.  
“Objection,” Mora croaked. “Too saccharine; liable to cause cavities.”  
“Sustained,” Dream murmured, rubbing the bridge of his aquiline nose. “Continue.”

Exhibit B—The Invasion

Same meadow, same boy. Crush straddles him; sunlight gutters. Her face liquefies, features stretching like hot taffy until cheekbones jut, eyes sink to smouldering coals. She settles on Max’s sternum with the proprietary air of a cat discovering an unmade bed. His breath exits in pale ribbons; she slurps them like spaghetti.  
A juror—accountant, semi-lucid—whimpers. Another applauds politely, unsure of etiquette.  
Mora preened. “Nutritious. Plenty of panic, pinch of longing—perfectly balanced, as all meals should be.”

Cross-Examination: First Love v. Mora

First Love: “Did you, on or about the third instant, trespass?”  
Mora: “Trespass implies property. Nightmares are sovereign territory, sweetling.”  
First Love, blushing so hard the bailiff squinted: “The lease was exclusive!”  
Dream lifted an eyebrow. “Lease?”  
Both entities shrugged, embarrassed. Bureaucracy again.

Witness: The Crush Herself

Summoned: a translucent girl-shape, half daisy-crown, half terror. She took the stand, feet dangling inches above gravity.  
First Love: “State your feelings pre-invasion.”  
Crush (voice like wind chimes in a shy breeze): “Butterflies. Warm fizzy soda in my veins.”  
Mora leaned forward. “And post?”  
Crush shuddered. “Like… butterflies marinated in battery acid. Also I taste pennies?”  
She wilted; petals browned at her edges. Dream noted the damage, folded a memo into a moth that fluttered away toward the Existential Contamination Unit.

Character Evidence

First Love called Max—asleep in Wichita, represented by a thought-figure in Pokémon pajamas. He yawned, waved to jury.  
“Son,” First Love began gently, “describe the dream you wanted.”  
Max: “Kissing, maybe tongue, but polite tongue. Then I’d wake up happy-sad, write poetry on my phone.”  
“And now?”  
Max’s avatar flickered. “I wake up with my lungs screaming elevator music. Also my phone autocorrects ‘flower’ to ‘terror.’ Kinda cramping my aesthetic.”  
Dismissed; the pajama silhouette dissolved into fog and mild resentment.

Mora’s Defence

She hopped to the floor, black shift unravelling into living shadow. “Your Honour, craving oxygen is basic survival. Nightmares occupy the gaps sweetness leaves empty. Without me, the boy would lack contrast; without contrast, no appreciation for future kisses. I provide essential curriculum.”  
She bowed; frost trailed across the marble. One juror scribbled: “Existential vitamins?”

Rebuttal: First Love

“Curriculum is consent-based. You breached pastoral protocol.”  
“Consent?” Mora cackled; the ceiling cracked an inch. “He’s fourteen. Half his dreams are pirated anyway.”  
Bailiff winced, logged contempt.

Dream’s Irritation

Dream rose—every heart skipped, then checked if the skip wished to appeal. “Enough. I am arbitrator of the unspoken, not babysitter to hormonal metaphors.” He glared; the docket smoked. “Remedies?”  
First Love: “Eviction, restoration, public apology in the form of a lullaby.”  
Mora: “Shared custody. Alternate nights. I supply thrills, he supplies windpipes. Everybody wins.”  
The jury waited. Dream seemed to count invisible stars.

Deliberation in Open Court

Dream closed his eyes; the room politely tilted ninety degrees. He rifled precedents:  
Sandman v. Incubus (night terrors taxable as entertainment).  
Morpheus Decree 12-B: No entity shall obstruct puberty’s awkward majesty.  
Aurora borealis leaked from his lips. “Very well. My ruling—”

The Verdict (with Mischief)

“—is compromise. The meadow shall be time-shared. Oneiric week partitioned thus:  
Sunday-Tuesday: First Love supervises gentle courtship, pastel palette, regulated heartbeat.  
Wednesday-Thursday: Mora may harvest dread, but only to the metric of one sharp inhale per minute, no bruising of the soul.  
Friday: Open mic—boy decides genre by coin flip.  
Saturday: Both parties vacate; dream shall run public-service messages on dental hygiene.”  
He smiled, thinly. “Failure to comply forfeits the offending party to my library—binding optional, reading compulsory.”  
Mora’s cackle faltered. First Love beamed, then noticed Dream’s smile never reaches good outcomes.

Contemptuous Epilogue

Mora leaned toward First Love as pages were stamped. “Wednesday I’ll sit light. Thursday, though—” She mimed cracking a rib like a wishbone.  
First Love, voice syrupy: “Enjoy it while you can. I’ve commissioned new flowers: touch-me-nots. They bite.”  
They shook hands—ice meets peach, both recoiled.

Exit, Pursued by Ambiguity

Jury faded back to respective REM. Max will wake tomorrow unsure whether his chest feels lighter or simply pre-chewed. The moon-courtroom folded into a silver coin; Dream flipped it, pocketed it, already bored.  
Somewhere a typewriter clacked, updating the boy’s subconscious:  
Flower – soft, perishable, liable to morph.  
Kiss – approaching, terms and conditions apply.  
Terror – now with user-controlled volume.  
Mora’s laughter, faint, followed him out, but the echo was duct-taped, metered, only half as infinite as before—dream bureaucracy at its finest.
