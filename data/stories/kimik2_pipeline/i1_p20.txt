The jukebox needle is stuck, spitting the same three-second guitar snarl into the dim bar like a skipping heartbeat. Spike pushes through the door three strides ahead of Revy; both stop when the holographic wanted poster above the beer taps flickers the same face, same code, same price on the same head. Neon light swims over their gun butts. Neither reaches. Between the booths lies exactly three meters of nothing special at all.  

Two paper slips hit the counter a breath apart, edges overlapping like bad origami. Bartender wipes a glass, eyes the shells balanced on Spike’s left knuckle, the fresh cigarette burning between Revy’s. “Rotgut’s on the house,” he mutters, sliding a half-empty bottle forward. “Call it last rites.”  

Spike pours; Revy tips her chair back, boots on the table, dragon tail curling around her ankle. He counts her blinks—one, two, three—while she times the click-cycle of his fake eye, that tiny servo whine whenever he looks left. The bottle empties; shells stand like fence posts around his glass, each one a toast she answers with sparks from a muzzle strike, lighting fresh smokes she never finishes. The jukebox hiccups again. The exit sign seems farther, a green star they’ll never reach.  

Target saunters past the plateglass window, phone to ear, oblivious. Both see. Neither rises.  

“Corporate slavery with a prettier leash,” Revy growls, watching the figure disappear into traffic.  

Spike exhales smoke, punctuation mark. “Whatever happens, happens.”  

She leans forward, mock-seductive whisper: “You planning to shoot me after you kiss me, space cowboy?”  

“Only if you ask nicely.” His stomach cramps; her trigger finger twitches from the cheap whiskey wearing off. The stuck guitar snarl counts them down.  

“Fifty-fifty,” she says suddenly. “We walk, split vaporized take, never talk again.”  

Outside, a hydrant gushes over asphalt; neon reflections ripple like fresh blood. They flank the mark without planning, backs brushing. Before either can claim it, the bodyguards open fire from a parked crawler. Synchronized somersault behind a dumpster—cooperation born of pure spite. Firefight ends with the mark dead on the curb, gasoline-soaked briefcase leaking green smoke. Value zero.  

“You twitched early,” she snaps.  

“Your fake eye miscounted distance,” he fires back.  

They march, guns still warm, to the only open sign still buzzing: MOTEL—HOURLY.  

One key, one room. One bed, one chair, one bulb flickering the same stuck beat. They sit back-to-back, barrels reversed under ribs—mirror heartbeats. The clock on the nightstand ticks but never changes; battery died hours ago.  

Two ounces of trigger pressure wait. Neither gives.  

She digs a crushed pack from her pocket; he fishes a single bent cigarette. Shared match, shared drag, shared silence. Ash lengthens, a gray fuse between their knuckles.  

The bulb buzzes like the jukebox needle that opened the night. Time stalls; vinyl groove locked. Story ends before the ash decides who breathes first—blackout on the endless three-second snarl.
