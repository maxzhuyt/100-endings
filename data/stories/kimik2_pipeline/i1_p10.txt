“Off-duty, civilian, reformed,” I tell myself while the restaurant Wi-Fi handshake scrolls across the inside of my eyelids like ticker tape. I don’t break things anymore—unless you count the menu QR code that just crashed my browser. Lily-’94, her pin says, next to a faded Jace the Mind Sculptor. First name, birth year, two security answers for the price of an appetizer. My thumb twitches, ghosting the curve of the phone in my pocket.

She arrives in a laugh first, hair second. I inventory: auburn, shoulder length, no root-regrowth—prioritizes maintenance. Phone already on the table, screen-up, no privacy filter. Background color, I insist. We overlap at EDH; she name-drops her “first deck” like it’s scripture: “Mom bought me the pre-con for my twelfth.” Twelfth. Year number three. I file it under harmless and let the soundtrack swell to rom-com.

She doodles a basic island on the napkin, pen pressing hard enough to emboss tomorrow’s stylus strokes. Stop, you’re not that person. Mr. Muffin, 2012—cat photo, third answer served in fur and whiskers. I wasn’t parsing the EXIF—well, only for a millisecond.

“So why’d you quit?” Rain becomes FBI glass; I’m back in the interview room, writing exploit on Hello-Kitty stationery because no one suspects the kitty. Institutional echo: We decide what progress looks like. Lily’s hand pulls me out: “Sideboards,” I lie.

Check arrives. She tilts the phone, five-point pattern, top-left to top-left, rainbow smudge painting an L. Security is a process, I chant— “—not a product,” she finishes, quoting Schneier. The words snap my leash: “You know that pattern’s basically 1234, right?” Stomach acid, cheek heat, guillotine.

She leans in. “Show me.” I demo on my own glass, catch her watching my reflection in the ketchup bottle—shoulder-surfing me back. “I do red-team for fintech,” she grins. “Left the gate open to see if you’d wander in.” Every breadcrumb was canary bait; the date was a double-blind penetration test and I just popped shell.

Outside, snow erases footprints. No numbers, no promises. “What color’s my case?” “P9 Ghost White.” She nods: perfect. I didn’t get her number—just her hash value, and that’s enough for tonight. We walk opposite directions, parallel lies, smiles syncing in mutual refusal to turn around.
