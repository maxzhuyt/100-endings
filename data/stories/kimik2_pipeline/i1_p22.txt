The corridor light snaps on—27 seconds to blackout.  
Jolyne’s sneakers scuff wet concrete as two guards frog-march her forward, wrists zip-tied so tight the plastic hums with Stone Free’s thin wire already sawing underneath. At the far end, back-lit by the dying bulb, stands Makima—heels together, hands clasped, haloed by a crown of glowing rings that blink like warning lights on a predator’s dash.  

“If there’s no Mickey,” Jolyne rasps, “this shit ain’t Disney.”  
Makima bows, polite as a hotel concierge. The gesture yanks the corridor’s air a notch tighter around Jolyne’s throat; she feels the bow like a collar snicking shut.  

Light pops—darkness.  
Count: 26.  

––––––  

Orientation room smells of bleach and rust. Guards dump her belongings—butterfly Polaroid, empty pockets, the invisible spool of Stone Free—onto a steel table already sweating condensation. No words, only the clang of metal.  

Makima translates the silence: “Your body is now a text. I read footnotes.”  
Jolyne’s gaze flicks to the pegboard behind the woman: leather leashes ranked by size, tags engraved with names that must have belonged to people once. Makima’s shoes—ivory, spotless—glide across wet concrete without the tiniest squeak. Predator glide.  

She stops an inch from the Polaroid. “Joestar blood. I can smell the ocean on you.” The word ocean drips like a hand stroking a puppy before pushing it under.  

“Protection?” she offers.  
Sounds like a guillotine being polite.  

––––––  

Night. The cell doors don’t close; they yawn, mouths bored of screaming.  

Stone Free’s thread slips under eight doors, mapping exits. Halfway back, the filament freezes—Makima stands barefoot in the corridor, manicured fingers pinching the wire. Paralysis rockets into Jolyne’s spine; her own Stand threads whip and choke her circulation.  

“Keep your leash fetish away from me,” she growls through locked teeth.  
“Dogs bark,” Makima whispers, “then obey.”  

She flicks her wrist. The thread snaps home, slashing across Jolyne’s tattoo. Blood beads under the inked butterfly, coloring the wings scarlet—her body writing disobedience into skin.  

Stakes hang unspoken between them: first to name the other, wins.  

––––––  

Alarms howl. Forty-seven expendables flood the block, eyes rolled back, foaming at orders only they can hear. Guards vanish; keys jangle mockingly behind reinforced glass.  

Jolyne strings a web between railings, turns two rioters into marionettes—fists knock out teeth, knees fold backward. From the mezzanine Makima watches, munching popcorn, savoring the product test.  

When the woman in red raises her hand, the riot ends mid-scream. Forty-seven bodies drop to their knees in perfect unison, foreheads smacking tile. Jolyne alone stays standing, sweat sewing barbed wire across her brow.  

––––––  

Chapel fluorescents burn without flicker—an interrogation sun. Makima sits on the front pew, clipboard balanced like a sermon notebook.  

“Help me reel in Chainsaw Man,” she proposes, voice echoing off crucifix. “I’ll delete your sentence, stamp your record clean.”  

Jolyne shrugs, fingers working the rosary on the altar, turning beads into microscopic filaments that orbit Makima’s ankles.  

Makima leans in, kisses Jolyne’s forehead—maternal, scalding. Lips taste of copper cable.  

Stone Free yanks. The garrote rosary cinches. Blood halos Makima’s white skin—she shivers, pupils dilating in pleasure. “Good girl,” she breathes.  

––––––  

Bulb pops for good. Total dark.  

Jolyne shuts her eyes, lets thread map space—sonar pulses paint Makima’s heartbeat: red comets orbiting a black hole. Makima speaks through convict mouths, voices ventriloquized from every cell—“Submit, submit, submit.”  

Stone Free stitches doors shut, locks the harvest outside its own field. The heartbeat falters. Makima cannot name what refuses heaven; Joestar blood hums a frequency older than dominion.  

A single click—her shoe finally touches earth—safety off.  

––––––  

Generator coughs; light returns in strobes—no one’s weapon now. Both women breathe hard, clothes slashed in mirrored stripes. Jolyne’s butterfly tattoo split so the wings flutter with each pulse. Makima licks blood from her thumb.  

“Round one to you, Joestar.”  

Yeah, Jolyne thinks, but the corridor’s still mine… for the next twenty-seven seconds.  

The bulb resets, counting down.
