The fountain’s white noise hit him first—roar, gull-shriek, coin-plop—then the squeal of brakes carved straight through. Ben stepped backward, guidebook raised like a shield, and the turquoise Vespa fishtailed across wet travertine, rear tire smoking. The fender kissed his tweed elbow with a soft metallic sigh. Silence followed, as if Rome itself had drawn breath.

Her eyes were burnt espresso, flecked with gold. “Ma sei matto?” Helmet off, hair snapped like a carnival sparkler. “Walking backwards at the Trevi—what, inviting the gods to kill you?”

“I—io… I was reading about the baroque aqueducts…”

She laughed, accent flipping to Oxford silk. “Aqueducts won’t save your ankles, Professore.”

The word pinned him; he almost bowed. Traffic restarted around them, coins resumed their clatter into water.

She patted the cracked leather seat. “Get on. I’ll show you Rome that isn’t in books.”

Ben hesitated—one heartbeat, two—then swung his leg. Briefcase smacked her hip like awkward applause. At the first red light she leaned back, plucked the guidebook from his fingers, and lobbed it into a postcard vendor’s basket. The vendor waved gratefully; Ben never noticed.

They idled outside the Colosseum, engine ticking. She produced a rusted key, kissed by previous thefts. “Gladiators didn’t wait for tickets.” Inside, the hypogeum swallowed them in powdered darkness. She snapped phone-photos of him faux-lecturing stone lions, palm against his ribs to stifle laughter—there, almost a kiss, but dust tickled and they broke apart coughing sunlight.

Campo de’ Fiori bustled. She knotted a stolen apron round his waist, taught him Romanaccio slang: “Annà, che te vò?” He repeated, ridiculous, while figs vanished into his pockets beneath her sleight-of-hand. When he seized her wrist mid-pilfer he felt her pulse racing, hummingbird against thumb. “Don’t worry, Professore—fruit doesn’t bruise easily.”

Up on the Gianicolo balustrade Rome poured gold at their feet. He confessed footnotes, footlights, a relationship calcifying like old plaster. She flicked the silver lighter—clack, clack—never lighting the unlit cigarette. “I collect moments people don’t notice they’re missing.” The horizon flared; bells aborted their second almost-kiss.

Midnight backstreets, exhaust hanging like lace. Engine cough. “End of the line, Professore.” She pressed the warm lighter into his palm: *Rubare il tempo*. “How do I find you?” She tapped the dented fender. “Look for Rome’s scratches.” Vespa buzzed away, swallowed by stone echoes.

He stood mapless, lighter branding skin, grinning like a man who’d misplaced an entire day—and found a new century.

When the metal cooled he shook it. A paper scroll no thicker than a hair dropped: coordinates, sunrise, Palatine. Ben pocketed the promise, already rehearsing Italian verbs for stealing time.
