Milo Velasquez found the box on hisSaturday-morning doorstep—no card, no return address, just a claw-machine crane stuffed with prizes. A flap lifted by itself and a single silver watch popped out like it had won *him*. The band unfurled, a tiny screen lighting with confetti emojis and a cheery ping. “Welcome, Milo! Let’s turn back time!” No manual, no charger. He strapped it on, heart ticking faster.

Monday noon, the cafeteria battlefield: spaghetti avalanche, fifty witnesses. Milo bent for napkins, felt death-by-embarrassment. Then he noticed the watch face glowing: REWIND 30 SEC. He tapped. The world hiccuped—sound whooshed backward, trays leaping into place. Milo stood upright, lunch intact, no eyes staring. He grinned. *Best glitch ever*.

That night he brushed teeth—and froze. A red mountain on his forehead, voice warbling like Dad’s old radio, sleeves ending above his wrists. In the mirror he looked… thirteen? Fourteen? The watch ticked louder, as if proud.

Outside Tuesday dawn, a yellow-eyed tabby crouched on the bike rack, tail twitching in perfect rhythm with the second hand. Milo rewound a missed binder grab; the cat blinked exactly twice, counting along.

Second-period joke bomb: punch line fizzled first run. Milo rewound, landed the gag. Laughter detonated—and suddenly eighth-graders clapped his shoulders. “That’s Milo, been cracking us up since fifth!” He blinked. Had he? Their memories felt solid, his own like wet sand.

Lunch with Maya. He reached to compliment her bracelet, knocked her strawberry smoothie instead. Rewind. This time he sidestepped, “tripped,” sent Kyle Munson flying. Tray splatter, bully fury, Kyle stomped off early—and missed the 3:15 bus. As Milo biked home, sirens screamed toward Maple Street.

Math class. Window glass showed a tall stranger in a varsity jacket, five-o’clock shadow, same watch on his wrist. The reflection raised a hand *before* Milo did, grinning crooked. Milo yelped; Mrs. Diaz shushed him.

Seventeen rewinds, the log said. Size-11 sneakers split his old laces. Razor stubble scraped like sandpaper. When the secretary summoned “Meee-loo,” his baritone cracked the PA speaker. Kids gawked at the man in gym shorts.

After final bell the cat blocked his path. Its collar tag—blank yesterday—now showed “-1.” As Milo stared it flipped to “-2,” metal clicking like a stadium counter. One sharp meow, then it sprinted toward the abandoned lot where sirens had gathered.

Ambulance lights painted the sky red. Under a sheet, the outline of sneakers—Kyle’s neon high-tops. Milo’s stomach imploded. Erase a person as easy as a spilled drink.

Hands shaking, he found RESET in tiny print beneath the band. REWIND TO FIRST USE. He long-pressed. The world spun backward: smoothie un-spilled, joke un-laughed, tray un-dropped. Each reversal sucked color from the sky until everything felt like old photo negatives.

Milo woke on his bedroom carpet, the watch gone. Mirror showed normal twelve-year-old skin, baggy sleeves—except for a single silver hair glinting above his ear. Outside, the cat licked its paw on the sill, collar shining: “0.” It met his eyes, tail ticking side to side like a clock that still believes in midnight.
