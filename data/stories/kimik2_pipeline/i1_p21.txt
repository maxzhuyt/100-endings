On the night the moon turns red, the devil walks in the shape of a gentleman.

Alucard’s boots touch the flagstones with the hush of a bedtime story. The village is a throat cleared of voices; shutters clap, but no faces peer out. Market awnings billow like empty lungs, and the air smells of pennies kissed by rust. A single lilac petal clings to a doorpost, purple and improbable against the bleached wood.

He tips his hat to no one, twirls a spent silver bullet between gloved fingers. The cartridge bears the Hellsing sigil—cross folded into a cruciform ax. A credential, a promise, a joke.

Behind him, space coughs. A violet seam unzips, spitting lilac-scented ozone. Yennefer steps through, boots hovering an inch above ground, black silk flapping like wet wings. The rift’s rim coils rope-tight, then snaps shut; beneath it, stone glosses to glass. She does not turn, yet the portal obeys the off-beat of her heart.

“State your claim to this hunting ground,” Alucard murmurs from the shadowed porch.

“I ask the same of you, corpse.”

They stand still, trading territories of silence. He flicks the bullet; it rings against cobble, rolls, stops. She lifts an obsidian star etched with the Chapter’s sigil—five-pointed, edge thin enough to shave souls. Both icons gleam under the crimson harvest moon.

“No innocents left,” he observes.

“Only rules,” she answers.

They begin to walk, opposite planets around a dying sun. With every step, he drips blood runes that soak between stones like hot mercury. She sketches glyphs in violet fire that hang, sizzling, then sink. Where symbols meet, the air develops teeth.

“Silver,” she sneers, eyeing his bullet. “How modern.”

“Superstition,” he replies, tasting her lilac. “How medieval.”

The circles close; the village becomes a duelling compass. At the center: two predators and a moon that refuses to blink.

First test: Alucard scatters into wolves. Twelve shadows pour through the stalls, fangs glinting like sewing needles. Yennefer answers with a whirl of flame that turns the square into a kiln. Cobblestones blister, burst, rise as glass daggers that pin phantom lupine hides to doors. One drop of his blood—dark, older than scripture—splashes her cheek.

She does not flinch, yet her next sigil warps, circles sagging into ellipses. Lilac floods her lungs; vampire vintage stirs memories of mortality. She could teleport away. He could re-form beyond the ridge. Neither budges. They want the measure.

Later—hours or centuries, time is negotiable—they perch on the church roof like mismatched gargoyles. His coat hangs in ribbons; her left sleeve is ash, revealing shoulder-knotted scars she never magicked smooth.

“Looking for a worthy end,” he admits, voice low as coffin velvet.

“I traded fertility for beauty,” she offers back, wind tugging black strands across her mouth. “Got loneliness instead.”

He leans close, breath cold enough to crack slate. “Let me sire you a child of endless night.”

She lays a fingertip to his lips, heat meeting frost in a hiss. “Let me gift you dreamless, eternal sleep.”

Both proposals hover, twin moons, unaccepted.

Below, the blood moon founders. Crimson light recedes like a tide uncovering bones. Their perfect circles shudder, collapse inward, herding hunter against hunter into the square. No spells left—only meat and will.

She conjures a silver rapier from moonlight and resentment, drives it through his heart with the precision of a notary. He thanks her by biting the curve of her throat, drinking just deep enough to taste every orphan year she has lived. Sunrise threatens; shadows retreat like ripped silk.

They stand ankle-deep in shared gore—his ancient, hers bright as young wine. He tips his broken hat. She wipes from her lips a smear of lilac and blood. No victor, no promise, only two new constellations inside each other’s skins, stepping opposite ways out of the empty village.
