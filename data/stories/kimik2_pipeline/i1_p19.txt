Duty matters, I tell myself, even while I count the minutes until the next smoke.  
The Whampoa anchorage breathes tar, gunpowder and the ghost-scent of opium that drifts from the very hold I am ordered to search. I rub the jade figurine once—just a habit of old campaigns, a monkey carved smaller than my thumb.

A barefoot boy threads the wharf crates, pigtail flicking like a semaphore flag.  
“Chit for tiger-devil,” Li pants, pressing creased paper into my palm.  
“Naval business,” I answer, though the characters are a merchant’s scrawl.  
“Hungry tiger wakes,” he warns.  
“Even a hungry tiger must hunt,” I reply, and pocket the chit he refuses to reclaim.

Down the tea-house ladder the cellar dark is syrupy. The opium pellet rests on my knife point, parenthetical: it has no tongue, yet it speaks.  
Bombay, ’28, rain on red tiles—memory laps itself like a tide. The voice is poison, confessor, old shipmate; I inhale and the cellar becomes a gun-deck echoing with heartbeats.

Waiting for the dealer, I thumb the jade. The monkey’s grin recalls the prize macaque I once shipped home to my sister—confiscated by the Admiralty as contraband of war. She drew mystery with mystery: ink on a creature that already mocked the crown. The memory feels like mutiny committed in miniature, a crime for which the sentence is private and endless.

Deal collapses; coins clink away. Headache rolls in like a bronze cannon across deck ribs. Jade burns cold; my sweat stinks of cordite.  
“Matthew Arnold,” I mutter, and recite: “The hippo, with a nimble tread—”  
“—climbs the steep temple-scaffold,” Li finishes, steady as a boatswain.  
A single sob escapes, half syllable, half curse; I hide inside my bicorne as though it were a confessional.

Li hauls me to a riverside godown. Crates tower, each bale stencilled with my own seal—M for Merrick, neat as a brand on skin. The officer charged with suppression is the pipeline; poor judgment picks its own jailer. Weariness laughs louder than outrage.

Last possession: the jade monkey. I lay it on the dealer’s mat.  
“Colour?” he asks.  
“Sea-bottom green just before the light dies.”  
They nod; scales balance. No drums, no farewell volley—only my whisper: “I loved her once.” Ship, sister, self, the word stays unassigned.  
The pellet slides warm into my pocket; my fingers still curl where the figurine used to sit. Companionship is the drug; that is enough.
