**FIELD DIARY — N. PRATEEP**
**Case: MUDGE, D.F.**
**Entry 001 — 16 March 2024**

First contact made today with Declan Mudge at approximately 14:20, at a noodle shop on Rue Hengboun. He was not difficult to locate.¹ He was seated alone at a plastic table, eating *feu* with a Beerlao tall, wearing a linen shirt that had been white perhaps three launderings ago and cargo shorts with an improbable number of pockets.² His sunburn had the quality of an ongoing event rather than a completed one — as if the sun were still working on him in real time.

I introduced myself. I told him the consulate had been made aware of his situation and that I was available to assist with his welfare, to facilitate contact with family, or to arrange repatriation if desired. These are the words I always use. They have a practiced gentleness to them, I think. A soft bureaucratic padding. Most people hear them and feel held, or at least feel that someone is holding something on their behalf, even if what is being held is merely a form.

Declan Mudge heard them and began to grin.

"You're the embassy man," he said. He said it the way you would say *you're the helicopter* to a man who has arrived on a rooftop in an action film. With relief, yes, but also with the unmistakable energy of someone who believes the plot is now *activating*. That things are proceeding according to a design.

"I'm a consular liaison," I said. "Contract basis. I'm here to help you get home."

"No, no, no," he said, leaning across the table with broth on his chin. "You're here because *this is how it starts.*"

He then spoke for approximately forty minutes.³

I will summarize: Declan Mudge believes he is positioned — uniquely, critically, perhaps historically — to dismantle what he refers to, with visible relish, as "the two kingdoms." He means the Lao counterfeit pharmaceutical operation and the larger, more dangerous Cambodian syndicate.⁴ He believes his knowledge of the regional pharma supply chain, combined with his — and I am quoting precisely — "sexual intelligence capabilities," will allow him to infiltrate both organizations, turn them against each other, and emerge as an informant of such value that some institution (he gestured vaguely at me, at the sky, at the concept of institutions generally) will have no choice but to extract him, protect him, and perhaps tell his story.

"I've read le Carré," he said. "All of them. Well — most of them. The main ones. The point is I know how this works."

He asked me to document everything. He said documentation was "the currency." He said when the time came, the dossier would be his proof. His insurance. His "ticket out."

I told him I would file the appropriate reports with the consulate. I told him the consulate's recommendation was that he return to Ireland.

"Noom," he said — already using my first name, already making it a camaraderie I had not offered — "I'm not going home as the man who got sacked by Pfizer. I'm going home as the man who cracked the Mekong pill trade wide open."⁵

I paid for his noodles. He did not notice. Or he noticed and added it to the architecture of the plan — the fixer, funding the operative. It is, at this early stage, genuinely difficult to tell what is obliviousness and what is *narrative construction*. He experiences his own life as a thriller. This is, I suspect, the central problem.

**FIELD DIARY — N. PRATEEP**
**Entry 002 — 18 March 2024**

I have now read the full file forwarded by ██████████. I have cross-referenced it with the open-source intelligence that is available to a person with my clearance level, which is, as always, a clearance level designed to be high enough to make me useful and low enough to ensure I am deniable. I have also had a brief conversation with ████████ at the ████████████ office, who was helpful in the way that intelligence professionals are helpful when they want you to know that they know more than you but not what they know. A posture of informational lordliness. I am accustomed to it.

Here is what I understand.

**The Lao Operation** (Noom's working designation: *"The Artisans"*)

Based near Savannakhet, with distribution nodes along the Mekong and into Udon Thani on the Thai side. Led by — or at least organized around — a figure known in intercepts as *Ajahn Lek*, whose real name may be ████████ and who is, by most accounts, a former chemistry teacher.⁶ The operation produces counterfeit sildenafil (marketed under the names "BluStar," "MaxRise 100," and, bewilderingly, "Gentleman's Agreement") using a formula that appears to contain sildenafil citrate in lower quantities than claimed, supplemented by ████████ and ████████, the combination of which produces — in approximately 70% of users, per anecdotal reporting — a state of sustained, low-grade euphoria lasting four to six hours.⁷ The pills have developed a following entirely separate from their intended market. Backpackers purchase them in Vang Vieng. A Finnish wellness influencer posted a video about them in January, calling them "heart-opening." The video has 2.3 million views. Ajahn Lek reportedly watched it and wept — though whether from pride, horror, or commercial excitement is unclear.

The Artisans are not, by the standards of regional organized crime, violent. They are, however, *criminals*, and their product is unregulated, untested, and unpredictable. The euphoria is an accident. Accidents have second acts.⁸
