**PORTLAND APIARIST SYNDICATE COOPERATIVE**
**Minutes of the Regular Meeting — May 16, 2024**

**Location:** Back room, Hawthorne Brew & Bean (by arrangement with management)
**Called to Order:** 7:04 PM by Captain L. Voss
**Members Present:** L. Voss (Captain), M. Chu (Secretary), G. Fenn (Treasurer), H. Tanaka, D. Davalos, P. Okafor, J. Varga
**Members Absent:** None

---

**1. Approval of Minutes**
Minutes from the May 2 meeting were approved as circulated. Motion by G. Fenn, seconded by D. Davalos. Carried unanimously.

**2. Treasurer's Report**
G. Fenn reported a current balance of $2,314.18 in the operating account and $890.00 in the competition fund. Anticipated expenses through August: supplemental feed, replacement frames, nationals registration ($425), travel ($1,200 estimated). Fenn noted that the competition fund is adequate provided travel arrangements are shared. Captain Voss stated that she would investigate group lodging options. Report accepted on motion by H. Tanaka, seconded by P. Okafor.

**3. Competition Colony Update**
Captain Voss presented the inspection report for the Belmont rooftop colony (see Discord #general, May 14, attached). Varroa mite load at 1.2/100, well below the 3/100 treatment threshold. Queen performance rated exceptional. Voss distributed the draft care protocol for the colony and requested member sign-ups for the biweekly inspection rotation.

Secretary notes: J. Varga accepted the May 28 and June 11 inspection slots. These are the only slots Varga has volunteered for since February. All other members are scheduled for three or more rotations.

**4. New Apiary Site — Tabor Community Garden**
H. Tanaka proposed that the cooperative establish a secondary apiary at a newly available plot in the Mount Tabor Community Garden. Plot fee: $120/season. Access to water, partial sun, adjacent to native wildflower planting. Tanaka presented photos and a site assessment.

Discussion: D. Davalos raised concern about proximity to the Belmont rooftop (1.3 miles). Captain Voss noted that foraging radius overlap was minimal and that a secondary site would provide genetic backup and expand the cooperative's neighborhood presence. G. Fenn confirmed funding was available.

Motion to approve the Mount Tabor site, moved by H. Tanaka, seconded by L. Voss. Vote: 6 in favor, 0 opposed, 1 abstaining (J. Varga). Motion carries.

Secretary notes: Member Varga's abstention was not accompanied by a stated reason. When asked by the Captain if he had concerns, Varga replied, "No, it's fine." Secretary records this for completeness.

**5. HOA Correspondence — 4412 SE Division**
Captain Voss presented the notice of violation received from the Sunnyside Neighborhood Association regarding the hive installation at the Okafor residence (4412 SE Division St.), alleging noncompliance with Portland Municipal Code §13.40.030(c), requiring that "bee colonies be maintained not less than 25 feet from any property line."

Discussion was lively. P. Okafor stated that the hives are 27 feet from the nearest property line and presented her own measurements. G. Fenn suggested the complaint originated from the neighbor at 4410 SE Division, who has previously objected to Okafor's compost system, her rain barrel, and her wind chimes. Captain Voss described the notice as "HOA cosplay" and proposed drafting a formal response citing the actual code, the actual measurements, and "a copy of the relevant statute in a large enough font for Mr. Driscoll to read without his glasses."

Motion to authorize Captain Voss and Secretary Chu to draft a formal response. Moved by P. Okafor, seconded by D. Davalos. Carried unanimously. (Secretary notes: H. Tanaka seconded simultaneously and conceded to Davalos with approximately good grace.)

**6. Other Business**
No items raised.

**7. Adjournment**
Meeting adjourned at 8:22 PM on motion by G. Fenn. Members remained for informal discussion and beverages. Captain Voss ordered a second pint and told the Nationals '22 judging story again. Secretary notes that the story has improved with age in ways the facts do not support.

**"The Portland Problem, Part III: Who's Really Winning?"**
*Published May 20, 2024*

I want to talk about something I've been noticing.

Every spring, the Portland Apiarist Syndicate Cooperative — that name, my God, they really chose that name for themselves — does their little victory lap on social media. The queen is laying! The varroa counts are low! Nationals, here we come! And every year, the Portland beekeeping community is supposed to genuflect.

I've written before about my issues with competitive apiculture (see "Bees Are Not Athletes" and "The Extraction Games"). I won't rehash. What I want to note today is a shift in the competitive landscape that the PASC seems not to have noticed, or has chosen to ignore.

At last year's regionals in Corvallis, I sat next to a very polished young man from an organization called Apivecta. Business cards on heavy stock. Logo that looked like it cost more than PASC's entire annual budget. He told me they were "reimagining apiological excellence through scalable best practices." I laughed. He didn't.

I've since learned that Apivecta has financial relationships with at least two agrochemical companies and that their "competition team" has access to proprietary genetic stock, professional entomological consultants, and a travel budget that would make a mid-tier college athletic department weep. They placed third at nationals last year. First time entering.

Maybe this is the future. Maybe competitive beekeeping was always going to be swallowed by the same forces that swallowed everything else. I don't know. What I know is that seven people in Portland who share a rooftop and argue about frames probably aren't going to be enough anymore.

But I'm sure Lorna Voss has a plan. Lorna Voss always has a plan.

*— B.C.*
