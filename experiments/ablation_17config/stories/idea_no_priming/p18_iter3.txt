**INTAKE SUMMARY — PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT**

Subject community is an unincorporated planned residential development located approximately ██ miles ██████ of ████████, ████ County, Ohio, bearing the commercial designation "Briarwood Estates." Development consists of 214 single-family residential structures arranged across six curvilinear street networks on approximately 310 acres of former agricultural land. Permanent population at time of initial assessment was estimated at 794–812 persons. Community was identified for preliminary assessment following referral from ████████ County Sheriff's Office (see Attachment A-1) documenting anomalous coordinated civilian behavior inconsistent with known patterns of organized subversive activity, mass psychogenic illness, or religious/ideological group action. Preliminary field contact was established 03 June 1977 by SA ██████████ and SA ██████████ of the Cincinnati Field Office. Initial assessment classified Priority C (Deferred / Low Threat). Priority was upgraded to B on 09 July 1977 after receipt of supplementary materials (see § III–IV). ## § I. DESCRIPTION OF PRIMARY ANOMALY

### 1.1 — Initial Report (████████ County Sheriff's Office)

On 14 May 1977, Deputy ██████████ of the ████████ County Sheriff's Office submitted an incident report (Ref. No. 77-4412, reproduced as Attachment A-1) describing the following observation. Deputy ██████████ was conducting routine patrol along ████████ Road, which forms the eastern boundary of the Briarwood Estates development, at approximately 1510 hours EST. Deputy slowed vehicle to observe a reported mailbox obstruction (see complaint log 77-4398). While stopped, Deputy noted "unusual simultaneous vocalization" by multiple residents within visual range.

Deputy's narrative statement, transcribed in full:

> *I was sitting in the cruiser at the corner of ████████ and Tanager Lane looking at the mailbox situation when I heard something I have not been able to get out of my mind since. It was like everyone talked at once. I could see Mrs. ████████ in her front yard and Mr. ████████ across the street working on his garage door and there were two kids on bikes down the block and a woman I didn't recognize walking a dog. They all said something at the exact same time. The exact same words. It lasted maybe three or four seconds. Then they all just kept doing what they were doing. Nobody looked at each other. Nobody stopped. The woman kept walking the dog. The kids kept riding. I sat there for I think ten minutes and then I drove back to the station and I didn't say anything about it for two days because I thought there was something wrong with me.*

Deputy ██████████ subsequently submitted two supplementary statements (Attachments A-2, A-3) documenting repeat observations on 21 May and 28 May 1977, both occurring at or within thirty seconds of 1512 hours EST. In the third observation, Deputy was accompanied by Deputy ██████████, who independently corroborated the event and noted that the vocalization appeared to be produced by residents indoors as well — audible through open windows — and that "it sounded like it went on past where I could see, like it was the whole neighborhood."

### 1.2 — FBI Field Verification

On 06 June 1977, SA ██████████ and SA ██████████ conducted the first of four scheduled observation sessions at Briarwood Estates. Agents were positioned at two fixed observation points (see diagram, Attachment B-1) providing visual and auditory coverage of approximately 40% of the development's outdoor common areas.

**Observation Record — 06 June 1977:**

| TIME (EST) | EVENT |
|---|---|
| 1430 | Agents in position. Weather clear, 74°F. Normal suburban activity observed. |
| 1500 | No anomalous activity. Agents note community appears "unusually well-maintained" but within normal parameters. |
| 1511 | Agent ██████████ reports "feeling of anticipation, likely observer bias." |
| 1512:03 | **EVENT.** Simultaneous vocalization detected across full observable area. Duration: 3.7 seconds (stopwatch, Agent ██████████). All visible residents vocalized. Agent ██████████ counted fourteen individuals in direct line of sight. Agent ██████████ counted nine from secondary position. No individual paused before, during, or after vocalization. No change in posture, gait, activity, or facial expression observed. |
| 1512:10 | Event concluded. No residual effects observed. |
| 1515 | Agents conferred. Both reported identical auditory impression of the phrase (see § 1.3). |
| 1530 | Agents departed. |

Repeat observations on 08, 11, and 15 June 1977 produced identical results. The event occurred each day at 1512 EST (± 8 seconds). No variation in duration, apparent participation, or post-event behavior was detected.

### 1.3 — The Phrase: Phonetic Record

The vocalization was transcribed independently by both field agents, by Deputy ██████████ (re-interviewed 07 June 1977), and by four of the five civilian subjects described in § III. All transcriptions are consistent to within minor phonetic variation attributable to observer background and dialect expectation.

**Consensus phonetic transcription (IPA):**

> /wɪ ɑːɹ sɛˈɹiːn ɪn ðə ɡɑːɹ.dən ʌv ˈɔːlwɛɪz/

**Approximate English rendering:**

> *"We are serene in the garden of always."*

Linguistic analysis (Attachment C-1, prepared by ██████████, Linguistics Department, Georgetown University, under contract ████████) concluded the following: (1) The phrase is grammatically well-formed English. (2) Semantic content is coherent but "non-referential in any pragmatic context — it does not respond to a question, describe an observable state, or function as any recognized speech act." (3) Prosodic analysis of field recordings (reel-to-reel, Attachment C-2) indicates the phrase is spoken with "flat, uniform intonation inconsistent with any known English dialect, affect pattern, or speech pathology. Stress falls on every syllable equally. The effect is of a sentence being *pronounced* rather than *spoken.*" (4) The phrase does not correspond to any known liturgical text, advertising slogan, political motto, song lyric, or therapeutic script in the analyst's reference corpus.

No resident of Briarwood Estates, when engaged in conversation by agents or other investigators, ever referenced the phrase, acknowledged its occurrence, or demonstrated awareness of the 1512 event. When asked directly (see interview transcripts, Attachment D, 1–17), residents responded with polite confusion or mild amusement. Representative response (Mrs. ██████████, 42, resident since 1974): "I'm sorry, I don't know what you're referring to. 3:12? I'm usually starting dinner around then, I think. Is this about the mailboxes?"

## § II. PRELIMINARY HYPOTHESES (ASSESSED AND DISCARDED)

*[The following subsection has been compressed for this annotation. Original runs 43 pages. — D.M.]*

Between 06 June and 14 July 1977, the Cincinnati Field Office explored the following explanatory frameworks for the Briarwood anomaly. Each was assessed and discarded for the reasons noted.

**2.1 — Organized Subversive / Cult Activity.** Investigated per standard COINTELPRO-adjacent protocols. No evidence of charismatic leadership, hierarchical organizational structure, financial extraction, sexual coercion, weapons stockpiling, radical political ideology, or any other marker of domestic cult activity was identified. Residents maintained normal employment (██% commuted to ████████ or ████████ for work), normal kinship patterns, normal consumer behavior. Church attendance was distributed across seven denominations and was statistically unremarkable for the region. *Discarded.*

**2.2 — Mass Psychogenic Illness / Shared Psychotic Disorder (Folie à Plusieurs).** Consultation with ████████████ at ████████ University (see Attachment E-1) concluded that the observed phenomenon was inconsistent with any documented form of mass psychogenic illness. Key objections: (a) psychogenic events produce variable symptoms across subjects; Briarwood event is perfectly uniform; (b) psychogenic events are typically preceded by identifiable stressors and propagate through visible social contagion; no such precursors or vectors were identified; (c) psychogenic events produce distress or at minimum *awareness* in affected individuals; Briarwood residents demonstrate neither. Consultant's summary: "Whatever this is, it is not a known psychiatry." *Discarded.*

**2.3 — Chemical / Environmental Exposure.** Water, soil, and air samples (Attachments F-1 through F-14) submitted to ████████████████████████. All results within normal parameters. No unusual industrial activity within a twenty-mile radius. Municipal water supply shared with three adjacent communities displaying no anomalous behavior. *Discarded.*

**2.4 — Coordinated Performance / Hoax.** Assessed by SA ██████████ through covert observation. Note that during the 15 June observation session, Agent ██████████ observed a Briarwood resident (Mr. ████████, 67) speaking the phrase at 1512 while *asleep in a lawn chair.* Agent confirmed the subject's eyes were closed, respiration was regular, and no change in body position occurred before, during, or after vocalization. A sleeping man cannot participate in a coordinated hoax. *Discarded.*

**2.5 — External Broadcast / Acoustic Phenomenon.** Swept for unusual radio, microwave, and infrasonic emissions on three occasions (Attachments G-1 through G-3). No anomalous signals detected at any point during the observation window. The phrase originates in the throats of the residents. There is no external trigger. Section Chief ██████████ wrote in his internal assessment: "We are left with the observation that approximately eight hundred civilians are doing something simultaneously that none of them appear to be doing voluntarily or consciously, and for which we currently possess no explanatory model." He then recommended continued monitoring at current priority level.

### SUBJECT 1: ██████████ ██████████
**Case Ref:** 9P-CI-140771-S1
**Profile:** Male, age 54 at time of contact. Licensed psychiatrist (license revoked 19██ by the State of Ohio Medical Board following ████████████████████████████████████████; revocation was under appeal at the time of events described herein). Divorced. Residing in ████████, Ohio. No criminal record. No known political affiliations. Described by former colleagues as "brilliant, rigorous, and not someone you'd invite to a party" (see character assessment, Attachment H-1).

**Contact with Anomaly:**

On 22 May 1977, Subject 1 traveled to Briarwood Estates to conduct an informal follow-up visit with a former patient, Mrs. ██████████, who had relocated to the community in early 1976. Subject 1 had continued intermittent contact with this patient despite the license revocation, a fact he acknowledged in his Bureau interview as "ethically borderline but clinically justified — she'd been my patient for nine years and the Board's problems with me had nothing to do with her care."

During the visit, Subject 1 was present in the patient's living room at 1512 hours. The patient was mid-sentence. Per Subject 1's account:

> *She stopped — but she didn't stop. That's what I can't make anyone understand. Her mouth kept moving, but the words changed. She said the thing — "we are serene in the garden of always" — in a voice that was hers and wasn't hers. It was like a radio station bleeding through a phone call. Three seconds, maybe four. Then she resumed her sentence at the exact syllable where she'd departed from it. She did not know anything had happened. There was no micro-expression of confusion, no pause, no reorientation. I have spent thirty years studying dissociative phenomena. I have treated patients with the most severe fragmentation you can imagine. None of them — not one — could switch states and return with zero latency. The mechanism required for what I observed would be, to the best of my professional knowledge, neurologically impossible.*
