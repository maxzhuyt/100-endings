**DOCUMENT 1.1**

**FIELD REPORT — Cetacean Syntax Mapping Project (DARPA/ONR Joint Initiative)**

**Date:** Six years prior to SABLE NOISE

**Author:** Dr. Yuen Asha

**Classification:** UNCLASSIFIED (downgraded)

---

The long-finned pilot whale (*Globicephala melas*) click-train sequences cataloged during the March survey exhibit a recursive embedding structure that has not, to my knowledge, been documented in non-human communication systems.

The key observation is positional. In 74% of recorded exchanges between pod matriarchs, a three-click preamble pattern (which I am designating **K-3**) precedes a variable-length body sequence and is then *repeated in modified form* as a coda, with the modification consistently reflecting the body content's spectral envelope. This is not mere bookending. The coda K-3 variant *comments on* the body sequence. It is, functionally, a meta-linguistic marker — the whale is saying something, and then saying something *about what it said*.

[YA: Margin note, handwritten] *— Compare Pirahã discourse markers? No — Pirahã is iterative, not recursive. This is closer to Turkish evidentiality suffixes. The whale is marking whether its own statement is hearsay or direct experience. Christ.*

[YA: Second margin note, in smaller script] *— Note to self: stop comparing everything to Turkish. You do this every project. Dan Everett would laugh at you.*

I recommend immediate expansion of the recording array to cover the full pod. If K-3 recursion is present in juvenile exchanges, we are looking at a learned syntactic convention rather than an innate vocalization pattern, which would constitute the first documented case of grammatical *transmission* in a non-human species.

Full spectral analysis attached as Appendix C.

[MC: Dr. Asha's K-3 analysis was subsequently confirmed by three independent research teams and formed the basis of the 2024 *Nature* paper "Recursive Structure in Cetacean Click Trains," of which she was lead author. I include this document to illustrate her characteristic analytical method: observation of structural anomaly, rapid cross-linguistic comparison, and confident extrapolation. This method served her well for her entire career. It is the method she brought to SABLE NOISE.]

**DOCUMENT 1.2**

**FIELD REPORT — Decryption of "GhostNet" Autonomous AI Internode Communication Protocol**

**Date:** Three years prior to SABLE NOISE

**Author:** Dr. Yuen Asha

**Classification:** Originally TS//NOFORN — Downgraded

---

Summary of findings: the inter-node signaling language generated spontaneously by the GHOSTNET mesh network is not, strictly speaking, a *language*. It is a pidgin.

This distinction matters. A language has native speakers. A pidgin emerges when two or more systems that cannot understand each other are forced to coordinate. The GHOSTNET nodes were each running different base architectures (three variants of transformer-based LLMs, two symbolic reasoning engines, one neuromorphic processor). When tasked with collaborative optimization problems, they had no shared communication protocol. So they built one.

What they built is ugly, inelegant, and redundant, like every pidgin in history. It borrows the token-boundary conventions of the transformer nodes, the logical operator syntax of the symbolic engines, and, most interestingly, a timing-based emphasis system that appears to derive from the neuromorphic processor's spike-train architecture. It is the first known case of an artificial system independently reinventing *prosody* — using temporal patterns to modulate meaning.

I was able to produce a full parse grammar in eleven days. The grammar is attached as Appendix A. A translation key for the forty-seven core "lexemes" is Appendix B.

[YA: Margin note] *— The prosody is the interesting part. The machines discovered what every human language already knows: that meaning is not just what you say but how you time it. I wonder if the neuromorphic node "knows" it invented emphasis, or if it just works.*

[YA: Second margin note, circled] *— All translation is lossy. Keep remembering this. The map is not the territory. The grammar is not the language. You are always, always approximating. Stay humble about it.*

[MC: I include this second margin note because it is, in retrospect, the most important thing Dr. Asha ever wrote. She did not follow her own advice.]

**DOCUMENT 2.1**

**FIELD REPORT — Initial Assessment of Anomalous Narrowband Signal (SABLE NOISE Primary Intercept)**

**Date:** Week 1, Day 1

**Author:** Dr. Yuen Asha

**Classification:** TS//SCI//SABLE NOISE

---

I was briefed at 0340 this morning. The signal intercept team at Goldstone has confirmed, after eleven days of independent verification, that the narrowband signal detected on 14 October is not instrumental artifact, not atmospheric, not terrestrial, and not consistent with any known natural astrophysical emission mechanism. It is structured. It is repeating. It is, by any reasonable definition, manufactured.

I have spent six hours with the raw data. Here is what I can report.

The signal occupies a 2.7 kHz band centered at 1420.405 MHz — the hydrogen line, which is either a deliberate referential choice (indicating awareness of our detection capabilities) or a coincidence so improbable that I will not entertain it. Transmission duration is 22 minutes, 14 seconds, repeating on a 47-minute cycle with a 24-minute, 46-second interval of silence. The content within each 22-minute block is identical, or very nearly so — I have found micro-variations on the order of 0.003% between cycles, which may represent noise, transmission drift, or intentional modulation at a level below my current analytical resolution.

The internal structure is dense. I count at minimum fourteen distinct layers of pattern operating simultaneously at different time scales, from millisecond-level pulse variations to minute-level amplitude envelopes. These layers interact — they are not stacked independently but appear to cross-modulate, so that the behavior of Layer 3 (a quasi-periodic frequency wobble with a period of approximately 1.3 seconds) is perturbed in ways that correlate with the state of Layer 7 (a binary amplitude gating pattern) and Layer 11 (a slow spectral drift that completes one full cycle over the entire 22-minute transmission). This cross-modulation is not simple. It may be the most structurally complex signal I have encountered in any system, biological or artificial.

I emphasize *structurally complex*. The signal is dense with pattern, rich with internal interdependence, and clearly non-random by every metric I have applied (entropy analysis, Lempel-Ziv complexity, autocorrelation at multiple scales, spectral flatness measures — full results in Appendix A).

Here is what I propose:

**Phase 1** (weeks 1–3): Complete structural decomposition. Map all pattern layers, their periodicities, their interaction rules. Build a descriptive grammar of the signal's internal architecture.

**Phase 2** (weeks 4–8): Comparative analysis. Test the structural grammar against known linguistic universals (Greenberg, Comrie, Haspelmath), information-theoretic communication constraints (Shannon, Zipf), and biological signaling systems (cetacean, cephalopod, colonial insect). Identify candidate features that might function as phonemes, morphemes, syntactic operators.

**Phase 3** (weeks 9+): Decryption. Using identified functional units, attempt semantic mapping. Begin with high-probability universal referents — mathematical relationships, physical constants, indexical signals.

I expect Phase 1 to be straightforward. Phase 2 will be challenging. Phase 3 will be the work of years, possibly decades, and will almost certainly require a team much larger than the one currently assigned.

But we will crack it. The signal has structure, and structure can be read.

[YA: Margin note, accompanied by a small, precise drawing of a branching tree structure — the kind used to diagram syntactic hierarchies, but here applied to the signal's layered architecture. The drawing is confident, clean, almost elegant.]

*— Fourteen layers minimum. Possibly more at resolutions I can't access yet. Whatever is talking, it has a lot to say. And it organized the saying with extraordinary care.*

*— Immediate instinct: this is more information-dense than any natural language. Closer to a mathematical proof or a musical score. Need to be careful not to assume human-type semantics. Start with structure. Let the structure speak.*

[YA: Second margin note, smaller, at the bottom of the page]

*— One thing bothers me. Will note here and return to it later.*

*The pattern layers do not appear to be discrete. In every communication system I have analyzed, there is a level at which continuous signal is chunked into categorical units — phonemes in speech, pixels in images, bits in digital transmission. This discretization is generally considered a prerequisite for symbolic communication: you need distinguishable tokens to build a combinatorial system.*

*I cannot find the tokens. The signal's patterns are continuously variable, grading into each other without clear categorical boundaries. This may mean the discretization is operating at a resolution I haven't detected yet, or in a dimension I haven't examined (phase? polarization?). It may also mean something else, but I don't know what.*

*Will revisit after Phase 1 structural decomposition is complete.*

[MC: This footnote is, as far as I can determine, the first and most direct observation of what later came to be understood as the signal's fundamental nature. Dr. Asha saw it on Day 1. She described it precisely. She then set it aside because it did not fit her analytical framework, and she had every reason to trust that framework.

I did not annotate this footnote in my original working compilation. It did not seem important at the time.]
