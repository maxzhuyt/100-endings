# The Bittercreek Accords

### Being a Partial Reconstruction of Events at the Peralta Lode, Bittercreek, Wyoming Territory, November 8–11, 1891, Assembled from Postal Records, Telegraph Transcripts, Legal Filings, and Physical Evidence

*Compiled and annotated by Opal Hutchins, Postal Clerk, Bittercreek Station, U.S. Mail*

---

## I.

---

**DOCUMENT 1-A**

WESTERN UNION TELEGRAPH COMPANY

*Received at Bittercreek Station, November 2, 1891, 3:47 p.m.*

TO: DEP. U.S. MARSHAL HARLAN BRIGGS — CHEYENNE OFFICE

RELIABLE INTELLIGENCE PLACES CLARA DEVEREAUX ALIAS BLOODY CLARA AT OR NEAR PERALTA LODE SILVER MINE TWELVE MILES EAST OF BITTERCREEK STOP SHE IS BELIEVED TRAVELING WITHOUT ARMED ESCORT STOP YOUR DEMOCRATS COURAGE AT RAWLINS AFFAIR MAKES YOU IDEAL FOR APPREHENSION STOP COORDINATE WITH LOCAL LAND OFFICE RE: ACCESS TO MINE ROAD STOP CLAIMHOLDER IS CORNELIUS PEET WHO HAS OFFERED FULL COOPERATION STOP ACT SWIFTLY STOP

ORIGIN: UNSIGNED ¹

---

¹ The phrase "Democrats Courage" is a wire garble. The original draft, which I handled at the relay, read "demonstrated courage at the Rawlins Affair." I can confirm Deputy Marshal Briggs was present at Rawlins in the technical sense that his body occupied space within the territorial boundary. He spent the forty-one-hour standoff in the root cellar of the Hesserman boardinghouse composing letters to his mother in Ottumwa. I know this because I processed seven of them. They concerned, in order: a sour-cream pie recipe he wished her to attempt, a dog he had seen, his conviction that he was developing a corn on his left foot, the dog again, a rumor about the widow Stassik, a lengthy and unprompted denial that he had ever been afraid of anything, and a postscript asking whether she had attempted the pie. He did not once mention the standoff happening directly above his head. The word "courage" has never appeared in any correspondence handled by this office bearing his name, inbound or outbound, in eleven years of postal service. The telegram is unsigned. It was filed by a clerk at the Rawlins office who accepted payment in cash and did not record the sender's name, though the filing fee was paid in the same denomination of banknote — an 1888 series silver certificate — that Mr. Cornelius Peet has used exclusively at this office for three years. — O.H.

**DOCUMENT 1-B**

*Letter, posted Cheyenne, October 29, 1891. Received Bittercreek Station November 4 (delayed — snow on the Laramie spur). Addressed to "Mrs. C. Marchand, General Delivery, Bittercreek."*

Dear Clara,

You do not know me, but I am a friend to friends of yours from the Hole-in-the-Wall days. I understand you have been living quiet and that is wise and I respect it. I am writing because there is a cabin at the Peralta Lode mine east of town that is empty and secure and not known to the law. A woman I will not name stayed there for four months last winter without trouble. The mine is closed and the land is held by a speculator named Peet who does not visit his claims. You would be undisturbed. I have left supplies. Come on the 8th. Use the mine road.

A Friend ²

---

² There is no cabin at the Peralta Lode. There has never been a cabin at the Peralta Lode. The mine office was a canvas tent that blew into the creek in 1887 and was not replaced. "Mrs. C. Marchand" is the name Clara Devereaux has used at general delivery in four Wyoming towns since abandoning her gang in the spring of 1889. I know this because mail follows patterns, and patterns are my occupation. The letter is written on stationery from the Gilchrist Hotel in Cheyenne. Mr. Peet keeps a standing room at the Gilchrist. The handwriting is disguised — the letter slants are inconsistent and the pressure varies in a manner that suggests a right-handed person writing with deliberate awkwardness — but the author twice writes "I respect it" and "I will not name," constructions I have seen in fourteen separate letters filed by Mr. Peet's land office. The woman supposedly sheltered at the nonexistent cabin is not named because she does not exist. Clara Devereaux has a competing possessory claim on the Peralta drainage registered under her legal name in 1886, before the warrants. She filed it in person. I recall her clearly. She was polite. She had a toothache she was ignoring with visible effort. She asked me whether Bittercreek had a dentist. It did not. — O.H.

**DOCUMENT 1-C**

*Contract offer, hand-delivered to the office of Karl Bremer, Mining Engineer, Casper, Wyoming, and forwarded to Mr. Eamon Kirsch at his last known address (a boarding house in Thermopolis). Postmarked Bittercreek, October 27, 1891.*

Mr. Kirsch:

I have a silver mine in need of development blasting. The Peralta Lode, 12 miles east of Bittercreek, contains a promising vein at the 200-foot level that requires expert clearance of the east drift. Your reputation for work in difficult terrain precedes you. I am aware that your recent difficulties with the Northern Pacific Railroad have made regular employment challenging and I am not a man who holds another man's politics against him. I can offer a flat fee of $200 and a 2% stake in future assay returns. Come prepared to work on the 8th of November. Sincerely, Cornelius Peet. ³

---

³ Eamon Kirsch was blacklisted by the Northern Pacific Railroad in 1889. The railroad's formal statement cited "political agitation among the labor crews." Mr. Kirsch has described himself, in letters I have processed to three separate newspapers and one territorial representative, as a man "punished for the crime of organizing." The actual reason for his dismissal, as described in the railroad's internal correspondence — a copy of which was routed through this office due to an addressing error and which I read, as is my duty when routing requires verification of the intended recipient — was that he collapsed a grade tunnel outside Billings by miscalculating the load-bearing capacity of the support timbers by a factor of three. No one was killed. Four men were hospitalized. The railroad chose to describe the firing as political because a wrongly-dismissed agitator does not invite an engineering inquest, and an engineering inquest would have delayed the Billings spur by eighteen months. Mr. Kirsch accepted the story because a political martyr gets sympathy contracts and a man who cannot do arithmetic gets nothing. This arrangement suited everyone except the four men in the hospital, none of whom, to my knowledge, were consulted. The Peralta Lode does not contain a promising vein at the 200-foot level. The mine was abandoned in 1885 when assay reports confirmed the silver played out at 140 feet. I know this because the assay office used our post to file its reports, and because nothing that enters this office leaves my memory. — O.H.

**DOCUMENT 1-D**

*Note, undated, found folded inside a month-old copy of the Cheyenne Daily Leader mailed to "J. Fontaine, Poste Restante, Thermopolis." The newspaper was postmarked Bittercreek. The note was written in pencil on the margin of an unrelated patent-medicine advertisement.*

Fontaine — I know what you owe Dietrich and I know you can't pay it. I have a private job that would clear the full amount. Be at the Peralta Lode mine east of Bittercreek on Nov 8. Come in through the lower adit on the creek side. Tell no one. There's nothing in this for anyone but you and me. ⁴

---

⁴ Jubal Fontaine owes $eleven hundred to a man named Dietrich in Cheyenne. This is true. The debt, however, is not a gambling debt, as Mr. Fontaine has described it in three separate letters to associates asking for loans. It is payment for a horse — a particular horse — which Mr. Fontaine stole in 1888 from a ranch near Lander and which turned out to belong to Dietrich's daughter. Dietrich does not want the money. He wants the horse back. The horse has been dead for two years, a fact Mr. Fontaine has not communicated to anyone in writing. The note is unsigned. The pencil is the same make — a Dixon Ticonderoga №2, sharpened with a left-hand cant — that Mr. Peet uses for his personal correspondence and which I have identified by its distinctive graphite smear on forty-six pieces of outgoing mail. I wish to observe that of the four communications documented above, this is the only one that does not bother to construct a plausible reason for its recipient to appear at the mine. Mr. Peet apparently assessed Mr. Fontaine as a man who would walk into a hole in the ground if you told him it would solve his problems. Mr. Peet was correct. — O.H.

---

⁵ I am aware that the reader of this compilation may wonder why, having identified a clear pattern of manipulation in these four communications, I did not intervene. I did not intervene because I am a postal clerk. My authority extends to the handling, routing, and delivery of U.S. mail. I do not have the legal right to open, suppress, or editorialize upon correspondence in transit, except where postal regulations require inspection, and even then my role is ministerial rather than discretionary. I read what the work requires me to read. I remember what I read. These are professional attributes, not investigative ones. I delivered each of these communications to its intended recipient on time and without comment, because that is what the position requires. Whether I lay awake at night after November 8 is not a matter for the postal record. — O.H.

TO: U.S. MARSHALS SERVICE — CHEYENNE

ARRIVED PERALTA LODE 11AM STOP IDENTIFIED DEVEREAUX INSIDE MINE STOP ALSO PRESENT TWO MALES IDENTITY UNK STOP SITUATION IS NOT AS DESC

---

⁶ The transmission ends here. The telegraph wire connecting the Peralta relay shack to the Bittercreek main line was found severed at 2:31 p.m. by a repair rider who attributed the break to weather. It was not weather. The cut was clean. I have the repair rider's report. Weather does not use pliers. — O.H.

**DOCUMENT 2-B**

*TERRITORY OF WYOMING — FUGITIVE NOTICE — AMENDED*

WANTED: CLARA DEVEREAUX, alias "BLOODY CLARA," alias MRS. C. MARCHAND

*For: armed robbery (3 counts), assault upon a peace officer, horse theft, conspiracy*

AMENDED NOVEMBER 12, 1891: Subject is BELIEVED DEAD following collapse of the Peralta Lode mine, Bittercreek, on or about November 8, 1891. Outstanding warrants SUSPENDED pending confirmation of remains. Claimants to the posted reward of $500 must present physical proof of death to the U.S. Marshals Service, Cheyenne.

*Filed by: Clerk of the Territorial Court, Cheyenne* ⁷

---

⁷ The amendment was filed four days after the collapse and before any excavation had been attempted. The filing was requested by letter from the law office of Pruett & Sayles, which represents Mr. Cornelius Peet in his land dealings. The suspension of warrants had the immediate legal effect of suspending Clara Devereaux's possessory claim on the Peralta drainage, as a dead woman cannot hold territorial land rights and a woman under suspended warrant cannot appear in person to reassert them. Mr. Peet filed a consolidating claim on the drainage on November 13. I note this because the filing passed through this office. I note everything because everything passes through this office. — O.H.

**DOCUMENT 2-C**

*Receipt, Klausen's General Merchandise, Bittercreek, Wyoming. Dated November 7, 1891.*

1 case blasting caps (24 ct.) — $6.00
3 sticks dynamite — $4.50
1 coil fuse (50 ft.) — $2.00
1 bull's-eye lantern — $1.25
Matches (2 boxes) — $0.20

TOTAL: $13.95
PAID: Cash

*On the reverse, in pencil, in a hand matching other samples of Mr. Kirsch's writing:*

"timbers wrong — angle wrong — whole drift gone. charges set per standard calc but the rock isite notite iteiteite" ⁸

---

⁸ The final words are not, as they appear, repetition from panic or injury, though they are both. Mr. Kirsch was attempting to write "ite" as a suffix — as in "granite" or "ite" — and could not recall or settle on the specific mineral designation. The Peralta Lode sits in a formation of heavily fractured rhyolite overlying decomposed granite, a combination that is notoriously unpredictable under blast loading. Any competent demolitions man would have identified this from the first core sample. Mr. Kirsch set charges calibrated for solid sandstone in a mine made of rock that shatters like wet bread. The seismic event was recorded at the Union Pacific rail office in Rawlins, forty-seven miles southeast, at 2:19 p.m. on November 8, as a tremor of "moderate" intensity. The rail office logged it as probable natural activity. It was not natural. It was arithmetic. — O.H.

*Meteorological and seismic notation, personal log.*

November 8, 1891. Bittercreek, Wyoming Territory.

Temperature at noon: 29°F. Wind NW at 12 mph. Skies overcast, light snow beginning at 1 p.m. Barometric pressure falling (28.91 at the rail office, per their evening wire).
