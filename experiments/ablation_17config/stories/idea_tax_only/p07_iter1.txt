# THE BURNISHED ROSE ORNITHOLOGICAL SOCIETY

### Being an Annotated Collection of Documents, Telegrams, Meeting Minutes, Anonymous Correspondence, and Marginal Commentary Pertaining to Parcel 1177-C, Fremont County, Wyoming Territory, 1881–1882

*Compiled and annotated by Elwin Briggs, Postmaster, Lark Creek, W.T.*

---

## I. THE CLERICAL ERROR — FIVE NAMES ON ONE DEED

---

**UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR**
**GENERAL LAND OFFICE**
**Cheyenne, Wyoming Territory**

**Certificate of Stewardship and Scientific Occupation**
*Filed Under the Act of March 3, 1879, as amended*

**No. 4,471**

BE IT KNOWN that pursuant to application received and survey completed, the following-described tract of public land is hereby granted in stewardship for purposes of scientific inquiry, conservation, and related occupation:

**PARCEL 1177-C**: Two hundred (200) acres, more or less, situated in the southwest quarter of Township 33 North, Range 99 West of the 6th Principal Meridian, Fremont County, Wyoming Territory, bounded on the north by Lark Creek, on the east by the wagon road to Casper, on the south by the ridgeline locally known as Bittern's Spine, and on the west by unmarked federal land extending to the Wind River Range foothills.

Said parcel is granted under the designation of **SCIENTIFIC SOCIETY CLAIM**, requiring quarterly demonstration of active scientific inquiry as condition of continued occupancy.

STEWARD(S) OF RECORD:

1. Miss Cecilia Farquhar Aldworth, care of Mrs. J. Pennington Curtis, 114 Beacon Street, Boston, Massachusetts
2. Mr. Jesse R. Creel, care of General Delivery, Rawlins, Wyoming Territory
3. Mr. Francis Xavier Mulvaney, care of The Continental Hotel, Room 14, Denver, Colorado
4. Mr. Arthur Emmons Vane, care of Union Pacific Railroad, Division of Survey, Omaha, Nebraska
5. Mrs. Pearl Kittredge, care of Post Office Box 7, Hole-in-the-Wall, Johnson County, Wyoming Territory

Given under my hand and the seal of the General Land Office this 14th day of April, 1881.

[Seal]

*Horace P. Dilworth*
*Deputy Clerk, Filing Division*

---

**[Annotation, in black ink, hand of E. Briggs:]** *Five names. One parcel. I have handled land office filings for eleven years and have never seen the like. The survey plat attached—which I have not reproduced here, as it is a single map bearing five application numbers in five different inks, each circling the identical acreage—tells the story plainly enough. Deputy Clerk Dilworth appears to have received five separate applications for five adjacent but distinct parcels, misread the survey coordinates on all five (or, more charitably, worked from a plat that had been drawn by a man in the grip of either whiskey or malaria), and filed a single omnibus certificate. Five separate filing fees were collected. Five separate applications were approved. One piece of land was granted, five times, to five people who have never met. I recognized the collision the moment the documents arrived. I considered warning someone. I could not determine whom to disappoint, since all five seemed the type to shoot a messenger.*

**[Annotation, in black ink, hand of E. Briggs:]** *"Professional affiliations which need not be enumerated." A Pinkerton man, then, or a former one—the diction of a man accustomed to writing reports for superiors he does not respect. The misspelling of "received" and "professional" is consistent with dictation: he spoke the telegram aloud to the Denver operator, likely from across a hotel bar. Note also "send whiskey if able," which is not customarily included in formal correspondence with the federal government.*

**[Annotation, in black ink, hand of E. Briggs:]** *He enclosed a stamped envelope in a telegram. This is a man who has had paperwork go wrong before and has developed the countermeasures of the perpetually correct. I anticipate he will be the first to notice the irregularity and the last to accept it.*

**[Annotation, in black ink, hand of E. Briggs:]** *Four words. Not even a courtesy "stop" after "way." The confidence of a woman who has filed land claims before—some of them, I would wager, for land she had a somewhat negotiable relationship with. I note that her forwarding address is Hole-in-the-Wall, which is less a town than a geographical condition. It is where people go when their previous addresses have become inadvisable. I processed all five acceptance telegrams. I knew. I said nothing. I have since asked myself whether this constituted a dereliction of duty or an act of mercy and have concluded it was neither: it was cowardice dressed in the uniform of professional discretion.*

**[Annotation, in black ink, hand of E. Briggs:]** *I processed all ten telegrams—five in, five out—inside of twelve days. The outgoing five cost me more in conscience than the office earned in fees. I watched each of them compose their complaint at the counter, each certain that theirs was the rightful claim and that the others were interlopers, squatters, or worse. Miss Aldworth wrote hers standing, with gloves on. Creel wrote his in pencil on the back of a feed receipt. Mulvaney dictated his while pacing and twice changed "fraud" to a stronger word I declined to transmit. Vane arrived with his telegram pre-written, cross-referenced, and annotated with footnotes that do not reproduce in telegraph format. Mrs. Kittredge wrote hers in nine seconds, smiled at me, and left a nickel tip. They have not yet spoken to each other in any productive sense. They have, I understand, divided the single standing cabin into territories demarcated by rope and bedsheets. I give it a week before blood or before something stranger.*
