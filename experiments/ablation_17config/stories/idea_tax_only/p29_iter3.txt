## SECTION I: Preliminary Attestation and Establishment of Documenting Authority

My name is Pyotr Alexeevich Falin. I have served as Grade III Zoning Clerk in Office 414-B for eleven years. My employee number is MZC-4417. My desk faces north-northwest, which I confirmed with a compass on my first day and reconfirm annually, a practice the department does not require but which I maintain because orientation matters. If you do not know where your desk faces, you do not, in a meaningful administrative sense, know where you are.

Office 414-B measures 4.2 meters by 3.8 meters. It contains two desks, two filing cabinets (one functioning, one prone to seizing in its lower drawer due to a humidity-warped rail I have reported nine times, see Maintenance Requests 2019-0041 through 2019-0049), one shared wastebasket, and my officemate, Galkin.

I will describe Galkin, because he becomes relevant later in ways I could not have predicted and find, even now, professionally distressing.

Galkin, Viktor Igorovich. Grade III Zoning Clerk, same as myself, though his tenure is two years shorter and his output per quarter measurably lower, a fact I have noted in four annual peer-review supplements, none of which produced administrative action. He has a lazy eye — the left — which drifts outward during periods of concentration or emotional disturbance, giving him the perpetual appearance of a man trying to monitor two conversations in opposite corners of a room. He sweats. Not in response to heat, specifically, but as a kind of general bodily policy, a default state, so that his shirts darken under the arms by ten in the morning regardless of season. His ties are, without exception, stained. Soup, primarily. He eats at his desk, leaning forward over a thermos of whatever broth his mother has packed — she still packs his lunches, though he is forty-three — and the angle of his lean, combined with what I can only call a constitutional imprecision of hand, ensures that each tie receives its baptism before noon. I have cataloged the stains over the years not out of any personal fixation but because, as a zoning clerk, I am trained to observe and record the conditions of shared space, and Galkin's tie is, regrettably, part of my shared space. His desk is positioned one hundred and forty centimeters from mine. I have submitted three requests to Facilities to increase this distance. All three were denied on the grounds that the room's dimensions do not permit reconfiguration. The room's dimensions are, as I have noted, 4.2 by 3.8 meters. I am aware they do not permit reconfiguration. I submitted the requests anyway, because the act of filing a request is not the same as the expectation of its fulfillment. Some documents exist to establish that a condition was known and protested. This is, I have come to understand, the purpose of most documents. It is the purpose of this one.

I should note, as a matter of completeness, that a warm, wet, faintly moaning fog has dissolved most of the Eastern European continent.

I include this detail in Section I rather than in a subsequent section because, per Filing Protocol 7.3 (Establishing Context for Subsequent Evidentiary Claims), all environmental conditions materially affecting the scope of testimony must be disclosed in the preliminary attestation. The fog is, I believe, material. It has consumed approximately 1.6 million square kilometers of territory, including but not limited to: eleven national capitals, the entirety of the Carpathian basin, most of the Baltic coastline, and the Facilities department that was responsible for denying my desk-relocation requests. This last fact I record without satisfaction. I am a procedural man. The loss of Facilities is an administrative inconvenience, not a source of vindication, and I would appreciate it if the reader — assuming a reader exists, assuming reading persists as a practice in whatever remains — did not project emotional motive onto a straightforward notation of institutional loss. The Facilities department is gone. My desk remains one hundred and forty centimeters from Galkin's. These are facts. I file them.

The fog smells of something I will not yet describe because I have not determined the appropriate form category.

## SECTION II: Establishment of Atmospheric Anomaly and Discovery of Reactive Properties

**Reference:** Incident Reports 2024-0001 through 2024-1,407 (abridged; full archive available in Filing Cabinet 2, assuming Cabinet 2 has not been dissolved at time of reading)

The fog arrived without meteorological precedent. I note this not as a personal observation but as the official position of the National Weather Bureau in the seventy-two hours before its building, staff, and instrumentation were absorbed into a wall of warm, pale vapor that witnesses described as smelling faintly of skin after sleep.

I would not have used that description. I include it because it appears in Incident Report 2024-0003, filed by a night-shift security guard at the Bureau whose testimony was taken while he was still shaking and who, I feel compelled to note, used an inappropriate number of exclamation points for an official document. But the description has been corroborated with sufficient frequency that I must accept it as functionally standard: the fog smells of skin after sleep. Warm skin. Skin that has been under blankets. The fog pressed against windows the way — and I am quoting Incident Report 2024-0087 here, not exercising personal lyricism — "something hungry and curious presses its face to glass."

Its method of dissolution was not violent. This is important to establish because the language of disaster — *destruction*, *devastation*, *annihilation* — implies force, impact, the physics of collision. The fog did not collide with anything. It *entered*. It moved through walls the way moisture moves through cheap plaster, slowly, patiently, and what it touched did not shatter or burn but softened, grew translucent, and eventually became fog itself. Concrete became mist. Steel became mist. Glass became the memory of glass, briefly holding its shape as a shimmering outline before exhaling into the general vapor. The process was, witnesses consistently reported, quiet. Almost gentle. The fog moaned — a low, resonant, continuous sound at the threshold of hearing, the frequency of which (between 18 and 22 Hz, per the last functioning infrasound monitor) is known to produce feelings of unease, awe, and, in certain controlled studies, involuntary physiological arousal.

I note the arousal detail here because it becomes administratively relevant.

The dissolution consumed, on average, one city block per hour. Municipal emergency response followed standard protocol: evacuation corridors, sheltering directives, deployment of barriers (sand, concrete, polymer sheeting) that the fog absorbed with what I will call — because I have no form field for this — *amusement*. It consumed the barriers exactly as it consumed everything else. The polymer sheeting dissolved with a faint sound that three independent witnesses described as a sigh.

I do not believe fog sighs. I record that three independent witnesses described it as such.

The discovery of the fog's reactive property occurred on Tuesday + 7, in Sector 12-East, during what Incident Report 2024-0119 describes as a "shelter-capacity exceedance event." I will translate from the bureaucratic, which — though it is my native and preferred language — functions here as a kind of modesty screen that the facts no longer deserve.

What happened was this: four hundred and twelve people were crowded into the gymnasium of Municipal School 9, a building rated for an occupancy of two hundred and sixty. The fog was three blocks away and advancing. Conditions in the gymnasium were hot, close, and characterized by the particular desperation that arises when large numbers of frightened people are pressed together in insufficient space with insufficient ventilation. Fear became proximity, proximity became contact, contact became — per the incident report — "a deviation from standard shelter-conduct protocol."

The report does not elaborate. I have read seventeen witness statements from that night. I will summarize them in the language appropriate to this filing:

In the gymnasium of Municipal School 9, under fluorescent lights and a motivational banner reading REACH FOR THE STARS!, approximately one hundred and sixty adults engaged in escalating acts of physical intimacy over a period of ninety minutes. The acts ranged from — I am consulting Appendix C of the consolidated witness dossier — "desperate clutching" to "fully realized coupling" and included, per witness statement 9-F, "things I didn't know I wanted until I thought I was going to die, and I'm not sorry, and you can put that in your file."

I put it in the file.

At 02:47, the fog, which had advanced to within forty meters of Municipal School 9 — close enough that the gymnasium windows had begun to soften and pearl at the edges — stopped.

Then it retreated.

It pulled back seventeen meters in the space of four minutes, an unprecedented reversal. The infrasound frequency dropped. The moaning — I will use the word because it is accurate — changed in pitch. Became, per three acoustic witnesses, lower. Slower. Satisfied.

I am not comfortable with the word *satisfied*. I include it because the acoustic data supports it and because discomfort is not an administratively valid reason to omit relevant evidence.

The correlation was confirmed over the following eleven days through a series of controlled and uncontrolled repetitions across nine sectors. I will not enumerate every incident. The data set is extensive, internally consistent, and — frankly — exhausting to document. The findings can be summarized as follows:

The fog recedes in the presence of collective human arousal that meets or exceeds a threshold measurable in aggregate biometric output (elevated skin temperature, increased heart rate, specific hormonal and pheromonal dispersion patterns, and vocalizations above 74 decibels in the frequency range associated with — I am reading directly from the scientific liaison's report — "genuine sexual pleasure as distinct from performative or simulated vocalizations, which the anomaly appears to distinguish with a sensitivity exceeding current voice-analysis software").

The fog can tell if you mean it.

That sentence is not mine. It appeared, spray-painted on the side of the Water Treatment Facility on Tuesday + 12, in lettering large enough to be read from the temporary command center across the river. I include it because it is the most efficient summary of 1,407 incident reports I have encountered, and because efficiency, not propriety, is the filing clerk's highest virtue.

I have printed the relevant biometric thresholds in Appendix D, along with the correlated fog-recession distances, organized by sector and date. I note, for the record, that I found the preparation of Appendix D to be the least pleasant task of my professional career, which includes the 2019 reassessment of sewer-easement boundaries for the entire Third District.

The fog, in the interim between documented arousal events, continued its advance. It pressed against the city's remaining perimeter with the patience of something that had nowhere else to be. At night, when the city's collective output dropped — as it will, as human beings will insist on sleeping, on growing tired, on being unable to sustain what is being asked of them — the fog inched forward. It pressed against windows. It moaned. Not aggressively. Curiously. The way you might press your hand to an aquarium to see if the fish respond.

I have been told that I sometimes anthropomorphize the fog in my reports. I reject the accusation. I describe what I observe. If the fog behaves like something with appetites, patience, and preferences, that is the fog's business, and I will document it as observed.

The fog is the most honest entity I have encountered in twenty-three years of municipal service. It wants what it wants. It responds when it gets it. It advances when denied. It has never submitted a misleading form, falsified a report, or positioned its desk one hundred and forty centimeters from mine despite repeated formal objections.

I will proceed to Section III.

## SECTION III: Appointment of the Director of Sustained Atmospheric Repulsion

**Reference:** Emergency Administrative Order 2024-0009; Personnel File: Galkin, Viktor Igorovich

The position of Director of Sustained Atmospheric Repulsion was created on Tuesday + 14, following an emergency session of the Municipal Council that lasted eleven hours, during which fourteen council members resigned, three feigned medical emergencies, and one — Councilwoman Breshko — simply walked into the fog and did not return. Her letter of resignation was found on her desk after the fact. It read, in its entirety: "I'd rather."

I respect the brevity.

The Directorship was offered first to Deputy Mayor Kozlov, who declined, citing a heart condition. Then to the head of Public Health, Dr. Aminova, who declined, citing ethical concerns. Then to the chief of Emergency Services, Colonel Petrosyan, who declined, citing — and I record this with the fidelity his exact words deserve — "not a chance in frozen hell." It was offered to the head of the Department of Sanitation, the registrar of the Office of Vital Statistics, the assistant deputy comptroller, the chief librarian, and a janitor named Musa who happened to be mopping the corridor outside the council chamber and was briefly mistaken for a candidate. Musa also declined.

The position was then offered, by a chain of delegation so convoluted that I have diagrammed it in Appendix E and still cannot identify the moment of decision, to Viktor Igorovich Galkin.

My officemate. Grade III Zoning Clerk. One hundred and forty centimeters from my desk. Soup on his tie — mushroom, that morning, a thick bisque his mother makes with too much cream, and I know it was too much cream because I could smell it from my desk, which is, as I have established, too close. Lazy eye drifting left. Sweat already darkening his collar though it was not yet nine.

Galkin was not selected for competence. He was selected because he was in the room when the chain of refusal reached the bottom of the organizational chart, and because he did not refuse quickly enough. I have reviewed the meeting minutes. There is a pause noted in the transcript — [SILENCE, APPROX. 4 SEC.] — between the moment the acting council president said "Galkin, Viktor Igorovich, you are hereby—" and the moment Galkin responded. In that four-second silence, the entire surviving government apparatus apparently interpreted his failure to immediately say "no" as consent.

He consented.

I want the reader to understand who consented. I want the reader to see him as I see him from one hundred and forty centimeters: the patches of razor burn along his jaw where he shaves in inconsistent directions. The way his left eye floats outward when he is nervous, so that he appears to be simultaneously facing you and fleeing from you. The stammer that surfaces whenever he is required to speak to more than two people at once, which transforms the simplest declarative sentence into a navigational ordeal — words colliding, reversing, piling up behind his teeth like commuters at a blocked turnstile. The way he holds a pen too tightly, leaving grooves in his middle finger that I can see from my desk, which is too close. The way he tucks papers into folders with a strange tenderness, aligning the edges as if tucking in a child. The persistent, unnameable odor — not unpleasant, exactly, but present, inescapable at one hundred and forty centimeters, a smell like warm bread and anxiety, like a body that has never fully learned what to do with itself.

I catalog these details because they are relevant to the absurdity of his appointment. A man who cannot coordinate his own razor is now responsible for coordinating the erotic output of eight hundred thousand surviving residents. This is the kind of administrative outcome that makes one question the fundamental architecture of institutional decision-making. I note it. I file it.
