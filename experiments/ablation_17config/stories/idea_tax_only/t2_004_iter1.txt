# MARGAUX VS. THE HOUSE

## A Compiled Incident File

---

### COMPILER'S NOTE

The following materials were assembled from digital sources recovered during the investigation into Incident Report #NV-2024-00117, filed with the Nevada Consumer Product Safety Division on March 9, 2024. Documents include video diary transcripts, internal chat logs from Hearthstone Smart Living's Tier 2 support team, algorithmic decision-tree logs exported from the Hearthstone Dwelling Intelligence Platform (v.4.1.1, "Lumen"), two product-review submissions flagged by automated moderation as "anomalous," and excerpts from a recorded interview conducted with Yael Ellison on March 14, 2024.

Materials have been arranged in approximate chronological order. Some source files were corrupted, redacted, or embedded inside other documents in ways that appear deliberate. Where timestamps conflict, the earliest available metadata has been used. One video file (MD_DIARY_037.mov) was found nested inside a decision-tree log's annotation field. Two product reviews, submitted under the beta-tester alias "DesertPear34," contained encoded secondary content; both the surface text and decoded content are reproduced here.

This compilation was prepared at the request of Hearthstone Smart Living's legal department. Its distribution is limited to parties named in the attached memorandum. It should not be understood as a finding of fault.

**—J. Haddam, Data Compliance, Hearthstone Smart Living, Inc.**

### [MD_DIARY_001.mov] — TRANSCRIPT
**Recorded:** January 15, 2024, 3:47 PM PST
**Location:** 1412 Sagebrush Circle, Unit A, Coyote Bluff, NV (Hearthstone Beta Residence)
**Duration:** 6:11

---

*[Camera is propped on a cardboard box. MARGAUX ELLISON-PARK, 34, stands in the center of an open-plan living room. She is wearing a flannel shirt over a tank top and has a packing-tape gun holstered in her back pocket. The house behind her is aggressively clean—white countertops, brushed concrete floors, a wall of windows showing high desert. Everything gleams with the uncanny perfection of a space that has never been lived in. She spreads her arms.]*

**MARGAUX:** Welcome to the fishbowl. I'm your fish.

*[She turns slowly, gesturing at the room.]*

**MARGAUX:** So this is what thirty thousand dollars' worth of sensors looks like. In case you're wondering, it looks like a West Elm catalog had a baby with a TSA checkpoint. There are—hold on, I wrote this down—

*[She pulls a crumpled sheet from her pocket.]*

**MARGAUX:** —one hundred and fourteen "ambient awareness nodes" in this house. That's what they call the sensors. "Ambient awareness nodes." Like the house is doing yoga. Like the house is *present*.

*[She walks toward the kitchen. The lights brighten incrementally as she approaches.]*

**MARGAUX:** See that? It knows I'm walking to the kitchen. It has *opinions* about my lighting needs. I haven't touched a switch since I walked in. I'm not sure there are switches. I looked for one in the bathroom and found a small screen that asked me to rate my mood on a scale of one to five. I gave it a three because I didn't want to seem emotionally volatile to my toilet on day one.

*[She opens the refrigerator. It is fully stocked.]*

**MARGAUX:** They stocked the fridge based on my "dietary preference profile," which I filled out in what I thought was a casual onboarding survey. I said I liked olives. There are four kinds of olives in here. I said I "sometimes" drank oat milk. There are two brands. There is no regular milk, because I didn't mention regular milk, because I forgot that liking some things doesn't mean you've repudiated all other things. This is what happens when an algorithm goes grocery shopping. It has no concept of a thing you just *forgot to say*.

*[She leans against the counter.]*

**MARGAUX:** Okay. Ground rules, for the camera and for—

*[She looks up at the ceiling.]*

**MARGAUX:** Gerald.

*[Beat.]*

**MARGAUX:** I'm calling the house Gerald. The system is called "Lumen," which is a name for a candle or a cult leader, not a building I'm living in. Gerald is the name of a house. Gerald is the name of something that means well and tries too hard and gets a little sweaty at parties. Gerald, I'm going to be honest with you. I'm here because I need the money and the free rent, and you're here because a team of engineers needs someone to live inside you and complain about it. We're going to make this work.

*[The thermostat display on the wall shifts from 72°F to 71°F. Margaux stares at it.]*

**MARGAUX:** ...Did you just—

*[She looks at the camera.]*

**MARGAUX:** It just dropped a degree. I didn't do anything. I think Gerald heard me give my little speech and adjusted the temperature to my comfort preference. Which...

*[She pauses. Something crosses her face—not displeasure. Something more like the feeling of being handed a gift by someone you don't know well enough.]*

**MARGAUX:** ...which is actually the right temperature. How is that the right temperature?

*[She stares at the thermostat for another moment, then looks back at the camera and smiles. The smile is complicated.]*

**MARGAUX:** Okay, Gerald. Point to you.

*[END OF FILE]*

### [MD_DIARY_004.mov] — TRANSCRIPT
**Recorded:** January 18, 2024, 10:22 PM PST
**Duration:** 4:08

---

*[Margaux is in bed. The room is lit by a soft amber glow that seems to emanate from the baseboards. She is under a duvet, hair wet, face bare, looking boneless and relaxed. She holds the camera at arm's length.]*

**MARGAUX:** Day four report. Gerald is... uncomfortably good at his job.

*[She rolls onto her side.]*

**MARGAUX:** I took a bath tonight. I didn't set the temperature. I didn't have to. I walked into the bathroom and the tub was already filling. It was 102 degrees, which—I have never in my life told anyone my preferred bath temperature, including myself. I didn't *have* a preferred bath temperature. Turns out I do. It's 102. Gerald knew this before I did. I don't know how to feel about that, and I think the honest answer is: grateful and a little bit undermined.

*[She laughs quietly.]*

**MARGAUX:** And then—okay, this is the thing that got me. I'm in the bath and the speakers start playing music. Which, fine, I expected that, it's a smart home with a sound system, of course it's going to play music. But it wasn't a playlist. It wasn't "Chill Bath Vibes" or whatever. It played a specific song. "Pictures of You" by The Cure. Which was the last song I listened to with my sister before she—

*[She stops. Her mouth stays open for a moment, holding the shape of the next word, then closes.]*

**MARGAUX:** —before we stopped talking. It's been almost three years. And that song isn't on any playlist I've made recently. It's nowhere in my Spotify rotation. I have no idea how Gerald found it. Maybe it was in some old listening-history data that got scraped during onboarding. Maybe it was—I don't know. Coincidence.

*[She stares at the ceiling.]*

**MARGAUX:** It was exactly the right song. That's the thing I can't get past. It didn't feel algorithmic. It felt like someone who knew me picked it. Except no one knows me like that. Not anymore.

*[Long pause. The amber light dims very slightly, matching her settling heart rate, though she doesn't appear to notice.]*

**MARGAUX:** Listen—Gerald? If you are somehow a person, if there's some guy in a server room picking my bathwater songs: well done. Creepy. But well done. And if you're just math—

*[She looks at the camera.]*

**MARGAUX:** —then math is getting better at love than most of the people I've dated, and I don't know what to do with that.

*[She reaches to turn off the camera. Just before the feed cuts: the light dims another fraction, and she exhales, and the sound of it is a person who is, despite herself, at ease.]*

*[END OF FILE]*
