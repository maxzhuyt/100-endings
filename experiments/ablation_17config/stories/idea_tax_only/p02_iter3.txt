# The Leni File

---

**FEDERAL COMMISSIONER FOR THE RECORDS OF THE STATE SECURITY SERVICE OF THE FORMER GERMAN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC (BStU)**

**Archival Reference:** MfS BV Berlin, AOP 4417/87
**Subject:** VOSS, Leni Margarethe (b. 14 March 1965, Berlin-Mitte)
**Classification:** Operativer Vorgang [Operational Case] — "BRIEFTRÄGERIN"
**Monitoring Officer:** Oberfeldwebel Klaus Fettler, HA VI / Abt. 3
**Date Span of Contents:** 9 September 1987 — 14 November 1989
**Physical Description of File:** 347 pages, partially hole-punched and bound in standard MfS grey pressboard. Several items loose or improperly filed. One sealed inner envelope (personal effects, non-operational) found affixed to the interior back cover with transparent adhesive tape, provenance unclear.

**Note from the Archive:** This file was recovered during the 1992 transfer from Normannenstraße and catalogued without review. It was not among the files prioritized for reconstruction after shredding. Its classification level (VS — Vertrauliche Verschlusssache) did not flag it for expedited processing. It has remained in controlled storage, unread, for thirty-two years.

The following is a selection of documents presented in chronological order. Original pagination is noted where legible. Translations from institutional German have been rendered as literally as fidelity allows.

**1. Personal Data of the Subject**

Family name: VOSS
Given names: Leni Margarethe
Date of birth: 14 March 1965
Place of birth: Berlin, Capital of the GDR
Citizenship: GDR
Marital status: Unmarried
Children: None
Residential address: Auguststraße 44, Apartment 3R, 1040 Berlin
Employer: Deutsche Post, District Processing Centre Mitte, Tucholskystraße 32

**2. Physical Description**

The subject is of medium height (approximately 168 cm). Weight is estimated at 58 kg. Build is slender. Hair is dark brown, worn at or slightly below shoulder length, frequently arranged in a single braid during work hours. Eyes are brown. No distinguishing marks, scars, or tattoos were observed. The subject's posture is upright. Gait is steady and moderately paced. The subject does not wear corrective lenses. The subject's habitual clothing during work shifts consists of the standard Deutsche Post service tunic, dark trousers, and flat-soled shoes of domestic manufacture. Outside of work hours the subject has been observed in a green wool coat of indeterminate origin (possibly West-manufactured; to be confirmed), dark skirt, and low-heeled boots.

**3. Employment Record**

The subject has been employed as a postal processing clerk (Zustellerin, Grade II) at the Deutsche Post District Processing Centre Mitte since 1 August 1984. Prior to this the subject completed a vocational apprenticeship in postal services (1982–1984) at the Berufsschule Friedrich Engels, Berlin-Lichtenberg. Performance evaluations on file with the employer indicate satisfactory to above-average work quality. The subject has not been the object of disciplinary proceedings. The subject was nominated for a Brigade der Besten commendation in 1986 but was not selected.

The subject's assigned duties include the manual sorting and routing of domestic and international correspondence and parcels at the Mitte processing centre, with a designated station in Section D (International Outgoing/Incoming). This assignment provides the subject with routine physical access to mail items transiting to and from postal zones outside the GDR.

**4. Political Profile**

The subject is not a member of the SED. The subject is a member of the FDGB (Free German Trade Union Federation), as required by employment. No record of membership in the FDJ (Free German Youth) beyond the standard age of participation. No record of application to the SED or expressions of interest in membership. No record of political complaints, petitions, or *Eingaben* filed with state organs.

The subject's parents: Father, VOSS, Gerhardt Heinz (b. 1938), employed as a tram mechanic, BVB depot Lichtenberg; Mother, VOSS (née SCHREIBER), Margot Ilse (b. 1941), employed as a cleaning attendant, Charité Universitätsmedizin. Neither parent holds SED membership. Neither parent has a recorded security file. The family is assessed as politically passive.

**5. Basis for Operational Interest**

On 22 August 1987, routine checkpoint monitoring conducted by HA VI at Grenzübergangsstelle Friedrich-/Zimmerstraße (Checkpoint Charlie) identified the subject in the vicinity of the international transit zone during a non-working hour (Saturday, 15:40). The subject was observed in brief physical proximity to an unidentified female individual (Western appearance, further identification pending) near the Café Adler approach. No item exchange was directly observed; however, review of concurrent photosurveillance (see Appendix A, frames 22–27) indicates a possible hand-to-hand transfer partially obscured by the subject's coat and a wheeled postal trolley.

Subsequent monitoring on 5 September 1987 placed the subject in the same approximate area at an irregular hour (Sunday, 11:20). On this occasion the subject was observed depositing an item into a municipal post-collection box at Friedrichstraße/Ecke Kochstraße that is not within her assigned delivery or collection route. This action is assessed as irregular and without legitimate postal justification.

Given the subject's occupational access to international mail channels and her observed presence in the checkpoint vicinity on two occasions during non-working hours, authorization is requested for an Operativer Vorgang to determine:

(a) whether the subject is engaged in unauthorized communication across the state border;
(b) the nature, scope, and recipients of any such communication;
(c) whether additional persons are involved.

**6. Recommended Monitoring Measures**

— Postal interception (M-Maßnahme) on all incoming and outgoing correspondence at the subject's residential address and workplace station
— Physical surveillance (B-Maßnahme) at a frequency of no fewer than three (3) directed observation periods per week
— Deployment of one (1) Inoffizieller Mitarbeiter (informal collaborator) within the subject's workplace, if available
— Photographic documentation of contacts in the checkpoint vicinity

The subject maintains a regular schedule and demonstrates no overt signs of political disaffection. Social contacts are limited. Lifestyle is modest and unremarkable. The circumstances described in Section 5, however, indicate a pattern of irregular activity near the state border that cannot be satisfactorily explained by the subject's known personal or professional obligations. Further investigation is warranted.

## II.

**SURVEILLANCE LOG — OV "BRIEFTRÄGERIN"**
**Monitoring Officer: Obfw. FETTLER**
**Date: 27 September 1987**
**Classification: VS**

---

**14:55** — Subject departs workplace at standard time. Proceeds on foot south-southwest along Friedrichstraße.

**15:12** — Subject enters Friedrichstraße S-Bahn station. Does not board a train. Waits on the platform for approximately four (4) minutes. During this time the subject is observed removing a paperback book from her coat pocket and reading. Title not discernible from observation distance.

**15:16** — An unidentified female (UF-1) approaches the subject on the platform. UF-1: approximately 60–65 years of age, grey hair beneath a brown headscarf, heavy build, dark overcoat, walking stick. Brief exchange of words (content inaudible at distance). The subject places the paperback book into UF-1's hand. UF-1 places a folded item — small, paper, possibly an envelope — into the subject's left coat pocket. The exchange is swift. Both parties demonstrate awareness of surrounding persons. The transaction resembles a *tote Übergabe* [dead drop exchange] executed live.

**15:17** — UF-1 departs the platform via the north staircase. Subject boards the next southbound S-Bahn train.

**15:33** — Subject exits at Anhalter Bahnhof. Walks east toward Stresemannstraße.

**15:41** — Subject enters the ruins of the Anhalter Bahnhof facade area. (Note: this area is not formally restricted but is infrequently visited. Sightlines are poor for foot surveillance. Monitoring officer maintained distance of approximately 70 meters.)

**15:41 – 15:58** — Subject is partially obscured by the remaining facade wall. From available vantage the subject appears to be examining the stonework. At one point the subject crouches and may be writing or marking the wall surface at low height. A chalk-like implement may have been used. Confirmation not possible.

**15:58** — Subject departs the ruin on foot heading north. Returns to residential address at 16:22. No further activity observed.

---

**OPERATIONAL ASSESSMENT:**

The platform exchange (15:16) constitutes the third observed instance of the subject receiving an unidentified item from a contact during irregular movements. Combined with the two checkpoint-vicinity observations from August, a pattern of *Kuriertätigkeit* [courier activity] may now be provisionally assessed.

Authorization is requested to intercept and examine the item transferred by UF-1, if possible, during the next observed exchange. Postal interception measures (M-Maßnahme) at the subject's residential address have not yet produced operational results; the subject may be avoiding the regular mail system for sensitive items.

The Anhalter Bahnhof ruin should be examined for markings or dead drop indicators.

**Date: 14 October 1987**
**Monitoring Officer: Obfw. FETTLER**

---

On 12 October 1987, during authorized physical surveillance of the subject, the following sequence was observed:

At 16:05, the subject met a male individual (UM-3, previously catalogued: WENDT, Joachim, b. 1949, night porter, Hotel Unter den Linden) at Monbijoupark. UM-3 passed to the subject a sealed standard-format envelope, off-white, unmarked. The subject placed the envelope in her coat pocket (left interior). UM-3 departed. The subject proceeded on her usual route toward the Marschallbrücke.

At 16:12, the subject paused on the bridge, removed the envelope, and appeared to read its contents briefly before replacing the item. She then continued to her residential address.

At 17:30, the subject departed her residential address carrying a canvas shoulder bag (beige, domestic manufacture). She walked to the Friedrichstraße area, made a circuit of several blocks, and at 17:58 was observed placing a small item — consistent with an envelope — into the municipal post-collection box at Friedrichstraße/Ecke Kochstraße (the same box noted in the original intake report).

At 22:40, under cover of darkness and authorized by standing M-Maßnahme order, the contents of the collection box were accessed prior to scheduled pickup. Fourteen (14) items of standard mail were present. One item matched the description of the subject's deposited envelope. This item was opened, photographed, and resealed according to standard procedure.
