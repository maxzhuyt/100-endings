# The Box of Evidence

---

## I.

What survives of Dr. Celeste Yoon's practice at 1414 East Camelback Road, Suite C — between Lux Nails & Spa and Cloud9 Vapor Lounge — fits in a single banker's box. The box was submitted to the Arizona Medical Board on March 14th as part of Case No. 2024-AMB-00671, and it is from this box that the following account has been assembled. Everything here is a document. Nothing here is fiction. Whether anything here is true is a question the Board, in its final ruling, declined to address.

The box contains: forty-seven medical journal entries written in a leather-bound Moleskine, undated but sequential; nineteen unsent letters on unlined paper, folded into thirds; eleven voice-to-text transcripts recovered from a Samsung Galaxy A14; nine insurance claim denials from Concordia Preferred Health Partners; six incident reports filed with the Tonto National Forest Service, Payson District; four cease-and-desist letters from the Arizona Department of Health Services; one lease-default notice from Camelback Commercial Properties LLC; and a BPA-free water bottle containing fourteen dead honeybees in what appears to be a solution of glycerin and mesquite honey.

We will begin with the denials.

---

**CONCORDIA PREFERRED HEALTH PARTNERS**
**Explanation of Benefits — Claim #CPH-2023-88712**

**Member:** Gustafson, Dale R.
**Provider:** Yoon, Celeste M., MD — Allergy & Immunology
**Date of Service:** 06/14/2023
**Procedure Code:** 95165 (Antigen Therapy — Professional Services)
**Amount Billed:** $310.00
**Amount Approved:** $0.00
**Reason for Denial:** Procedure not deemed medically necessary. Subcutaneous immunotherapy protocol exceeds standard schedule for allergen class. Peer review notes: *Provider documentation describes "custom titration protocol responsive to patient's idiosyncratic histamine narrative." Terminology not recognized. Recommend re-submission with standard clinical language.*

---

The same day — it must have been the same evening, because the handwriting shifts from the careful print of Celeste's clinical notes to the looser cursive she used after wine — the Moleskine records the following:

---

*June 14. Dale Gustafson, 60, roofer, Scottsdale. Skin like a topographic map — the welts from his prick test rose in mountain ranges. Dust mite, Bermuda grass, cat dander, mesquite, the entire Sonoran atmosphere conspiring against his respiratory tract for six decades and he has simply endured it, breathing through a narrowing aperture, because he was told by a previous physician (I use the term loosely) that "everybody sneezes." I held the entire immune system of a sixty-year-old roofer in my hands today. I held it in the form of forty-three tiny punctures across his forearms, each one a question posed to his body, and his body answered every single one. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. I am allergic to the world. No one before me had bothered to listen to the full testimony. Concordia will deny the claim. Concordia will always deny the claim. Concordia is the church that tells the mystic her visions are indigestion.*

---

The Moleskine does not record whether Dale Gustafson's histamine narrative was, in any clinical sense, a real concept. It does not record what Dale Gustafson himself thought of the analogy. What survives of Dale Gustafson in the box of evidence is the denied claim form, the journal entry, and—tucked between the Moleskine's pages at this point, as though used as a bookmark—a sticky note in a different hand, blocky and male, that reads: *Doc, the shots work. I can breathe through my nose for the first time since Carter was president. Do I owe you more money? —Dale G.*

This is one of only two documents in the box written by a patient. The other, which we will come to, is a Google review.

---

The waiting room of Suite C held four chairs, mauve, bolted together in a unit designed for airport gates. The landlord's inspection report from January 2023 notes "acceptable condition, minor water damage to ceiling tile above reception." Celeste's journal entry from the same month describes the water stain as "a Rorschach blot I have chosen to interpret as a butterfly, because the alternative — a collapsing infrastructure — is too on the nose, even for me."

She was funny. It should be said early, because the documents sometimes obscure it, and because what happened later can make it difficult to remember that she was, before anything else, funny. The journal performs many operations — self-mythologizing, clinical reasoning, existential complaint — but its dominant mode, on a line-by-line basis, is comedy. Celeste Yoon knew exactly how absurd it was to practice immunology in a strip mall between acrylic nails and mango-flavored nicotine. The comedy was not a failure of self-awareness. It was the specific shape her self-awareness took.

Whether that distinction matters — to a licensing board, to an insurance auditor, to the people who eventually came to her and placed their bodies in her care — is what the box of evidence does not resolve.

---

Three more denial letters follow, dated July through September 2023. The procedures grow more creative; the denial language grows more bewildered. Claim #CPH-2023-91447 is for "environmental immune recalibration — comprehensive" and is denied with the note: *No CPT code found. Provider encouraged to contact Concordia coding department for guidance.* Claim #CPH-2023-93008 is for standard allergy testing but is denied on the grounds that the provider's license address does not match the service location, a discrepancy that arose because Celeste had, for a period of several weeks in August, been seeing patients in her car in the parking lot of Suite C after the air conditioning failed and she could not afford repair. Her journal entry about this period is five pages long and contains the phrase "medicine originated in fields AND I WILL NOT FORGET THAT even if the fields in question are a parking lot adjacent to a Discount Tire."

The last claim denial in the strip-mall sequence is dated October 2, 2023. It is for a patient identified only by initials — R.K. — and the procedure is listed as "allergen provocation challenge, supervised, extended." The amount billed is $0.00. Celeste had stopped charging. The denial is for a claim she apparently filed not for payment but for the record — so that someone, someday, opening the file, would see that she had treated R.K. for a severe allergy to latex, that the treatment had required nine hours of supervised exposure, that R.K. was a single mother who worked in a hospital laundry room and could not avoid latex without losing her job, and that no one — not Concordia, not the hospital, not R.K.'s previous allergist who had recommended "avoidance strategies" as though a woman could avoid her own livelihood — no one had tried.

The claim was denied. Reason: *Provider is not a participating member of the Concordia network.*

Celeste had been removed from the network two weeks earlier for "documentation irregularities."

Her journal entry that night is short:

*October 2. R.K. can go back to work. I cannot.*

---

## II.

**CAMELBACK COMMERCIAL PROPERTIES LLC**
**Notice of Lease Default — Suite C, 1414 E. Camelback Rd.**

*Dear Dr. Yoon: This letter serves as formal notice that payment for the months of September and October 2023 has not been received. Per Section 7(b) of your lease agreement, you are hereby given thirty (30) days to remit all outstanding balances or vacate the premises . . .*

---

*October 8. I have decided to leave. Not "leave" in the way the lease default implies — not slinking, not failing, not crawling back to a system that measures a physician's worth by her claims-per-hour ratio. I am emerging. The word is precise. A butterfly does not "vacate" the chrysalis due to lease default. It emerges because the old structure can no longer contain the organism it has become. I have been reading about the Sonoran Desert. Did you know that Larrea tridentata — the creosote bush — is functionally immortal? Its root system can survive eleven thousand years. Eleven thousand years of immune response to a hostile environment, and it does not move, does not adapt, does not compromise. It simply persists. I will learn from the creosote bush. I have ordered a yurt.*

---

The yurt was a 24-foot Pacific Yurt, model "Eagle," purchased on a credit card that was, at the time of purchase, $4,200 from its limit. The shipping confirmation is in the box. So is a printout from a website called DesertHomesteadForum.com, on which a user named CelesteImmuno posted the following question on October 10:

*Subject: Water filtration for off-grid medical practice?*
*Hi all, new member here. I'm relocating a small medical practice to an off-grid site in the Sonoran Desert (Tonto NF area, east of Payson). I need to maintain sterile conditions for allergen preparation and storage, including reliable refrigeration for epinephrine and serum. Has anyone set up a solar-powered medical-grade mini-fridge? Also: any experience with composting toilets in a clinical setting? I know how that sounds. I'm asking anyway.*

She received three replies. One was from a man who lived in a converted school bus and recommended a specific brand of solar panel. One was from a woman who warned her about scorpions in composting toilets. The third said simply: *Are you licensed to practice medicine in a yurt?*

Celeste did not respond to the third reply.

---

The drive from Phoenix to the site she'd chosen — six miles east of Payson, on a dirt road that terminated in a wash, technically within the buffer zone of federal land but on a parcel whose ownership was, as a subsequent title search would reveal, ambiguously documented — the drive took three hours. She dictated into her phone the entire way.

The voice-to-text transcripts from this drive are the first in the box, and they establish a pattern that will recur: the software's garbled interpretations running beneath Celeste's intended words like a subconscious, translating her certainty into something stranger.

---

**Voice-to-Text Transcript — 10/15/2023, 2:47 PM**

*I am heading east on the 87 toward Payson and I want to record my thoughts because this is the beginning and beginnings* [unintelligible] *should be documented. The air changed twenty minutes ago. You can feel it. The desert isn't the absence of something it's the* [pause] *presence of immune oh therapy at its most fundamental. The body encounters the world. The world is hostile. The body learns. That's all I've ever done is help bodies learn and if the institution won't let me do it in a strip mall then I will do it where the creosote bush has been doing it for eleven thousand years. So no ran* [Sonoran?] *desert. I'm coming.*

*Note: my phone has decided that "immunotherapy" is "immune oh therapy" and "Sonoran" is "so no ran" and I think there's something in that. Immune, oh, therapy. Like a sigh. Like the body saying oh. Like the body saying I didn't know there was another way to respond. So no ran. Nobody ran. I'm not running. I'm* [unintelligible, wind noise] *arriving.*

---

The narrator — this narrator, the one arranging the documents — pauses here to note that Celeste recorded herself noting the transcription errors. She was, even in the moment of departure, composing the story of her departure. The errors were instantly absorbed into the mythology. The software's failure became evidence of the software's inadvertent wisdom. This is what she did. This is what she could not stop doing.

It is also, in fairness, genuinely charming. *So no ran.* Nobody ran. It is difficult, reading the transcript in the banker's box under fluorescent board-hearing light, not to smile. It is difficult, knowing what comes next, to stop smiling, because the smile has already formed and there is no document in the box that quite undoes it. The Board's final ruling, seventeen pages of administrative prose, does not address whether Celeste Yoon was charming. It did not need to. Everyone who testified already knew.

---

*October 18. The yurt is up. It took two days, one dislocated thumb (mine, left, relocated without difficulty — I am still a physician), and the assistance of a man named Carl who was camping illegally in the wash and who asked no questions, which I respected, and who accepted payment in the form of a case of Modelo and an expired EpiPen, which I did not ask him to explain, which he respected.*

*The light in here. I need to record this. The light in the yurt comes through the canvas and it is not the light of a room. It is the light of a body. Diffuse. Warm. The color of the inside of a wrist held up to the sun. I have hung a partition — canvas, not drywall, because drywall is the architecture of defeat — and on one side is the clinical space: exam table (portable, purchased from a veterinary supply company, this is fine, the human body is also an animal), mini-fridge (solar, as recommended by the school-bus man, running at a steady 38°F, God bless him), and my supplies. On the other side: a cot, a camp stove, the Moleskine, a copy of Janeway's Immunobiology, eleventh edition, which is the only textbook I have ever loved.*

*The creosote bushes are outside. I went out at dusk and stood among them and breathed. The smell is rain. Not actual rain — the scent the creosote releases in humidity, which is the scent of a chemical called nordihydroguaiaretic acid, which is the plant's immune response to moisture, which is to say: the desert smells like an organism defending itself, and I am home.*

---

The lease-default notice and the journal entry are dated three days apart. One says she couldn't make rent. The other says she was called to the wilderness. Both are in the box. The box does not reconcile them. That work—the work of deciding which document tells the truth—has been left, apparently, to whoever opens the box. To the Board. To us.

The narrator, who has opened the box, who has read every page, who knows how the yurt ends and what was found inside it and what was not found and what Gil's hands looked like when he sealed this box with packing tape and wrote on the side in black marker *YOON — EVIDENCE — DO NOT DISCARD* — the narrator declines to reconcile them either.

She could not make rent. She was called to the wilderness. Next document.

---

## III.

The first impossible patient arrived on November 3, 2023.

She is identified in Celeste's records as M.E., age 34, referred by no one, arriving — as the journal entry notes with undisguised wonder — "as though the desert itself had sent her."

---

*November 3. M.E. presented today with a complaint I have never encountered in twelve years of practice, four years of fellowship, or 1,400 pages of Janeway's. She is allergic to her own tears.*

*I will be precise, because precision is the only defense against the temptation to allegorize, and the temptation, with this patient, is enormous: M.E. exhibits a consistent, reproducible anaphylactoid response to lacrimal fluid. Specifically, the contact of tears with the periorbital skin triggers acute angioedema — swelling of the eyelids, cheeks, and upper lip — within ninety seconds of lacrimation onset. The response is dose-dependent: tearing from a yawn produces mild edema; emotional crying triggers swelling severe enough to close the airway. She carries an EpiPen. She has used it eleven times in two years. She has not cried — fully, freely, with the abandon that crying requires — in those two years, because the last time she did, at her mother's funeral, her throat closed and she was intubated in the ambulance.*
