## Compiled Materials from the Personal Effects of Janet Leigh Morrow (1951–?), Court Reporter, 37th Judicial District, Sutton County, Texas

### Recovered from Ms. Morrow's residence, November 14, 1988, by Sutton County Sheriff's Department following welfare check requested by her sister, Colleen Morrow-Bradley of Lubbock, TX. Catalogued as found. Archival sequence maintained per original filing order.

## ITEM 1: CASSETTE TAPE, MAXELL UR-90, LABELED "J.M. — PERSONAL LOG #1 — MAR 3 1987"

*[Transcription by Colleen Morrow-Bradley, 1991]*

&nbsp;

[Sound of ice in a glass. Chair settling.]

Testing, testing. Okay.

So this is me starting something I probably shouldn't bother starting, but Dr. Keene says I need an "outlet for processing" since I won't talk to him about work. Fine. Here's my outlet. A tape recorder from RadioShack and a glass of Chablis.

My name is Janet Morrow. I am thirty-six years old. I am a certified court reporter for the 37th Judicial District, which covers Sutton, Schleicher, and Menard Counties, and I have been in that position for nine years. Before that I worked in Bexar County under Judge Alderman. Before that I was a legal secretary. Before that I was a girl who could type ninety words a minute and thought that meant something.

[Pause.]

Tomorrow we begin *State of Texas v. Eleanor Ruth Vance*. Three counts of capital murder. Three children. Foster children in her care: David Oleander Bell, age nine. Maria Theresa Campos, age seven. Lily Anne Pace, age four.

Four years old.

I've sat through eleven murder trials. I sat through the Greenwald case in '82, which was the dismemberment, and I did not flinch. I am a professional. I record what is said. I do not editorialize. I do not react. Allen — that's Judge Allen Cooperman — he once told me I had the best courtroom composure he'd ever seen in a reporter, and I was proud of that. I'm still proud of that.

But I'll admit something to this tape that I would not admit to Dr. Keene. I've read the pre-trial motions. I know what the prosecution is alleging. Not just murder — *ritual* murder. Satanic ritual abuse. The children were found in a configuration. There are photographs I have not seen and do not want to see.

And the thing that's sitting in my stomach tonight, the thing that made me pour the wine and dig this recorder out of the junk drawer, is not the horror. I've metabolized horror. It's that Eleanor Vance was a social worker for eleven years with commendations. She taught Sunday school at First Methodist. The neighbors on Delano Street describe her as "the kindest woman I ever met" — I'm quoting from the *Sonora Sentinel*, not from sealed documents. And either she did something unspeakable to three children, or something unspeakable was done to her by a system that decided she was a monster because the country has lost its mind about Satan.

I don't know which possibility is worse.

[Long pause. Ice clinking.]

I need to be impartial. I need to record the words as they are spoken. That is my function. The words, and nothing but the words.

Goodnight.

[Click.]

## ITEM 2: OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT — *STATE OF TEXAS V. ELEANOR RUTH VANCE*, CASE NO. 87-0114-CR, 37TH JUDICIAL DISTRICT COURT, SUTTON COUNTY, TEXAS

### Day 1 — March 4, 1987 — Morning Session

&nbsp;

THE COURT: This court is now in session. The Honorable Allen J. Cooperman presiding. We are here in the matter of *The State of Texas versus Eleanor Ruth Vance*, Case Number 87-0114-CR. Will the parties state their appearances for the record?

MR. HARKER: Russell Harker for the State, Your Honor. With me at counsel table is Assistant District Attorney Patricia Nolan.

MR. DE LEON: Miguel De Leon, court-appointed counsel for Ms. Vance, Your Honor.

THE COURT: Thank you. Is the State ready to proceed?

MR. HARKER: The State is ready, Your Honor.

THE COURT: And the defense?

MR. DE LEON: We are, Your Honor.

THE COURT: Very well. Mr. Harker, you may make your opening statement.

MR. HARKER: Thank you, Your Honor. Ladies and gentlemen of the jury, the evidence in this case will show that on the night of October the seventeenth, 1986, Eleanor Ruth Vance, the defendant, did willfully and with premeditation cause the deaths of three children in her legal custody as a licensed foster care provider in Sutton County. The evidence will show that she did so in a manner consistent with ritualized practice. You will hear testimony from Detective Arnold Pike of the Sutton County Sheriff's Department, who was the first officer to enter the Vance residence at 1412 Delano Street at approximately 11:40 p.m. on the night in question. You will hear his description of what he found.

[Pause.]

Ladies and gentlemen, I want to prepare you. What Detective Pike found — what you will see in the photographic evidence — is deeply disturbing. It is meant to be deeply disturbing. Because what Eleanor Vance did to David Bell, Maria Campos, and Lily Pace was not the act of a woman who lost her temper, or who suffered some momentary psychotic break. The State will prove that it was planned. That it was practiced. That there was, in fact, a *rehearsal* — a previous incident involving animal remains discovered in the Vance garage by a meter reader on September the fifth that Ms. Vance explained away as evidence of coyote predation.

The State will prove that on October the seventeenth, Eleanor Vance sedated these three children using veterinary-grade ketamine obtained from the Sutton County Large Animal Clinic, where she had volunteered as a bookkeeper. That she arranged their bodies in a triangular configuration in the living room of her home. That she inflicted specific, symmetrical incisions on each child. That she placed objects — candles, a chalice, specific herbs and a substance later identified as animal blood — at measured intervals around and between the bodies. That David, Maria, and Lily died from a combination of exsanguination and acute ketamine toxicity.

And the State will prove, ladies and gentlemen, that none of this was madness. It was *worship*.

THE COURT: Mr. De Leon, your opening.

MR. DE LEON: Thank you, Your Honor. Ladies and gentlemen, the defense will show that Eleanor Vance was a woman in crisis — a woman failed by the systems she herself had devoted her career to serving. Ms. Vance had been reporting escalating threats from a biological parent of one of these children for months, and those reports were not investigated. The defense will show that the physical evidence in this case is not what the prosecution claims. That the so-called "configuration" was the interpretation of officers primed by the current national hysteria surrounding allegations of satanic ritual abuse — a hysteria that has, in communities across this country, destroyed innocent lives based on the testimony of suggestible children and the projections of frightened adults. There are no suggestible children left to testify here, and that is a tragedy. But the question of *who* is responsible for that tragedy is exactly what this trial must determine, soberly and rationally, without recourse to —

THE DEFENDANT: They were not asleep.

MR. DE LEON: Eleanor —

THE DEFENDANT: You need to know. You all need to know they were not asleep. He doesn't understand what he's saying. He doesn't understand what *asleep* means in this house, what it means when the house goes to sleep, when the —

THE COURT: Ms. Vance —

THE DEFENDANT: — roots go down below the floor, below the foundation, you can hear them, you could *always* hear them if you listened, the roots and the singing, the singing that comes up through the —

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, you will be silent or I will have you removed.

MR. DE LEON: Eleanor, please. Your Honor, I apologize.

THE DEFENDANT: [inaudible]

THE COURT: What was that?

THE DEFENDANT: I said they weren't mine. The hands that did it were mine, but the doing was not mine. I need someone to write that down. I need the woman with the machine to write that down. Is she writing it down? Are you writing this down?

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, everything said in this courtroom is being recorded. Now I am instructing you —

THE DEFENDANT: Not *recorded*. Written. There's a difference. What's written can be read backward. What's recorded only goes one way. I need her to —

MR. DE LEON: Your Honor, may I have a moment with my client?

THE COURT: You may. We will take a ten-minute recess.

[Recess: 9:47 a.m.]

*[Transcription by Colleen Morrow-Bradley, 1991]*

— first day and she's already been warned twice. De Leon is going to lose control of her, I can see it. I've seen defendants like this before, the ones who can't stop talking, who think if they can just find the right words the jury will understand. They never understand. The jury sees crazy. The jury is already writing its verdict in the first five minutes when the defendant starts talking about roots under the floor and singing.

She said something about the difference between recording and writing. That what's written can be read backward. I wrote it down because that's my job. I write down everything.

And I keep thinking about it.

Not — not in a mystical way. I'm not — look, I grew up Church of Christ, and I left that behind when I was twenty, and I have no patience for the Satan panic. None. I've seen what it does. I followed the McMartin case. I know how this works. Therapists with leading questions, children parroting what adults want to hear, a community that needs a villain. I know.

But she looked at me. She looked at me and she said, "the woman with the machine," and her voice changed. It dropped — the pitch dropped. I've been listening to voices in courtrooms for almost a decade. I know what stress sounds like, what deception sounds like, what psychosis sounds like. This was none of those.

She sounded like someone delivering a message she'd been asked to deliver.

[pause]

I'm going to bed. There are twenty-some-odd more days of this. I need to not do this to myself.

[Click.]

## ITEM 4: OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT — DAY 4 — MARCH 9, 1987 — AFTERNOON SESSION

### Direct Examination of Detective Arnold Pike by Mr. Harker

&nbsp;

MR. HARKER: Detective Pike, I'd like to direct your attention to People's Exhibit 14. Can you describe for the jury what this photograph depicts?

DET. PIKE: Yes sir. This is the living room of 1412 Delano Street, the Vance residence, as we found it on the night of October the seventeenth. You can see the three victims positioned roughly equidistant from each other, forming a triangle shape. Head to center in each case. The dark staining on the carpet is blood.

MR. HARKER: And the objects visible between the bodies?

DET. PIKE: Between each pair of victims there was a white taper candle, unlit at the time of our arrival but with evidence of recent burning — wax pooling, shortened wicks. At the center of the triangle, equidistant from all three heads, was a ceramic bowl containing what was later identified as a mixture of dried sage, sulfur powder, and goat's blood.

MR. HARKER: And on the walls, Detective?

DET. PIKE: There were markings on the walls. Drawn in the same blood — goat's blood, the lab confirmed. Symbols.

MR. HARKER: Can you describe the symbols?
