## PART ONE: THE TRIAL

### TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT — DAY 1
### *Commonwealth of Virginia v. Eleanor Ruth Vance*
### Case No. 87-CR-4419
### The Honorable William R. Beckford, Presiding
### September 8, 1987

---

THE COURT: This court is now in session. We are here on the matter of the Commonwealth of Virginia versus Eleanor Ruth Vance. The defendant is charged with three counts of murder in the first degree, one count of conspiracy to commit murder, and one count of desecration of human remains. Ms. Vance, you are present with counsel?

THE DEFENDANT: I am present. Whether counsel can help me is another question.

MR. HARWICK: Your Honor, my client is present and we are prepared to proceed.

THE COURT: Very well. Mr. Garrett, is the Commonwealth prepared?

MR. GARRETT: We are, Your Honor.

THE COURT: Then let us begin with opening statements. Mr. Garrett.

MR. GARRETT: Thank you, Your Honor. Ladies and gentlemen of the jury, over the course of this trial, the Commonwealth will present evidence demonstrating that the defendant, Eleanor Vance, a licensed social worker with the Sutton County Department of Family Services, did willfully and with premeditation cause the deaths of three children in her foster care. Their names were Dillon Marsh, age seven. Abigail Fosse, age nine. And Tyler James Watts, age four.

[Long pause.]

MR. GARRETT: The evidence will show that between March and August of 1986, Ms. Vance, operating under the delusion that these children were — and I want to be precise, because this matters — that these children were, quote, "marked for harvest by the Magistrate of the Lower House," subjected them to ritualistic abuse culminating in their deaths. The Commonwealth's theory is simple. This is not a case about the supernatural. This is a case about what happens when untreated mental illness is given authority over the vulnerable. Ms. Vance believed she was saving these children. She was not. She drowned them. She carved symbols into their bodies postmortem. And she kept detailed journals describing her actions as acts of mercy.

The defense will tell you that Eleanor Vance was sick. That she is sick. They may even try to tell you that what she did was rational within her frame of belief. Do not be deceived. Three children are dead. Someone did it. She confessed. The rest is noise.

THE COURT: Mr. Harwick.

MR. HARWICK: Thank you, Your Honor. Ladies and gentlemen, my client does not deny that three children died. She grieves them. She has grieved them every day since. But the story you just heard — neat, simple, satisfying — leaves out almost everything that matters. It leaves out that Eleanor Vance reported signs of organized abuse involving these children to her supervisors on *four separate occasions* before the first death. It leaves out that those reports disappeared from the Department's files. It leaves out that the so-called confession was obtained during a fourteen-hour interrogation without counsel, during which Ms. Vance was denied medication for a diagnosed seizure disorder. And it leaves out the forensic evidence suggesting that at least two of these children died in a manner physically inconsistent with the actions of a single person.

Eleanor Vance is not a murderer. Eleanor Vance is a scapegoat. And by the end of this trial, you will understand for whom.

*[Harwick was good. Better than I expected for a PD. – M.H.]*

First day of the Vance trial. I'm recording these because Dr. Parris keeps saying I should "process externally" and I keep telling her I don't know what that means, and she keeps saying that's exactly why I should do it. So here I am. Talking to a tape recorder like a crazy person. Processing externally.

[Pause.]

The defendant is — she's not what I expected. The papers have been running that photograph, the booking photo, where she looks like — I don't know. Like a Margaret Keane painting. Huge eyes, no color in her face, just this look of — not fear. More like someone who's already been told the ending and is just waiting for everyone else to catch up. But in person she's — composed. Small woman. Thin wrists. She has this way of sitting perfectly still and then suddenly turning her head to look at something no one else is looking at. I've been doing this eighteen years and you develop an instinct for the fakers. The ones who perform instability. She's not performing.

[Pause.]

Three dead children. God.

[Sound of liquid being poured.]

The gallery was full. This one's attracted the usual crowd. National press in the back two rows. Couple of women from that Believe the Ritual Survivors group, or whatever they're calling themselves now. And then there were — I don't know how to describe them. Four or five men in the gallery I didn't recognize. Not press. Not family. They sat together but didn't speak to each other. Dark suits. One of them was watching me. Not the defendant. Not the attorneys. Me. I kept catching it in my peripheral vision while I was working the machine.

Probably nothing. I'm an idiot. Going to bed.

### TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT — DAY 3
### September 10, 1987

---

DIRECT EXAMINATION OF DR. PAUL KEMMER
BY MR. GARRETT:

Q: Dr. Kemmer, you were the medical examiner who performed the autopsies on all three children, correct?

A: That's correct.

Q: Starting with Dillon Marsh. Can you describe for the jury the cause of death?

A: Dillon Marsh, seven years old, died of asphyxia by drowning. There was freshwater in the lungs, consistent with submersion. Time of death was estimated between 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. on the night of March 14, 1986.

Q: Were there other notable findings?

A: Yes. Postmortem, a series of incisions had been made to the child's torso. They formed a pattern. Geometric, deliberate, clearly done with a thin, sharp instrument. A scalpel or an X-Acto knife.

Q: Were you able to identify the pattern?

A: I'm a pathologist, not a — I noted in my report that the markings appeared symbolic or ritualistic in nature. I subsequently consulted with Dr. Annalise Roth at the University of Virginia's Department of Religious Studies, who identified them as consistent with late-medieval European sigil work. Specifically, she connected them to a tradition of so-called "binding marks" intended to — and I'm quoting her report — "prevent the departure of the soul from the physical vessel."

*[Jury member #4 (Hanson) visibly shaken. Jury member #11 (Oberlin) already checked out. Has been since voir dire, frankly. — M.H.]*

Q: The same markings were found on all three children?

A: Similar but not identical. Each set of markings was unique, but Dr. Roth indicated they appeared to belong to a unified system. Like — she used the analogy of three pages from the same book.

Q: Three pages from the same book. And in your professional opinion, could one person have inflicted these markings alone?

A: The markings themselves, yes. They would require some precision but not extraordinary strength or assistance.

Q: Thank you, Doctor. Now, with regard to the second child, Abigail Fosse—

THE DEFENDANT: He's not telling you about the water.

THE COURT: Ms. Vance—

THE DEFENDANT: The water. Ask him about the water. Ask him where the water *came from.*

MR. HARWICK: Eleanor, please—

THE DEFENDANT: [Speaking rapidly, volume increasing] It was not from a bathtub. It was not from a river. You test it. You test the isotopes. It's not from *here.* The water in their lungs is not from *anywhere in this county* and he KNOWS that, he knows that because—

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, you will be silent or you will be removed. Mr. Harwick, control your client.

MR. HARWICK: I apologize, Your Honor. May I have a moment?

THE COURT: You may.

[Recess — 4 minutes.]

*[I want to note something here. During the outburst, my hands were moving on the machine, transcribing automatically as I'm trained to do. But when I replayed the section to check my work, there's a portion where Vance's speech becomes — I don't know the word. Layered? Like she's saying two things at once. The surface language is English, clearly, just someone shouting about water. But underneath, or woven through — there's a rhythmic quality. Almost metric. I marked the phonemes. It's probably nothing. It's just how agitated speech sounds when you compress it on a stenotype. But I circled the section. — M.H.]*

### TRANSCRIPT EXCERPT — DAY 7
### September 16, 1987

---

DIRECT EXAMINATION OF DETECTIVE RICHARD CALVINO
BY MR. GARRETT:

Q: Detective Calvino, you were the lead investigator on this case for the Sutton County Sheriff's Office?

A: I was.

Q: Can you walk us through how the investigation began?

A: On August 12, 1986, we received a call from a neighbor, Mrs. Patricia Dunn, who lived adjacent to the Vance property on Route 9. Mrs. Dunn reported hearing sounds she described as "chanting or singing" coming from the Vance residence at approximately 3 a.m. She also reported seeing what she described as lights in a pattern in the windows. Blue lights. Not consistent with normal household lighting.

Q: What did you find when you responded?

A: Ms. Vance was in the home. She was alone. She was seated in the kitchen. She was calm. She appeared to have been expecting us. The remains of Tyler James Watts were in the basement.

Q: Can you describe what you observed in the basement?

A: [Pause.] The room had been — prepared. The concrete floor had been covered with a white substance that we later determined was a mixture of chalk powder and salt. On the floor, drawn in what appeared to be charcoal, was a large geometric pattern. A circle with interior lines. Tyler was in the center. He was deceased. He was undressed and had been — the markings had already been made on his body. Postmortem, we later confirmed.

Q: Was there anything else in the room?

A: There were nine glass jars arranged in a semicircle around the body. Each was filled with water. And there was a book.

Q: A book?

A: A handwritten journal. Ms. Vance's journal, which has been entered as Commonwealth Exhibit 14. It contained entries spanning approximately eighteen months.

Q: Did you read the journal?

A: Portions of it, at the scene. I read it in its entirety subsequently. It describes — [pause] — it describes a system. She had names for — entities. There was one she referred to as the Magistrate. And others. She wrote about cycles of what she called "collection" that had to be completed by certain dates or something would — I'm trying to be precise —

Q: Take your time, Detective.

A: She believed that the children had already been "claimed." That the markings she found on them when they first entered her care — I should say, markings she *alleged* were already present, though no medical intake records corroborate this — that these marks indicated they'd been designated for some form of spiritual consumption. And she wrote that the ritual she performed was not murder. It was — she called it "sealing." She believed she was preventing something from taking them.

*[When he said "sealing," Vance reacted. Not the way she reacted on Day 3, not an outburst. She closed her eyes and her lips moved. Harwick didn't notice, or didn't care. I was the only one watching her face because I was recording and my position gives me a direct sightline. She mouthed something. I couldn't read all of it, but I am certain — certain — that one of the words was "scriptoris." — M.H.]*

Q: In your experience, Detective, was there any physical evidence at the scene inconsistent with Ms. Vance having acted alone?

A: [Pause.] The physical evidence at the scene was consistent with a single actor.

*[He paused too long. He paused much too long before that answer. — M.H.]*

### AUDIO DIARY — TAPE 2, SIDE A
### DATED: September 17, 1987

---

[No ambient noise. Close recording, slight tremor in the voice.]

I have to say this out loud so I can hear how insane it sounds.

I took my steno notes from the Day 3 outburst — Vance's outburst about the water — and I isolated every phonetic cluster that deviated from standard English stress patterns. The ones that didn't fit. The ones that sounded layered, or rhythmic, or — wrong. And I wrote them out. And I rearranged them. And I couldn't make them mean anything.

But then I remembered something from a continuing ed seminar I attended in 1983. It was about audio forensics, backward masking in music, all that stuff that was popular then. The satanic messages in rock albums. The lecturer said something I wrote down at the time, which is that the human vocal apparatus can produce phonemes that, when reversed, create coherent patterns in tonal languages. It's rare. It requires specific phonemic conditions. But it's documented.

So I tried reversing the clusters.

[Long pause.]

"Nomen scriptoris intra ossa sedet vas."

[Pause.]

My Latin is — I took two years in high school and I've forgotten almost everything. But I looked it up. Every word.

"The name of the scribe sits in the bones. Vessel."

Or — depending on how you parse it — "The scribe's name sits within the bones of the vessel."

[Sound of breathing.]

It doesn't mean anything. It's pareidolia. It's the same thing as seeing faces in clouds. I took random syllables from an agitated woman's shouting and rearranged and reversed them until they accidentally formed something that looked like Latin. The human brain is a pattern-matching engine. That's all this is.

[Long pause.]

But she mouthed the word "scriptoris" on Day 7. While no one was speaking. While Detective Calvino was talking about something else entirely. And she was looking at me when she did it.

I need to call Dr. Parris.

[End of recording.]
