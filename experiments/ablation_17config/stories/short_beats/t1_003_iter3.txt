# THE COLLECTORS

## I.

The lock has a name. Margo gave it one years ago—*Gerald*—because Gerald was a brand of padlock that hadn't been manufactured since 1987, and because she'd found him at the bottom of a bin at an estate sale in Eugene, and because a thing without a name is a thing that can be thrown away, and nothing can be thrown away.

She works the combination with her left hand while her right hand holds the flashlight between her teeth, tilting it upward so the beam hits the corrugated door of Unit 6-F and splashes back against the hallway ceiling. The Self-Store-It on Burnside Avenue closes at ten. It is eleven forty-seven. Margo has a key to the side gate because the night manager, Derek, has a thing for disaster movies and Margo once brought him a sealed VHS copy of *Dante's Peak* from 1997, shrink-wrap intact, which she found in a box that also contained fourteen matchbooks, a taxidermy sparrow, and a child's shoe. Derek didn't ask why she needed after-hours access. People who manage storage facilities understand that certain tenants keep hours the way the devout keep vigils.

Gerald opens with a click that she feels in her sternum.
