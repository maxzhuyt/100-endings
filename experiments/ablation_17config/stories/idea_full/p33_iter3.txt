# The Greenhouse Archive

## A Dossier Compiled for the Diocese of Coimbra Concerning the Case of Brother Tomás dos Santos, Lay Healer, Resident of the Abbey of São Martinho, 1959–1977

---

**Prepared by:** The Office of the Librarian, Abbey of São Martinho
**Compiler:** Sr.ª Inês Margarida Soares, O.S.B., Librarian and Keeper of Records
**Date of Preparation:** 14 March 1977
**Recipient:** His Excellency the Diocesan Bishop, per formal request dated 9 January 1977 (ref. DC-77/014)

---

### Compiler's Prefatory Note

The Diocese of Coimbra has requested that all records pertaining to the residence, conduct, and pastoral circumstances of Brother Tomás dos Santos be organized, indexed, and transmitted in advance of Brother Tomás's transfer to the Hospice of Our Lady of Fátima, scheduled for the first week of April 1977. The compiler has endeavored to fulfill this request with the thoroughness the matter requires.

The enclosed materials are arranged in chronological order and comprise the following:

1. **Document A** — Intake Questionnaire, completed upon Brother Tomás's admission to the Abbey of São Martinho, dated 17 September 1959. Original ink on standard diocesan form (Formulário D-3, revised 1954).

2. **Document B** — Photographed Marginalia from Selected Texts Annotated by the Subject During His Residence, ca. 1960–1975. Seventeen plates in total, with the compiler's identifications and contextual notes. The source volumes remain in the abbey library, catalogued under their original call numbers.

3. **Document C** — Transcript of the Neurological Examination of Brother Tomás dos Santos, conducted by Dr. Henrique Fonseca, M.D., F.R.C.P., at the request of the Diocese, 14 October 1975. Transcription prepared by the compiler from handwritten clinical notes and verified against the examining physician's summary letter.

4. **Document D** — Observations on the Subject's Condition, November 1975–April 1976, compiled from the librarian's records and supplementary notes.

5. **Document E** — The compiler's final account of the incident of June 1976 and subsequent developments.

The compiler has made every effort to present these materials without interpretive distortion. Where annotation has been necessary for context—botanical identifications, architectural details, the condition of specific texts—the compiler has restricted herself to matters of verifiable fact. Any departure from this principle is unintentional and should be attributed to the difficulty of the material rather than to any lapse in professional judgment.

A note on the condition of the documents: several pages in the latter portion of this dossier bear water damage that the compiler was unable to prevent. The affected passages remain legible in all cases. The compiler regrets any inconvenience.

The compiler further wishes to state, for the record, that this dossier was assembled voluntarily and without duress, and that it represents a complete account of the materials in the librarian's possession.

Sr.ª I. M. Soares, O.S.B.
14 March 1977

## DOCUMENT A

### Intake Questionnaire — Formulário D-3 (Rev. 1954)
### Diocese of Coimbra — Abbey of São Martinho

**Date of admission:** 17 September 1959
**Questionnaire completed by:** Abbot Joaquim Pereira de Almeida

---

**Full name of applicant:** Tomás Manuel dos Santos

**Date of birth:** 3 August 1921

**Place of birth:** Aldeia da Serra, parish of São Lourenço, district of Viseu

**Diocese of origin:** Viseu

**Date of transfer to Diocese of Coimbra:** Not applicable. Brother Tomás presented himself directly to the abbey, bearing a letter of introduction from Padre Gonçalves of São Lourenço (letter filed separately; see parish correspondence, 1959, ref. PC-59/038).

**Marital status:** Unmarried

**Literacy:** Literate. Brother Tomás reads and writes Portuguese with facility. He reports a working knowledge of Latin acquired through self-study and liturgical attendance. The compiler notes that subsequent examination of the subject's marginalia (see Document B) confirms Latin comprehension well beyond what this modest self-description suggests.

**Education, formal:** Primary school, Aldeia da Serra, completed 1933. No secondary education. No seminary training. No university.

**Previous occupation or employment:** Agricultural laborer (family smallholding, olives and goats). Itinerant healer, serving the villages of the upper Dão valley, approximately 1945–1959.

**Skills or competencies relevant to monastery life:**

Herbalism, bone-setting, knowledge of plants. [The compiler notes that the abbot's handwriting here is notably more compressed than in the preceding entries, as though the pen were being held with unusual deliberation. The word "knowledge" has been written over what appears to have been an earlier word, now illegible, though "understanding" or possibly "communion" may be inferred from the underlying strokes.]

**Health conditions or infirmities:**

None observed. Subject is in excellent physical health. Strong hands. Walks with a slight unevenness attributed to a childhood injury (left ankle, poorly set fracture). No history of fever, consumption, or other chronic illness. Subject reports occasional headaches "when the weather changes," which he attributes to atmospheric pressure. He did not elaborate.

**Spiritual gifts or notable devotions:**

[The compiler records the following as written, preserving the original punctuation and lineation.]

Claims to hear the voice of God in specific locations. Has experienced this since approximately 1947, initially in a grove of cork oaks near his family's property, subsequently in the nave of the chapel at São Lourenço (north transept specifically), and in one instance at a roadside spring between Aldeia da Serra and Mangualde, the site of which he can identify precisely but which holds no recorded devotional significance. The experiences are accompanied by what the subject describes as "a change in the quality of light" and "a pressure, not unpleasant, behind the eyes." Investigated by prior parish. Padre Gonçalves consulted with the diocesan office in Viseu in 1956 (ref. unknown to this abbey). No finding of heresy. No finding of sanctity. The matter was characterized as "undetermined but not disruptive." Recommend observation.

**Reason for seeking residence at the abbey:**

Padre Gonçalves's letter states that Brother Tomás desires "a life of quiet service and proximity to God." When asked this question directly during the intake interview, Brother Tomás said: "I would like to be useful. And I would like to be somewhere that does not require me to explain."

**Assigned quarters:** Cell 14, east cloister.

**Assigned duties:** Garden labor (under Brother Eusébio). Infirmary assistance as needed.

**Additional observations by the admitting authority:**

Brother Tomás is courteous, self-contained, and shows every indication of genuine humility. He asked few questions about the abbey's routine but inquired in detail about the library and about a structure he had apparently seen from the road upon approach—the former botanical nursery behind the east cloister. I informed him that the structure has been disused since approximately 1943, when the last brother with botanical responsibilities passed away. He asked whether he might be permitted to visit it. I saw no reason to refuse.

Signed,
Abbot J. Pereira de Almeida
17 September 1959

---

**Compiler's annotation:**

The structure referred to by the abbot is the greenhouse appended to the rear wall of the east cloister, constructed in 1887 by Abbot Fernandes for the purpose of cultivating medicinal plants and liturgical flowers. It is a stone-and-glass construction approximately nine meters long and four meters wide, oriented southeast to catch the morning light. The original glass was imported from Porto. By 1959, the roof panes were substantially damaged—several missing, others cracked—and the stone walls showed deterioration consistent with decades of neglect. The interior retained its original planting beds, now overgrown, and a stone workbench along the north wall.

Brother Tomás began spending time in the greenhouse within days of his arrival. He did not request permission to do so beyond his initial inquiry to the abbot. No brother objected. The space was unused and had no formal function. By October 1959, he had cleared the planting beds and begun cultivating a selection of herbs whose identification the compiler provides in Document B.

The structure was already in considerable disrepair, which may account for the acoustic properties the subject attributed to divine origin. The broken panes created a variable airflow that, depending on wind direction and the state of the remaining glass, produced resonances that an untrained listener might characterize as unusual. The compiler visited the greenhouse on numerous occasions in its original state and can confirm that the acoustic environment was distinctive, though the compiler hesitates to speculate further on the nature of this distinction.

## DOCUMENT B

### Photographed Marginalia from Selected Texts Annotated by Brother Tomás dos Santos
### With Identifications and Contextual Notes by the Compiler

---

The following seventeen plates represent a selection, not the totality, of Brother Tomás's annotations to texts borrowed from the abbey library between 1960 and 1975. The compiler selected these plates on the basis of their relevance to the diocesan inquiry and their legibility. Brother Tomás's hand is generally clear, though subject to seasonal variation noted below.

The source texts are:

- *Herbário Prático para Uso dos Mosteiros e Casas Religiosas*, anonymous, printed Lisbon, 1794. Abbey call number H-14. [Herbal]
- *Psalterium Latinum*, printed Coimbra, 1901, standard abbey issue. Abbey call number L-02/7. [Psalter]
- *Confessionum Libri XIII*, S. Augustini, Maurist edition, Paris, 1679. Abbey call number P-41. [Augustine's *Confessions*]

The compiler notes that Brother Tomás borrowed many other texts during his residence—devotional works, agricultural manuals, a Portuguese translation of Virgil's *Georgics* that he returned without annotation—but that his most sustained engagement was with these three volumes. The herbal was in his keeping almost continuously. The psalter he borrowed and returned in a regular pattern corresponding to the liturgical seasons. The Augustine he kept for eleven years without returning it, an irregularity the compiler noted but did not act upon.

---

**Plate 1** *(Herbal, p. 34: "On the Cultivation of Rosemary")*

The printed text describes the preferred soil, light, and watering conditions for *Rosmarinus officinalis*. Brother Tomás has underlined the phrase "thrives in poor, stony ground" and written in the margin:

*"This is also true of prayer. The richer the soil the more distracted the roots. God is clearest where there is least between you and the rock."*

Below, in a different ink (suggesting a later visit to the same page):

*"Planted four cuttings along the south bed. The light at midday falls through the third pane from the east and makes a column. I sat in it today and the rosemary smelled like the inside of a word."*

**Compiler's annotation:** The four rosemary plants survived until the events described in Document E. They were well established and healthy. Brother Tomás's horticultural knowledge was genuine and extensive; the compiler has confirmed his plant identifications against the standard reference (*Flora de Portugal*, Pereira Coutinho, 2nd ed.) and found no errors in any of the 43 species he mentions across the annotated texts. Whatever else may be said about the subject's perceptual experiences, his empirical botanical knowledge was sound. The compiler wishes this to be noted.

---

**Plate 4** *(Psalter, Psalm 62/63: "O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee")*

Beside the verse *"My soul thirsteth for thee, my flesh longeth for thee in a dry and thirsty land, where no water is"*—

Brother Tomás has written:

*"Not thirst. Not longing. He has it backward, or I have it forward. In the greenhouse there is no distance to cross. The land is not dry. The water is in the walls. I put my hand on the stones after rain and felt the psalm happening inside them."*

And below, more hastily:

*"I must be careful how I write this. The words make it sound as though I am the one acting. I am not. I am the wall. The psalm moves through me. The greenhouse is the only place where I am porous enough to allow it."*

**Compiler's annotation:** The subject's references to porosity recur throughout his annotations. The compiler counts fourteen instances of the word "porous" or its cognates across the three source texts. The concept appears to describe a state of receptivity that the subject associated exclusively with the greenhouse environment. The compiler notes that the greenhouse walls were, in fact, porous—the mortar between the stones had degraded significantly, and moisture passed through them freely. Whether the subject intended a metaphorical or literal usage is unclear. The compiler suspects both.

---

**Plate 7** *(Augustine, Confessions, Book X, Chapter viii: on memory and the senses)*

The passage in question is Augustine's discussion of how sensory impressions are stored in the "vast palace of memory"—how colors enter through the eyes, sounds through the ears, scents through the nose, yet are retained in the mind as something other than themselves: *"non ipsa ibi sunt, sed imagines eorum."*

Brother Tomás has drawn a line through *"non ipsa"*—"not themselves"—and written:

*"Wrong. In the greenhouse they are themselves. The light is not a memory of light. The sound is not an image of sound. When I kneel at the bench and the afternoon comes through the broken panes, it is the thing itself, not a representation. Augustine could not find the place where God ceases to be metaphor. I have found it. It is eight meters long and smells of rosemary and wet calciumite."*

*"This is where God's voice has texture. I can touch it here. Nowhere else."*

The handwriting in this passage is extraordinarily steady—controlled and upright, each letter formed with deliberation, as though the act of writing were itself a form of the attention being described. The compiler has examined Brother Tomás's hand across more than fifteen years of annotations and can identify at least three distinct states: a rapid, excited script that accompanies new observations; a deteriorated, almost spasmodic hand that appears during what seem to have been periods of headache or confusion; and this third mode—slow, vertical, calm—that appears only in passages where the subject is attempting to describe the quality of direct experience. This third hand appears in fewer than a dozen annotations across the entire corpus. The compiler has photographed all instances.

The compiler further notes that the word "calcite" has been misspelled as "calcium ite," which is the only orthographic error the compiler has identified in the subject's Portuguese across all annotated texts.

---
