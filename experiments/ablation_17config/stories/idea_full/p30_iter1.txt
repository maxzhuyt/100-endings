**EVIDENCE INTAKE LOG — CASE NO. 2019-EC-04471**

**Date of Intake:** September 14, 2019

**Logging Officer:** Deputy Lyle Purvis, Badge No. 1187

**Incident Type:** Welfare Check / Narcotics Discovery (reclassified)

**Location:** 1412-B Chaparral Duplex Ct., Kettner, TX 78662

**Summary of Body-Camera Footage (Partial — camera lost power at 14:23:07):**

At 14:11, Deputy Purvis and Sheriff Dennis R. Bratchett arrive at the above address in response to a flood-welfare dispatch routed through the Elgin County Emergency Operations Center. Floodwater from Willow Creek has breached the south bank along Farm Road 36 and inundated the Chaparral Duplex Court subdivision, a four-unit complex approximately 0.3 miles from the creek bed. The duplex at 1412-B is occupied by one Dora Lucille Sweeney, age 78, who has not been reachable by phone since 09:40. The front door of Unit B is visibly swollen in its frame. At 14:13, after receiving no response to knocking and verbal identification, Sheriff Bratchett authorizes forced entry. Deputy Purvis kicks the door at the lock plate. It opens on the third attempt.

Interior footage shows approximately 8–10 inches of standing water throughout the living room. The furniture—a brown recliner, a pressboard television stand, a glass-top coffee table—is partially submerged. Floating among the furniture are eleven Rubbermaid storage tubs, commercial grade, 31-gallon capacity, gray with yellow lids. The lids are sealed. Several tubs have drifted into a cluster against the east wall. One tub has overturned and its lid has come partially unlatched, revealing a vacuum-sealed plastic parcel.

Deputy Purvis's camera, mounted on his chest, is aimed at Sheriff Bratchett's back as the Sheriff wades toward the overturned tub. At 14:14:22, Bratchett lifts the unsealed parcel, turns it over, and holds it up to the light from the front window. Neither officer speaks. The parcel is a crystalline substance later field-tested and confirmed as methamphetamine.

The footage shows both men standing in the water. Neither moves. The time stamp runs. Eleven seconds pass. At 14:14:33, Sheriff Bratchett says: "Well, Lyle."

Deputy Purvis does not respond on camera.

At 14:14:38, Bratchett says: "I suppose we ought to call someone."

The footage continues for an additional eight minutes and forty-nine seconds, during which the officers wade through the unit, catalog the visible tubs, and radio dispatch. At 14:23:07, the camera powers down mid-frame.

**Evidence Catalogued:** 40.2 kg crystalline methamphetamine (est. street value $1.1–1.4M), 11 Rubbermaid tubs, 1 partial roll of vacuum-seal plastic, 1 Chapin brand garden sprayer (contents pending lab), miscellaneous personal effects belonging to D. Sweeney (no narcotics connection established).

**Tenant Status:** Dora Lucille Sweeney was located at 15:10 at the Kettner First Baptist Church emergency shelter, unharmed. She stated she was unaware of any materials stored in her unit and suggested the items "must have floated in from the Mexicans next door." Residents of Unit A were not located. Lease records for Unit A were found to be filed under a nonexistent Social Security number. See attached.

**ELGIN COUNTY GAZETTE-TRIBUNE**
**September 17, 2019, Page A1**

# SMALL-TOWN HEROES: ELGIN COUNTY SHERIFF'S DEPT. STUMBLES ON CARTEL PIPELINE

*By Darla Faye Hutchins, Staff Reporter*

KETTNER — What began as a routine welfare check during last Saturday's flash flooding has turned into one of the largest methamphetamine seizures in Central Texas history, and the tiny Elgin County Sheriff's Department—all fourteen sworn personnel—finds itself at the center of a story better suited to a Cormac McCarthy novel than a rural department whose most notable prior case was a 2016 livestock fraud investigation involving a disgruntled alpaca breeder.

Sheriff Dennis "Denny" Bratchett, 54, a twenty-six-year department veteran who describes himself as "basically a guy who does speed traps and writes permits," confirmed in a brief telephone interview that the seized methamphetamine has been linked to a distribution corridor stretching from Nuevo Laredo to San Antonio, with the Chaparral Duplex Court serving as a midpoint storage facility.

"We didn't go looking for this," Bratchett said. "The creek came up, we went out, and there it was floating in an old lady's living room. I don't know what you call that."

The Drug Enforcement Administration has dispatched a Special Agent from the San Antonio Division office to assess the scope of the discovery. Federal authorities would not comment on the ongoing investigation.

Deputy Lyle Purvis, 29, who accompanied Bratchett on the initial welfare check, declined to be interviewed, saying only, "I just went where the sheriff went."

**TRANSCRIPT — DEPARTMENT-WIDE BRIEFING**

**Date:** September 25, 2019
**Time:** 09:00
**Location:** Main Briefing Room, Elgin County Sheriff's Department
**Present:** [See attached sign-in sheet, 14 of 14 sworn; 3 civilian staff incl. M. Kettlewell; 1 guest]
**Recorded by:** Maureen Kettlewell (audio recorder, personal property, Panasonic RR-US571, placed on filing cabinet near southwest corner of room)

---

**BRATCHETT:** Alright, settle. Settle down. I know it's early. Lyle, put the kolache down. Listen, everybody, this is Special Agent Colton Rusk. He's DEA, out of the San Antonio Division office. He's been here since Wednesday and I've asked him to speak to all of us at once because I don't want this turning into a game of telephone. Agent Rusk.

**RUSK:** Thank you, Sheriff. Good morning. I'll keep this brief and direct because I respect your time and frankly we're already behind where I'd like to be operationally. What your department stumbled into three weeks ago—

**BRATCHETT:** Found.

**RUSK:** I apologize. What your department *found* three weeks ago is the first physical confirmation of a distribution corridor we've been mapping for about fourteen months. We knew the route. What we didn't have was a confirmed stash point. Now we do. And more importantly, now *they* know we do. Which creates a window.

**BRATCHETT:** A window.

**RUSK:** A window. They'll be relocating assets. Rerouting. That takes time. During that interval, they're operationally exposed, and that's where you come in. What I'm proposing—and this comes with full federal backing, funding, equipment, support—is a joint task force using Elgin County personnel as the primary operational front. Your people, your vehicles, your local knowledge. We supply the intelligence infrastructure and the legal framework. You supply the face.

**MEEKS:** Why us?

**RUSK:** Because you have something we can't manufacture, Sergeant. Authenticity. An authentic local profile. If I send in agents from Houston or Dallas, they stick out. They've got the wrong plates, the wrong haircuts, and the wrong way of ordering breakfast. Sorry. It's true. You all *belong* here. You know the roads, the diners, the sight lines. You are exactly what a cartel advance team would expect to see in this county doing exactly what law enforcement in this county does. Which is, no offense, not much narcotics work. That's the advantage. You're invisible because you're local.

**BORREGO:** [inaudible]

**BRATCHETT:** Angie, speak up if you've got something.

**BORREGO:** I said, "so we're bait." Is that what he's saying?

**RUSK:** I'd characterize it as a *front*. There's a difference. Bait implies exposure without support. This is fully supported. Full federal task force funding under Title I. Overtime, hazard pay, equipment upgrades. And to be frank, Sheriff, this is the kind of federal partnership that can mean budgetary things for a department of your size that I don't have to spell out.

**BRATCHETT:** No, you don't.

[Pause — 7 seconds by audio timestamp.]

**BRATCHETT:** Alright. I'm not making this decision by fiat. This affects everybody. I want a show of hands. Who's in? And I want honest hands, not polite ones.

[Pause — 3 seconds.]

**BRATCHETT:** I'll go first. I'm in.

[Sheriff Bratchett raises his hand. Audio record cannot capture visual details; the following is based on my direct observation from the southwest corner of the room.]

[Within four seconds of Sheriff Bratchett's hand going up, all thirteen remaining sworn officers raise their hands. Sergeant Meeks is last. His hand rises approximately halfway before it fully extends.]

**BRATCHETT:** That's unanimous. Agent Rusk, you've got your task force.

**RUSK:** Outstanding. I'll have the operational framework to you by Friday. Maureen, is it? You'll be processing the federal intake paperwork. I'll send it over.

**KETTLEWELL:** I file what I receive.

**RUSK:** Great.

[Meeting adjourned 09:22.]

**MEMORANDUM — DRUG ENFORCEMENT ADMINISTRATION, SAN ANTONIO DIVISION**

**Date:** October 4, 2019
**From:** SA Colton Rusk, Joint Task Force CHAPARRAL (provisional)
**To:** ASAC Margaret Leong, San Antonio Division
**Re:** Operational Readiness Assessment — Elgin County JTF

Meg,

Quick status. I've completed my initial assessment of the Elgin County personnel who will form the operational arm of JTF CHAPARRAL. Below are my candid notes. These are *not* for the file.

- **Sheriff Bratchett:** Competent administrator. Knows his county cold. Has not conducted a narcotics operation in any capacity since a 2004 marijuana eradication detail that he described as "me and a machete in somebody's back forty." Enthusiastic about the federal partnership. Possibly too enthusiastic. I get the sense the budget implications are doing a lot of the heavy lifting in his decision calculus.
- **Deputy Purvis:** Young, earnest, nervous. Highest academy scores of anyone here. Strangely good rapport with strangers—there's a natural undercover quality, a kind of Everyman blankness. May be our best UC asset, but I need to stress-test him.
- **Sgt. Meeks (K-9):** Career handler. Very attached to the dog, a Belgian Malinois named—I am not making this up—*Señor Cody*. The dog is fine. Meeks has a medical note in his HR file referencing a sleep disorder. I flagged it. Bratchett said it was "managed." I'm choosing to trust local judgment on this one.
- **Dispatcher Borrego:** Smart. Possibly the smartest person in the department. Resistant. Asked the right question in the briefing ("so we're bait"). I want her running comms, not because she wants to but because she's the one I trust to get it right when it matters.

Overall: these are not operators. They are small-town deputies who process livestock complaints and direct traffic at the county fair. The gap between what they are and what I'm asking them to become is significant. But we've run the alternatives and there aren't any that survive a cost-benefit. We work with what the flood gave us.

I'll have the formal readiness package to you by the 11th.

— Rusk
