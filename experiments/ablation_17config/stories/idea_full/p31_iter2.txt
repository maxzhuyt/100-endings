## PREFATORY NOTE ON DOCUMENT ORGANIZATION

What follows is a chronological compilation of field documents generated between October 11 and October 16, 2019, during pre-production and principal photography for Episode 1 of the third season of *Cascadia Paranormal*, a program produced by BrightFire Media for the Altitude Discovery Network. I have organized these materials in the order in which they were received at my monitoring position. Where multiple simultaneous feeds existed, I have arranged them in parallel, with timestamps in the left margin to indicate synchronicity.

Interpolated between crew-generated documents are meteorological printouts from the NOAA automated weather station at Finney Creek (Station ID: FNYW1), barometric readings from the portable station I maintained at Ranger Station 9, and selected data from the U.S. Forest Service's animal tracking database for Management Unit 6-North, which I accessed through a terminal provided by District Ranger Ellen Huang.

My own walkie transmissions appear in the transcripts under the call sign **BASE**. I have not edited them.

Where I have added annotations after the fact, they appear in brackets and are marked **[D.O.]**.

I was the only crew member not on-site. My responsibilities included weather monitoring, emergency coordination, permit compliance, and real-time communication with the field team via dedicated walkie frequency (Channel 7, 462.7125 MHz). I had continuous access to all six body-cam feeds via satellite uplink, with a standard 4-second transmission delay.

The satellite uplink was one-directional for video. I could see them. They could not see me. I could speak to them only via walkie, Channel 7.

The location — designated in production documents as "Camp Gehenna" — is a decommissioned Boy Scouts of America camp on the western slope of Sauk Mountain, formally known as **Camp Elwell**, operational 1958–1979, closed after a structural fire in the mess hall. The camp became the subject of regional folklore in the early 1980s following unsubstantiated claims, attributed to former Scoutmaster Dale R. Voigt (deceased 1994), that Troop 116 conducted occult rituals in the dining hall during a 1977 winter campout and "opened a doorway that shouldn't have been opened." No evidence supports this claim. A 1983 investigation by the Skagit County Sheriff's Office found no indication of occult activity. The fire was attributed to a faulty propane connection.

## BODY-CAM COMPOSITE LOG — CAMP ELWELL
## October 11, 2019, 17:44–23:58 PDT

---

**BC-01 (HARKIN) — 17:44:03**
Arrival at camp perimeter. Camera shows a clearing approximately 200 feet in diameter, surrounded by Douglas fir and western red cedar. Three standing structures visible: a log cabin (former office), a collapsed A-frame (former mess hall, fire-damaged), and a concrete pad with rusted pipe fittings (former latrine block). Ground cover is dense moss. No trail maintenance in evidence. Cedar debris across all surfaces.

**[D.O.: The site matched the Forest Service structural survey from 2012. Nothing about its physical appearance deviated from what an abandoned camp in the Cascades would be expected to look like after forty years of neglect. I noted this in my log at 17:50. I did not transmit it to the crew because I knew it would not be useful.]**
