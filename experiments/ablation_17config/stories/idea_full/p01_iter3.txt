# The Handler's File

---

## PREFATORY NOTE

### Regarding the Compilation and Purpose of This Dossier

This file represents, to my best professional judgment, the complete operational record of Special Agent Elise Maren Corado (cover designation LINNET) during her assignment to the Philadelphia Organized Crime Task Force, specifically her deep-cover infiltration of the Ferrante syndicate from March 2016 through the events of November 2019. I have assembled it at the request of no one.

Let me be precise about that. This is not a response to a subpoena, an internal review, or any outstanding investigative matter. The Bureau closed its file on Agent Corado in February 2020. The Office of Professional Responsibility completed its inquiry the following April. I retired in June of that year, as I had always planned to do, and my pension came through without incident, and I bought the house in Chincoteague I'd been looking at since my second marriage ended, and I have lived there quietly since, and none of this is relevant, and I mention it only because someone eventually will ask, and I would rather the asking happen inside a document I control.

The record assembled here includes the following:

- Surveillance transcripts (wiretap and ambient audio)
- Operational briefing memoranda
- Agent field reports (LINNET-filed)
- Mandatory psychological evaluation recordings (audio, transcribed)
- Internal Bureau communications (selected)
- Personal correspondence (Corado to her husband, James Aldiss; unsent)
- Private journal entries (provenance explained below)
- My own annotations, appearing as footnotes, provided solely for context

I have organized these documents chronologically. I have redacted nothing, though certain names have been replaced with functional descriptors where the individuals are not essential to the narrative—to the record. I have not editorialized. Where I offer interpretive comment, it is clearly marked, and the reader should feel free to disregard it. The documents speak. I have merely ordered them, which is, I recognize, itself a kind of speech, but the alternative was disorder, and disorder serves no one.

My purpose is completeness. After everything that happened, and after everything that was said and left unsaid in the official record, someone should be able to sit down with the full file and understand what occurred. Not what was alleged. Not what was concluded. What occurred.

One note on the journal entries, which will not appear until later in the file but which I should address now, since their inclusion is unusual. Agent Corado kept a personal journal during the final months of her assignment. These entries were not filed through any official channel. They were not recovered during the search of her apartment in December 2019—I'm told the search was thorough, but I have opinions about the thoroughness of that search that I will keep to myself. The journals came into my possession through a route I will describe when they appear in the chronological sequence. I include them because completeness demands it. I am aware that their inclusion will be seen by some as self-serving, or as an attempt to characterize Agent Corado in a particular light. I have no response to this except to say that light is not something I control. I put the documents in order. The light is whatever comes through them.

I knew Elise Corado for three years and four months. I was her handler, her primary point of contact with an institution that asked her to become someone else and then grew suspicious when the act was convincing. I owed her more than I gave, though I would argue—I would have argued, I did argue, at the time—that what I gave was everything the structure permitted. Whether the structure was adequate is not a question I can answer from inside it.

But this is editorializing. I said I wouldn't do that.

The file begins.

## I. THE CLEAN FILE

### OPERATIONAL BRIEFING MEMORANDUM
**Classification: SECRET / ORCON / NOFORN**
**Date: March 14, 2016**
**Subject: FERRANTE CRIMINAL ENTERPRISE — Operational Overview for Tasked Personnel**
**Prepared by: Organized Crime Intelligence Unit, Philadelphia Field Office**

The Ferrante organization operates as a multi-generational criminal enterprise with primary operations in southeastern Pennsylvania and southern New Jersey. The family's legitimate business holdings include Ferrante & Sons Construction (Bucks County), three commercial real estate entities operating under the umbrella of Ridley Property Group, and a controlling interest in a regional waste management contractor servicing municipal accounts in Delaware and Chester Counties.

Current family leadership is attributed to **Dominic Ferrante Sr.** (DOB 4/12/1951), who assumed control following the death of his father, Aldo Ferrante, in 1998. Operational authority is shared with his sons **Dominic Jr.** (DOB 9/3/1978) and **Marco** (DOB 1/15/1982). A third son, **Tomás** (DOB 8/22/1989), maintains no known role in family business operations but is a person of interest due to his access and family position.

Known criminal activities include: racketeering, extortion, money laundering through real estate transactions, loansharking, illegal gambling operations, and suspected involvement in at least three unsolved homicides (see Appendix C, not included in this dossier).¹

**[REDACTED PARAGRAPH — concerns an unrelated ongoing investigation]**

A note on operational considerations: Field personnel should be aware that certain informant and intelligence sources have, over the past decade, attributed to members of the Ferrante family an unusual degree of interpersonal perception sometimes characterized in source reporting as a "psychic" or "intuitive" capacity to detect deception or ascertain hidden information. These claims are unconfirmed and are assessed by this unit as likely reflecting the Ferrantes' well-documented skill in psychological intimidation and interrogation technique. They are noted here for completeness and to ensure that tasked personnel do not accord undue significance to anecdotal source characterizations. Standard operational security protocols are considered adequate.²

---

¹ *Handler's note: The Ferrantes were, by the standards of their world, a middleweight operation — not the Gambinos, not even close, but durable, careful, and very well-lawyered. The Bureau had been circling them since the early 2000s with nothing to show for it. Three previous informant placements had failed, though "failed" covers a range of outcomes. One informant simply stopped reporting. One was found to have been feeding the Ferrantes Bureau intelligence for approximately eight months before discovery. The third requested extraction after eleven days and subsequently left the Bureau. The Corado assignment was the fourth attempt. I flag this history because it matters. By the time Elise went in, the Philadelphia OC unit had developed a certain institutional frustration with the Ferrantes that I believe colored what followed. When an office fails three times at the same target, the fourth attempt carries pressure that distorts judgment. Including mine.*

² *Handler's note: I want to be transparent about my own assessment of this paragraph at the time I first read it. I thought it was a joke. Not formally — the briefing was serious, and the analyst who prepared it was thorough — but the psychic claim had the flavor of something included to inoculate the file against later accusations of withholding, in the way that agencies sometimes document the implausible so that no one can say it wasn't considered. I treated it as bureaucratic prophylaxis. I am no longer certain that assessment was correct, but I want the reader to understand what I believed at the time and what Elise would have believed reading this briefing: that it was a throwaway line. We both walked past it. Everyone did.*

### FIELD REPORT — LINNET
**Date: April 3, 2016**
**Assignment: FERRANTE / Deep Cover**
**Reporting Agent: E. Corado**
**Contact: WREN [handler designation]**

First week summary. Cover identity (Elena Marcetti, independent real estate consultant, recently relocated from New York) is established and functioning. Leased office space on Sansom Street as directed. Initial introductions to Ferrante-adjacent business contacts proceeding through the networking channels we identified — the Rittenhouse business luncheon series and the Italian-American Chamber of Commerce events in South Philly.

Have not yet made direct contact with any Ferrante family member. This is expected and, frankly, desirable. Three prior placements failed, and at least one was likely identified early, so I'm taking the long approach. Elena Marcetti needs to exist in the ecosystem for a while before she attracts attention. She needs to win a few small listings, be seen at the right restaurants, develop a reputation as competent and discreet. The Ferrantes don't recruit — they absorb. You have to be *there* long enough that working with them feels like a natural development rather than an approach.

Note for the file: my apartment is on the second floor of a building on Christian Street in the Italian Market area. The landlord is not connected, as far as I can determine. The unit is small, adequate, and has excellent sightlines to the street. I have a clear egress route through the back and a secondary route through the basement. These are reflex observations, noted for my own comfort more than operational necessity. I am aware that I am here for a long time.

Personal status: fine. Checked in with James by phone (clean line, personal cell, per protocol). He's adjusted to the separation better than expected. Or he's performing adjustment, which is what the spouses of agents learn to do, and which I have learned not to interrogate too carefully.³

---

³ *Handler's note: Elise's early field reports had a quality I came to think of as "disciplined informality." She wrote precisely but with a register that suggested she was talking to someone — which she was, of course; she was talking to me, since I was the only person who read these reports in full before they were summarized upward. The observation about her husband is characteristic: self-aware, slightly deflective, and more honest than it appears on first reading. She knew that James's "adjustment" was a problem. She also knew that noting it this way — as an aside, with a wry half-acknowledgment — was a way of placing it in the record without having to act on it. She was good at that. Most deep-cover agents are. It is a skill that looks like emotional intelligence and functions as emotional avoidance, and I say this not as a criticism but as a description of a trait I share.*

### FIELD REPORT — LINNET
**Date: July 19, 2016**
**Assignment: FERRANTE / Deep Cover**
**Reporting Agent: E. Corado**

Contact established with Marco Ferrante through the Ridley Property Group. Details in operational supplement (attached separately — commercial real estate referral, legitimate transaction, no entrapment concerns). He's the younger of the two operational sons and the one our intelligence suggests handles the family's property portfolio, including the vehicles used for laundering.

Impression: smart, careful, unexpectedly funny. Referred to himself at one point as "the family's legitimate disappointment," which got a laugh from the two associates at the table and which I read as a rehearsed line that probably contains real resentment. He has an MBA from Villanova and seems to genuinely enjoy the real estate work, or at least to be genuinely good at it, which may or may not be the same thing.

He asked me three personal questions during a forty-five-minute meeting. Where I went to school (prepared answer: NYU, Stern). Whether I was married (prepared answer: divorced, no children, which is close enough to a half-truth that I could deliver it without the micro-expressions that accompany total fabrication). And — this was interesting — whether I liked Philadelphia yet. Not whether I was "settling in" or "finding my way around," which would have been the standard pleasantries. Whether I *liked* it. There was a quality of directness to the question that I noted. I told him I liked the food and distrusted the weather, and he said that was the correct answer, and we moved on.

I'm filing this because first contact is always the moment of maximum exposure, and I want it documented that the meeting went cleanly. He did not appear suspicious. He appeared to be a man in his mid-thirties who is good at reading people and who read me as what I was presenting: a capable, somewhat guarded woman from New York who is slightly out of her depth socially but not professionally. That reading is, in all respects but one, accurate.⁴

---

⁴ *Handler's note: Her assessment of Marco was precisely correct and also, I think, the beginning of a pattern I should have recognized earlier. Elise was drawn to people who were legible to her — whose motives and performances she could map with confidence. Marco Ferrante was legible. What she didn't account for, because the briefing hadn't given her reason to, was that legibility can run in both directions.*

### FIELD REPORT — LINNET
**Date: October 8, 2016**
**Assignment: FERRANTE / Deep Cover**
**Reporting Agent: E. Corado**

Six-month check-in. Summary of operational progress:

I am now regularly involved in legitimate real estate transactions through Ridley Property Group. I have met Dominic Jr. twice (both times at family events I was invited to by Marco — a Labor Day cookout and a christening for one of Dominic Jr.'s children). I have not yet met Dominic Sr., who does not attend these functions or does not attend them when outsiders are present.

Tomás Ferrante was at the christening. He is the youngest son, the one the briefing describes as having "no known role in family business operations." This is accurate in the sense that he doesn't appear on any corporate filings or attend any business meetings. It is inaccurate in the sense that he was the only person at the christening who seemed to be paying sustained attention. He stood near the drinks table for most of the event and watched people. When I was introduced to him by Marco, he shook my hand and held it for a moment longer than usual — not aggressively, not flirtatiously, but as though he were finishing a sentence in his head. He said, "Marco says you're good at your job," and I said I tried to be, and he said, "That's not what I meant," and then someone called him away and that was the end of it.

I'm filing this under general observations. It may be nothing. Some people are strange at parties.⁵

---

⁵ *Handler's note: I did not flag this interaction for follow-up. In retrospect, this was the first point at which a more careful handler might have revisited the psychic-capacity note in the original briefing. Tomás Ferrante appears infrequently in the official record — he has no criminal history, no known involvement in family operations, and his file at the Bureau is thin. But he appears repeatedly in Elise's reports from this point forward, always peripherally, always briefly, and always with that particular quality she had of noticing something precisely without naming what she had noticed. I should have asked her more about him. I asked about Marco, about Dominic Jr., about business operations and money flows and evidentiary targets, because those were the questions the operation required. Tomás was not an evidentiary target. He was, I would later come to understand, something else entirely.*

### PSYCHOLOGICAL EVALUATION — Mandatory Deep-Cover Assessment
**Date: November 2, 2016**
**Subject: Agent Elise Corado (LINNET)**
**Evaluator: Dr. Sandra Hess, Bureau Clinical Services**
**Session 3 (quarterly)**

**[Audio transcription begins]**

**DR. HESS:** How are you sleeping?

**CORADO:** In a bed. With a pillow. The usual arrangement.

**DR. HESS:** Elise.

**CORADO:** Sorry. I'm sleeping fine. Six, seven hours. Some adjustment dreams — I dream about the apartment on Christian Street, but in the dreams it's my real apartment, the one James and I have in Arlington. The rooms are wrong but the feeling is right. I assume that's normal.

**DR. HESS:** It's not unusual for deep-cover agents to experience spatial displacement in dreams. Your cover environment becomes the default setting. How is the emotional adjustment?

**CORADO:** Good. Fine. Elena Marcetti is settling in. She's easier to be than I expected.

**DR. HESS:** What do you mean by "easier"?

**CORADO:** I mean the cover fits well. There's enough of me in her that I don't have to perform constantly.
