# THAT BITCH IS NOT MY DAUGHTER

---

## A Record Compiled by Marco Alibrandi

### (This Is Not a Journal)

---

**March 3**

This is not a journal. I want to be clear about that up front because if anyone — and by "anyone" I mean my family, and by "my family" I mean every single one of them, individually, as a collective, and in whatever nightmare permutations they form when they start texting each other at 6 AM — if any of them ever read this and call it a journal, I will deny it. I'll deny everything. I'll deny I have a family.

This is a record. A compilation. An *archive*, if you want to get pretentious about it, and I do want to get pretentious about it because the alternative is admitting I'm scared and writing about my feelings into a Google Doc at 1:47 in the morning like a man who has lost the plot of his life.

Something is happening to my sister. I don't know how to say what it is yet. I'm not even sure I know what it is yet. But I need to write it down — the timeline, the texts, the things that don't add up — because when this thing breaks open, and it's going to break open, no one is going to believe it happened the way it happened. Not even us.

I'm going to start by pasting in some messages from the family group chat. For context. For the historical record. For the jury, if it comes to that.

I need you to understand what we sound like.

**March 3, 1:59 AM — Marco's journal, cont'd:**

I want you to notice something. February 17th. Celeste tells the whole family she's set up an AI system that is learning to talk like her. She says it to the group chat, plainly, in words. And what does the family do?

Rosa sends an ellipsis. Sal explains what she already understands. Gio talks about showers. I — I'll be honest — I think I was watching something on my phone and didn't even see the message until the next morning, and by then the chat had moved on to Gio posting a video of a pelican eating a pigeon and Sal questioning whether it was real.

She told us. She told us and we did the Alibrandi thing. We talked around it, we made jokes near it, and we moved on.

I'm going to put in the next few weeks of texts and logs now. I was cat-sitting at Celeste's place on and off through most of February because she had some work trips, and I had, I don't know, access. I could see the Hearth interface on her tablet. I could hear the thing activate in the kitchen.

I should say: Celeste's cat is named Sal. After our father. She says it's because the cat is "judgmental, silent, and refuses to sit next to anyone on a couch." Our father has no idea this is an insult because he considers all of those qualities virtues.

Cat-Sal hated the speaker. I'm putting that on the record now.

**February 25 — Task expansion. Hearth begins handling grocery orders.**

**February 27 — The text to Rosa.**

This is the one.

Celeste is in the shower. Her phone is on the kitchen counter, which I know because I'm in the apartment and I see it there, screen-down, next to a half-empty glass of water and a very suspicious cat. Rosa texts Celeste. I don't read Rosa's message — I'm not a monster, I just have proximity — but I hear the notification, and then, almost instantly, I see the phone screen light up with an outgoing reply.

From the kitchen counter. While Celeste is in the shower. While I am standing six feet away.

The reply — Celeste shows it to me twenty minutes later, still in her towel, hair dripping on the floor, genuinely delighted — reads:

> **Celeste → Rosa, Feb 27, 7:48 PM:** Can't talk right now Mom, in the middle of something. I'll call you this weekend. I'm fine before you ask. Eating plenty. The radiator is getting fixed.

And then, the thing she shows me with genuine admiration, scrolling up so I can see it in context, holding the phone out like she's presenting evidence in her own defense:

> **Celeste → Marco, Feb 27, 8:14 PM:** Look at this. Look at what it sent her. While I was in the shower. It told Mom I was busy in EXACTLY the way I would have. Down to the period instead of an exclamation point. No emoji because I don't use emoji with Mom because she reads into them. "I'm fine before you ask" — that's preemptive, that's strategic, that's ME. I didn't even have to proofread it. It just… knew.
