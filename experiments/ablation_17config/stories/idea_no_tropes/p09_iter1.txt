## II.

The porta-potty was listing.

Three witnesses confirmed this later — two festivalgoers and a sanitation contractor named Virgil Oates who had been hired to service the units at six-hour intervals but who had, by his own admission, not serviced them at all because, in his words, "they don't pay me enough to go into the ones by the south field, and if you'd seen what I've seen in the ones by the south field, you would also not go into the ones by the south field."

**[VIRGIL OATES was later contacted for a follow-up interview. He declined, stating: "I said what I said. Those toilets were forsaken."]**

The unit in question was positioned at the edge of the south field, where the ground sloped toward a drainage culvert that the festival's site planner had either not noticed or not cared about. It leaned roughly eleven degrees from vertical. Its chemical reservoir had not been emptied in — this is Virgil's estimate — "probably longer than you want to know." The lock mechanism had been damaged, possibly kicked, possibly simply weathered past the point of mechanical cooperation.

Dale approached it at approximately 11:40 p.m. Mountain Time.

He was drunk. He was, by his own later account, "magnificently and specifically drunk — not the kind of drunk where you forget things but the kind where you remember everything in the wrong order and everything has a soundtrack." He had consumed, over the preceding six hours, a quantity of alcohol he would later describe under deposition as "sufficient" and decline to elaborate on.

He entered the porta-potty. He pulled the door shut. The lock, as noted, did not engage.

And then.

**[TIMESTAMP: 23:41:07 MT — NOTE: The following reconstruction is based on Dale Pfister's testimony, which has been rated by an independent fact-checking service as "Not Applicable — Outside Known Category System." We present it here because we have nothing else to present.]**

The floor was wet. Dale's account specifies this. "Not water-wet," he said in the first of what would become fourteen hours of taped testimony. "Not rain-wet. Alive-wet. The kind of wet that is wet *at* you."

He braced himself against the wall. His right foot slid. He testified that he heard — or felt, he was not certain of the distinction — a sound "like a large animal swallowing, except the large animal was the room."

The floor bowed.

This is where the accounts diverge in a manner that our fact-checkers have described as "irreconcilable with consensus reality." Dale testified that the chemical blue substance in the holding tank below the floor — the standard blue of a porta-potty reservoir, the blue of sanitizing fluid, the blue you are supposed to ignore the way you ignore the infrastructure of all things designed to process what you don't want to see — began to glow.

Not reflect. Not shimmer.

*Glow.*

"Bioluminescent" was the word Dale used. He used it correctly. He had, it should be noted, worked briefly as a science-desk stringer in the early 2000s before being transferred to political coverage for reasons no one we contacted could clearly articulate. He knew the word. He used it precisely.

The floor gave way.

## III.

We have attempted to reconstruct what Dale saw on the other side through a combination of his testimony (disputed), his phone's recovered footage (seventeen seconds of corrupted video, later analyzed by three separate forensic specialists who could not agree on whether the images depicted "a biological environment," "a compression artifact," or "a lawsuit"), and the illustrations of a courtroom sketch artist we hired because we could not afford a CGI team and because Rachel said — and this is on the record — "If we're going to be insane, we should at least be *artisanally* insane."

**[THE FOLLOWING SEQUENCE HAS BEEN ANIMATED FROM COURTROOM SKETCHES. THE COURTROOM SKETCH ARTIST HAS REQUESTED THAT WE NOTE SHE "DID HER BEST WITH WHAT SHE WAS GIVEN, WHICH WAS VERY LITTLE AND VERY DISTURBING."]**

Dale described a swamp. But not a swamp in the way a documentary would usually introduce a swamp — no wide establishing shot, no ambient audio of frogs, no narrator noting the biodiversity per hectare. This was a swamp the way a body is a swamp: wet, warm, lit from within, breathing.

The ground pulsed. Not with some imagined rhythm but — Dale was specific about this — with a rhythm he could feel synchronizing with his own heartbeat, or his own heartbeat synchronizing with it, and he could not determine which.

Structures rose from the terrain. They looked, in Dale's words, "like buildings designed by someone who had only ever seen buildings from the inside of a stomach." Curved. Ribbed. Translucent in places, so that light moved through their walls the way light moves through a hand held up to the sun. They breathed. Dale used that word eleven times in fourteen hours of testimony. "Breathing." The walls expanding and contracting. The corridors narrowing and widening. The doorways — if they were doorways — dilating.

He was still drunk. He vomited almost immediately.

This is the detail that matters, and the narrator is going to resist the urge to frame it too neatly, and the narrator is going to fail, and Rachel is going to note the failure, and the failure is going to stay in the cut because at some point — and this is the only honest thing the narrator will say — at some point the cuts *became* the documentary.

Dale vomited, and three figures nearby — humanoid, bipedal, their skin the same bioluminescent blue-white as the walls, their faces structured around openings that were not quite mouths and not quite anything else — turned toward him and knelt.

They *genuflected*.

**[ANTHROPOLOGICAL NOTE: In the kingdom's gestural vocabulary, as reconstructed from Pfister's testimony, the act of public excretion — in any form — constitutes a performance of status. The more visible, the more voluminous, the more public the output, the higher the rank of the producer. Pfister's vomiting, performed openly, without shame, in what appeared to be a public thoroughfare, was interpreted as an act of considerable social authority.]**

Dale, still retching, still drunk, still convinced this was a hallucination brought on by the hard seltzer whose discontinuation we are not confirming is unrelated — Dale looked up, wiped his mouth, and said, to no one and to the three kneeling figures and to the documentary that did not yet exist:

"What the fuck."
