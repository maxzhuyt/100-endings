**LT. CMDR. FONG:** Can you describe what you experienced on the morning of September fourteenth?

**SUBJECT:** I was making coffee. I was — it was a normal morning. The kids were still asleep. And then I was somewhere else.

**LT. CMDR. FONG:** Can you describe "somewhere else"?

**SUBJECT:** Not somewhere. Some*one*. I was inside someone. I could feel — [subject pauses, 11 seconds] — do you know what it's like to be so alone that you forget what other people are *for?* Not lonely. Past lonely. Like lonely is a city you drove through years ago and now you're in the desert past the city and there's no road and you weren't even aware you'd stopped looking for one. I felt that. For — I don't know. Twenty minutes? An hour? It didn't have time inside it. It just had this — I'm sorry. I'm sorry. [Subject crying.]

**LT. CMDR. FONG:** Take your time.

**SUBJECT:** I didn't know a person could feel like that and survive it. That's what I keep telling my husband. That whoever this is — wherever they are — they have been surviving something that should have killed them and they have been doing it *completely alone.* And I was in it. I was wearing it. For twenty minutes I understood exactly what it means to be that person and now I can't —

[Subject unable to continue. Interview suspended.]

**LT. CMDR. FONG — FIELD NOTE (APPENDED):**

Subject is the fourteenth interview to produce a substantially identical account. Repeat: *fourteenth.* The emotional content described by affected individuals is consistent to a degree that is, in my professional experience, without precedent. This is not mass hysteria. Hysterical contagion produces convergent narratives; it does not produce convergent *phenomenology.* These subjects are not telling each other a story. They are reporting the same experience from the inside.

I have been doing this work for nine years and I am requesting additional personnel because I am not confident in my own clinical objectivity after conducting this many sequential interviews with this content.

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** Thank you for coming in. I know the travel was —

**ATLAS:** Twenty-seven hours from acceptance of the request to arrival. You were going to say "difficult." It wasn't. The difficult part was the fourteen minutes at Gate B6 in Salt Lake where the woman in 14C considered and then did not purchase the trip insurance, which created a .0003 probability branch affecting the on-time departure percentage of Delta 1412, which — I'm sorry. You asked me something. Or you were about to.

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** I hadn't asked anything yet.

**ATLAS:** You were going to ask about my educational background. It's on page four of the file in your lap. You'll look at it in approximately forty seconds and feel mildly embarrassed for asking something you already have the answer to, and then you'll redirect to an open-ended question about my "experience" of the probability field, which is a clinician's move designed to let the subject self-narrate rather than —

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** [Pause.] How long has it been like this?

**ATLAS:** Which "this"? The math, since I was nine. The part where I can't hold a conversation because I've already had the conversation six ways and none of them end with me feeling less — [Subject stops. Eleven-second silence.] I was going to say "less weird." That's not what I mean. You know that's not what I mean. You're about to write something in your notebook. The margin note will say something about isolation. You use that word a lot in marginalia. More than you realize.

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** I'd like to do a brief physical assessment, if you're comfortable. I'll need to touch your hand.

**ATLAS:** I know. You'll reach with your left hand. You're right-handed but you lead with the left during contact assessments because the sensory integration is faster through the — yes, that's — oh.

[Recording registers a seven-second silence. SSA ████████'s observation log notes that Subject's posture changed significantly upon physical contact.]

**ATLAS:** Oh, you — you can feel all of it?

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** Please keep your hand still.

**ATLAS:** Every branch?

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** [Pause.] Yes.

**ATLAS:** Oh. [Subject's voice pitch shifts. Recording quality note: possible subvocalization.] No one has ever — I didn't know anyone could —

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** Thank you. That's enough for now.

**ECHO:** Why?

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** Because I'll be able to understand your ability better through direct contact.

**ECHO:** You'll be able to feel what I feel.

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** Yes.

**ECHO:** [Twenty-one-second silence.] And what if it — what if you feel it and you don't want to — what if —

**DR. OLIVEIRA:** I won't look away.

[Recording registers a long silence. SSA ████████'s observation log: "Subject extended hand without further prompting.
