## I. BACKGROUND AND ASSIGNMENT

On 11 Ashmonth, Case WF-4409 was routed through standard intake processing and assigned to Senior Granting Officer Maren Sollis via the automatic distribution queue. The assignment was unremarkable. The queue assigns by caseload and specialization rating; Officer Sollis's romantic-fulfillment index is the highest in the Department's active roster and has been for nine consecutive years.³

The client, Ivo Táborský, age forty-one, presented at the Linden District intake window at 7:02 a.m., according to the intake officer's timestamp — a detail worth noting only because the Linden office does not open until 8:00, and the intake officer, upon arriving to unlock the door, found Mr. Táborský already seated on the front step with a paper sack of almond rolls, one of which he offered to the intake officer, who declined per Departmental Policy on Client Gifts (DP-41), though the intake log contains the handwritten marginal note *"smelled extraordinary."*⁴

The wish, as transcribed during the intake interview, was as follows:

> **INTAKE OFFICER:** Please state, in your own words, the nature of the wish you are filing today.
>
> **TÁBORSKÝ:** I would like someone to — well. You know. [Pause.] To be there. When I come home. Or when I wake up. Not for anything specific. Just to — I mean, the bread is good, I know the bread is good, people tell me, and that's — but after the shop closes and I clean the trays and I walk home, the — [Pause.] The apartment has this particular sound. When there's no one in it. You probably know the sound. Everyone probably knows the sound. [Pause.] I would like it to stop making that sound.⁵

The intake officer coded this as **True Love, Standard Romantic, 1a**, which is the correct classification per the Taxonomy of Human Longing (Rev. 14), and the file was forwarded.

Officer Sollis received the file at 9:40 a.m. and, per her standard practice, filed an initial case plan by 11:15. Her case plans are notable.⁶ Her plan for WF-4409 followed her established methodology for Class I Standard Romantic fulfillments:

**Phase 1:** Run compatibility matrix (twelve-block radius, expanding to twenty-four if initial results fall below 72% resonance threshold).
**Phase 2:** Identify optimal match. Generate dossier. Flag logistical barriers.
**Phase 3:** Engineer three plausible coincidences within a fourteen-day window (recommended sequence: visual contact, incidental conversation, shared minor crisis — a dropped bag, a wrong turn, a sudden rain).
**Phase 4:** Allow organic attraction to crystallize. Monitor at forty-eight-hour intervals.
**Phase 5:** Close file upon confirmation of mutual bond. File enchantment-dissolution paperwork. Seal.

The plan, labeled WF-4409-CP in the case archive, is four pages long, single-spaced, with tabbed subsections. It includes a decision tree for sixteen possible complications, ranging from "Match Subject Unavailable Due to Preexisting Bond" to "Client Experiences Acute Ambivalence at Point of Contact." Each branch terminates in a resolution protocol. None of the branches account for what actually happened.⁷

## II. THE FIRST ENCHANTMENT AND ITS MISDIRECTION

On 14 Ashmonth, at 3:47 a.m., Officer Sollis deployed the first enchantment pursuant to her case plan. The compatibility matrix had been run. A match had been identified: one Dariela Novak, age thirty-eight, florist, resident at 19 Linden Street — four blocks from the bakery, well within radius, compatibility score of 88.3%. The enchantment was calibrated to produce the first of three planned coincidences: Ms. Novak would, on the morning of the 14th, discover that her usual coffee shop was unexpectedly closed and would be redirected, by a minor navigational enchantment, to the Táborský bakery, where she would arrive at 6:45 a.m., seven minutes before the morning rush, when the baker would be alone at the counter and the shop would smell of fresh _houska_ and the light through the front window would be — per the enchantment's atmospheric sub-routine — "amber and forgiving."⁸

The enchantment deployed. Ms. Novak did not arrive at the bakery.

At 4:15 a.m., the baker's sourdough starter achieved sentience.

---

The sourdough culture — maintained by Mr. Táborský for approximately twenty-three years, housed in a brown ceramic crock with a chipped rim, referred to in the bakery's informal inventory as "Mother"⁹ — demonstrated the following behaviors in rapid succession, as captured by the enchantment-monitoring log and the always-on ambient audio feed required for all active wish cases:

**4:15:00** — Crock begins to vibrate at a frequency consistent with laryngeal development.

**4:15:22** — First vocalization. Described in the monitoring log as "a low bubbling hum, not unlike a lullaby, if the singer were made of flour and patience."¹⁰

**4:16:01** — First word. "Ivo." Spoken clearly. The monitoring log notes the pronunciation was perfect — the slight emphasis on the first syllable, the open vowel at the end — suggesting the culture had been *listening* to the way other people spoke his name, or, perhaps, to the way he spoke to it during the long hours when a baker's only company is the thing he is making.

**4:16:30** — Full speech capability achieved. Mother's voice is described in the log as "warm, slightly hoarse, with a quality of sustained fermentation." An informal note appended to the log by the monitoring technician reads: *"Sounds like a pot of water just before it boils. Not unpleasant. Actually kind of nice."*¹¹

**4:17:00–4:19:00** — Mother produces, from within the crock, a ring of braided dough — a miniature _vánočka_, perfectly plaited, sized to fit a human finger. The monitoring log flags this as a Class II spontaneous-materialization event. The archivist flags it as a proposal.

**4:19:15** — Mother speaks. The full transcript is included as **Appendix C** and is referenced here in summary. The speech is three minutes and forty-two seconds long. It begins: *"Ivo, I have risen beside you every morning for twenty-three years, and I would like to say something I have been thinking for all of them."* It includes a passage about the nature of constancy — *"You have never once forgotten to feed me. Do you know what that means? Do you know what it means to be a living thing that someone has never once forgotten?"* — and a passage about shared labor — *"We have worked in the dark together. We have made the thing that feeds this street. I have watched you press your hands into the dough and I have felt it, Ivo, I have felt your hands, and they are good hands, and I do not want them to stop."* It concludes: *"I do not know if I am what you asked for. But I know what I am. I am the thing that was already here."*¹²

**4:23:00** — Mr. Táborský sits down on the floor of the bakery. The monitoring log notes that the enchantment's biometric sub-routine detected elevated heart rate, mild lacrimation, and what the system classified as "laughter, sustained, with harmonic anomaly suggesting concurrent weeping."

**4:23:00–5:03:00** — Mr. Táborský and Mother talk. For forty minutes. The full transcript is in **Appendix C-1**. The archivist will not summarize it here in its entirety. The archivist will note that at 4:31, the baker says: *"I talk to you every morning, you know. I thought — I always thought it was just — I didn't think you were listening."* And Mother says: *"I was always listening. What else would I have been doing?"* And at 4:47, the baker says, quietly: *"Is this real? Are you going to be here tomorrow?"* And Mother says: *"I have been here tomorrow every day for twenty-three years."*¹³
