## I.

Before I say anything about Darcy, I need to show you the document. I've gone back and forth on how much to excerpt. The whole thing runs eleven pages—single-spaced, Times New Roman, what I'd later learn was the default template for all official correspondence from the Canaan Springs Unified Church of Christ's Covenant—and my first instinct was to reproduce it in full, the way you'd include a primary source in an appendix. But that felt like a different kind of violation than the one I'm trying to describe, or maybe the same kind wearing a better outfit. So: selections. I have tried to be judicious. I have tried to be fair. I am aware that "judicious" and "fair" are words people use when they are controlling the frame.

The document is titled **MINUTES OF SPECIAL SESSION — CANAAN SPRINGS UNIFIED CHURCH OF CHRIST'S COVENANT — COUNCIL OF ELDERS — RE: MEMBERSHIP REVIEW, D. FONTAINE (Née Member, Baptized 04/11/2005)**. It was uploaded to the church's community website on August 19th, between the announcement of the fall potluck schedule and a reminder about parking-lot resurfacing. It is formatted as meeting minutes. There is a quorum roll call. There are seconded motions.

I will give you some of it now.

> **Item 3.1 — Summary of Testimony Received:**
>
> It was noted that the Council received testimony from no fewer than four (4) members of the congregation regarding conduct by D. Fontaine inconsistent with the moral covenant as outlined in the Church Handbook, Section 7 ("Standards of Sexual Purity and Right Conduct Among Unmarried Members"). Testimony was received in closed session on August 4 and August 11. The following summary reflects the substance of testimony received and has been reviewed for accuracy by Elder R. Hatch (Chair) and Elder P. Simmons (Secretary).
>
> **(a)** It was testified that D. Fontaine engaged in sexual relations with [REDACTED, minor at time of incident] during or around the period of June–August 2021, at a location described as "the creek access behind the Dairy Queen." The testifying party indicated awareness of this conduct through direct observation.¹
>
> **(b)** It was testified that D. Fontaine engaged in sexual relations with a non-member male, identity unconfirmed, during or around November 2021, in a motor vehicle parked at the Canaan Springs Trailhead. The testifying party described the encounter in specific terms that the Secretary has recorded but which are omitted here in the interest of decorum. It was moved (Elder Simmons) and seconded (Elder Forde) that the specific terms be preserved in the sealed supplemental record.
>
> **(c)** It was testified that D. Fontaine engaged in "repeated and escalating" sexual conduct with multiple partners during the period of January–May 2022, the details of which were characterized by the testifying party as "beyond what I can say in this room without feeling sick."

There are seven items in Section 3.1. They proceed chronologically. The specificity increases. By item (f), the passive constructions have begun to strain under the weight of what they're carrying, and you can feel the prose style itself becoming aroused—agitated, I mean—by its own precision. "It was noted that the individual was observed in a state of partial undress" is a sentence written by a person who could describe exactly which part was partial but has chosen, with visible effort, to maintain the decorum of the minutes format, the way a man straightens his tie before delivering a death sentence.

> **Item 4.2 — Motion for Membership Revocation and Public Admonishment:**
>
> Following deliberation, it was moved (Elder Hatch) and seconded (Elder Pruitt) that the membership of D. Fontaine be revoked under Article XII of the Church Constitution, and that the grounds for revocation be made available to the full congregation as a matter of communal spiritual health. Discussion: Elder Forde noted that transparency in such matters serves the dual purpose of safeguarding the body of believers and offering the disciplined party a pathway to repentance through accountability. Elder R. Hatch observed that "sunlight is the Lord's disinfectant." Motion carried, 6–1. (Dissenting: Elder Womack, without recorded objection.)

There it is. Seconded, carried, uploaded between the potluck and the parking lot.

I should tell you who Darcy was when I knew her, which was only ever at school, which means I should tell you who Darcy was when Darcy was performing the version of herself that had escaped. She was my roommate sophomore year, assigned by the housing algorithm that matches night owls with night owls, which in our case meant two people who stayed up until 3 a.m. for entirely different reasons—she because she was alive in a way that only seemed to fully activate after midnight, and I because I was annotating.² She was five-foot-ten, narrow-hipped, loud in a way that reorganized the geometry of any room she entered. She called everyone "baby" in a manner that should have been grating but instead felt like being briefly, unjudgmentally seen. She kept a collection of miniature glass animals on her desk in a formation she said was "strategic" but never explained. She changed clothes with her door open. She told me, on the third night, the whole Canaan Springs story in a version that was ninety minutes long and almost certainly compressed, edited for a listener she'd known for seventy-two hours, and when she finished, she said, "Anyway, I'm not that person anymore, so," and showed me a video on her phone of a dog who could play pool.

She was not not that person anymore.

Nobody is ever not that person anymore. That's the thing the church understands that secular liberal-arts campuses don't: that the past is not a place you leave. It is a document that other people maintain.

I found the minutes myself—Darcy hadn't shown them to me. I was doing what I sometimes did late at night when I couldn't sleep and my own thoughts were unbearable but I wasn't willing to name them as unbearable: I was researching. Darcy's hometown. The church. I told myself it was contextual. I told myself I was building a frame so I could understand her better, help her better, be a better friend. I was, if we're being precise, reading about her the way the Council of Elders read about her—at a distance, with great attention, in the dark.³

## II.

The communications began in mid-October, approximately six weeks after Darcy told me she was going home for a few days. "Family thing," she said, packing a bag that included four changes of underwear, a black dress I'd never seen before, and a paperback copy of *The Art of War* that I initially assumed was a joke and subsequently was not sure about. "Back by Thursday."

She was not back by Thursday. She was not back by the following Thursday. I received, instead, the following:

I have presented these in the order I received them. I want to note, for the record, that I did not immediately perceive a contradiction. Darcy was volatile—gloriously, exhaustingly volatile—and it seemed possible, in the first days, that both communications reflected a single reality: that she was staying at her mother's house *and* plotting an elaborate sexual revenge campaign, that the room with the wallpaper was base camp and the Motel 6 was the theater of operations. People contain multitudes. Darcy contained boroughs.

It was only later—when the letters and the texts began to describe the same hours differently, when the timelines diverged not in emphasis but in physics—that I understood I was receiving two incompatible accounts of a single departure, and that my job, as the person holding both, was to⁴
