## COLD OPEN

### EXT. CAMP ALDERSGATE — NIGHT — SUMMER, 2004

Crickets. A lake black as oil under a quarter moon. Four cabins on a hillside. A flagpole. A mess hall with a sagging roof.

Title card: **CAMP ALDERSGATE, NEW HAMPSHIRE — AUGUST 9, 2004**

Through the trees, four flashlight beams bounce and weave. Giggling. Shushing. The sounds of twelve-year-olds who believe they are being stealthy.

**YOUNG MEREDITH** (12, Chinese-American, hair in a braid, cargo shorts with every pocket bulging) leads the group. She consults a hand-drawn map that's been folded and refolded so many times the creases have become canyons.

Behind her: **YOUNG DESHAWN** (13, Black, tall for his age, nimble hands that are always touching things—tree bark, fence posts, his own pockets). **YOUNG RAE** (12, white, round-faced, chewing her thumbnail until it bleeds). **YOUNG TOMMY** (13, white, red-haired, a manic energy that makes him look like he's vibrating).

They're carrying something heavy between them. A metal lockbox, the kind you'd buy at a hardware store. It takes two of them to manage it.

**YOUNG TOMMY**
We gotta go deeper. Someone'll find it here.

**YOUNG MEREDITH**
We're already past the property markers.

**YOUNG RAE**
I think I'm going to be sick.

**YOUNG DESHAWN**
Don't. We talked about this. We bury it, we never talk about it, we go back to our cabins, and what happened—

**YOUNG MEREDITH**
—what happened was an accident.

They all stop walking. The word *accident* hangs in the air like smoke. None of them entirely believe it's the right word, but none of them have a better one.

Tommy drops to his knees and starts digging with his hands. Mere joins him. Then Rae. Then Deshawn.

Behind them, unnoticed: a figure stands at the tree line. **GLENN MARSH** (32, their camp counselor, khaki shorts, a lanyard with a whistle, a face that looks like it's watching something shatter in slow motion).

Glenn doesn't move. Doesn't call out. Just watches.

The kids lower the lockbox into the hole. Fill it with dirt. Cover it with leaves and branches. Young Meredith places a specific rock—flat, triangular, striated with quartz—on top.

They stand. They don't hug. They don't cry. They look at each other with the terrible blank seriousness of children who have crossed a line they can't identify and will spend the next twenty years trying to name.

One by one, they walk back toward camp. Four flashlights moving apart through the trees like binary stars losing their orbit.

Glenn watches them go. His hands are trembling. On his forearm, a fresh burn so severe the skin has begun to blister and weep. He cradles it against his chest.

**SMASH TO BLACK.**

## ACT ONE: "WHAT WE CARRY"

---

### INT. MEREDITH'S APARTMENT — WASHINGTON, D.C. — MORNING — PRESENT DAY

We hear an alarm clock, but it takes four full seconds to find it—because every surface in this apartment is covered. Stacked. Layered. Colonized.

This is not filth. This is *architecture*. Newspapers bundled with baker's twine rise in columns like Roman ruins. Shoeboxes labeled in precise handwriting ("RECEIPTS 2019-2020 / TAX-ADJACENT") form load-bearing walls. Books are shelved two-deep on shelves, then stacked horizontally on top of those, then pyramided upward until they press against the ceiling.

**MEREDITH CHEN** (32) navigates this landscape with the practiced fluidity of a woman who has memorized the topography of her own dysfunction. She's sharp-featured, precise, her hair pinned back severely. She wears a blazer that's slightly too small—because the one that fits is in a dry-cleaning bag she picked up eight months ago and cannot bring herself to unpackage because the bag itself has become part of the constellation.

She reaches the alarm clock on the third try, silencing it beneath a stack of *National Geographic* magazines (2006-2011, the good years, she'd tell you, the years they still invested in cartography).

Her morning routine is a symphony of near-collisions. She showers in the one room she keeps ruthlessly clear—the bathroom—and returns to dress by selecting from a clothes rack wedged between a filing cabinet and a collection of ceramic planters (empty, eighteen of them, she'll need them someday, she's *going* to start a garden).

She pauses at the door. Checks her bag. Wallet, keys, phone, hand sanitizer, a granola bar, a backup granola bar, a phone charger, a backup phone charger, a tide pen, a zip-lock bag containing three zip-lock bags.

She exhales. Leaves.

The camera stays behind. Slowly tilts down.

Near the door, half-hidden under a pile of unopened mail, a single envelope. Cream-colored. No return address. Hand-delivered. Already opened—the letter inside has been pulled out, read, stuffed back in, pulled out again. The creases are fresh.

We catch a single line of typed text:

*I KNOW WHAT YOU BURIED.*

### INT. DESHAWN'S LAW OFFICE — BOSTON — DAY

A corner office. Tasteful. Minimal. The desk is bare except for a laptop and a single pen.

**DESHAWN WATTS** (33) sits across from a client. He's broad-shouldered, handsome, his suit impeccable. He radiates competence. He is taking handwritten notes with intense focus. He is also, beneath the desk and out of the client's sightline, turning a stapler over and over in his left hand.

It's the client's stapler. It was on the client's side of the desk when the meeting started. It migrated to Deshawn's side approximately forty seconds in.

**CLIENT**
—so the question is whether the non-compete survives the acquisition, because my read on the—

**DESHAWN**
The non-compete is enforceable in Massachusetts but the scope is overbroad. We'll challenge the geographic restriction. Paragraph nine, subsection C—your former employer defined "competing business" so loosely it would include a lemonade stand. We'll win.

**CLIENT**
(relieved)
That's—yeah. That's great. Thank you.

They stand. Shake hands. The client gathers his things. Looks around.

**CLIENT (CONT'D)**
Did you—I had a stapler?

**DESHAWN**
(without missing a beat)
You didn't bring one in.

**CLIENT**
Huh. I could've sworn—

**DESHAWN**
Mandela Effect. Happens to everyone.

The client leaves. Deshawn waits for the door to close. Then he opens his bottom desk drawer.

It is full. Not with files. With *objects*. A snow globe from the reception desk. A pen from the partner's office. A coaster from the conference room. Three keycards. A small cactus that used to live on the paralegal's desk. The client's stapler joins the collection.

Deshawn stares at the drawer. His jaw works. He closes it with the careful reverence of a man closing a coffin.

His phone buzzes. A text from an unknown number.

*Did you get my letter, Deshawn? Or did you steal someone else's by mistake?*

He stands so fast his chair hits the credenza.

---

### INT. RAE'S KITCHEN — BROOKLYN — DAY

A kitchen designed for a food blog. Ring lights. A camera on a tripod. Copper pots hanging from a rack. A marble countertop dusted with flour.

**RACHEL "RAE" KOWALSKI** (32) stands in front of the camera. She's in a clean apron. Her hair is done. Her smile is precise.

**RAE**
(to camera)
—and the key to this béchamel, which I will die on this hill, is *nutmeg*. Fresh. You grate it yourself. Don't come at me with the pre-ground stuff, that's beige dust, that's the spice equivalent of giving up.

She tastes from a wooden spoon. Her face communicates pleasure. Warmth. *Home*.

**RAE (CONT'D)**
Mm. Okay. That's it. That's the one. I'll finish the gratin in the next video, but for now—save this, share this, and remember: food is love, and love is worth the effort.

She clicks the camera off. The smile stays for one second. Two. Then it falls like a theater curtain, and behind it there's nothing—not sadness exactly, just absence. The flat affect of someone who has performed warmth so long she's forgotten the original sensation.

She looks at the pot of béchamel. At the gratin ingredients arrayed on the counter. She picks up a spoon and begins to eat. Not taste. *Eat*. Standing at the counter, straight from the pot, steadily and without pleasure, with the mechanical efficiency of someone filling a machine.

She eats for forty-five seconds. The pot is half-empty.

She stops. Sets the spoon down. Walks to the bathroom. Closes the door.

We stay in the kitchen. We hear water running. Only water. She's learned to be quiet.

When she comes back, she washes the spoon. Dries it. Hangs it on the rack. Pours the remaining béchamel down the sink. Cleans the pot.

Then she sees the envelope on the counter, half-tucked under a cookbook. She already knows what it says. She's read it four times.

She picks up her phone. Her contact list is open to "M" but she hasn't called. Her thumb hovers over **Meredith Chen — DO NOT CALL**.

She calls.

---

### INT. TOMMY'S CAR — ATLANTIC CITY EXPRESSWAY — NIGHT

**TOMMY BANKS** (33, still red-haired, still vibrating) is driving a Hyundai Elantra with a cracked windshield and a pine tree air freshener that ran out of pine three months ago. He's on speakerphone.

**RAE (V.O.)**
—so Mere got one. I got one. You got one.

**TOMMY**
Yeah.

**DESHAWN (V.O.)**
And we're just not going to talk about the fact that none of us have spoken in eight years.

**TOMMY**
Seven. I drunk-texted Rae on New Year's.

**RAE (V.O.)**
He sent me a picture of a roulette wheel and the words "red or black give me a sign."

**TOMMY**
And you said black! And it hit black! You owe me a spiritual debt.

**MEREDITH (V.O.)**
Can we focus.

The line gets quiet. Even now, even after all this time, Mere can still do that. Collapse a conversation into its essential components.

**MEREDITH (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
Someone knows. About that night. About the fire. About Owen.

No one speaks for five seconds.

**DESHAWN (V.O.)**
The letter says we have to go back.

**RAE (V.O.)**
To the camp.

**TOMMY**
Why would we do that? Why would we go back to the place—

**MEREDITH (V.O.)**
Because the letter also says there's evidence. Physical evidence. In the lockbox. And if we don't retrieve it and bring it to whoever wrote this letter, by sunrise Saturday—

**DESHAWN (V.O.)**
—they send copies of everything to the local PD, the New Hampshire State Attorney, and "every person you've ever pretended to be normal in front of." That's a direct quote.

**TOMMY**
That's every person I've ever met.

Silence.

**MEREDITH (V.O.)**
I'm going. Saturday night. I'm driving up.

**DESHAWN (V.O.)**
Mere—

**MEREDITH
