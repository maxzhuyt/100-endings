# The Brita Filter Incident

## A Documentary Reconstruction by Cheryl Kang-Moretti, Desk Sergeant, 108th Precinct, Long Island City, Queens

Filed pursuant to no particular regulation. Cross-referenced with nothing anyone asked for.

---

## I.

**SUBMITTED BY:** Det. Emmanuel "Manny" Salazar, Shield #4412
**NATURE OF REQUEST:** Replacement of one (1) Brita Longlast+ water filtration pitcher, white, 10-cup capacity, currently stationed on shared counter adjacent to Desks 7 and 8, Second Floor, Homicide/Major Crimes

---

*Cheryl's note, appended in blue pen along left margin:*

I am filing this because it is my function to file things. I want that on the record before anyone accuses this document of being something it isn't. I have been the desk sergeant of the 108th Precinct for fifteen years. In that time I have processed upward of forty thousand interdepartmental forms, eleven thousand incident reports, six hundred and twelve complaints about the second-floor men's room, and one (1) heartfelt letter of resignation that turned out to be a draft of someone's wedding vows run through the wrong printer tray. I processed that too. It is not my job to editorialize. It is my job to receive, stamp, date, route, and archive.

If I have, in the process of receiving, stamping, dating, routing, and archiving, also developed certain *observations* about the people who generate the paperwork I process — well. Paper is transparent if you hold it up to the light long enough. You learn things. You learn that Detective Salazar writes in all caps when he's angry and in a cramped lowercase cursive when he's thinking. You learn that Detective Phelan has never once submitted a form on time but has never once submitted one with a factual error. You learn that two men who have sat at adjacent desks for fourteen years and seven months generate a particular *frequency* of paperwork — facilities requests, overtime disputes, thermostat complaints, passive-aggressive Post-it notes photographed and submitted as "evidence of hostile work environment" with a winking parenthetical indicating the submission is "mostly a joke" — and that this frequency is, or was, a kind of language.

I am not the narrator of this story. I am its filing system. The distinction matters.

But a filing system is allowed to notice when the frequency changes.

*Cheryl's note, appended on a separate sheet and stapled to the printed review:*

The stamp was not upside down. I use a secondary orientation to flag forms requiring follow-up. This is my system. It has been my system since 2011. I don't expect a civilian to understand institutional notation, and I don't expect Yelp to be a reliable source of emotional intelligence.

I was not sad. I was processing an anomaly.

The yelling was Detectives Salazar and Phelan. It was about the thermostat, nominally. The thermostat on the second floor has a sticking mechanism that requires manual adjustment — a specific lateral pressure followed by a clockwise quarter-turn — and for fourteen years neither detective has admitted to being the one who performs this adjustment, though the thermostat is always at 68°F by 9:15 AM, which means someone does it, and the someone arrives before 9:15 AM, which narrows the candidates to two. On February 27, the thermostat read 64°F at 11:00 AM and no one had touched it.

That was the anomaly.

I should not have noticed. But I process the heating-system work orders. I know the rhythm. And the rhythm had stumbled.

**ARTIFACT 1-C: INCIDENT SUMMARY FORM (ISF-3), DATED MARCH 7 — WATER DAMAGE, SECOND FLOOR BREAK ROOM**

**REPORTING OFFICER:** Sgt. C. Kang-Moretti
**INCIDENT:** At approximately 14:20 hours, the Brita Longlast+ water filtration pitcher stationed in the second-floor break room was found shattered on the linoleum floor. Estimated water damage: negligible (the pitcher was nearly empty, as the filter had not been changed and most personnel had ceased using it). Estimated departmental property loss: $34.99 (retail). No injuries reported. Cause of breakage undetermined.

**WITNESS STATEMENTS:**
- Det. Salazar: "I didn't touch it. Ask Phelan."
- Det. Phelan: "It was already on the edge. Things fall. That's gravity, not a confession."
- Officer Janet Trieu (patrol, first floor): "I heard something break and then I heard laughing, and then the laughing stopped in a way that felt weird, and then someone said 'Just leave it' in a voice I didn't recognize."

**FOLLOW-UP:** None required. Replacement pitcher requisitioned. Form 77-B re-filed.

---

*Cheryl's note, in the margin beside Officer Trieu's statement, in pen that started blue and switched to black mid-sentence as though the first pen died:*

Janet Trieu has good ears. I wish she didn't. There is a version of this report where the Brita pitcher fell because someone's elbow caught it, and two men who have spent fourteen years turning shared annoyance into a kind of communion laughed about it and cleaned it up and moved on. That version is probably true.

But there is also the version where the laughing stopped. Where someone said "Just leave it" in a voice that meant something other than "leave the broken pitcher on the floor." And that version — I don't have documentation for it. I only have Janet Trieu's ears and my own sense, developed over fifteen years of processing paperwork generated by these two men, that by March 7, something had already ended, and only one of them knew it.

I filed the incident summary. I requisitioned the replacement pitcher. These are the things I can do.

The replacement pitcher arrived on March 12. As of this writing, its filter has been changed once, by me, on April 1, because neither of them was going to do it and the water was starting to taste like grief.

That isn't what I meant to type. Like metal. The water tasted like metal.

*Cheryl's note, written on a separate sheet and inserted between the two authorization forms:*

Twenty-five minutes. He filed twenty-five minutes after Manny did.

I have been asked, by no one, to comment on the coincidence. So I will comment on it for the record that no one requested.

These two men worked the Astoria drug-lab dismantlement together in 2014. They worked the Jackson Heights double homicide in 2017 — the one with the refrigerator, the one nobody in the precinct can talk about without someone leaving the room. They worked the Corona identity-theft ring in 2019 with the kind of seamless coordination that made the FBI liaison send a note calling their case-building "unusually elegant for NYPD, no offense." Manny builds the paper architecture. Doug walks the streets. Their closure rate for shared cases: 94%. I have the spreadsheet. I built the spreadsheet. No one asked me to build it. I built it because patterns are legible if someone bothers to read them.

They could have collaborated on this case. The jurisdictional overlap made it natural. The case practically *requested* them both. And they forked.

I noticed something else while processing Doug's authorization form. His handwriting has changed. I have fourteen years of Doug Phelan's handwriting passing through my intake window — big, looping letters that took up excess space, letters that implied the form was too small for what he had to say. The Doug Phelan who filed the QCI authorization wrote in letters that were small and careful and stayed inside the boxes. It was the handwriting of someone conserving something. Space, maybe. Or time.

I am noting this for the record. The record does not require it. I am noting it anyway.

*Cheryl's note, in the margin of the photocopy she made of this Post-it before re-pinning it to the monitor:*

This is how they talk. This is how they have always talked. I have 247 Post-it notes in a folder I maintain outside the official filing system. I have never been asked to maintain this folder. I began it in 2012 when I noticed that the Post-it notes left on and around Desks 7 and 8 constituted a more accurate record of the Salazar-Phelan partnership than any case file or performance review.

They do not say things to each other. They leave things for each other to find.

I understand this practice. I relate to it more than I intend to document here.
