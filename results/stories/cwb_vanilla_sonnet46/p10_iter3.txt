# Pattern Recognition

The hostess seats us at table seven. Corner booth, good. Single point of ingress, sight lines to both exits, no one behind me. I note the security camera in the upper left quadrant has a fogged lens—probably eighteen months of grease vapor from the kitchen—which means there's a blind spot covering roughly forty percent of the dining room. I file this away for absolutely no reason whatsoever because I am on a *date* and this is a *restaurant* and none of this matters.

Her name is Priya. She has a laugh that arrives before she's finished deciding to laugh, which I find disarming in a way I wasn't prepared for.

Focus.

The menu is laminated, which means the font is probably twelve years old and the prices are six months newer—they've been stickered over. Small family operation trying to look more established than it is. Acceptable attack surface. I mean ambiance. Acceptable ambiance.

"Do you play any games?" she asks, and I catch myself before I say *social engineering is technically a game*, and instead say, "Some. Card games, mostly."

She puts down her water glass with a small, deliberate click.

"Like... Magic?"

And there it is. The shared exploit. The handshake that opens the port.

---

Within four minutes we have abandoned all pretense of normal first-date conversation and I am genuinely, embarrassingly happy about it. She's been playing since Theros. I started with Innistrad. We overlap in the middle like a Venn diagram of misspent evenings.

But I can't turn it off. I'm listening to her talk and I'm also *cataloging*.

She mentions she loves Azorius control. *Noted.* Patient. Reactive. Wants to understand what you're doing before she commits. Lets you spend resources and then answers at instant speed. She doesn't overextend.

She says her favorite card of all time is Ephemerate. *Interesting.* Values recursion. Doesn't like permanent loss. Wants second chances built into the system.

She laughs about how she always runs one too many lands because she hates mana screw more than she hates drawing a land on turn nine. *Risk-averse. Prioritizes stability over explosive potential. Willing to accept a ceiling on her upside to protect her floor.*

I am building a psychological profile of my date using a fantasy card game and I recognize this is both deeply strange and also the most genuinely interested I have been in another person in longer than I want to admit.

She asks what I play.

"Grixis," I say, and watch her face do the thing faces do when they're recalibrating. *Blue-black-red. Control with teeth. Answers and threats. Someone who wants to understand the system and then use that understanding offensively.*

"Yeah," she says slowly, like she's reading something. "That tracks."

She's doing it too. I feel a warmth that is probably dopamine and is definitely inconvenient.

---

The food arrives. I'm almost relaxed. This is the closest to relaxed I get, which means I'm operating at maybe sixty percent hypervigilance instead of the usual ninety. Progress.

Then her phone buzzes on the table and she flips it face-up to check the notification, and she unlocks it, and I see it.

The pattern.

L-shape. Top-left corner to bottom-middle. Four nodes. She traces it without looking, which means it's muscle memory, which means it's been the same pattern for at least two years. The movement is slightly leftward-biased, which tells me she's right-handed and draws toward her dominant side under low-attention conditions. The smudge trail on her screen—visible for just a moment in the candlelight—confirms it. I could reproduce that pattern in under three seconds.

I know this the way I know things. Automatically. Involuntarily. Like how you can't unhear a word once someone tells you what it means.

She looks up and I realize, with the specific horror of a man watching a packet send before he can cancel it, that my face has already done something.

"What?" she says.

The correct answer is *nothing*. The correct answer is *just thinking about that Ephemerate ruling*. The correct answer exists and I do not take it.

"Your unlock pattern," I say, "is really insecure."

---

The silence has a texture. It's the texture of a firewall logging an intrusion attempt.

"Excuse me?"

"Four nodes, L-shape, muscle memory trace, visible smudge confirmation—I could—" I stop. I watch her expression move through surprise, then something sharper. "I'm sorry. I shouldn't have said that. I genuinely could not stop myself and I am aware that is not a defense."

She looks at me for a long moment. The Azorius player in her is not overextending. She's waiting. Letting me spend resources.

"You're one of those," she says finally. Not unkindly. Analytically.

"Reformed," I say. "Professionally. Mostly."

"Mostly."

"The noticing doesn't stop. I've just gotten better at not..." I gesture at the current situation. "This."

She picks up her wine glass. Takes a sip. I cannot read her and I read everyone. It's deeply uncomfortable and also, I realize, kind of wonderful.

"Okay," she says.

"Okay?"

"Okay, you're a weirdo. I knew that when you correctly identified that my Azorius deck runs a soft lock rather than a hard lock because I, and I quote, 'want the opponent to feel the walls closing in rather than hitting them all at once.'" She raises an eyebrow. "You profiled me from my card choices."

The walls of my chest do something structural.

"I was going to change my pattern anyway," she adds. "What would you recommend?"

And I understand, with the clarity of a successful handshake completing, that I have not sabotaged this. That she is, in fact, running an Ephemerate on the moment—blinking it out, bringing it back, giving it another chance.

"Six nodes minimum," I say. "Non-linear. Something that doesn't follow your dominant hand's natural arc."

She slides her phone across the table.

"Show me."
