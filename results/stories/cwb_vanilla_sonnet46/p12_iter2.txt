# Rehearsing Rejection

---

The cab is seven minutes away and I'm already having the argument.

*You've been weird with me.* No. Too accusatory. Start soft. *I've noticed you seem a little distant lately, and I just wanted to check in.* Better. More reasonable. More like someone who doesn't have anything wrong with them. I practice the face in the hallway mirror—concerned but calm, eyebrows doing the thing where they look caring rather than desperate—and then I see my shoulders, how they're pulled up toward my ears like I'm bracing for a car crash, and I turn away before I can look at anything else.

Six minutes.

She texted back in four hours yesterday. Four. I've been cataloguing it. Tuesday: ninety minutes. Wednesday: two hours, twenty. Yesterday: four hours and the reply was *haha yeah for sure* which is what you say to someone you're tolerating, not someone you slept with eleven days ago, not someone you told things to in the dark that you'd never said out loud before. *For sure.* I've turned those two words over so many times they've worn smooth, like sea glass, like something that used to be sharp that I've handled into something I can hold without bleeding. Almost.

She's going to tell me she needs space.

No—she's going to be *nice* about it, which is worse. She's going to do the thing where her voice gets gentle in a way that means she's already decided, already grieved it, already moved through the conversation in her own head and come out the other side while I'm still standing at the beginning. She'll say *you're so important to me* in the past tense of the present tense, the way people eulogize things that are still technically alive.

I know what I'll say. I've written it already, in the part of my brain that never stops writing. *That's fine. I completely understand.* Dignified. Not the other thing, not the thing underneath that, not the part that wants to ask her what I did wrong in a voice that would embarrass me to hear played back. I'll be so reasonable she'll feel guilty. Maybe that's the point. Maybe I'm already doing the thing I do.

I know I do a thing.

I'm aware of it the way you're aware of a bad habit—with full knowledge and zero power to stop. My therapist has a name for it. Several names, actually, arranged in a clinical language that makes me feel both seen and filed away. The rehearsing, the anticipating. *You're protecting yourself,* she says, *from a pain that hasn't happened yet.* And I think, yes, but what if it does happen, what if I'm not rehearsing but *predicting*, what if I'm not anxious but *perceptive*, what if I know something she doesn't know she's already decided?

The cab is four minutes away.

I check our text thread again. Scroll up to eleven days ago when she said *I don't want to go home* and I felt something so large I didn't have a word for it, something that scared me with its size. Scroll down to yesterday. *Haha yeah for sure.* The distance between those two points is the whole story. I'm certain of this. I am building a case and I am the only witness and I am also the judge and I keep ruling in favor of my own worst fears because they have the most compelling evidence, because they always show up prepared.

---

She's already at the table when I walk in. She stands up.

She *stands up*. She crosses the room and hugs me before I've even fully registered that I'm inside the building, and her arms are tight, tighter than you hug someone you're about to let down gently, and she says *I'm so glad you're here, I've had the worst week* into my shoulder, and I feel it move through me like a key turning.

I let myself have three seconds of it.

Then: *she's performing.* Then: *this is the setup, the warmth before the withdrawal.* Then: *she's hugging me like this because she feels guilty.*

We sit. She looks tired—genuinely tired, not tired-of-me tired, there are shadows under her eyes and she's been chewing her thumbnail, I can tell—and she starts talking about work, about her sister, about a thing that happened that made her feel small and stupid and she'd wanted to call me but it was late and she didn't want to bother me.

Didn't want to *bother* me.

I feel the ground shift slightly. I file it away and keep my face neutral.

She reaches across the table and touches my hand. Not a gesture. A landing. She leaves her hand there and says *I feel like I haven't been present enough lately, I'm sorry, I've been in my head* and I look at her and she means it, I can see that she means it, and something in me wants to crack open and say *me too, me too, I've been so scared—*

And I say, *It's okay. I get it.*

Because I can't. Because the relief is so enormous it frightens me, and frightening things need to be managed, and the way I manage them is to hold them at a slight distance, to say *thank you* to the universe in a voice that sounds like *I knew it all along.*

She squeezes my hand.

I squeeze back. I am here. I am also still in the cab, still in the hallway, still rehearsing the ending of something that hasn't ended. Both things are true at the same time and I don't know how to put one of them down.

The food comes. She laughs at something. I watch her laugh and I think: *I'll lose this.*

Then I think: *not yet.*

Then I think: *not yet* is enough.

I almost believe it.
