# Hell is Other Demons

The morning report was, as usual, catastrophic.

Lucifer read it at his desk with a cup of coffee that had gone cold approximately three seconds after Beelzebub placed it there, because everything in Hell went cold eventually. That was rather the point. He scanned the figures—seventeen million souls processed, two mass rebellions in the Eighth Circle, a bureaucratic backlog in Purgatorial Intake that had apparently been growing since 1987—and tried to feel something about any of it.

Beneath his desk, Gnash was chewing on the iron throne again.

"Stop that," Lucifer said, without looking up.

Gnash did not stop. Gnash was a hellhound the size of a small carriage, with three heads, eyes like cooling embers, and the emotional intelligence of a golden retriever. He had been chewing the throne's left leg for eleven days. The iron leg. Lucifer had watched the teeth marks deepen with a kind of resigned awe.

"The Sixth Circle is requesting a budget increase," Beelzebub said, hovering two inches off the floor in the way he did when he was nervous about something. "The wrathful are becoming *too* wrathful. They're damaging infrastructure."

"Tell them that's the idea."

"They've destroyed four bridges."

"We have an infinite number of bridges."

"Three of them were load-bearing."

Lucifer set down the report. "In Hell."

"Yes."

"Load-bearing bridges. In Hell."

Beelzebub made a gesture that suggested he agreed this was philosophically complicated. "The engineers are very committed."

Gnash made a sound like a millstone grinding and redoubled his efforts on the throne leg. A flake of ancient iron skittered across the obsidian floor. Lucifer watched it go.

He was about to say something cutting about the engineers when the doors opened—not because anyone had knocked, because she never knocked—and Lilith walked in carrying what appeared to be a severed head and a bottle of wine.

"The head is for you," she said. "The wine is mine."

"Whose head?"

"Someone who deserved it." She set it on the corner of his desk. It was, Lucifer noted, making a moderately apologetic expression. "He kept calling me *sweetie.*"

"Fair enough."

She poured herself a glass of wine that immediately began to boil slightly at the surface, the way wine did around her, and looked around the office with the particular expression she wore when she was cataloguing everything that had changed and everything that hadn't. Her eyes settled on the throne.

"Is your dog eating the furniture again."

"He's not eating it. He's *chewing* it. There's a distinction."

"There are teeth marks."

"He finds it soothing."

Lilith looked at Gnash. Gnash looked at Lilith with two of his three heads. The third head continued chewing. It was, Lucifer thought, a fairly accurate summary of their whole dynamic.

"I came," she said, settling into the chair across from him without being invited, which was also accurate to their dynamic, "because there's a situation in the northern territories."

"There's always a situation in the northern territories."

"This one involves your ex-subordinate Asmodeus attempting to establish what he's calling a *sovereign infernal principality.*"

Lucifer was quiet for a moment. "He's trying to start his own Hell."

"A smaller one. With, apparently, a loyalty program."

"A—"

"Points. For suffering. Redeemable for slightly less suffering." She sipped her wine. "He's calling it *Damnation Rewards.*"

Lucifer stood up, which caused Gnash to briefly pause and look up with the hopeful expression of a creature who believed that standing up meant walkies. It did not mean walkies. Gnash resumed chewing.

"I'll have him flayed," Lucifer said.

"You had him flayed last time."

"I'll have him flayed *differently.*"

"He's also," Lilith added, with the careful neutrality of someone placing a grenade on a table, "been telling people that you've gone soft."

The office was quiet except for the rhythmic grinding of iron between hellhound teeth.

"Has he."

"He mentioned the throne." She nodded toward it. "Said a ruler who lets his dog chew his throne is a ruler who's lost his edge."

"Gnash," Lucifer said, "is a symbol of—"

"Of what?"

He looked at the dog. Gnash looked back with two heads, tail sweeping the floor like a slow pendulum, a piece of iron filigree hanging from his middle mouth.

"Unconditional loyalty," Lucifer said, with less conviction than he'd intended.

Lilith made a sound that was not quite a laugh. It was the sound that preceded a laugh, the anteroom of amusement. He had always found it maddening. He had always found it extraordinary.

"I could stay," she said, studying her wine, "and help manage the Asmodeus situation. If you wanted."

"You hate it here."

"I hate *you* here. Specifically. The geography is fine."

"That's not—that's not what I asked."

"You didn't ask anything."

This was true. He hadn't. He stood behind his desk in his kingdom of eternal suffering, surrounded by the morning's catastrophe reports, with a severed head on his desk and a dog destroying his furniture, and he had not asked anything. He was very good at not asking things. It was, perhaps, his oldest habit.

Gnash finally wrenched the iron leg free with a sound like a cathedral bell dropped from a great height. The throne listed badly to one side. Lucifer looked at it. Lilith looked at it. The throne, now three-legged, maintained a kind of crooked dignity.

"I'll have someone weld it," he said.

"Yes," she said.

She didn't leave.

He sat back down.

Outside, seventeen million souls went about their eternal business, Asmodeus plotted his small treasonous empire, the load-bearing bridges burned, and Hell continued in its ancient, exhausting, irreplaceable way—which was, Lucifer supposed, more or less what you could say about everything worth keeping.
