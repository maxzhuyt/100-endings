# Blood Debt

The temple smelled of wet stone and old smoke.

Torchlight guttered along the collapsed walls, throwing orange shadows across perhaps forty spectators pressed shoulder to shoulder in the ruins. They'd come quietly, in twos and threes, filtering through the pine forest after midnight. Now they crowded the perimeter of what had once been the main hall, their breath rising in pale columns against the cold air. Coins changed hands in whispers. Someone's dog, left tied outside, barked twice and went silent.

In the center of the cracked floor, a thin ribbon of water seeped through a fissure running diagonally from the northeast corner toward the altar steps. It caught the torchlight like a vein of dark silver.

Kenji Arima stood at the south end of the hall.

He was twenty-three, lean as a bowstring, with the particular stillness of a man who has been taught since childhood that movement without purpose is waste. His right hand rested near his hip though he carried no sword tonight—Jigen-ryū's principles lived in the body itself, in the coiled readiness of the shoulders, the forward pitch of the weight, the eyes that didn't blink so much as they consumed. He wore a plain dark keikogi, and his jaw was set with the expression of someone who has already decided the outcome.

Across the hall, Wei Longshan rolled his neck and let his arms hang loose.

He was broader through the chest than Kenji, twenty-five, with hands that had been conditioned on wooden posts since he was nine years old. His family had run a transport company three generations back, and the men in his lineage had learned Piguaquan because merchants needed fighters who could actually survive—not perform. He bounced lightly on his heels, his weight distributed evenly, his arms beginning a slow, almost lazy rotation at his sides that looked like warming up and was, in fact, already combat.

The crowd pressed inward.

Someone said *begin* and the word barely finished before Kenji moved.

He crossed the distance in two explosive steps, the burst so sudden that several spectators flinched backward. His right hand drove forward in a vertical hammering strike aimed at Wei's collarbone—the foundational attack of his school, delivered with the entire torso's rotation compressed into a single devastating point. Fast. Decisive. Intended to end.

Wei stepped left and let it pass his right ear by four inches.

Not a retreat. A redirection. His left arm came up in a sweeping outward arc, the forearm meeting Kenji's extended arm at the wrist and riding it outward while his right palm whipped horizontally toward Kenji's temple—a piguaquan chopping strike powered by the shoulder's full rotation, the arm loose until the final instant of impact.

Kenji ducked under it by dropping his chin to his chest, already resetting his feet.

The crowd exhaled.

They circled. Kenji moved in a tight, controlled arc, keeping his dominant right side forward, eyes locked on Wei's sternum rather than his hands—his teacher's teaching, *watch the river's source, not its tributaries*. Wei moved differently, drifting in wider curves, his arms still rotating in that loose pendulum rhythm, his footwork crossing and uncrossing in patterns that made his next angle of attack genuinely difficult to predict.

Kenji attacked again—a feint low, then a driving palm strike upward toward Wei's chin.

Wei slipped outside it, and this time his right arm descended in a full whipping arc, the back of his forearm catching Kenji across the left shoulder with enough force to stagger him sideways. Kenji's left foot came down on the wet fissure.

He slipped.

Not badly—he caught himself on one knee, left hand briefly touching the floor—but the moment existed, and Wei was already moving through it. A low sweeping kick targeted Kenji's planted right foot. Kenji rolled with the impact rather than against it, letting his body continue the fall's momentum into a controlled sideways roll that brought him back upright two meters away, near the altar steps.

The crowd was loud now. Wei's faction shouted in Mandarin. Kenji's supporters called his name with the clipped urgency of people watching money evaporate.

Kenji's shoulder ached. He didn't show it.

He changed. Instead of the explosive linear charges, he began pressuring in shorter increments, cutting angles, using the altar steps behind him as a boundary he could work against. He threw three rapid strikes in succession—not committing fully to any of them, reading Wei's responses, cataloguing the evasions. *You learn a man by making him move*, his master had said. *Then you make him move where you've already been.*

Wei read the recalibration and responded by closing distance himself, taking away Kenji's room to generate power. Inside, the whipping strikes lost their arc. They traded forearm blocks in close quarters, each man absorbing impact, the sound like wood on wood.

Then Kenji found it.

Wei's left-side evasion was fractionally slower than his right. A tendency, not a flaw—but enough.

He threw a right-hand strike to Wei's left, drawing the evasion, then pivoted on his left foot and drove his right elbow in a tight horizontal arc directly into Wei's cheekbone.

The impact was clean and total.

Wei's head snapped sideways. His legs buckled. He went down onto one knee, left hand catching the floor over the wet fissure, water darkening his knuckles.

Silence held the room for one breath.

Then Wei looked up. His cheek was already swelling, one eye narrowing. He was breathing hard. So was Kenji, standing over him, chest heaving, fist still clenched.

Neither man moved.

The water trickled through the crack between them, indifferent, carrying nothing but itself toward the dark drain at the hall's far edge.

Outside, the dog began barking again.
