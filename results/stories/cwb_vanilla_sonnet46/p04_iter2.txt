# My Apocalypse Buddy

The world had ended on a Tuesday, which Midas had always felt was cosmically rude. Mondays were built for catastrophe. Tuesdays implied the universe had given things a fair shot and still decided to quit.

He sat cross-legged on the floor of what had once been a Subway sandwich franchise—the irony of shelter being provided by a restaurant named for underground transit was not lost on him—and regarded Chewy with the particular expression of a man consulting someone he knew was going to be right and resented for it.

Chewy regarded him back. The koala's synthetic fur had gone the color of old dishwater, one ear was held on by a paperclip that Midas had found in the ruins of what he suspected had been an accountant's office, and her eyes—small, round, originally designed to simulate warmth for children who had presumably never experienced the specific horror of the Collapse—flickered a gentle, contemplative amber.

"The pharmacy on Hendricks," Midas said.

The eyes went yellow. Cautionary.

"I know."

They went more yellow.

"I *know*, Chewy. But we need antibiotics and we need them before my thumb falls off, which, based on current trajectory, is—" he held up the offending digit, wrapped in cloth that had once been a motivational poster reading *HANG IN THERE*, "—Thursday."

The eyes considered this. They settled into a resigned, muted gold. The koala equivalent of *fine, but I want it noted that I objected.*

"Noted," Midas said, and stood up, and immediately hit his head on a hanging menu board. This was, in the grand tradition of his life, an omen he chose to ignore.

---

The pharmacy on Hendricks was four blocks away, which in the post-Collapse cartography of their particular city meant four blocks of negotiating rubble, two blocks of avoiding the territory of a man named Gerald who had appointed himself Duke of the intersection and took the role with a seriousness that the previous government might have envied, and approximately one block of what Midas privately called The Smell, which required no further elaboration.

They made it through the rubble without incident, which should have been the first warning.

Gerald was asleep in his lawn chair, his ceremonial sash—a repurposed beauty pageant banner reading MISS TRI-COUNTY 2019—rising and falling with his snores. Midas tiptoed past with the exaggerated care of a man who had learned through experience that Gerald woke at precisely the volume of a mouse's ambition. Chewy, tucked under his arm, had gone a very still, very dark green. Focused. Alert.

They made it through. Second warning, ignored.

The pharmacy door was unlocked, which was a miracle, and also a trap, which are frequently the same thing.

Inside, it was dim and smelled of dust and the ghost of synthetic grape flavor. Midas moved through the aisles with the focused efficiency of a man who had done this before and still managed to do it wrong. He found the antibiotics in the third aisle—a small, beautiful, nearly-full bottle of amoxicillin that he clutched to his chest like a religious artifact.

Chewy's eyes went red.

Midas turned around.

There were three of them, which was two more than he preferred in any social situation, post-Collapse or otherwise. They had the look of people who had also decided the pharmacy on Hendricks was a good idea today, and had additionally decided that Midas having found the antibiotics first was a personal affront.

"Hey," said the tallest one, whose name was almost certainly not relevant to the narrative but was, in fact, Derek.

"Hey," said Midas.

There was a pause in which everyone assessed everyone else's commitment to the situation.

Chewy's eyes went a deep, pulsing red, and then—because she was, at her core, a piece of animatronic technology that had never quite gotten over the Collapse—she emitted a sound. Not a threatening sound. Not a useful sound. The sound she made was the opening four notes of *Baby Shark*, which was what happened when her audio system was stressed, a fact Midas had been meaning to address for six months.

Derek blinked.

Midas ran.

---

He made it one block before he remembered Gerald.

He made it half a block after Gerald before Gerald, now awake and deeply offended by the territorial violation, began shouting in the particular register that carried approximately four city blocks in every direction.

He made it two more blocks before he tripped on a piece of decorative concrete that had, in better times, spelled out the word COMMUNITY, and went down hard on his left knee, which was the knee that already had opinions about stairs.

He lay on the ground for a moment, staring at the sky, which was doing something beautiful and indifferent with the clouds.

Chewy had landed face-up beside him. Her eyes cycled through red, then orange, then settled, slowly, into a soft and luminous blue.

Blue meant something like: *well, we're alive.*

Blue meant something like: *I suppose that's the baseline.*

Midas held up the antibiotics. Still in his hand. He had not, in the chaos, dropped them.

"See," he said, to no one, to Chewy, to the uncaring sky. "Knack for it."

The eyes flickered. Something that might have been amusement, if amusement had a frequency.

He sat up. His knee made a sound like a bureaucratic complaint. His thumb throbbed with the righteous indignation of the infected. His head still hurt from the menu board, which felt, in retrospect, like the universe's most honest moment of the day.

Four blocks home. Gerald would need at least twenty minutes to settle.

"Right," Midas said, and got up, and tucked Chewy under his arm, and started walking.

Her eyes glowed a steady, amber warmth against his ribs.

The world had ended on a Tuesday.

They were still here on a Wednesday.

It wasn't much, but it was, Midas thought, a start.
