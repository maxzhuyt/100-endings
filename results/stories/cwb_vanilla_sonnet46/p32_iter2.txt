# The Underhold

The door groaned on its hinges like something wounded.

Aya's hand fell from the iron handle as the gas lamps sputtered to life one by one, a slow illumination that crept down the length of the underhold like a tide coming in. The chamber was vast and low-ceilinged, its stone walls sweating with the particular damp of deep places, and the light did not so much banish the darkness as negotiate with it — casting long, trembling shadows that stretched and writhed across the floor like living things. Like things Aya knew.

She felt Simeon press close behind her, heard the soft hiss of Katra's breath through her teeth.

"Too quiet," Katra murmured. The words had barely left her lips before the answer came.

"I've always preferred it that way."

Maren Voss stood at the far end of the chamber.

She was not, as it happened, incarcerated. She was not, as it happened, in any particular distress. She stood before the iron vault with her arms loosely clasped behind her back, dressed in the same dark wool coat she wore to every tribunal, every negotiation, every quiet and terrible meeting where the fate of lesser people was decided. Her silver hair was pinned with characteristic severity. At her left shoulder stood a man the size of a wardrobe, and at her right, two more whose faces Aya did not recognise, which was itself a kind of answer. These were not guild muscle. These were something else entirely.

The artifact vault gleamed behind her, sealed and untouched.

*She was never arrested,* Aya thought, the realisation settling through her like cold water finding the lowest point of a room. *It was theatre. All of it was theatre, and we walked into it like—*

"Come in," said Maren, with the particular graciousness of someone who has already won. "All three of you. Don't be shy."

They had no choice. The door behind them clicked with the unmistakable authority of a bolt thrown from the outside.

Simeon made a sound low in his throat. Katra's hand moved toward the blade at her hip and stopped, because the large man had shifted his weight in a way that communicated, eloquently and without words, that this would be inadvisable.

They crossed the floor. Their footsteps rang against the stone. The gas lamps hissed and flickered, and the shadows moved with them, and Aya kept her chin level and her breathing measured and thought very carefully about what she was going to say.

"You're surprised," Maren observed, studying them each in turn with those pale, assessing eyes. "I'd hoped for that, at least. It's so rarely one gets to see genuine surprise anymore." She paused on Aya. Held there. "And you. You I am *very* curious about."

"The feeling," said Aya, "is mutual."

"Take the mask off."

"I'd rather not."

Maren tilted her head, a gesture of mild interest, as one might regard an insect attempting to negotiate the terms of its pinning. She said nothing. The large man stepped forward and removed the mask himself, with rather less ceremony than Aya would have preferred. It came away in his fist and left her standing there, bare-faced in the gaslight, blinking.

The silence that followed had a particular texture.

She watched Maren's expression shift — not dramatically, not with any vulgarity of reaction, but in the subtle recalibration of someone updating a ledger. Those eyes moved across Aya's face with the efficiency of a customs officer cataloguing contraband.

"Well," said Maren, at last. "Well."

*She knows,* Aya thought. *Of course she knows. She knows everything that happens in this city. She has always known.*

"Bind them," Maren said.

"Wait." The word came out of Aya before the decision to speak had fully formed, sharp and clear and carrying rather more composure than she felt. "Before you do anything irreversible, I'd ask you to consider the value of what's standing in front of you."

Maren regarded her with something approaching amusement. "I'm listening."

"You've just arrested — or staged the arrest of — the most visible figure in this guild." Aya kept her voice even, kept her hands still at her sides, kept every muscle in her body from betraying the particular cold terror that was currently making its way up her spine. "The guild is in chaos. Every lieutenant above my rank is either running or positioning. You have a succession problem."

"Do I."

"You also have, standing before you, the only surviving daughter of Lady Corvan Aldiss." She let the name fall into the room and watched it land. "A woman who, before the magistracy saw fit to hang her, was considered the foremost practitioner of immateria craft in the northern territories." Aya paused. "I inherited more than her library."

The silence this time was different. Heavier.

*There it is,* she thought grimly. *The card I swore I would never play. The thing that gets women killed in this city.* She could feel Katra's tension radiating from two feet to her left, could feel Simeon's horror without looking at him. She pressed on regardless. "Kill me, and you dispose of an asset you cannot replace and cannot replicate. Hand me to the magistracy, and you hand them a scandal that implicates this guild in harbouring an immateria practitioner for the better part of two years." She met Maren's gaze and held it. "Or you consider what I'm worth to you intact."

Maren was quiet for a long moment. One of the lamps guttered and threw a wild shadow across the vault door, something that briefly resembled a reaching hand before it steadied.

"Bind them," Maren said again, in the same tone as before. "Her separately. And gently." She turned back toward the vault, unhurried. "I want to have a proper conversation with Lady Aldiss's daughter."

Aya exhaled. Slowly. Through her nose.

*Not dead,* she thought. *Not yet. That's something.*

The manacles were cold, and the underhold smelled of rust and old stone, and somewhere above them the city went about its business in brass and oil and ignorance, and Aya began, very carefully, to think.
