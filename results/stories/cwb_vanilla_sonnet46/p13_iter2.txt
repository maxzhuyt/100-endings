# Blood Debt

The moon had no business being so bright tonight.

It poured through the collapsed roof of the Shōrin Temple like an accusation, illuminating the cracked flagstones, the moss-eaten pillars, the crowd of perhaps forty souls pressed shoulder to shoulder along the perimeter walls. They passed ceramic flasks and muttered in urgent clusters, their breath rising in pale threads against the autumn cold. Money changed hands in the shadows. Reputations were being quietly staked and quietly surrendered.

At the center of the open floor, two young men faced each other across twelve feet of empty stone.

Kenji Murakami wore his red-and-black armband knotted tight above his right elbow, the Jigen-ryū colors. He was twenty-three, lean as stripped timber, with the coiled stillness of a man who had spent eight years learning that the fastest draw in existence still required absolute patience in the moment before it. His right hand rested near the wooden practice sword tucked through his belt—a *bokken*, because this was supposed to be a civilized arrangement. His jaw was set. His eyes were already moving.

Across the flagstones, Wei Longshan rolled his shoulders in slow, deliberate circles, loosening the joints. He was a year younger, broader through the chest, wearing his own armband on the left wrist where it wouldn't interfere with his dominant arm's rotation. The Piguaquan lineage had given him something that looked, from a distance, almost like laziness—a low, unhurried readiness that disguised itself as indifference. He had no weapon. He hadn't wanted one.

"You came without a sword," Kenji said. His Mandarin was functional, contemptuous.

"You came with a toy." Wei's Japanese was better than Kenji's Mandarin, which seemed to irritate Kenji further.

Someone in the crowd counted down in a low, flat voice. Three. Two. One.

Kenji moved first.

He covered the twelve feet in a single explosive surge, the *bokken* clearing his belt and rising in the diagonal overhead cut that Jigen-ryū called *kirioroshi*—a strike designed not to threaten but to end, delivered at a velocity that had historically made the style's practitioners nearly impossible to defend against at close quarters. The bokken sang downward toward the junction of Wei's neck and left shoulder.

Wei was no longer there.

He had pivoted left on his rear foot the instant Kenji's weight committed forward, rotating his entire body counterclockwise so the descending weapon passed within inches of his right ear. The evasion wasn't backward—it was *around*, circular, and as the bokken completed its arc and Kenji's momentum carried him a half-step past, Wei's right arm was already swinging. The Piguaquan whipping strike—*pi*, the splitting chop—came from high and behind Wei's own shoulder, rotating through the full circumference of his torso, and the forearm connected across Kenji's upper back with a sound like a bat striking a fence post.

Kenji stumbled forward three steps, caught himself against a broken pillar, and spun.

The crowd exhaled.

He came again immediately, unwilling to grant recovery time, launching a horizontal cut at Wei's midsection. Wei stepped inside it—dangerously close, close enough that the bokken's handle grazed his ribs—and drove his left palm upward beneath Kenji's chin in a *zhang*, a heel-palm strike. Kenji's head snapped back. His teeth clicked together. He backpedaled, vision swimming, and Wei followed without pause, right foot sweeping wide to reposition himself to Kenji's left flank.

The next exchange happened in under two seconds. Kenji recovered his sight, pivoted, and launched a thrusting strike at Wei's sternum—a direct, committed thrust that traded elegance for pure linear force. Wei deflected it with his left forearm, angling the bokken outward and past his hip, simultaneously stepping diagonally forward and to the right, which brought him inside Kenji's guard and positioned his right shoulder directly against Kenji's chest. The shoulder strike—*kao*—landed with Wei's full bodyweight behind it. Kenji left the ground briefly. He came down hard on his right knee against the flagstones and the impact traveled up through his hip like a hammer blow.

He didn't stay down.

Jigen-ryū practitioners were taught that falling was dying. He rose immediately, and something had changed in his expression—the contempt had burned away, replaced by something colder and more honest. He adjusted his grip on the bokken with both hands and settled into a lower stance, weight distributed, the weapon held back and diagonal rather than raised.

Wei recognized the shift. He stopped circling and watched.

They held the distance for four full seconds—an eternity in a fight—reading each other across the moonlit stone.

Kenji feinted left, drew Wei's weight into a lateral step, then reversed direction and came right, the bokken rising in a tight upward arc rather than the overhead cut Wei had been anticipating. The strike caught Wei's forearm as he moved to intercept, and the crack of wood against bone was sharp and clean. Wei's right arm dropped involuntarily. He hissed through his teeth.

But dropping was not stopping. He was already spinning away from the blow's source, letting the struck arm trail behind him as momentum carried his body in a full rotation, and the left arm—the undamaged one—came around in a wide horizontal sweep, the forearm connecting against the side of Kenji's head with the full rotational force of his body behind it.

Kenji went sideways. The bokken clattered across the flagstones.

Both men stood breathing in the sudden silence, separated again by several feet. Wei's right forearm hung at an angle that suggested something structural had been compromised. Kenji had blood on his lip and his left eye was already beginning to swell.

The crowd was absolutely still.

Neither fighter moved to retrieve the weapon. Neither stepped back.

Above them, through the broken roof, the moon continued its indifferent observation—bright and cold and old enough to have watched a thousand such moments resolve themselves into either wisdom or ruin.

The night was not finished with them yet.
