# Blood Moon

The village has been dead for three days.

Yennefer knows this the moment she rides through the gate—knows it the way she knows weather and poison and the particular silence that follows massacre. No dogs. No hearth smoke. Thirty-seven cottages standing perfectly intact, which is somehow worse than ruin.

The harvest moon hangs swollen and red above the thatch rooftops, painting everything the color of old wounds.

She dismounts, lets her horse go. The animal doesn't need to be told twice.

The cobblestones beneath her boots are clean. That troubles her most of all.

---

She finds the bodies in the church.

All of them—men, women, children, the priest still clutching his useless cross—arranged in the pews with a fastidiousness that borders on aesthetic. Hands folded. Eyes closed. As though someone wanted them to look peaceful and couldn't quite resist the urge to *arrange* things.

"Impressive, isn't it?"

The voice comes from the rafters.

Yennefer doesn't flinch. She does, however, position her fingers for a shield-casting and turn slowly, violet eyes scanning upward until she finds him—a figure draped across a beam like a cat, red coat hanging down, wide-brimmed hat tilted at an angle that shouldn't be physically possible given gravity's usual opinions.

"I've seen tidier," she says.

A laugh. Low, genuine, and deeply unsettling. He drops from the rafter—*falls* would be the wrong word, because falling implies a lack of control—and lands in the aisle between the pews without sound. He's tall. She'd expected that. She hadn't expected the orange-tinted spectacles, which strike her as either absurd or deliberate, and she suspects both.

"A sorceress." He tilts his head. The moonlight through the church windows catches nothing in his eyes. "I can smell the chaos magic on you. Old magic. Older than this village by several centuries."

"And I can smell *you*," Yennefer replies, "from twenty feet away. You're not subtle."

"I've never needed to be."

The power dynamic announces itself immediately—two ancient things in a dead room, each taking the other's measure with the particular wariness of predators who've never encountered equal resistance. Yennefer keeps her expression composed. She's stood before kings and demons and things that wore human faces as costumes. She will not be the first to show her hand.

"You did this," she says. Not a question.

"I was bored." He moves between the pews with a liquid ease that her eyes want to track and can't quite manage. "A village this remote, no witcher contract for fifty miles. They'd been feeding something far less interesting than me for months. I simply—" he gestures at the arranged dead, "—consolidated."

"You killed thirty-seven people because you were *bored*."

"I killed thirty-seven people because something else was killing them *badly*." He stops at the end of the aisle, perhaps fifteen feet away. "There's a difference. Sloppy work offends me."

Yennefer stares at him. "That's genuinely the most monstrous justification I've ever heard."

"You're welcome to leave."

She doesn't move. He notices—she can tell by the way his smile shifts, becomes something sharper and more attentive. She's passed some first test she didn't know she was taking.

"What are you?" she asks. Not fearfully. Clinically.

"The same thing you are," he says, "only older and less concerned with appearances." His gaze moves over her with an assessment that isn't quite lust and isn't quite hunger—something more complicated than either. "Though your appearances are quite the achievement. The transformation wasn't painless."

Her jaw tightens. "Stay out of my history."

"It's written in your posture." He tilts his head the other direction. "You carry yourself like someone who spent years being looked through. Now you make certain you're looked *at*." A pause. "I find that more interesting than the magic, actually."

"Don't." The word comes out with more edge than she'd intended. She recovers smoothly. "Flattery from something that smells like a mass grave carries limited appeal."

He laughs again—that same sound, like gravel and old bells—and this time he moves, circling left. She turns to track him, which means she's moving too, and suddenly they're orbiting each other around the altar like a very slow, very dangerous celestial event.

"You haven't cast anything," he observes.

"You haven't attacked."

"I'm curious about you."

"I'm curious about you," she returns. "What I'm *feeling* is something else entirely."

"Tell me."

"Repulsion, mostly." She watches his hands—those massive, strange pistols holstered at his sides. "And the specific irritation of encountering something that can't be easily killed."

"Ah." He sounds genuinely pleased. "You've tried to calculate it already."

"I've been calculating since the rafters." She stops moving. Forces him to be the one to adjust. "Silver doesn't work on you. Fire, possibly. Decapitation seems optimistic given what I've observed. You regenerate."

"I do."

"So do I," she says. "Not identically, but effectively."

The silence that follows has texture. He stops circling. Studies her with those lightless eyes behind their ridiculous amber glass, and Yennefer holds his gaze with the absolute steadiness of a woman who learned centuries ago that looking away is the first surrender.

"You're not afraid of me," he says.

"I'm afraid of very little."

"Everyone says that."

"Everyone else is lying." She tilts her chin up slightly. "I'm simply experienced enough to know that fear is only dangerous when it makes you stupid. I'm not stupid."

Another long moment. The red moonlight shifts through the windows, and the dead sit patient in their pews, and the two of them stand at the altar of an empty church like participants in some profane ceremony neither has agreed to perform.

"No," he says finally, and something in his voice has changed—less performance, more weight. "You're not."

He reaches up and removes his hat. It's such a human gesture that it startles her more than anything else he's done.

"My name," he says, "is Alucard."

Yennefer considers him for three full seconds.

"Yennefer of Vengerberg," she replies. "And I want to know everything about what you are."

The blood moon watches them through ancient glass, and neither moves to leave.
