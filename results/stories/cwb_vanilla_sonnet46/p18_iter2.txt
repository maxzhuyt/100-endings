# PUSH COMES TO SHOVE
## *An Advice Column by Sisyphus, King of Ephyra (Ret.)*

---

The boulder was approximately three-quarters of the way up the slope when the morning post arrived.

Crow landed on Sisyphus's left shoulder, which required some adjustment on Sisyphus's part, as his left shoulder was currently bearing a significant portion of the weight of an enormous granite rock. He shifted. The boulder held. After four thousand years, you develop a feel for these things.

"Seventeen letters," said Crow.

"Read me the first one."

---

*Dear Sisyphus,*

*My husband leaves his socks on the bathroom floor every single morning. I have asked him to stop approximately one thousand times. He says he will change. He does not change. I am beginning to wonder if I should leave him. — Exhausted in Edinburgh*

---

Sisyphus was quiet for a moment. The mountain made its usual sounds. Somewhere, a goat.

"All right," he said. "Write this down."

Crow produced a small reed stylus from somewhere Sisyphus had learned not to inquire about.

---

*Dear Exhausted,*

*The socks are not the problem, and I suspect you already know this. The problem is that you have asked him one thousand times and he has heard you approximately zero of them. These are different issues.*

*Here is what I would like you to try: ask him once more, but this time, tell him not about the socks. Tell him that when he leaves them there, you feel like your mornings don't matter to him. That your small daily life doesn't register. This is, I think, what you actually mean.*

*If he hears that and still leaves the socks, you will have learned something important. If he hears it and finally picks them up, you will also have learned something important.*

*Either way, you'll know more than you do now, and that's always worth the conversation.*

*Warmly,*
*Sisyphus*

---

"Next," said Sisyphus.

The boulder slipped two inches. He caught it with his hip, exhaled slowly through his nose, and resumed.

---

*Dear Sisyphus,*

*I have worked at the same company for eleven years. I am good at my job. Last week, my manager gave the promotion I was due to someone who has been here for eight months and who, I have it on good authority, cannot operate the coffee machine. I am so angry I can barely speak. What do I do? — Overlooked in Oslo*

---

*Dear Overlooked,*

*Your anger is correct. I want to say that first, because people will try to talk you out of it and they shouldn't.*

*Now. You have two separate questions hiding inside your one question. The first is: what do I do about this specific injustice? The second is: what does this tell me about where I am?*

*For the first: document everything, have the direct conversation with your manager about your expectations and their reasoning, and do it calmly, because calm is more frightening to the incompetent than fury is.*

*For the second: eleven years is long enough to know whether this is an aberration or a pattern. I suspect you already know which it is. The coffee machine detail suggests you've been watching this place carefully for some time. People who are happy don't usually track their colleagues' appliance literacy.*

*You're not overlooked. You're just in the wrong place. Those are different problems with very different solutions.*

*Warmly,*
*Sisyphus*

---

The summit was close now. Sisyphus could feel the particular quality of the air that meant he had perhaps forty steps left, and then the long, familiar slide back down, and the whole lovely business of beginning again. He had stopped minding this somewhere around the fourteenth century. The descent, he'd eventually realized, was actually quite pleasant. Good views.

"Next letter," he said. "Quickly."

---

*Dear Sisyphus,*

*I am forty-three years old and I have just realized I have no idea what my life is for. I thought I would feel like an adult by now. I thought there would be a moment where everything clicked. It hasn't clicked. Is something wrong with me? — Lost in Lyon*

---

He took three more steps before he answered.

---

*Dear Lost,*

*No.*

*The clicking sound you're waiting for is a myth propagated by people who are also waiting for it and don't want to feel alone in the waiting. There is no click. There is just Tuesday, and then Wednesday, and the things you chose to do with them.*

*You are forty-three and asking what your life is for. This is not a sign that you have failed. This is a sign that you are paying attention. Most people spend enormous energy not asking this question. You're asking it. That's the work. That IS the thing.*

*What do you do on the days when you forget to feel lost?*

*Start there.*

*Warmly,*
*Sisyphus*

---

The boulder reached the top.

It rolled back down.

Sisyphus watched it go with the expression of a man watching a familiar train depart. He began his descent. "Next."

Crow held up a letter. It had been rained on, sat on, and apparently used as a napkin at some point. Three words were legible: *"situation,"* *"unfortunately,"* and what appeared to be either *"uncle"* or *"unclear"* or possibly *"uncles."*

Crow turned it over. Nothing.

"Best guess?" said Sisyphus.

"Romantic," said Crow. "Something went wrong. Possibly at a family gathering."

Sisyphus considered this for several steps.

---

*Dear Friend,*

*Whatever happened, it's not as permanent as it feels right now, and you are almost certainly not as responsible as you fear.*

*If I am wrong about this, write again with more legible stationery.*

*Warmly,*
*Sisyphus*

---

He reached the bottom. The boulder sat there, patient as ever, waiting to be pushed.

He put his hands against it.

"Last letter," he said. "Then we work."
