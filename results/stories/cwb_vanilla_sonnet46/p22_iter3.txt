# Prison Strings

The facility has no name on any official document.

It sits somewhere between jurisdictions, between countries, between the kind of reality that shows up on maps. The walls are reinforced with something older than concrete. The guards carry weapons that don't appear in any military catalog. Inmates here are the kind of problem that governments prefer to file under *miscellaneous* and forget.

Jolyne Cujoh has been here for six days and she already hates everything about it.

---

She notices the woman during morning count.

Auburn hair. Neat uniform that somehow looks tailored despite being identical to everyone else's. Yellow eyes that catch the fluorescent light wrong, like a cat's, like something that learned to blink by watching humans do it first.

Every security camera in the corridor tilts slightly as the woman passes beneath it. Jolyne watches this happen. She watches it happen *twice*.

*The hell?*

The woman turns and smiles at her.

It's a perfect smile. That's the problem. Jolyne has seen plenty of smiles in plenty of prisons and they all have something real underneath them — fear, aggression, desperation, hunger. This one has *nothing* underneath it. It's a smile the way a painting of a fire is a fire.

"You must be new," the woman says. Her voice is soft. Gentle, even. "I'm Makima."

"Jolyne." She doesn't offer her hand. "You always make the cameras do that?"

Makima glances back at the tilted lens with what appears to be mild curiosity. "Do what?"

*Right.*

---

By day two of knowing her, Makima has already acquired three favors from other inmates, resolved a dispute between two rival factions in the east wing, and gotten herself assigned to the library.

Jolyne watches all of this from a careful distance and feels the hair on the back of her neck stand up in a way that has nothing to do with her Stand.

She's met manipulative people. She's met dangerous people. She's met people who wore kindness like a Halloween mask and let it slip in the dark.

Makima doesn't let it slip. That's what's wrong. The mask never slips because there's nothing underneath it working to hold it in place. It just *is* the face.

On the third day, Makima finds her in the yard.

"Your ability," Makima says, sitting beside her uninvited, tilting her face toward the weak winter sun like she's performing the concept of enjoying warmth. "It's remarkable. I've never seen anything quite like it."

Jolyne lets a single thread of Stone Free drift from her fingertip, invisible to anyone without the sensitivity to see it. It circles Makima's wrist once, loosely.

Nothing happens. No reaction. But Makima's smile shifts by approximately two degrees.

She knows. She felt it.

"Don't flatter me," Jolyne says.

"I'm not flattering you. I'm being accurate." Makima folds her hands in her lap. "String that can carry your consciousness. That can gather information, restrain, cut. You could do almost anything with it." A pause. "You could do *anything* with the right guidance."

"I've got guidance. Thanks."

"Your father." Makima's voice stays perfectly pleasant. "He abandoned you. You were convicted of a crime you didn't commit. You've spent years in institutions while he stayed silent." She says this the way someone might read a grocery list. "And yet you still defend his name. That's very loyal of you."

The string around Makima's wrist tightens.

Makima doesn't flinch.

"You're trying to make me angry," Jolyne says.

"I'm trying to understand you."

"Same thing, coming from you."

---

That night, Jolyne sits on her bunk and thinks carefully.

She's been in bad situations. She's faced enemies who could stop time, enemies who could manipulate gravity, enemies who wore other people's faces. She knows how to read a fight.

This isn't a fight. That's the problem.

Makima hasn't threatened her. Hasn't moved against her. Has been, by every observable measure, *pleasant*. Helpful, even — she'd gotten Jolyne's work assignment switched to something less miserable without being asked.

Jolyne hadn't asked. That was the point. Makima had done it anyway, building something that looked like goodwill, and Jolyne could feel the shape of it, could feel the strings of obligation being laid down so carefully they were almost invisible.

*Almost.*

She unravels her hand slowly in the dark, watches the threads drift upward.

She's spent her whole life learning that strings can be weapons. Can be tools. Can connect you to people or trap you or hold a wound together long enough to survive.

Makima deals in strings too. Just a different kind.

---

In the morning, Makima is waiting outside the mess hall with two cups of coffee.

She holds one out. Her ringed eyes are warm and patient and completely empty.

"No thank you," Jolyne says.

"You didn't sleep well."

"I sleep fine."

"Jolyne." Makima says her name like she's owned it for years. "I'm not your enemy."

"I didn't say you were." Jolyne looks at her directly. She has her father's eyes when she wants to use them — the kind of stare that doesn't move, doesn't blink, doesn't give anything away. "But I've noticed that every person in this facility who you've helped is now doing something for you. Real small things. Easy things." She smiles, and unlike Makima's, it has something sharp underneath it. "I'm not gonna be one of your dogs."

Something moves behind Makima's eyes. Not anger. Not even surprise.

*Interest.*

"Fascinating," she says softly.

She keeps the second coffee. The cameras in the corridor behind her all tilt inward, lenses pointing nowhere useful, recording nothing.

Jolyne walks away and starts counting her strings.

She's going to need all of them.
