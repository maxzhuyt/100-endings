# The Court of the Dreaming: In the Matter of the Field of Wildflowers

The courtroom existed in the way most things in the Dreaming existed: imperfectly, and with intent.

The gallery was full. It always was for territorial disputes. Dream entities attended these proceedings the way mortals attended executions — with great solemnity and the private hope that something would go wrong.

Dream sat at the bench. He had been sitting at the bench for what felt like several geological epochs, and his expression suggested he was aware of this.

The bailiff — a tall, hollow thing that produced sound the way a cave produces echoes — cleared its throat.

"NOW ENTERING: THE ENTITY KNOWN AS—" it consulted its parchment, "—*FRIST LOUV.*"

A small, rosy-cheeked figure bustled through the doors, cheeks blazing, clutching a sheaf of papers to its chest. First Love was wearing what could only be described as a cardigan of emotional significance. It looked exactly as embarrassed as its portfolio required.

"It's *First* Love," it whispered to the bailiff.

The bailiff stared at it with the patience of a stone wall.

Dream steepled his fingers. "Proceed."

First Love approached the stand, arranged its papers three times, rearranged them, and began.

"My Lord Dream, I have been crafting formative romantic experiences for—"

"We know what you do," said Dream.

"Yes. Right. The dreamscape in question." First Love produced a small, glowing diorama from somewhere in its cardigan. A field materialized above the evidence table: wildflowers, afternoon light, two small figures. The boy. The girl. The eternal almost-kiss, rendered in miniature, hovering in a perpetual state of *nearly*. "I spent considerable time on the tilt of her head," First Love said, with audible feeling. "The confusion of emotions in the third act took me weeks. *Weeks.* The boy was responding beautifully. He was going to remember this dream when he was forty-three and inexplicably melancholy in the cereal aisle—"

"Yes," said Dream. "And then."

First Love's rosy cheeks darkened. "And then *she* happened."

The gallery stirred.

"NOW ENTERING," the bailiff announced, "THE ENTITY KNOWN AS—" a pause of tremendous duration, "—*MORE-AH. MOOR-AH. MORRA.*"

"Mora," said Mora, from the doorway.

She was not what the courtroom expected, which was, of course, the point. She was small, dark-haired, and wore an expression of profound reasonableness. She took the stand with the air of someone who had been deeply wronged by the scheduling of this hearing.

"You are Mora," said Dream. "The Slavic nightmare spirit."

"I prefer 'chest-presser,'" said Mora. "It's more descriptive."

"I'm sure it is." Dream looked at her. His gaze was the kind that made entities reconsider their life choices back to the beginning of time. Mora met it with equanimity. "You entered a protected dreamscape."

"I entered an *adjacent* dreamscape," Mora said, "and the borders were frankly ambiguous."

"The borders," said First Love, in a voice of controlled anguish, "were a *hedgerow*. I put in a hedgerow."

"Permeable," said Mora.

"It was *decorative*."

Dream raised one hand. Both fell silent.

"You will describe," Dream said to Mora, "what occurred."

Mora folded her hands. "The boy was lying in the field. The girl approached. Standard First Love staging — I'll grant you," she nodded to First Love, who did not appear to feel granted anything, "it was competent work. The girl sat on him." A pause. "I merely accelerated certain elements already present in the scene."

"You transformed her into a terror-vision and fed on his breath," said First Love.

"I *built* on what was there," said Mora, with some dignity. "There was already a girl sitting on a boy. There was already an element of paralysis — that's *First Love's* work, not mine, the inability to move or speak in the presence of one's beloved—"

First Love made a sound.

"—there was already breathlessness. I simply..." Mora gestured. "Completed the composition."

"You ate him," said First Love.

"I had a small snack," said Mora. "A girl's gotta eat too, you know?"

The gallery murmured. Dream's expression did not change, but something behind his eyes shifted, the way weather shifts before it commits to anything.

"The boy," Dream said. "What was his experience?"

First Love consulted its papers. "Paralysis. Chest compression. Terror." A reluctant pause. "He did describe the vision as—" First Love's cheeks achieved a new shade, "—*confusingly attractive*. Which I find professionally insulting."

"Flattering, I thought," said Mora.

"It is not *flattering*—"

"My work and your work," Mora said, with the air of someone making a reasonable business proposal, "are not so different. Both involve a girl. Both involve a boy who cannot move. Both involve an overwhelming experience he will carry into waking life and never quite explain." She spread her hands. "I am simply the more honest version."

The gallery was very quiet.

Dream looked at the diorama. The field. The flowers. The frozen almost-kiss. He looked at it for a long moment with an expression that, on a less ancient face, might have been called *amused*.

He reached a decision.

"The dreamscape," Dream said, "remains under First Love's jurisdiction." Mora began to speak. "However." She stopped. "The boy is seventeen. The confusion is already there. It was always going to be there." He looked at the diorama once more. "I find no reason to remove it."

First Love looked up. "My Lord?"

"The dream will continue," Dream said, "as originally constructed." A pause, precise and deliberate. "With the hedgerow removed."

First Love's mouth opened.

Mora smiled, slowly, like a door opening onto a dark room.

Dream stood. The court rose.

"This proceeding is concluded," he said, and he sounded, very nearly, like someone who had enjoyed himself.

The bailiff cleared its throat.

"ALL RISE FOR THE LORD OF—" it consulted nothing, "—*DREEM.*"
