# **The Watch That Ate My Life**
## Chapter Seven: Rewind

The watch came in a plain brown box with no return address.

Maya Chen had assumed it was from her grandmother—Nǎi Nai was always sending weird stuff—but there was no card, no note, nothing except the watch itself, sitting in a nest of black foam like something precious. Or dangerous.

It was beautiful, though. Silver case, midnight-blue face, a single button on the side shaped like a backward arrow.

Maya strapped it on and didn't think about it again.

---

The first time she used it was an accident.

Thursday morning, seventh period, Mr. Holloway's science class. Maya had knocked her entire tray of frog dissection equipment onto the floor—forceps, scalpel, the works—and the whole class had erupted laughing. Brianna Kowalski had actually *filmed* it.

Maya's face had gone nuclear-hot.

She'd grabbed the edge of the lab table, squeezing until her knuckles whitened, and her thumb had found the button on the watch without her even thinking about it.

She pressed it.

The world *lurched*.

Like a movie skipping backward. Beakers slid across counters in reverse. Mr. Holloway's mouth unsaid words. The tray of equipment lifted off the floor and landed back in Maya's hands, and suddenly she was standing there, perfectly fine, holding everything, while Brianna's phone was pointed at a wall.

Nobody laughed. Nothing had happened yet.

Maya's heart hammered. She set the tray down *carefully* this time.

The watch face read **:58** where a normal watch would show the time.

She assumed it was a battery readout.

---

By Friday she'd used it six more times.

Failed a pop quiz? Rewind. Said the wrong thing to Tyler Marsh in the hallway and watched his face go confused and vaguely disgusted? Rewind. Tripped on the gym bleachers in front of literally everyone? *Rewind.*

Each time, the number on the watch face ticked down.

**:52. :48. :41.**

She noticed her jeans felt tighter on Friday afternoon but figured she'd eaten too much at lunch.

She noticed her voice sounded strange—slightly lower, slightly rougher—but figured she was getting a cold.

She noticed her reflection in the bathroom mirror between fifth and sixth period, and she stopped walking.

The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead. The mirror was old, slightly warped at the edges, and for just a moment—*one single horrible moment*—the reflection wasn't quite hers.

It was *her*, definitely her same dark hair and sharp cheekbones, but the face was older. Seventeen, maybe eighteen. The eyes looked tired in a way Maya's never had.

Then she blinked and it was just her normal twelve-year-old face staring back.

*Bathroom mirrors are weird*, she told herself, and went to class.

---

The disaster happened Saturday.

Maya was at the mall with her best friend Dani when she spotted Brianna Kowalski at the food court, phone already raised, clearly filming something. Maya followed the camera angle and felt her stomach drop.

Brianna was filming *Tyler Marsh*, who was sitting alone, eating a sandwich, completely unaware. And Brianna was laughing with her group, and Maya knew—she just *knew*—that whatever caption Brianna put on that video was going to be awful.

Maya hit rewind before she'd even decided to.

**:30.**

The world lurched. Brianna's phone was suddenly in her pocket. Tyler was still eating, unbothered.

But something felt different this time. The lurch had been bigger, rougher, like rewinding a tape too many times. Maya grabbed a food court chair to steady herself.

And then she heard the crash.

The rewind had gone back *further* than she'd intended—she could tell by how disoriented she felt—and in the rearranged sequence of events, a mall maintenance worker named Gerald had walked a different route, which meant he *hadn't* caught the wobbly display stand outside the sporting goods store before it tipped, which meant sixteen glass trophies from the regional soccer tournament had just exploded across the tile floor, which meant the little kid who'd been standing there a moment ago—

The little kid was crying, bleeding from her hand, and her mother was screaming for help.

Maya stood frozen.

**:28**, said the watch.

---

She locked herself in a bathroom stall and tried to breathe.

The watch number glowed in the dimness. Twenty-eight left. Twenty-eight of *what*, exactly? She'd been so stupid. So unbelievably, catastrophically stupid.

She held her hand up and stared at it.

Her knuckles looked different. The skin looked—*older*. Not ancient, not wrinkled, but not twelve either. She pulled down her sleeve and found a faint dark hair on her forearm that definitely hadn't been there a week ago.

*Each use is aging them.*

The thought arrived fully formed, like she'd known it somewhere underneath all along.

She thought about how her mom had looked at her strangely this morning. How Dani had said, *you seem different lately*, and then laughed nervously. How Mr. Holloway had called her "young lady" instead of "Maya" for the first time ever, like he wasn't quite sure what he was looking at.

The little girl outside was still crying.

Maya looked at the watch.

**:28.**

She could fix the display stand. She could put Gerald back on his original route. She could undo the whole chain of events. She *knew* she could.

She would just get older doing it.

She thought about the mirror. The tired eyes. The face that was hers but wasn't.

*How old do I get before I run out of numbers?*

She pressed her thumb against the backward arrow and held it there, feeling the cool metal, the slight vibration, like something alive and waiting.

Outside the stall, she could hear a child crying.

The watch read **:28**.

Maya closed her eyes.
