# Rehearsing Rejection

---

The television next door is doing that thing again, voices without words, the shape of a conversation I can't quite make out. Someone laughing. Someone responding. It sounds like agreement, like everything is fine over there, like people are simply *talking* to each other without running the whole thing through a machine first.

Maya is coming at seven.

It's four-fifteen.

I've already cleaned the apartment twice. Not because it was dirty. Because when my hands are moving I can pretend the other thing isn't happening, the other thing being: I am constructing the trial. I am building the courtroom where Maya will finally say what she's been meaning to say for the last eleven days, since she started taking longer to text back, since she went quiet after Tuesday, since she said *I've just had a lot going on* which means *you are too much* which means *I have been talking to someone about you* which means *the verdict is coming and you already know what it is.*

I know what it is.

I've known for weeks, probably. The body knows before the mind catches up. That low hum behind my sternum, that's not anxiety, that's *recognition*.

*She's going to say she thinks we moved too fast.* 

I'm folding the same dish towel for the third time.

*She's going to sit down on the couch and she's going to do that thing where she touches her own collarbone, which means she's nervous, which means she's rehearsed this too, which means it's real, which means—*

Okay. I know I do this. I *know* I do this. Dr. Farrow has a name for it, something about threat anticipation, something about the nervous system staying in a state of—I can't remember the exact words because right now the words are being crowded out by Maya's voice, the imagined version, the honest version, the version she'd use if she weren't kind:

*I care about you, but I don't think I can be what you need.*

And then me: *What does that mean, what does that actually mean, say the real thing.*

And then her: *You're very intense.*

And then me, in this version, I am very calm. I am almost frightening in my calm. I say: *I know. I've always known that about myself. I'm sorry I wasted your time.*

And she looks relieved, which is the worst part, the part that makes my throat close up even though none of this has happened, none of this is *happening*, it's four-twenty-two and the neighbors' television is still murmuring, a woman's voice now, something that sounds like *but I told you—*

I sit on the edge of the bed.

The thing is, I can feel myself doing it. I can stand slightly outside the machine and watch the gears and think: *this is the pattern, this is what it does, it makes the bad thing feel inevitable so that when it doesn't happen you're relieved, or when it does happen you were ready.* I know the function of it. I understand it like I understand how a lock works without being able to pick it.

Doesn't stop anything.

Because what if this time it's not the pattern? What if this time the pattern is just *accurate*? What if eleven days of slower texts is just eleven days of slower texts, what if it means exactly what it means, and I'm the one sitting here calling my own perception a disorder so I don't have to trust it?

I go back to the kitchen.

---

She arrives at seven-oh-four and she's carrying wine and her cheeks are cold from the walk and the first thing she does, before she even takes her coat off, is put both arms around me from behind while I'm reaching for the glasses, and she presses her face against the back of my neck and says, *I missed you, I'm sorry I've been weird, work has been genuinely insane.*

I feel it move through me like something structural giving way.

*I missed you.*

I turn around and she's looking at me with that specific look, the one that still startles me, like I'm something she's glad exists. She tucks a piece of hair behind my ear. She says, *You okay?*

I say, *Yeah, of course, I'm good.*

And I am. I am flooded. I am so relieved I could cry and I don't because I'm also already watching the relief from a distance, already noting how much I needed that, how much those four words rearranged something in my chest, and isn't that—isn't that exactly what she'd say? *You need too much.* Isn't the fact of my own relief evidence of the problem?

We sit on the couch. She puts her legs across my lap. The wine is something she picked because she remembered I liked it, she says, *I saw it and thought of you,* and I think: *I thought of you eleven hundred times in the last eleven days and built a courtroom and convicted you in absentia and you were just busy.*

I almost say it. I almost say: *I convinced myself you were ending this.*

But I don't, because then she'd know how far the machine went, how elaborate the architecture of it, the cross-examinations, the imagined relief on her face.

She's laughing at something now, something on her phone she's showing me, and her shoulder is against mine, and I laugh too, genuinely, it's actually funny, and for approximately forty seconds I am just a person on a couch with someone who brought wine because she thought of me.

Then: *but why was she distant for eleven days.*

The television next door. Still going. Still that shapeless murmur of people talking to each other without the words coming through.

It sounds, almost, like everything is fine.
