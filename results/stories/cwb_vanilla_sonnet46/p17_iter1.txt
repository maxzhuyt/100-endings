# The Matter of Dreamscape 7,441-B: Field of Wildflowers (Contested)

The Court of the Dreaming was not, strictly speaking, a courtroom. It simply became one when Dream required it to, which was arguably worse.

The vaulted ceiling stretched upward into a darkness that suggested infinite height without committing to it. Two lecterns had materialized facing the bench, which was less a piece of furniture than a statement. Dream sat behind it with the particular stillness of someone who has existed since before the concept of patience and finds the current proceedings beneath that distinction.

He had not wanted to be here. This was evident to everyone present, which was everyone.

"We will begin," said Dream.

---

First Love went first, because First Love always went first. The entity was pink-cheeked and faintly luminous, wearing something that suggested a pastoral watercolor had learned to dress itself. It clutched a sheaf of documents with the anxious energy of someone who had prepared extensively and suspected it wouldn't matter.

"My Lord," it began, voice carrying the particular earnestness of its domain, "I submit that Dreamscape 7,441-B, colloquially designated *Field of Wildflowers*, was established under my jurisdiction some fourteen months ago in the sleeping mind of one Maxwell Hooper, aged thirteen, for the express purpose of—"

"I know what it's for," said Dream.

First Love's cheeks deepened approximately two shades. "Yes. Of course. The dream has operated without incident, providing the subject with formative emotional experiences of the appropriate developmental complexity. A girl. Wildflowers. An almost-kiss. The requisite confusion." It shuffled its papers. "It was, I would venture, quite good work."

"It was adequate," said Dream.

First Love absorbed this. "Thank you, my Lord."

From the opposing lectern came a sound. It was not quite a laugh. It was what a laugh would sound like if it had learned the behavior from something that had never found anything funny but recognized the social utility.

Mora occupied her space in the courtroom the way a cold draft occupies a room—pervasively, and without apology. She was not large. She didn't need to be. She was the sort of presence that made adjacent furniture seem to lean slightly away. Her eyes, when they caught the light, suggested the light had made an error.

She cackled again. It climbed the vaulted ceiling and didn't come back down.

"The defense will contain itself," said Dream.

"Apologies," said Mora, who was not sorry.

---

The evidence was presented. Dream watched it with the expression of a man reading a bill he did not incur.

The dreamscape played out in miniature above the court: the field, the wildflowers, the boy on his back with the particular expression of someone experiencing feelings he doesn't have vocabulary for yet. The girl approaching. The playful, tentative quality of it. The almost-kiss, blooming and dissolving like it always did, leaving behind something sweet and unresolved.

Then Mora's intervention. The same scene, the same boy, the same girl—until she wasn't. The mounting, the morphing, the chest, the breath rising from parted lips and disappearing into something that smiled with too many teeth while remaining, in some structurally troubling way, still attractive.

First Love looked away. 

"She's *eating him,*" it said, with the tone of someone lodging a formal complaint about the weather.

"Breathing," Mora corrected pleasantly. "I'm eating his *breath*. There's a distinction."

"He wakes up unable to move."

"He wakes up," Mora said. "I consider that generous."

Another cackle. This one bounced between the walls twice before dissipating. Dream's jaw moved in a way that was not quite perceptible but somehow communicated volumes.

---

"The defense will present its argument," said Dream.

Mora turned to face the bench with the unhurried confidence of someone who has been frightening things since before most things existed to be frightened.

"My Lord," she said. "A girl's gotta eat."

Silence.

"That's it?" said First Love.

"I can elaborate." She spread her hands. The gesture managed to suggest both reasonableness and the inside of a very dark room. "The dreamscape was *accessible.* The emotional architecture was already charged—longing, confusion, the particular vulnerability of early desire. I simply—" she paused, appearing to search for the word— "redirected the existing infrastructure."

"You *corrupted* it," said First Love.

"I *monetized* it," said Mora. "The boy was going to lie in a field feeling things he couldn't name regardless. I provided a more efficient framework." She considered. "He still feels things he can't name. There's continuity."

"He's *terrified.*"

"He's *engaged.*"

"He hasn't slept properly in three weeks."

"He's sleeping right now," Mora said. "I checked before I came."

Another cackle. Dream's hand, resting on the bench, became very still.

---

Dream deliberated for eleven seconds, which in the Dreaming is either very fast or very long depending on the nature of the matter. Then something moved across his face that was not quite a smile and not quite its opposite.

"The dreamscape will be returned to the jurisdiction of First Love," he said. "Mora's modifications will be removed."

First Love brightened perceptibly.

"However," said Dream, and the however landed like a stone in still water, "the emotional residue of the experience will remain."

First Love stopped brightening.

"The boy will retain the impression," Dream continued, with the careful neutrality of someone choosing each word deliberately, "that something about the girl is faintly dangerous. That proximity to her involves a quality of breathlessness. That she could, under some circumstances, be frightening." He paused. "This is, after all, not inaccurate to the experience of first love."

First Love opened its mouth.

Closed it.

Looked at its documents.

"That's—" it started.

"Adequate," said Dream.

Mora's cackle, when it came, was quieter than the others. Almost appreciative. It rose to the ceiling and stayed there, settled in the dark like something that had found a comfortable place to sleep.

Dream had already stopped listening. He had other dreams to attend to. He always did.
