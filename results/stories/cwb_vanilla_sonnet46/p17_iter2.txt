# The Court of the Dreaming: In the Matter of Wildflowers v. Mora

The sand fell upward in the hourglass. It always did, here.

Dream sat at the apex of a courtroom that had no walls, only the suggestion of them — darkness arranged with architectural intent. His face, as ever, gave nothing away except, to those who knew how to look, the faint compression around his eyes that indicated he would rather be anywhere else in all the infinite reaches of his kingdom.

He had adjudicated the fall of civilizations. He had witnessed the last dreams of gods.

Today he was hearing a dispute about a schoolboy's feelings.

"The court will come to order," said the Clerk, a small grey figure whose function was largely ceremonial, since order in the Dreaming was Dream's to grant or withhold as he chose. Still. Procedure mattered. Even here.

Especially here.

---

First Love entered from the left, because it always did. It was a round-faced entity of indeterminate age, wearing something pink that might have been a waistcoat, and it carried its case files pressed against its chest as though they were love letters, which, in a sense, they were. It kept glancing at Mora and then glancing away, cheeks colouring.

Mora entered from wherever she wished.

She was tall. She wore her hair loose. She moved with the unhurried confidence of something that had been sitting on people's chests since before written language, and she took her seat at the respondent's table with the air of a woman who has never once been sorry about anything.

Dream looked at them both. The hourglass dripped.

"Proceed," he said.

---

First Love's advocate was a small, precise entity called Longing, who wore spectacles it didn't need and spoke in the careful tones of someone who had rehearsed.

"My lord," said Longing, "the facts of this matter are not in dispute. My client spent considerable time — seventeen months of the boy's subjective experience — constructing the dreamscape in question. The field. The wildflowers. The girl." It paused for effect. "The *almost*-kiss. A masterwork of formative emotional architecture. Confusion and sweetness in precise calibration. The boy was on a trajectory toward his first meaningful—"

"We know what he was on a trajectory toward," said Dream.

Longing cleared its throat. "Yes. Quite. The point is that the respondent has invaded this carefully constructed space and has—" another pause, this one less comfortable— "eaten it."

"I didn't eat *it*," said Mora, without being invited to speak. "I ate *him*. There's a distinction."

"You ate his *breath*."

"A portion of it. He had plenty."

Dream raised one finger. Mora subsided, though she did so with the energy of someone making a considered choice rather than complying with an instruction.

---

"Continue," said Dream.

Longing produced a small crystalline sphere and set it on the evidence table. Inside it, in miniature, the dreamscape played: the field, the golden light, the girl with her dandelion crown, the boy lying back in the grass with the particular expression of someone who has no idea what to do with his hands.

Then the girl laughed and climbed onto him, and the light changed, and she was still the girl but also not, and her eyes were the colour of the space between heartbeats, and she sat on his chest and leaned down and breathed him in, and the boy's expression became something that was not entirely different from what it had been before, which was perhaps the most disturbing part.

The courtroom was quiet.

"He wasn't even that upset," said Mora, gesturing at the sphere. "Look at him."

"He was paralyzed," said Longing.

"He was *already* paralyzed. That's what she does to him." Mora nodded toward First Love, who turned an extraordinary shade of pink. "I simply redirected an existing condition. Professionally speaking."

First Love made a small sound of protest and shuffled its files.

"My client," said Longing, with some effort, "wishes to convey that there is a meaningful difference between the paralysis of adolescent longing and the paralysis of a chest-sitting nightmare entity consuming one's vital breath."

"Meaningful to whom?" said Mora.

Dream's finger moved again. This time it was two fingers. Mora looked at them and decided to stop talking.

---

"The respondent's position," said Mora's advocate — a lean, dry creature called Dread who had been practicing in these courts since before the boy's grandparents were born — "is threefold. First: the dreamscape was not registered. First Love filed no claim. The field was open territory."

"It was *his recurring dream*," said Longing.

"Dreams belong to the Dreaming," said Dread, with the serenity of someone quoting settled law. "Second: my client's occupation of the space caused no lasting harm. The boy woke. He continues to wake. He is, by all accounts, fine, if somewhat confused about his feelings, which we would submit is not a condition my client introduced."

First Love buried its face in its files.

"Third," said Dread, and here it paused, "a girl's got to eat."

The courtroom absorbed this.

"That is your third argument," said Dream.

"It is, my lord."

Dream looked at the hourglass for a long moment. The sand fell upward, patient and indifferent.

---

He had been considering, throughout these proceedings, what a just resolution might look like.

He had also been considering what an *interesting* one might look like, and he was aware these were not the same thing, and he was aware that the awareness was not, by itself, sufficient to prevent him.

"The dreamscape," he said, "will be returned to its original custodian."

First Love exhaled.

"However." Dream's expression did not change. "The girl in the dream will retain, henceforth, a certain quality. Difficult to name. A weight to her presence. An edge."

He looked at Mora.

"The boy will not know why. He will simply find that she unsettles him in ways he cannot account for, and that this unsettlement is not entirely unwelcome."

Mora smiled slowly. It was a smile that had frightened people for centuries.

"I can work with that," she said.

First Love looked at its files. Its files did not help.

The sand fell upward. Court was adjourned.
