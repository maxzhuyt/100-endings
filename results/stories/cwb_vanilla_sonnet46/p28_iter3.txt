# **The Tick-Tock Trap**
### *Chapter Seven: Fast Forward*

The watch showed up in Maya's locker on a Monday.

No note. No box. Just a sleek silver smartwatch sitting on top of her math textbook like it had always belonged there, its face glowing a soft, pulsing blue.

Maya Reyes turned twelve last week. She'd wanted a smartwatch more than anything — had shown her mom the catalog page so many times the corners had gone soft. But her mom had said *maybe for Christmas*, which in mom-language meant *absolutely not*.

So where had this come from?

She strapped it on anyway.

---

The first time she used it was an accident.

During third period, Maya tripped over Derek Hollis's backpack strap and dumped her entire lunch tray — chocolate milk, spaghetti, the works — directly onto Principal Garret's blazer.

The cafeteria went silent. Then erupted.

Maya's face burned nuclear red. She was going to be *destroyed*. Socially. Permanently. They'd be talking about Spaghetti Girl at Birchwood Middle School until she graduated high school.

She grabbed the watch face in humiliation, twisting the crown the way you'd wind an old clock — backward, instinctively, like she could *undo* everything.

The watch face flickered.

*REWIND: -3 MIN*

Then the world lurched.

The cafeteria blurred. The milk un-splattered. Derek's backpack strap retreated. Maya found herself standing at the lunch line again, tray in hand, three minutes earlier, completely dry.

She stepped around the backpack.

Principal Garret walked past, blazer spotless, nodding at her pleasantly.

Maya stood very still, breathing hard.

*Okay*, she thought. *Okay. What just happened?*

---

She used it six more times that week.

Once to undo a failed quiz answer she'd blurted out loud. Twice to avoid embarrassing herself in front of Caleb Torres, who sat next to her in science and had the kind of smile that made her forget what oxygen was. Once to stop herself from texting her best friend Priya something she'd immediately regretted.

Each time, the watch face flickered with that same cold blue light before the world rewound.

Each time, something felt *different* afterward. A faint ache behind her eyes. A strange heaviness in her hands.

By Friday, she noticed her jeans were too short.

She figured she'd shrunk them in the wash.

---

The second week was when things got weird.

Maya's voice did something strange on Tuesday — not cracking exactly, just *shifting*, like a radio station sliding slightly off frequency. Her reflection in the bathroom mirror looked somehow more angular. Her cheekbones sharper. A small constellation of pimples had appeared on her forehead overnight.

"You look different," Priya said, squinting at her over lunch.

"Different how?"

"I don't know. Older, maybe?" Priya shrugged. "Like, did you do something with your hair?"

Maya hadn't touched her hair.

That night, she caught her mom staring at her across the dinner table with an expression Maya couldn't quite read. Somewhere between confused and *worried*.

"You seem tired, mija," her mom said carefully. "Are you sleeping okay?"

Maya was sleeping fine. She was also, she realized when she checked the bathroom scale, two inches taller than she'd been at her birthday physical.

Two inches. In two weeks.

She looked at the watch.

The face flickered.

*REMAINING: 14*

Fourteen *what*? Rewinds? She'd used it seven times. If she started with twenty-one, that meant—

Maya sat down hard on the bathroom floor.

Each rewind was costing her something. *Time*. Not the world's time. *Hers*.

---

She should have stopped. She knew she should have stopped.

But then came Thursday.

Caleb Torres was crossing Birchwood Avenue after school, earbuds in, not watching the intersection, and Maya saw the delivery truck running the red light a full second before it happened.

She grabbed the watch crown and *twisted*.

*REWIND: -45 SEC*

The world lurched harder this time. Her vision went white at the edges. When everything snapped back, Caleb was still on the curb, earbuds in, and Maya grabbed his sleeve and yanked him backward as the truck blasted through the red.

Caleb stumbled. Stared at her. The truck roared past, close enough to ruffle their hair.

"That truck just—" He went pale. "You just—"

"I saw it coming," Maya said.

He looked at her for a long moment, and something in his expression shifted. Not the smile-that-forgot-oxygen look. Something more like the way kids looked at *teachers*. Respectful. A little uncertain.

"Thanks," he said quietly. "How did you know?"

Maya didn't answer. Her hands were shaking. In the watch's reflection, she caught a glimpse of her own face and barely recognized it — jaw slightly longer, eyes somehow *deeper*, the face of a girl who was fourteen, maybe fifteen, maybe older.

She was twelve.

She was supposed to be twelve.

---

But here was the thing nobody tells you about saving someone's life: it changes the order of things.

Caleb missed the bus because of the delay. Which meant he wasn't home when his little sister called from the park. Which meant she waited alone until dark. Which meant his parents called the police. Which meant Officer Delaney — who was supposed to be across town — was instead driving down Ridgecroft Lane when the gas main broke, and nobody was there to redirect traffic, and—

By Friday morning, half of Maple Court was evacuated.

By Friday afternoon, the school was closed for an emergency assembly.

By Friday night, Maya was sitting in her bedroom staring at the watch, which now read:

*REMAINING: 7*

Seven rewinds left. Her face in the dark window looked sixteen. Maybe older.

The watch face flickered once, twice.

Then it showed something new.

Not a number.

A message, in tiny, ice-blue letters:

*TO FIX IT, YOU MUST GO BACK FURTHER THAN YOU'VE GONE BEFORE.*

*YOU MAY NOT COME BACK THE SAME.*

Maya pressed her palm flat against the cold watch face.

She thought about Maple Court. About Caleb's sister. About the gas main and the empty street and the chain of dominoes she'd set falling without ever meaning to.

She thought about her own face in the mirror. How much older could she get?

She thought about what *the same* even meant anymore.

Then she reached for the crown.

And twisted.
