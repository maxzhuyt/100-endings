# The Lighthouse Keeper

---

**14th September 1973**

Dear Mum,

Got here Tuesday. The ferry took four hours and the man who brought the supplies didn't speak much. The light itself is fine. Mechanisms all working. My predecessor left the place tidy enough though he'd papered one wall of the bedroom with pages from a seed catalogue. Vegetables mostly. I've left it up for now.

The sea here is different from Arbroath. Darker somehow. I'll write again when I've more to say.

Your son,
Callum

---

**2nd October 1973**

Dear Mum,

Thank you for the shortbread. It arrived broken but I ate it anyway. Three weeks now. The supply boat comes Tuesdays and sometimes Fridays if the weather allows.

I've been thinking about Dad. Not sure why. Maybe it's the work, the repetition of it. Wind the mechanism, log the weather, watch the light. Wind, log, watch. He'd have liked it I think. Or hated it. Hard to say.

There was a trawler in trouble last week, two miles north. I signalled and they corrected. They were fine. I didn't sleep after.

Callum

---

**19th October 1973**

Son,

Your Auntie Vera sends her love. She's had the hip done and is complaining about the nurses which I take as a sign she's recovering well.

I worry you're not eating properly. The shortbread was an experiment. I'll send the tin with the proper lid next time.

Are there birds out there? I always think of lighthouses as having birds.

Love,
Mum

---

**3rd November 1973**

Dear Mum,

Yes there are birds. Gannets mostly. They hit the glass sometimes at night, drawn by the light. I find them in the mornings. I've started burying them on the south side of the rock where there's a patch of something almost like soil. I don't know why I bother. It just seemed right.

Tell Vera I hope she's giving the nurses hell.

I found something in the stores cupboard last week. Previous keeper must have left it. It's a brass compass, old, the kind with a folding lid. The lid has an engraving on it, a small ship with all its sails out, and underneath in very small letters the word *Patience*. The hinge is stiff but it still works. North is north. I've put it on the windowsill above the log desk. I like having it there. Something about it being a compass inside a lighthouse seems right, though I can't explain why.

Callum

---

**28th November 1973**

Son,

I showed your father's brother your letters and he said you always did have a serious nature. I told him serious and miserable are not the same thing. Are they the same thing Callum?

The flat is cold this year. The boiler is making a sound.

Love,
Mum

---

**12th December 1973**

Dear Mum,

They're not the same thing. I'm not miserable. I'm just quiet. There's a difference and I've got six months left to work it out.

Get the boiler looked at. Don't wait on it.

I've been reading in the evenings. The previous keeper left a box of paperbacks, westerns mostly, which aren't really my thing, but I've read four of them now. There's something comforting about a landscape with no water in it.

I think about coming home at Christmas but the rota doesn't allow it. I'll phone on the day from the mainland when the relief man comes over.

Callum

---

**27th December 1973**

Son,

It was good to hear your voice. You sounded far away even on the phone which I suppose you were.

I made the steak pie. I ate it over three days which is too much steak pie for one person but there we are.

Happy New Year when it comes.

Love,
Mum

---

**8th January 1974**

Dear Mum,

Happy New Year. I watched it come in from the lantern room. The sea was completely flat which apparently almost never happens. It looked like something had ironed it out. I stood there for a long time.

I've been thinking about what comes after. When the posting ends. I applied for this because I thought I needed to be away from things for a while and I was right, I did need that. But I'm not sure I knew what things I was running from or whether being away has helped with them. That probably doesn't make much sense.

The compass on the windowsill. I've been thinking about the man who had it made. *Patience*. Whether that was his ship's name or a message to himself. Whether he needed reminding.

Callum

---

**22nd February 1974**

Son,

I understand more than you think. After your father died I went to Inverness for a fortnight, do you remember? I told you it was to see Margaret but Margaret and I had barely spoken in years. I just needed to be somewhere that didn't have his shape in every corner.

It helped and it didn't help. Both things were true.

Come home when it's done. We'll figure out the rest.

Love,
Mum

---

**14th March 1974**

Dear Mum,

Eight weeks left.

A fishing boat passed close this morning and one of the men on deck waved up at me. I waved back. It sounds like nothing but I've been thinking about it all day.

I'm going to bring the compass home when I go. I know it isn't mine but it's been company and I think whoever left it here has long since stopped coming back for it. I'll keep it on the windowsill in the flat if that's alright. I want to see north from somewhere that isn't here.

Callum

---

**9th May 1974**

Son,

The relief man phoned to say you'd got the ferry safely. I've made up your room. I've put a lamp on the windowsill so you'll see the light from the road when you come up.

I know you're not the same as when you left. I don't expect you to be. Neither am I.

Just come home.

Love,
Mum
