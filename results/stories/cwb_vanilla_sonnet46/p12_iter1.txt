# Rehearsing Rejection

The Uber is four minutes away and my phone is at eleven percent and I haven't eaten since this morning and none of that is the problem.

The problem is that Jordan took three hours to respond to my *confirm we're still on* text and the response was *yeah! see you soon :)* and I have been dissecting that punctuation for forty minutes. The exclamation point is performative. The smiley face is the kind of punctuation you use when you're trying to seem enthusiastic rather than *being* enthusiastic. Jordan used to send voice memos. Jordan used to send three texts before I'd finished reading the first one. *Yeah! see you soon :)* is the text you send to an obligation.

I know that's not necessarily true.

I know that.

---

The Uber smells like pine air freshener and the driver doesn't want to talk and my phone is at nine percent and I'm running the scenario again because I can't stop running the scenario. It goes like this:

Jordan is going to be weird. Jordan is going to be *polite* in that specific way that means something has shifted, that careful warmth that keeps you at exactly arm's length while appearing to embrace you. I will feel it immediately, that millimeter of new distance, and I will say *is everything okay?* too soon, too transparently, and Jordan will say *of course, why?* with that slight tilt of the head that means *why are you like this,* and then I'll have to perform normalcy for the rest of the evening while internally I'm already writing the ending.

And here's what I'll want to say, in the scenario, when it finally ruptures — because it will rupture, it always does — I'll want to say: *I knew. I knew three weeks ago when you started taking longer to respond. I knew when you canceled Tuesday and rescheduled instead of just apologizing. I clocked every single change in your behavior and I said nothing because I was trying to be* normal *and now you're going to stand here and act like I'm the one who created this distance?*

I won't say that.

I'll probably cry in the wrong moment and then apologize for crying, which will make everything worse.

Seven percent.

---

Jordan is already there when I arrive, which I didn't expect, and is standing up before I'm fully through the door, and the hug is — the hug is immediate and real, both arms, Jordan's chin coming down to my shoulder the way it does, the specific weight of it, and I feel something crack open in my chest that I immediately distrust.

*You look amazing,* Jordan says, and pulls back to look at me with that expression, the one that's always felt like being actually seen rather than observed, and I want to cry already, which is insane, it's been four minutes.

*So do you,* I say, and I mean it, and we sit down.

The conversation is easy. It's so easy it makes me suspicious. Jordan is funny and present and keeps touching my hand across the table when making a point, and each time I feel the touch I think: *this is what it will feel like to remember this. This is the last time this will feel normal.* I am collecting the evening as evidence of its own ending.

*I feel like I've been so in my head lately,* Jordan says, around the second drink. *Like, genuinely bad at being a person. I'm sorry if I've been MIA.*

And there it is. The acknowledgment I've been catastrophizing toward for three weeks, delivered without drama, without the confrontation I'd rehearsed seventeen variations of, just — offered. Casually. Genuinely.

*It's okay,* I say.

It is okay. I feel it actually land as okay, for approximately four seconds, and then the reframe begins: *Jordan is saying this preemptively. Jordan knows something is coming and is softening the ground. This is the kindest possible setup for —*

*I've just really missed you,* Jordan says, and looks at me directly, and I have to look away.

Five percent.

---

I excuse myself to the bathroom and plug my phone into the little charging station by the sink that the bar provides, which is a small mercy, and I stand there watching it blink from five to six percent and I think: *I am doing the thing again.*

I know I'm doing the thing again. I can see the architecture of it, the way I've constructed an entire abandonment narrative from a delayed text and normal human busyness, the way I have been *grieving a loss that hasn't happened* while sitting across from someone who just told me they missed me. I can see it. I can name it. I know what my therapist would say, the thing about how the fear of fire can make you burn everything down yourself just to control the timing.

I know all of that.

And still. The knowing doesn't reach all the way down to where the fear lives. The knowing is a light in a very large dark room. It illuminates the shape of things without making them less dark.

I go back to the table.

---

Jordan's hand is covering mine and we're talking about something stupid and funny, some shared memory, and I'm laughing, genuinely laughing, and Jordan is looking at me like I'm something worth looking at, and I think: *I want to live inside this moment.*

And then: *I don't trust it.*

And then: *I know I don't trust it.*

And then: *I laugh again anyway, because Jordan's still talking, and the evening is still happening, and my phone is at nine percent now, climbing,*

*and I am here,*

*and I am trying.*
