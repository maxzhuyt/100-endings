# Gunpowder Rendezvous

A lazy ceiling fan barely stirs the smoky air inside the bar. The place has no name worth remembering—just a hand-painted sign outside that's been weathered down to suggestion. The clientele matches the décor: scarred, tired, and deliberately forgettable.

Spike Spiegel folds himself into a corner booth like a question mark, all loose limbs and cigarette smoke, one eye tracking the room while the other stares at nothing in particular. He's nursing something amber and cheap. The bounty sheet sits face-down on the sticky table. He already has it memorized.

Viktor Brass. Arms dealer. Last seen in this port town. Worth sixty thousand.

He signals for another drink.

The door opens and the room shifts the way rooms do when something dangerous walks in—not loudly, but perceptibly, like a barometric drop before a storm. Spike doesn't turn his head. His good eye finds her in the bar mirror behind the bottles.

She's wearing less than the weather warrants. Dragon tattoo. Two holsters. The kind of walk that dares the room to say something about it.

She sits at the bar. Orders whiskey. Lights a cigarette before the glass arrives.

Spike watches her scan the room the same way he did. Watches her find him in the mirror the same way he found her.

They hold eye contact through the glass and warped reflection for three full seconds.

He picks up his drink and moves to the stool beside her.

---

"You're in my seat," she says, not looking at him.

"Booth's more comfortable." He sets the bounty sheet on the bar between them, face up. "Figured we should talk before one of us does something stupid."

She looks at the sheet. Looks at him. Her jaw works like she's deciding between words and a left hook. "How long have you been here?"

"Hour. You?"

"Twenty minutes." She drains half her whiskey. "So you've got a head start on surveillance and you're still sitting in a bar."

"I work better slow." He exhales smoke toward the ceiling fan. "You're Revy. Two Hands."

"You say that like it's supposed to make you nervous."

"Little bit," he says, which is honest enough to surprise her. "I'm Spike."

"I know who you are." She signals the bartender. "Another. And whatever he's having." She says it like it costs her something. "You're supposed to be dead."

"Rumor." He watches the bartender pour. "Where's your crew?"

"Where's yours?"

He smiles. It's the kind of smile that doesn't commit to anything. "So it's like that."

"It's always like that."

---

They drink for a while without talking, which is its own kind of conversation. The bar fills incrementally with the night's usual wreckage. Spike smokes. Revy smokes. The ashtray between them becomes a small monument to mutual stubbornness.

"Viktor's running product through the fish market," Spike says eventually. "Night shipments. He'll move tomorrow at the latest."

Revy's eyes cut sideways. "You've been busy for someone who works slow."

"I had an hour."

She's quiet for a moment. He can feel her recalibrating. "Southeast warehouse," she says. "Three guards on rotation. One inside with Brass."

He nods slowly. They've just handed each other information. Neither of them acknowledges how unusual that is.

"Split," she says.

"Sixty-forty."

"Fifty-fifty or I shoot you right now."

"You'd shoot me in a bar."

She turns to look at him fully for the first time. Her eyes are dark and entirely serious. "I've shot people in nicer places than this."

He believes her completely. "Fifty-fifty," he agrees.

---

The job goes sideways in the way jobs always go sideways—not catastrophically, just messily. Viktor has a fifth guard nobody counted, and the fifth guard has a shotgun, and the shotgun takes out a structural column that brings down part of the ceiling between Spike and the exit.

What happens in the next forty seconds is difficult to describe in sequence. Revy moves like weather, both guns out, and Spike watches her work with the detached appreciation of someone who recognizes a thing done exceptionally well. He takes the left flank. She takes everything else. Viktor Brass ends up zip-tied to a pipe, unhurt, complaining loudly.

In the aftermath, they stand in the settling dust. Revy is breathing hard. There's a cut above her eyebrow that she ignores. Spike's jacket has a new hole in the shoulder that missed him by less than he'd prefer.

"You're bleeding," he says.

"I know."

"You want—"

"No."

---

The motel is the only option within walking distance of the impound lot where they've stashed Viktor pending pickup. One room. Two beds. Revy's idea, which surprises him, though he doesn't show it.

Now they're standing on opposite sides of the room and both of them still have their guns.

Not aimed. Just present.

"This is stupid," Revy says.

"Probably."

"I don't trust you."

"I don't trust you either." He sets his Jericho on the nightstand. A gesture. Not a surrender. "But you covered my left side back there without being asked."

She stares at the gun on the nightstand. Something moves through her expression that she shuts down quickly. She doesn't holster her Cutlass. She does stop pointing it at anything in particular. "Don't make that mean something."

"I'm not."

She sits on the edge of the bed furthest from him. The dragon on her arm shifts with the movement. She lights a cigarette and looks at the water-stained ceiling. "Sixty thousand split two ways is nothing."

"It's something."

"It's barely something."

He sits on his bed. Lights his own cigarette. The fan in this room doesn't work at all. "You always do this alone?"

The question lands differently than he intended. He watches her decide not to answer it, then answer it anyway.

"Mostly."

Outside, the port town does its indifferent business. Inside, two people who have both died in their own ways sit three feet apart in the dark, smoking, not sleeping, not talking, not leaving.

Whatever happens, happens.
