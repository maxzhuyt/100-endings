# The Last Outpost
## Chapter One: Dreads and Dead Ends

The gravity hiccups at 21:00, same as always. I've learned to pour accordingly—a half-second pause mid-measure, waiting for the wobble to settle before I top off the glass. New patrons always flinch when their drinks shiver. Regulars don't even look up anymore.

Tonight the Outpost is running at about two-thirds capacity, which is comfortable. Comfortable is good. Comfortable means I can actually hear myself think between conversations, means the ventilation system isn't losing its battle against forty bodies worth of heat and breath and whatever chemical compounds the Velorian table in the corner keeps metabolizing into the air. Comfortable means I notice things.

I notice the stranger immediately.

She comes in quiet—which already sets her apart, because almost nobody comes into the Outpost quiet. Miners come in loud, shaking asteroid dust from their suits, calling names across the room. Merchants come in performing, already working some angle. Even the shy ones have a kind of purposeful shyness, a *look at how unassuming I am* energy that announces itself.

This woman just... arrives. Folds herself onto the end barstool like she's been deflated. Her tentacled dreads—the bioluminescent kind, pale violet, which tells me she's probably from the Cephalian settlements out past the fourth belt—are listing badly to one side, two of them still coiled around her neck like they forgot to let go. She's wearing a merchant's vest, but the insignia patch has been removed recently enough that the fabric is still a slightly different shade where it sat.

I give her fifteen seconds before I walk over. Not too eager.

"Vega whisky, neat." Her voice is low and controlled, like someone who has spent considerable energy making it that way. "Better make that a double."

"Sure." I pull the bottle without commentary. The good Vega, not the well stuff—her vest, patched or not, is quality fabric. I pour, pause for the wobble, finish the pour. Set it in front of her.

She wraps both hands around the glass and stares into it like it owes her something.

I wipe down the bar nearby. Not hovering. Just present.

After about a minute she says, without looking up, "You going to ask me what's wrong?"

"No," I say.

That gets her eyes up. They're a deep amber, which is unusual for Cephalians. "No?"

"You'll tell me or you won't. Either way, you paid for the drink, not the interrogation."

Something in her shoulders drops about two centimeters. "I like this place already."

"Most people do, eventually." I extend a hand. "Nets."

"Dara." She shakes it. Her grip is firm but her fingers are cold. "You're human."

"Mostly."

She almost smiles. One of her dreads uncurls slightly from her neck, which I've learned is a Cephalian autonomic response to mild positive stimulation. Like a cat's tail. She doesn't seem to notice she's doing it.

She takes a long sip of the whisky. I watch the liquid tremble faintly in the glass before it reaches her lips—the gravity doing its ambient, low-grade haunting.

"Does it always do that?" she asks.

"The wobble? Every night around this time. Compensator's been on the maintenance list for eight months."

"And nobody's fixed it?"

"Outpost runs on a particular philosophy," I say. "If it's not actively killing anyone, it's a low priority."

"Charming." But she doesn't say it unkindly.

I move away to refresh Ollo's fermented something-or-other at the other end of the bar—Ollo is a regular, a big slow-moving Brathian miner who communicates mostly in appreciative grunts—and when I come back, Dara is still sitting in exactly the same position, but one of her dreads is now tapping a very faint, anxious rhythm against the bartop.

"You came in from the merchant lane?" I ask, casual.

"Tried to." She takes another sip. "Docking authority flagged my ship for a secondary inspection."

"Routine?"

She looks at me steadily. "No."

I keep my expression easy. "That's a headache."

"That's one word for it." She sets the glass down and turns it slowly in her hands. "They've impounded it pending review. Which means I'm stuck here until—" she stops. Recalibrates. "Until things get sorted."

"How long is that likely to take?"

"Depends on who filed the complaint."

I wait.

"I don't know who filed the complaint," she says. "That's the problem."

One of the other dreads has joined the first in its tapping. She notices, and stills them both with a visible effort.

"You have somewhere to stay?" I ask.

"I was hoping the station had rooms."

"We have four. Two are occupied, one has a plumbing situation I wouldn't wish on anyone, and one is actually fine." I pull out the tab from under the bar. "I can sort you out after close if you need."

"Thank you." She means it. I can tell.

I'm about to move away again when the Outpost's main door opens and I feel, more than hear, a shift in the room's ambient noise. The comfortable hum drops half a register. I look up.

It's Commissar Veth from the docking authority, in his formal grey, and he has two port officers with him. He doesn't come to the Outpost socially. I've never once seen him come to the Outpost socially.

His eyes move across the room in that slow, deliberate way that means he already knows exactly what he's looking for.

They land on Dara.

She hasn't turned around, but I watch her go completely still. The dreads stop their tapping. Her hands tighten around the glass.

"Don't react," I say quietly, already reaching for a cloth to wipe the bar, already making myself look bored and occupied. "Finish your drink."

"Is there a back way out of here?" she murmurs.

I pause just long enough to be honest with her.

"Not one that's currently accessible."
