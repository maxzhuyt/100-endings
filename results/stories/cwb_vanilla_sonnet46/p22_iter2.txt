# Prison Strings

The yard smells like rust and something older than rust. Chain-link fences within the yard rattle whenever an inmate passes by, and Jolyne has learned to read the sound like a language — which direction trouble is coming from, how fast, how many.

She's learned a lot of languages since Green Dolphin Street.

This place isn't Green Dolphin Street. The fences here hum with something that isn't electricity. The guards don't blink often enough. And three women have gone missing from Block C since Jolyne arrived eleven days ago, each one last seen walking calmly toward a figure with auburn hair and a smile like a department store mannequin.

Jolyne watches that figure now from across the yard.

The woman sits on a concrete bench with her hands folded in her lap, prison-issue uniform somehow immaculate, ringed yellow eyes tracking a sparrow that has landed near her feet. She looks like she's posing for a photograph. She looks like she has never been uncomfortable in her life.

She looks up and catches Jolyne staring.

She smiles.

Jolyne doesn't smile back.

---

"You're Jolyne Cujoh." The woman appears beside her at the water fountain three minutes later. Not walked. *Appeared.* "I've heard about you. Your father is quite famous."

"Most people lead with their own name," Jolyne says, wiping her mouth with the back of her hand.

"Makima." She says it like she's offering a gift. "I was hoping we'd get a chance to speak."

"Yeah? Why's that?"

"You're interesting." Makima tilts her head, and the sparrow from the bench lands on her shoulder without any invitation. She doesn't acknowledge it. "The way you move. Like you're always measuring distances. Like you're always aware of where the strings are."

Jolyne goes very still.

She hasn't used Stone Free once since arriving. She's been careful. She's been *deliberate*, which doesn't come naturally and costs her something every day she manages it.

"Don't know what you're talking about," Jolyne says.

"Of course." Makima's smile doesn't change. "It must be lonely, being this careful all the time. You don't have to be, with me. I'm very good at keeping secrets."

The sparrow chirps once and flies away.

*This woman,* Jolyne thinks, *is the most dangerous person in this yard, and she wants me to think she isn't.*

"Sure," Jolyne says. "Thanks for the offer."

---

She starts pulling threads that afternoon. Not from her body — from the situation. She talks to the Block C women who are still present, still *themselves*, and what she gets is a pattern: Makima befriends, Makima listens, Makima asks small questions that don't seem like much until you realize she's been assembling a map of everything you want and everything you're afraid of. Then you walk toward her willingly. Then you don't come back the same.

One woman, a forger named Teri, grabs Jolyne's wrist when she stands to leave.

"Don't look her in the eyes," Teri says. "I looked her in the eyes for too long and I could feel something — like a hand around my—" She touches her own throat. "Just don't."

Jolyne covers Teri's hand with hers. "I won't."

She's already been looking Makima in the eyes every chance she gets. She doesn't feel anything. She wonders if that means she's already compromised, or if it means something else entirely.

She decides it means she's a Cujoh.

---

The confrontation happens at dusk, when the chain-link fences rattle with the last count of the day and the guards are distracted by their clipboards. Makima finds her near the east wall, which Jolyne has chosen specifically because it's a dead end and she wants to see how Makima handles the geography of a trap.

Makima walks into it like it isn't one.

"You've been asking questions about me," Makima says pleasantly.

"You've been making people disappear," Jolyne says.

"People make choices." She clasps her hands behind her back. "I simply help them understand what they actually want. It's a kindness, really."

"You know what I actually want?" Jolyne lets one finger unravel, just one, the string curling down toward the concrete where Makima can't see it. "I want you to drop the act. The soft voice, the little head tilt — you're not fooling me."

Something shifts in Makima's expression. Not much. Just enough.

"You're the first person here who's said that to me." She sounds genuinely curious. "Why aren't you afraid?"

"I've been afraid plenty," Jolyne says. "I just don't let it make my decisions."

"Admirable." Makima takes one step closer, and her ringed eyes catch the last of the yard's light. "What about lonely? Are you lonely, Jolyne? You came here without allies. Without your father. Without anyone who knows what you can—"

Stone Free moves.

String erupts from Jolyne's arm — not a little, not carefully, *fully* — wrapping around Makima's wrists and throat in a configuration Jolyne has been designing in her head for three days. Makima doesn't flinch. She looks down at the string with something that might be delight.

"*Don't,*" Jolyne says quietly, "*talk about my father.*"

The chain-link fences rattle. No one is walking by.

Makima looks up at her, bound and smiling, and Jolyne has the sudden horrible instinct that she has just shown this woman exactly what she wanted to see.

"Remarkable," Makima says softly. "Truly remarkable. You can unravel yourself." A pause. "I wonder if that works in reverse."

The string goes cold against Jolyne's palm.

She doesn't let go.

She doesn't let go because letting go is what Makima wants, and Jolyne Cujoh has spent her whole life refusing to give people what they want when what they want is *her.*

The yard is quiet.

The fences rattle once, for no reason at all.
