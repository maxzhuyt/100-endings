*(Trial proceedings commenced at 9:07 a.m.)*

THE COURT: Good morning. We're back on the record in Cause Number CR-97-0414, *State of Texas versus Eleanor Ruth Vance*. Let the record reflect the jury is present and seated, the defendant is present with counsel. Mr. Billings, you may call your first witness.

MR. BILLINGS: Thank you, Your Honor. The State calls DeAnne Willett.

THE COURT: Ma'am, if you'll come forward and be sworn.

*(Witness sworn.)*

MR. BILLINGS: Would you state your name for the record?

A: DeAnne Marie Willett.

MR. BILLINGS: Ms. Willett, how are you employed?

A: I'm a teacher's aide at Sonora Primary. I work with the pre-K class.

MR. BILLINGS: And in that capacity, did you have occasion to interact with the defendant, Eleanor Vance?

A: She was the — she ran the after-school program. The extended-day program, they called it. I'd see her every afternoon when the kids transitioned over.

MR. BILLINGS: Now, directing your attention to September of 1997. Did something happen that caused you concern?

A: Yes, sir. It was — I think the fourteenth. A Monday. I came into the extended-day room because I'd left my bag, and Eleanor had the children in a circle on the floor. Which wasn't unusual by itself, they did circle time. But the lights were off. She'd turned the lights off and pulled the blinds, and she had a candle going — one of those tall ones, like from a church — and she was talking in a voice I'd never heard her use. Real low. Almost like chanting.

MR. BILLINGS: What were the children doing?

A: Just sitting there. Real still. That's what scared me, honestly. You ever seen a room full of four-year-olds sit that still? They weren't moving. They weren't fidgeting. They were just — staring at the candle.

MR. ESCOBAR: Objection, Your Honor. Narrative. And if we could have the witness testify to what she observed rather than her editorial characterization —

THE COURT: Sustained in part. Ms. Willett, just tell us what you saw and heard.

A: Yes, sir. I'm sorry. She was — the words she was saying weren't English. I couldn't tell what language. I asked her what she was doing, and she startled, like she hadn't heard me come in. She looked at me and — well, she looked like she didn't know where she was for a second. Then she said she was telling a story. A folk story, she said, from her family.

MR. BILLINGS: Did you report this incident?

A: I told my supervisor the next morning.

MR. BILLINGS: And subsequent to your report, were there further observations made of the defendant's behavior?

A: I don't know about formally. But other people started saying things. Jenny — Jennifer Pruitt, the other aide — she said she'd heard the same kind of

THE DEFENDANT: *She's lying.*

THE COURT: Ms. Vance —

THE DEFENDANT: *She's lying, they put her up to — all of them, they put all of them up to this, Philip, do something, do something, they're —*

MR. ESCOBAR: Eleanor. Eleanor, please.

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, I understand this is difficult, but you cannot interrupt the proceedings. I've explained this to you. Mr. Escobar, please confer with your client.

MR. ESCOBAR: Yes, Your Honor. Could we have a brief recess?

THE COURT: Five minutes. Members of the jury, remain in your seats.

*(Off the record from 9:23 a.m. to 9:31 a.m.)*

THE COURT: Back on the record. Mr. Escobar?

MR. ESCOBAR: We're fine to proceed, Judge.

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, I need you to remain quiet while other people are testifying. You'll have every opportunity to present your side. Do you understand?

THE DEFENDANT: Yes. *(Inaudible.)* Yes.

THE COURT: All right. Mr. Billings, continue.

MR. BILLINGS: Ms. Willett, you were telling us about observations other staff members made.

A: Right. Jennifer Pruitt had noticed the same kind of — well, Eleanor talking to the children in a language that wasn't English. And there were the drawings.

MR. BILLINGS: I'm going to hand you what's been marked as State's Exhibit 4. Can you identify these?

A: These are drawings that some of the children in the extended-day program made. The teachers collected them because they were — they were concerning.

MR. BILLINGS: Can you describe them for the jury?

A: They're — well, they're children's drawings, so they're not exactly clear. But several of them show what appears to be a group of figures standing in a circle. Some of them have what looks like a fire or a tall shape in the center. This one here — this one has — the child drew what seems to be words coming out of the mouths of the figures. Scribbles, but arranged like words.

MR. BILLINGS: Like incantations?

MR. ESCOBAR:
