PROCEEDINGS

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Good morning. We are on the record in Cause Number 87-CR-4421, the State of Texas versus Eleanor Ruth Vance. This is the time set for trial. Let the record reflect the State is present through its attorney, Mr. Hunsaker, and the defendant is present in person and through counsel, Mr. Pfeiffer.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mr. Hunsaker, is the State ready to proceed?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: The State is ready, Your Honor.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Mr. Pfeiffer?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: We are ready, Judge.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Very well. Let the record reflect the jury panel has been seated and sworn. Ladies and gentlemen, I want to now read you the indictment in this case so you understand the charges that have been brought.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Grand Jury of Sutton County, Texas, charges that on or about the fourteenth day of March, 1986, and on divers other dates thereafter, the defendant, Eleanor Ruth Vance, did then and there intentionally and knowingly cause the death of a child younger than fifteen years of age, to wit: MICHAEL JAMES WHITLEY, DOB 11/02/1982, by asphyxiation, and did further cause the death of a child younger than fifteen years of age, to wit: SARAH ANN COLE, DOB 03/19/1984, by drowning, and did further cause the death of a child younger than fifteen years of age, to wit: CHRISTOPHER RYAN PRUITT, DOB 07/08/1983, by causing said child to ingest a toxic substance, against the peace and dignity of the State.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ms. Vance, how do you plead?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE DEFENDANT: Not guilty.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Let the record reflect the defendant has entered a plea of not guilty to all three counts.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: Your Honor, at this time we would renew our Motion to Suppress filed on September the eighth regarding statements obtained from my client during custodial interrogation on May the twenty-second, 1987, on the grounds that —

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Mr. Pfeiffer, that motion has been heard and ruled upon. The Court's written order denying same was entered September the nineteenth. Your objection is preserved for the record.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: Understood, Judge. We also renew our objection to the State's Exhibit 14 on the grounds of —

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Your Honor, we've been through this.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Gentlemen. Mr. Pfeiffer, your objection is noted and overruled consistent with my prior ruling. Are there any other preliminary matters?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: No, Your Honor.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: No, Your Honor.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Then Mr. Hunsaker, the State may proceed with its opening statement.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Thank you, Your Honor.

*(Whereupon the following proceedings were had in the presence and hearing of the jury:)*

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Ladies and gentlemen, what the evidence is going to show you over the next several days is that between March of 1986 and May of 1987, three children placed in the foster care of this woman — Eleanor Vance — died. One by one. In her home. Under her supervision. And each time, she had an explanation. And each time, that explanation involved the same extraordinary claim: that these children were, in her word, "chosen," and that she had done what she did to prevent them from being, again in her word, "used."

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Now, you're going to hear expert testimony about what that language means. You're going to hear from a psychiatrist and from investigators who have spent their careers studying a particular kind of delusion that swept through communities across this country in the early 1980s. And you may find yourselves thinking that this defendant is sick. That she is to be pitied. I expect Mr. Pfeiffer will ask you to think exactly that.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But here is what I ask you to keep in mind. Whether Eleanor Vance believed what she believed — whether she was sincere or whether she was calculated — Michael Whitley is dead. Sarah Cole is dead. Christopher Pruitt is dead. They are dead because she suffocated a four-year-old boy with a pillow. Because she held a two-year-old girl beneath the water of a bathtub. Because she fed a three-year-old boy —

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: Objection, Your Honor. Counsel is arguing facts not in evidence and characterizing the manner of death in language that is —

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Overruled. This is opening statement. Mr. Hunsaker, you may continue, but the Court reminds the jury that opening statements are not evidence.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Thank you, Your Honor. I'll let the evidence speak for itself.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: State calls Dr. Warren Lyle Britten.

*(Whereupon the witness was duly sworn.)*

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Dr. Britten, would you state your name, occupation, and qualifications for the record.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DR. BRITTEN: Warren Lyle Britten. I'm a forensic psychiatrist. I hold an MD from Baylor College of Medicine, completed my residency at UT Southwestern, and I've been in clinical and forensic practice for nineteen years. I'm board certified in both general and forensic psychiatry. I've consulted on approximately three hundred criminal cases in the state of Texas.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: And you were retained by the State to evaluate the defendant, Eleanor Vance?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DR. BRITTEN: I was. I conducted two interviews with Ms. Vance at the Sutton County jail. The first on July the fourteenth, the second on July the twenty-eighth, 1987. Each interview was approximately three hours in duration.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: What were your findings?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DR. BRITTEN: Ms. Vance presents as a highly intelligent, highly organized individual. She is articulate, cooperative, and oriented to time, place, and person. She shows no gross impairment of memory or cognition.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Is she competent to stand trial?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DR. BRITTEN: In my professional opinion, yes. She understands the charges, she understands the proceedings, and she is capable of assisting in her own defense.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: Did you form any diagnosis?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DR. BRITTEN: I did. Ms. Vance exhibits features consistent with a shared delusional framework — what we sometimes call folie à plusieurs, or a communicated psychosis — that has been documented with increasing frequency in cases involving allegations of Satanic Ritual Abuse. Her belief system is internally consistent, highly detailed, and resistant to challenge. She believes she was in contact with a specific entity — she uses the Latin term *malus pastor*, which translates roughly to "the bad shepherd" — and that this entity selected or "chose" children through a ritualized process.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. HUNSAKER: In your opinion, does this delusion affect her culpability?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MR. PFEIFFER: Objection. Calls for a legal conclusion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;THE COURT: Sustained. Rephrase, Mr. Hunsaker.
