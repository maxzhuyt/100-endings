# INCIDENT DOCUMENTATION: PROVISIONAL
**Site-19 | Researcher Designation: Dr. C. Maren | Clearance Level 3**
**SCP Assignment: SCP-████ | Rotation Week: 14**
**Document Type: Researcher Personal Log, Submitted for Review**
**Status: PENDING O5 AUTHORIZATION**

---

*The following document was compiled by Dr. C. Maren over a period of eleven days and submitted voluntarily to Site Command on [REDACTED]. It is reproduced here in full, including embedded materials, per O5-7's directive that no portion be summarized or paraphrased in preliminary review. Annotations by Site Psychologist Dr. R. Osei appear in brackets where contextually necessary.*

---

**Day 1.**

Standard rotation. SCP-████ is stable. I'm adjusting to the new workspace in B-Wing — smaller than my last posting, but closer to the containment observation deck, which cuts twenty minutes off my morning check. I take my coffee black. I keep my files in order. I have been a Foundation researcher for nine years and I am not, by disposition, a person who notices things that aren't there.

I found the note under my coffee mug on Tuesday.

I moved the mug to refill it and the paper was just there, folded once. My first thought — and I want to be precise about this, because the sequence matters — was that the handwriting was mine. I recognized it the way you recognize your own face in a window reflection. Before I read a word. Before I registered that I hadn't written anything on that paper, had no memory of writing anything on that paper, knew with the certainty of a person who keeps their workspace orderly that the paper had not been there when I set the mug down.

Handwriting first. Then I read it.

*Room 14. March 9.*

That was all. The date was four days away.

My first explanation was the reasonable one: I had written a reminder to myself and forgotten. I do this sometimes with rooms and dates — cross-lab coordination, equipment sign-out. I filed it with my other notes and went back to work.

I did not go to Room 14 on March 9. Nothing happened on March 9 that I noted as significant. I've checked my logs.

---

**Day 3.**

I submitted the note for materials analysis on Wednesday, after I found the second one. Protocol. I've spent nine years in this institution learning that the correct response to an anomalous object is not to test it with your intuition but to test it with a lab.

The second note said: *Don't trust the transfer schedule.*

Same handwriting. Same paper stock — standard Foundation stationery, available at any supply station, which at the time seemed like the detail that mattered. I found it in a personnel file I'd just pulled from the cabinet. The file was sealed. I opened it myself.

I logged both notes. I noted the handwriting match. I submitted them — the originals, in evidence bags — to Materials Analysis and went back to my rotation.

[*Dr. Osei note: The materials analysis referenced here corresponds to Lab Report 7741-C, filed March 11. Full report attached as Appendix A. Summary: Ink composition — trace elemental analysis returned results inconsistent with any pen in current Foundation inventory or any commercially available pen stock. Specific anomaly: elevated iridium content, approximately 340% above baseline for standard rollerball cartridges. Temperature isotope signature consistent with storage at approximately 4°C for an extended period — estimated range 3 months to 2 years — prior to use. The pens in Dr. Maren's workspace tested negative for iridium content. Dr. Maren does not own a pen with an iridium-based ink formulation. The handwriting is Dr. Maren's.*]

I read the lab report twice. Then I filed it. Then I made coffee. Then I sat at my desk for approximately twenty minutes doing nothing, which is not something I do.

The ink is not from any pen I own. The handwriting is mine.

I understand these two facts cannot both be true. I have not found a third option that resolves them. I went back to work.

---

**Day 5.**

More notes. I'll reproduce them here in the order I found them.

---

> *Don't let them move it to Wing C. The breach happens during the transfer. I don't know exactly when — I didn't know when I wrote this either — but it's the movement that triggers it. Keep it stationary. Argue procedure. Argue anything.*

---

> *You're going to want to know how I'm sending these. I don't know. I'm writing them and leaving them in the places I remember finding them and they're getting there, somehow, and if that sounds insane to you it sounds insane to me too. I'm you. I know how we feel about unfalsifiable claims.*

---

> *March 14 the observation log shows no anomalous activity. I've checked. I'm checking from [REDACTED] and the log is clean. It should be clean — nothing happened on March 14. But March 22 — C. I need you to protect March 22.*

---

> *—can't keep the sentences together. Writing this in the B-Wing bathroom because I don't know who is reading my notes in the office. Not the notes I'm sending. The other ones. My work notes. Someone is checking. I can feel them*

*checking I can feel*

*the handwriting is mine I know it's mine I swear to you it's mine please*

---

The fourth note is the one I've read most often. The handwriting is mine — the same recognition, before the words, the face in the window — but the letter formations are compressed, rushed, the pen having pressed so hard in places that the paper is raised on the reverse side. The word *checking* appears twice on the same line, overlapping.

I'm going to state clearly what I observed in reading these notes and what I concluded.

What I observed: an apparent escalation in distress across sequential written communications bearing my handwriting. The third note contains two dates. The first — March 14 — I can confirm. I reviewed the observation log for that date. It is clean. Nothing anomalous. The date is accurate. The second date — March 22 — is nine days from the moment I'm writing this. I cannot confirm or disconfirm it.

What I concluded: either these notes were produced by me, in a future state, under conditions I don't yet understand, and represent a genuine attempt to communicate temporal information across an interval — or they were produced by something else, using my handwriting, and the something else is capable of manufacturing not only the physical characteristics of my written hand but emotional states that map precisely onto how I would feel if I believed what the notes were telling me.

I would like to record that I find neither of these conclusions acceptable and that I have to operate inside one of them regardless.

I went back to work.

---

**Day 7.**

Site command initiated evaluation protocol this morning. I was expecting it — I submitted everything voluntarily, I know the procedures. Dr. Osei was correct that the sleep deprivation screening would return elevated markers. I haven't been sleeping well. This is noted in her report and she has noted it fairly.

The cognitohazard exposure screening returned negative. The memetic contamination panel returned negative.

The competing hypothesis, which I was informed of formally in a meeting I will describe below, is this:

SCP-████ — the entity on my current research rotation — has a documented behavioral profile that includes the production of false documentary evidence in subjects under prolonged exposure. I'm in week fourteen of this rotation. This is within the exposure window associated with onset of the effect. The effect, as documented, involves the subject receiving materials that appear to originate from themselves — notes, photographs, audio recordings — which do not. The materials are manufactured by the entity. The handwriting, the voice, the visual self-identification: manufactured.

The purpose, as documented across previous exposure cases, appears to be psychological destabilization. Specifically: the progressive erosion of the subject's confidence in their own perceptions, their own competence, their own account of events.

I was told this by my supervisors, who are correct to have investigated it, who are following protocol, who are doing exactly what I would do if one of my colleagues presented me with this situation.

I sat in that meeting and I understood every word of it and I agreed that the hypothesis was sound.

I also know what the notes feel like to read. I know the handwriting is mine. I know the difference between my face in a window and a photograph of someone who resembles me. I cannot prove this distinction to anyone else and I understand that I cannot prove it and I understand that an entity capable of manufacturing my handwriting might also be capable of manufacturing my certainty about my handwriting.

I don't know which explanation to want.

If the notes are real, a breach is coming, and I have information that could prevent it, and I am sitting in evaluation instead of acting on it.

If the notes are manufactured, I have been compromised, and the entity is more dangerous than its current classification reflects, and I am sitting in evaluation while it continues.

In neither version of this situation is the evaluation room the correct place for me to be. In neither version is there a correct place for me to be.

I was cleared to return to limited duties. I noted the outcome and went back to work.

---

**Day 9.**

I want to record what happened on the morning of March 18 with the precision the moment requires, because I understand it will be evaluated and I want the evaluation to have accurate material.

At 09:14, I attended the weekly cross-team briefing in Conference B. The briefing covered, among other items, a proposed schedule adjustment for SCP-████'s tertiary observation protocol. The adjustment was presented by Dr. F. Lau using a specific phrase — *operational adjacency conflict* — which I had not previously encountered in Foundation documentation and which struck me, during the meeting, as an unusual locution.

I noted the phrase because one of the notes — the first coherent one, the one I found on Day 5 — uses it.

After the meeting I retrieved the note from evidence storage and confirmed. *Operational adjacency conflict.* In my handwriting, on paper whose ink contains iridium isotopes consistent with storage at 4°C, submitted to materials analysis before March 18, before the briefing, before Dr. Lau used the phrase in a meeting that had not yet occurred when I submitted the note.

I confirmed the evidence submission timestamp with the lab. I confirmed my own log entries. I confirmed that I have not had prior professional contact with Dr. Lau that would have exposed me to her phrasing conventions.

The phrase was in the note before I heard it spoken.

I want to be careful here. I know what this feels like like to write. It feels like relief and it feels like confirmation and I know — I have spent nine years knowing — that the feeling of confirmation is not the same as confirmation, that the sensation of something clicking into place is one of the oldest cognitive vulnerabilities there is, that I may have manufactured a connection between a note and a phrase that any careful reader could find if they wanted one.

I do not believe I manufactured this. I cannot prove I didn't. I am recording it precisely and I am submitting it to review and I am noting that the breach is now four days away and I am asking, formally, in this log that will be submitted to O5, that the transfer to Wing C not be authorized.

I am asking. I understand asking may not be enough. I am asking anyway.

---

**Day 11.**

The last note arrived this morning.

I found it in my desk drawer, under a folder I opened to retrieve a form. The paper is the same stock. The handwriting is mine.

Most of it is illegible. There are words written over words — sentences begun and abandoned and begun again in the margins, the pen having traced the same letters so many times in some places that the paper is almost translucent. I can make out my name twice. I can make out a room number: 14, which was in the first note, which I did not visit on March 9.

There is one complete sentence, written at the bottom of the page, the letters very large and very even, the evenness of someone who had to slow down to produce it:

*You won't trust this either.*

Below it, something that may be a signature or may be a date or may be something that was never a word.

---

I'm going to stop writing the log entry here because I've been looking at the last note for an hour and I need to say something that isn't a clinical observation.

I don't know if I'm reading messages from myself. I don't know if I've been manufactured into a state of certainty by an entity that feeds on manufactured certainty. I don't know what happened in Room 14. I don't know what is going to happen on March 22, or whether it already happened somewhere I can't see yet, or whether the word *happen* applies correctly to events I might still be able to change.

What I know is that the note is right.

I'm reading it and I'm evaluating it and I'm assembling my evidence and I'm not trusting it — not fully, not the way the person who wrote it needed me to. I can feel myself not trusting it. I can feel the part of me that has spent nine years in institutions built to be skeptical, that has applied that skepticism correctly hundreds of times, applying it here, to this, to my own hand on a torn piece of paper that says *you won't trust this either.*

The person who wrote that sentence knew. Knew I would end up here, at my desk, in the position of evaluating the claim rather than answering it. Knew that the tools available to me — labs and protocols and psychologists and my own careful habits of mind — would all become the things standing between me and the message rather than the things helping me receive it.

She wrote it anyway. She kept writing, even after the sentences stopped working, even after the words folded over each other, even after — I think — she understood that this note, which is the last note, which means something, would be read by someone who would find the other notes and submit them and be evaluated and cleared to limited duties and still, still, not be able to trust it enough.

She wrote it anyway.

I'm submitting this log to O5 review. I'm formally reiterating my request that the Wing C transfer not be authorized. I'm noting that March 22 is three days away.

I'm going to go back to work.
