# Hacker Mindset

---

The WiFi network is called **GustosGuest** and the password is written on a chalkboard in chalk, which means it rotates never or monthly, and the server terminal behind the bar has a screen-saver but no lock icon in the corner, which means auto-lock is disabled, which means the bartender trusts his staff or hasn't thought about it, and the waiter who just passed me has a keycard clipped to his apron at hip height, logo-side out, which is an HID Proximity format, which can be cloned with hardware that fits in a jacket pocket, which I am not wearing tonight because I am on a date, I am on a date, I am here because I agreed to be here and I want to be here and I am going to stop doing this now.

I pick up the menu.

The menu has a QR code that links to the wine list.

I put the menu down and look at the candle.

---

Her name is Priya and she is eight minutes late, which is within normal variance, and she comes through the door already scanning the room, which I notice because I am doing the same thing and we make eye contact the way two people make eye contact when they have both been caught looking for an exit. She smiles. I smile. She crosses the room and there are things I should not be doing right now and I am doing all of them.

Gait: confident, not performative. No conscious squaring of the shoulders. She doesn't check her reflection in the window as she passes it, which people who are nervous about their appearance almost always do. Posture: easy, not rigid—she's comfortable or she's very good, and comfortable is more sustainable under load so I'm provisionally marking it as genuine. She's carrying the bag on her left side, hand free on the right, which is—that's nothing, that's just how people carry bags.

"Sorry, the train—"

"No, I was early," I say, which is true and also strategic, and I choose not to audit that.

She orders a Negroni. High-proof, bitter, spirit-forward—she's not ordering for palatability, she's ordering for herself. Low approval-seeking on the drink order, then. Either she's comfortable or she's decided that performing comfort is the right move, and I cannot tell the difference from a drink order and I should not be trying to.

She's looking at me directly. Not the practiced directness of someone who read an article about confident eye contact, which has a quality of held stillness to it, like a controlled experiment. This is something else. She glances away and comes back. She's actually looking at me, the way you look at something you're trying to understand.

I feel a version of guilt that I recognize as data about myself. I note the guilt. I note that I am noting it.

*Noting your own surveillance,* I think, *is not the same as stopping it.*

---

We are twenty minutes in and talking about jobs, which is the aperitif of conversation—something to hold while the real thing is being prepared—and she says she does risk assessment for a fintech firm, which makes me laugh, actually laugh, not a social-function laugh, and she looks at me like *what* and I say "I just—risk assessment," and she says "what do you do?" and I tell her, and she nods slowly with the specific nod of someone placing information into an existing framework rather than building a new one.

"So you break into things," she says.

"I find where the doors are weak. Someone else decides what to do about it."

"That's a very careful sentence."

It is. I wrote it, more or less, six years ago, and I have been using it since. This is the first time someone has said that out loud to me.

She's turning her Negroni glass by the stem, small rotations, not nervous, thoughtful. She says: "Do you play Magic?"

And something in my head goes very quiet.

---

It's the way she says it. Not *oh, do you play Magic, haha, nerd stuff*—she says it the way people say the name of a city they come from, with the assumption that it exists, with the assumption that it is a real place. It lands without self-consciousness.

"Yes," I say.

"What format?"

"I started in Standard, I live in Commander now. You?"

And her face does something. It reorganizes. It's like watching a room after someone turns on more lights—the same furniture, but you can see into the corners now. She leans forward and says "I *hate* Commander, there are seventeen people and it takes four hours and someone always brings an infinite combo and then acts offended when everyone hates them for it—"

I'm laughing again.

"—I Draft," she says. "I love Draft. I love that you have to build with what you're given."

"Constraints are good design," I say, and she points at me like *yes, exactly* and we are off.

The sentences come faster and they are not careful. I am recommending a set she hasn't played, describing an interaction between two cards with the specific lovingness that this kind of explanation requires, where you have to trust that the other person cares about the mechanics—and she does, she's following the ruling, she's already asking about the edge case—and I notice, somewhere in the middle of this, that the threat catalogue has stopped running.

I notice it the way you notice a loud refrigerator only when it stops humming. The absence of it is its own loud event.

I am, improbably, just here.

---

Except.

She mentions Siege Rhino, which is a nostalgia card, a card people remember fondly without necessarily playing with precision. She mentions Thoughtseize with slight reverence, which is a card control players love because it lets you see the opponent's hand, lets you know the shape of the threat before it arrives. She mentions—in passing, as an aside about a Limited format she hated—that she'd rather lose quickly than lose slowly.

I am not doing this. I am not building the profile.

*Thoughtseize*, I think privately. *Patient. Needs information before she commits. Wants to see the hand.*

Siege Rhino is an efficient threat with incidental life gain—someone who wants good value without flash, without spectacle. The preference for quick loss over slow: high agency, low tolerance for controlled deterioration. She'd rather pull the plug than run on life support.

I know things about her now. I know them from a card game. The things I know feel accurate and I cannot decide if that is a gift or a failure.

She's saying something about a Draft she played last week. I'm listening. I'm also building the last few entries. I can't stop and I know I can't stop, and I'm present enough at least to feel the specific sadness of that, which is something, which might not be enough.

---

She reaches for her phone. A notification—something small, nothing—and she glances at it and dismisses it and the screen lights up in the process, and I see her unlock it, and it is a Z.

A Z swipe pattern.

Starting from the top left, across, diagonal, across. Predictable. Brute-forceable. A pattern that a person chooses not because they're careless but because it feels satisfying to draw, because the letter means something, maybe her name, maybe someone's name, because people are not building threat models when they choose swipe patterns, they are choosing something that feels like theirs.

I know this.

I know this.

"That's a Z," I say. "Your swipe pattern."

There is a pause that I would place, precisely, at one and a half seconds.

Her face doesn't close—not fully—but something in it recalibrates. The lights don't go out, but the quality of the light changes. Her smile stays but it moves slightly, becomes something she is choosing rather than something that is simply happening.

"Yeah," she says.

One word, measured, doing a great deal of work. It is the word of someone deciding how much to give to this moment.

I hear myself as if from a distance: a man at a restaurant table who has just demonstrated, without invitation, that he was watching her at a level of detail she did not consent to. Who has just made the restaurant into a system he was reading when she thought they were just having dinner.

The candle between us is exactly the same. The room is exactly the same.

Something has changed.

---

The internal monologue becomes very fast and not very kind.

*Okay. Damage assessment. Genuine discomfort: moderate to high. Not a shutdown, because she didn't close her posture, she's still facing me. Recovery window: open but narrowing. Options:*

*One: Address it directly. Risk: over-explaining reads as deflection. Benefit: demonstrates awareness.*

*Two: Make a self-deprecating joke. Risk: jokes about the thing you just did can function as permission-seeking rather than apology. Benefit: changes the register quickly.*

*Three: Wait. Create space. Let her decide how large this is. Risk: silence gets read as non-remorse. Benefit: doesn't colonize her reaction with your management of it.*

I am, I note with something that is not quite pride, running an exploit on her patience and goodwill to repair the damage I caused with a different exploit on her privacy. I am aware of this. The awareness does not stop me.

"I'm sorry," I say, which is not on the options list, which surprised me a little. "I saw it and I said it before—" I stop. *Before what?* Before the part of me that knows better could close the connection. "I do this. I can't always tell when I'm doing it until I've already done it."

She looks at me for a long moment.

"Is that true," she says, "or is that the version of it that's most forgivable?"

The accuracy of this lands like a test I didn't study for and she can see that it has.

"Both," I say. "Probably."

She looks at me for another moment. Then she picks up her Negroni.

---

She laughs. It comes out of nowhere and it is real—I'm almost certain it's real, there is a physical quality to genuine laughter, a release of muscular tension in the shoulders and around the eyes, an involuntary showing of teeth—but the almost is a permanent qualification I cannot remove. I am not allowed to remove it.

"You're a lot," she says.

The check comes. We split it—she picks up the card reader before I do, which I'm choosing to read as reflex rather than message, which might be wrong.

---

I walk the twelve blocks to the parking garage because the night is cool and I need the movement and I am still running the session log, I can't close it, it loops back through itself every few blocks.

*Cards: Thoughtseize, Siege Rhino, would rather lose fast than slow. Control player who Drafts, who builds with constraints. Guarded but genuinely curious. High pattern recognition. Noticed that my sentence about breaking in was careful—first person to say that out loud to me in six years, possibly the first person who was in a position to notice.*

*Last entry: she said "you're a lot" and she was still there when she said it.*

I stop at an intersection.

What card is that.

What card do you assign to someone who looks at the thing that makes you hard to be with, and names it without leaving.

What card do you assign to yourself—to the person who sees every vulnerability, who catalogued a stranger's eye contact and drink order and swipe pattern and card preferences and called it understanding, who cannot always tell what they are supposed to close?

I don't have it.

I walk the last two blocks and sit in my car without starting it and I'm still not sure—about the laugh, about the last look when she said goodbye, about whether the evening was a success or a breach or whether those categories apply to this at all, whether they were ever designed to.

*The scanner went quiet,* I think. *For a few minutes. Between the Siege Rhino and the Thoughtseize. I was just there.*

That happened.

I don't know what to do with it but it happened and I'm not going to audit it.

I start the car.
