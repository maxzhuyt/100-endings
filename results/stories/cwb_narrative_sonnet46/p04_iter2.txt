# My Apocalypse Buddy

---

The map was, Midas had to admit, more of a suggestion than a document. It covered approximately one-third of the actual territory in question, had been annotated in three different colors of marker (two of which meant the same thing, and the third of which meant something Midas could no longer remember), and featured, in the upper-right corner, a drawing of a dog that Chewy had apparently added at some point during the night.

The dog was better rendered than the map.

"Right," said Midas, spreading it across the upturned recycling bin that served as their planning table, their dining table, and, on one occasion neither of them discussed, their operating table. "Here's where we are."

He pointed to the lower-left corner, where someone—himself, younger, more confident, more wrong—had written *HOME* in red marker and underlined it twice.

"And here"—he traced a route with his finger through several elided streets and one junction that the map indicated with a small, unexplained flame—"is where they are."

Chewy, seated across from him on the remains of a beanbag that had slowly digested itself into a flattened canvas pancake of polystyrene beads, regarded the map. Her eyes, which had been a calm and operational hazel when she woke up, shifted to amber.

The amber meant: *I am already skeptical and it is not yet eight in the morning.*

"It's not complicated," Midas said.

Her eyes maintained amber with the patient confidence of a traffic light that knows what it's doing.

"The distribution point opens at ten. They've got medical supplies—actual ones, not the kind that used to be medical supplies before someone rebuilt them into something else. Anya said she saw paracetamol. Unopened. Still in the blister packs."

Chewy looked at the map. She looked at the flame.

"The fire was last winter," Midas said. "The sorting office fire. You remember."

She remembered. Her eyes said she remembered and also that she had noticed he was using the sorting office fire as though it were an event that had concluded and would not recur, rather than an event that had concluded and had created, in its wake, a vacuum of sorted infrastructure that something would inevitably rush to fill, without any guarantee that the something would be an improvement on sorting.

"Look," Midas said. He put his hands flat on the map in the manner of a man who has watched other men put their hands flat on maps and found it convincing. He was not, technically, any taller than he'd been a moment ago, but he gave the impression of standing straighter, which was nearly the same thing. "We need the paracetamol because your left knee makes that noise. We need the knee to stop making that noise because if it does that when we're somewhere quiet, which is everywhere now, someone will hear it. And I need someone to not hear it because—"

Chewy's eyes went briefly to a color that was, medically speaking, yellow, and which Midas had come to understand meant *I am aware of my own knee, thank you.*

"Because you are my partner," Midas finished, "and I would prefer you to continue operating at peak capacity."

This was, he knew, not precisely the term for what Chewy did. Chewy was a stuffed dog standing approximately sixty centimeters tall, constructed from what appeared to be the fabric of a former North Face sleeping bag, with a face that a manufacturer had intended to make reassuring and which had achieved instead a expression of permanent, dignified assessment. Her ears were slightly asymmetrical. Her nose had been replaced at some point with a button of a different size and color than the original, giving her the air of someone who has made a practical decision and is at peace with it. She had, as far as Midas could determine, no formal operating specifications.

She also had the eyes, which were not a thing the manufacturer had put there, and which could not be explained in any way that Midas found sufficient, and which he had therefore stopped trying to explain approximately three months ago.

The eyes went back to amber.

"That's a yes," Midas said.

Her eyes said nothing about whether that was a yes.

---

The shelter was in what had been a double-fronted estate agent's office, which meant it had large windows they'd covered with the more optimistic property listings for insulation—five-bedroom semis, south-facing gardens, *priced to sell*—and carpet tiles in a shade of grey that had presumably once aspired to corporate professionalism and now simply aspired to continuing to exist. In one corner, Midas kept the equipment: a rucksack, a torch, a carefully maintained inventory of items that might be useful, and, in a ziplock bag he tried not to look at directly, the last three biscuits from a box he hadn't been able to bring himself to open since February.

In another corner, Chewy kept nothing, because she had no possessions in the conventional sense, but she sat there as though she did, which produced something like the same effect.

Midas shouldered the rucksack. Chewy's eyes cycled briefly through amber and something approaching ochre, which generally indicated she was running a private calculation she did not intend to share.

"Ready?" Midas said.

She looked at him.

"You're right," he said. "Ready is a strong word. Present. We're present."

They went out into the morning.

---

The street greeted them with the particular indifference of things that have outlasted the people who named them. Midas had lived here—well. Midas had lived somewhere around here. The city had rearranged itself over the last two years in ways that were not dramatic, exactly, but were consistent and cumulative, the way a face changes not in any single moment but across a span of time so that eventually someone looks in a mirror and performs a small, involuntary audit.

What had been the Tesco Express on the corner was now a Tesco Express in the sense that it still had the lettering, but the lettering was partly obscured by a handwritten sign reading COLLECTIVE RESOURCE FACILITATION POINT (TESCO) and the windows had been blacked out and the automatic doors had been propped open with a length of scaffolding pipe through which a brisk trade was being conducted in items that Tesco had not previously stocked: rope, repaired boots, small mechanical components of uncertain provenance, and what appeared to be a live chicken operating as its own form of currency.

The chicken observed Midas's passage without interest. It had, presumably, seen worse.

There was a roundabout further along the high street that had been planted, at some point during the early period when everyone still thought someone else would sort things out, with vegetables. The vegetables had grown into the roundabout with the enthusiasm of things that have been told to colonize, and the result was a traffic island approximately chest-high in kale. A road sign at the center had been amended by someone with a ladder and a sense of irony: the standard arrows indicating *RING ROAD* and *TOWN CENTRE* remained, but a third arrow pointed straight up, at the sky, with the words *PREVIOUS MANAGEMENT* written beneath it.

Midas had walked past this sign forty or fifty times. It still struck him as accurate.

Chewy walked beside him with the gait of an entity that does not technically have a gait and yet manages one anyway. Her knee, the left one, made a small sound as they rounded the kale roundabout.

*Tck.*

Not loud. Just present. Like a clock that has learned to be polite about it.

---

The route to the distribution point took them through what had been a retail park, which remained a retail park in the same way that a skeleton remains a person: technically continuous, spiritually distinct. The car park was being used for a collection of purposes that the architects had not anticipated, including but not limited to: a livestock pen (three goats, deeply committed to their own agenda), an informal repair workshop that had expanded its remit to cover items the repairer had not formally studied but was willing to attempt, and a series of small residences constructed inside and around the former Currys PC World, the signage of which had been adapted with the addition of two letters to read CURRYS PC WORLD IS FINE NOW.

No one had asked whether it was fine. The sign seemed content to assert it on its own terms.

The distribution point was in the multi-story, level three, because level three had a ceiling but also significant gaps in the outer wall that functioned as windows and served, in the mornings, to make the space feel almost optimistic. Someone had strung lights along the support pillars, not particularly straight, and balanced this out with a handmade banner that read SUPPLIES (TODAY) above a table staffed by three people in high-visibility vests that had long since ceased to indicate any specific function and now simply indicated that the wearers wished to be taken seriously.

Midas recognized one of them immediately.

And this was unfortunate, because she recognized him immediately, and in the moment between them both recognizing each other and finding something better to do with their faces, it became clear that the recognition was not the warm and nostalgic kind.

It was the kind of recognition that carries a full case file in its subtext.

"Midas," said the woman behind the table, in a voice that had decided to be neutral and had achieved it by force of will rather than inclination. Her name was Petra and she had a scar above her left eyebrow from what she described as an incident with a traffic barrier and what everyone who had been present described as a mutual incident that Midas had been extremely not at fault in, technically, but which Petra had categorized under a more personal heading.

"Petra," said Midas. "You're here."

"I'm here," said Petra.

"You're staffing the distribution point."

"I'm staffing the distribution point."

There was a silence of the type that could support a full orchestra.

Chewy's eyes, which Midas would very much have preferred not to look at in this moment, had shifted to a color that existed somewhere between amber and terracotta. It was the color of an entity that had been briefed that there might be complications and had allocated it one unit of anticipation, and was now experiencing the quiet satisfaction of having allocated correctly.

"Great," said Midas. "So. I was hoping to—"

"The medical supplies are allocated by need," Petra said. "Documented need. You've got the form?"

Midas had, and presented it, and it was rejected on the grounds that he had filled in section four twice, by accident, and left section seven blank, by design because he had not been able to determine what section seven was asking, and had written the wrong date in section two because he continued to get the month wrong and had for approximately the last year and a half.

"I can fix the form," Midas said.

"You'll need to resubmit."

"Resubmit to who?"

"To me."

"And you'll—"

"Be here Thursday."

It was Tuesday.

Chewy made the knee sound.

*Tck.*

Petra looked at Chewy for the first time. This was, usually, how it went with people who hadn't met Chewy before—a double-take that resolved not into the expected skepticism but into something more complicated, because Chewy looked back, and the looking-back had a quality that discouraged casual dismissal. Petra looked at Chewy. Chewy's eyes, which had settled into a deep, considered amber, looked at Petra with the patience of someone reading a situation and believing it containable.

"What's wrong with its knee?" Petra said.

"Her knee," Midas said, reflexively. "And it's—structural. We think. We don't know, technically. You'd need to—" He caught himself explaining things he didn't understand and abandoned the attempt. "It's why we need the paracetamol."

"Paracetamol doesn't fix structural—"

"She's not human," Midas said. "The effects aren't—look, it works differently. It just does. I know it shouldn't, and I've wondered about it, believe me, I've spent considerable time wondering about it, and the answer I have arrived at is that it works and I've decided not to press it."

Petra looked at him. She looked at Chewy. She looked at the damaged form.

"Thursday," she said, but with approximately three percent less conviction than before.

---

Outside the multi-story, in the not-quite-noon light, Midas sat on a low wall and looked at the form and knew he was going to have to go back inside to get a new form, because the process for getting a new form was separate from the process for submitting a form, and there was a good chance the forms were different people's problems, and he was going to have to explain all of it to someone who had never met him, which was statistically an improvement but logistically a new challenge.

Chewy sat beside him. Her knee made the sound. *Tck.*

"It's fine," Midas said. "This is a recoverable situation. We get a new form. Fill it in correctly. Come back Thursday."

Chewy's eyes were a color he hadn't quite seen before—a sort of dim ochre that had been thinking about itself.

"I know Thursday is two days," Midas said. "But two days is not—two days is fine. We have supplies for two days, mostly. We have the biscuits if—"

Something in the direction of the Currys PC World produced a sound that shouldn't have been produced by anything in the direction of the Currys PC World.

It was a large sound. The kind of sound that announces itself not by being loud but by displacing volume—by moving air and expectation simultaneously.

It was followed, with a pause that seemed almost courteous, by shouting.

Chewy's eyes went dark amber. Nearly brown.

"That's almost certainly nothing to do with us," Midas said.

They both looked at the direction of the sound.

"Almost certainly," he repeated.

From across the car park, one of the goats—the small one, the one that had been minding its own business—came past at a velocity that goats do not typically achieve, apparently having decided that its business was now elsewhere and was quite urgent about it.

Three seconds later, a man ran through roughly the same space, though unlike the goat he had the presence of mind to look embarrassed about it.

Three seconds after that, two more people appeared from behind the former Currys PC World, and then a fourth, and then a fifth, and the shouting became directional, and then there was a second, distinct, category of sound—mechanical, protesting—and the near side of the Currys PC World shed a portion of exterior cladding that had, until quite recently, been helping to hold in whatever CURRYS PC WORLD IS FINE NOW had been holding.

Midas stood up.

"We should—"

But Chewy was already on her feet, if the termination of her legs could be called feet, which was technically generous, and her eyes had gone a color he could only describe as *operative*—not a color he'd seen on an eye-color chart and not a color he'd have approved in advance, but a focused, purposeful, dense sort of color that meant she had assessed the situation and reached a conclusion about what would happen next and her conclusion was that he needed to move.

He moved.

---

Midas had never fully understood what the FINE NOW enterprise was conducting inside the former Currys PC World, and he remained, across the subsequent twelve minutes, imperfectly informed, because the picture he assembled was built from the outside of a rapidly developing situation rather than from any authoritative briefing, and the picture had gaps.

What he was able to determine was as follows:

Someone had been operating a fuel distribution arrangement from the building—not official, not authorized under the current municipal framework, which was not the word anyone used anymore but which described the thing that filled the space where the council had been. The arrangement had been tolerated because it worked and because the people running it had achieved a kind of ambient social legitimacy by being useful more often than they were a problem, which was the standard by which most things were now measured.

The fuel was the problem—not the fuel itself but a portion of it that had been stored in containers that had been certified for the storage of something else, and which had communicated their objection to the current arrangement in the time-honored tradition of containers that have been asked to do too much for too long.

The result was structural, spectacular, and had distributed itself laterally across a portion of the car park in a radius that included, Midas was now aware, the direct route back to the multi-story.

It also included, in the worst possible deployment of previously noted information, the woman walking toward him from the direction of the smoke, high-visibility vest still in place, form in hand, who was either looking for him specifically or had arrived at his specific location through a coincidence of such precision that it amounted to the same thing.

"Midas," Petra said.

"I was just leaving," Midas said.

"You were just—there's a fire."
