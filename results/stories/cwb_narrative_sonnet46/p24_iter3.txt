# The Dead Letters

The building had been a textile mill once, which meant it had been a place of noise, and now it was a place where noise had gone to be forgotten. Agent Farrow said this when they arrived, standing in the Łódź dark with her equipment bag over one shoulder, breath fogging in the November air. She said it the way she said most things—as if the observation were a minor bureaucratic notation, filed and released. Dr. Osei did not respond. Agent Chu checked his dosimeter. Through the mill's upper windows, which were not windows anymore but openings, wind moved in the way wind moves through structures that no longer resist it.

They had been briefed on the door.

The briefing had not been adequate.

---

You find it in complete darkness. That is the first thing—not its presence but the fact of its presence, the amber light sliding through the gap between the door and the wall like something being very careful not to announce itself. Farrow put her hand near the edge and held it there for a moment without touching.

"Warm," she said.

"That's in the report," Chu said.

"Reports don't have hands."

The door was steel, unpainted, without marking or handle or hinge that they could identify from the outside. It opened anyway. It opened without being touched, which was less surprising once you understood—as the briefing had failed to make clear—that the door was not the anomaly. The door was only the boundary. The anomaly was everything that came after.

Dr. Osei stepped through first.

---

Interior temperature: eighteen degrees Celsius. Humidity: controlled. Air quality: nominal. She made these notes as she entered because entering needed to be accompanied by something procedural or the mind found other ways to absorb what it was seeing, and the other ways were not preferable. Shelving units extended away from them in three directions, floor-to-ceiling in a space that did not have a visible ceiling, lit by a sourceless light the color of old paper. The shelves were full. The air smelled of card stock and something underneath that—something older, not the smell of decay but its opposite, a smell of deliberate preservation, of things maintained against loss.

No dust.

That was the detail that worked its way through the procedural notation and arrived somewhere more interior. No dust on any surface. The archive was being tended.

"Forty-seven minutes to first document recovery," Farrow said later, in debrief. "Felt like less. Can't explain that."

The first document was a fragment, partial translation possible, language archaic in structure but bureaucratic in character in a way that Dr. Osei would spend the next three days finding words to describe. She described it this way eventually: *not unlike our own.* The phrasing bothered her. She knew why it bothered her. She kept it anyway.

---

The filing section organized by proper name was Agent Chu's discovery, which he made at one thirty-three in the morning by the mission clock, approximately forty meters from the entry point in a direction that did not correlate to any compass bearing in any way that could be explained. Alphabetized. Orderly. The sections separated by handwritten tab dividers in a script that was not quite any script they recognized but that resolved, over a few seconds of looking, into something legible.

"We should check for ourselves," Farrow said.

Dr. Osei looked at her.

"I'm being rhetorical," Farrow said.

She was not being rhetorical.

---

Here is what Agent Farrow's file contained:

A physical description, accurate to a degree that suggested either direct observation or access to personnel records, and the difference between these options was not, in this context, a comfort. A psychological evaluation—four paragraphs in the archaic bureaucratic language, translated later by Dr. Osei as follows: *tends toward gallows humor under stress; reliable under field conditions; in matters of personal significance, will resist acknowledgment until acknowledgment is no longer optional.* Known associates, listed by name, accurate. Date of birth, accurate.

The file was dated in a calendar system that used some of the same numerical conventions as their own but not others. The year was not a year any of them recognized.

The paper was not new.

Farrow read it standing up. She did not put it down. She stood holding it for approximately six minutes before she looked up and said, in a voice that was doing considerable work to remain in its normal register, "Osei. Come here."

Dr. Osei's file was filed three positions later. It contained a photograph, which was the detail she declined to describe in any of her subsequent reports. She would say only that she did not need it translated.

---

She requested return to the entry point at one forty-four in the morning. The request was formal, logged, and ignored, because Farrow had by then moved three sections over and was pulling a second file and a third, cross-referencing something, operating on a logic that Dr. Osei recognized as the logic of someone who has discovered a thing and cannot now choose not to know it. Agent Chu had found his own file and was sitting on the floor reading it with his back against the shelving unit, which was not standard field protocol, and which Dr. Osei chose not to address because there was no protocol vocabulary for this particular circumstance.

The entry point remained where they had left it. The door's amber glow was visible at approximately fifty meters, which was useful information and was also, now, not the most pressing information available.

They stayed. Research requires staying. The Foundation had given them a phrase for this: *continued site assessment*, which meant remaining in a place that wanted you gone until you understood why it wanted you gone. The archive did not want them gone. The archive, as far as they could determine, was indifferent to them in a way that was its own specific kind of unsettling—to be present in a space that has already made its accounting of you, that has already categorized and filed you, and to understand that your arrival changes nothing, that whatever this place is it was prepared for you specifically and prepared for you long before you arrived.

At two nineteen in the morning, they found the entities.

---

Four of them, in white full-body protective suits of a cut that suggested both familiarity with the form's requirements and some divergence from any Foundation-adjacent model. They carried handled cases of the kind used for archival transport. They moved with coordinated efficiency through an aisle approximately eighty meters from the team's position, and they were removing files—this was clear from their movement, the systematic quality of it, the cases being loaded and sealed and passed to the next figure in sequence.

"Hey," Agent Chu said.

They stopped.

Chu moved toward them.

They fled.

Dr. Osei is precise about the verb in her debrief. She returns to it several times. *Running,* she says, *is fast walking. Running is still walking, still moving through space, still occupying the path between one point and another.* What they did was not that. What they did was move with a desperation that seemed oriented not toward a destination but away from witnesses—as if being seen was, for them, the event that could not be permitted. They disappeared into the shelving and the shelving swallowed them and the archive resumed its silence.

The aisle they had vacated held only empty brackets. Whatever section they had been clearing was gone—files, folders, the tab dividers, everything. Gone cleanly, efficiently, without trace except for the brackets, which were still warm when Agent Chu pressed his hand against them.

He held his hand there for a moment.

"Warm," he said.

No one said anything about reports.

---

Farrow had not put her file down. She was holding it in a way that was not reading—she was not looking at it, specifically—but she was not putting it down. Dr. Osei had her own file in her equipment bag because the alternative was leaving it, and she could not identify which option was worse, and the inability to identify which option was worse was itself a kind of data.

They returned through the archive's sourceless light, past the ordered shelving, past the smell of preservation, past the sections whose tab dividers they did not read because some accounting of things is premature when the full extent of the accounting is still being understood. The door was where they had left it. The amber light leaked through its edges. Beyond it, the textile mill, the Łódź dark, the cold, the equipment bags they had set outside as if they had been planning to come back.

They were planning to come back. They came back. The Foundation designation for this is *continued site assessment.*

---

From the document recovered from the locked subsection, alphabetized under *O*, partial translation:

*...the second Directorate acknowledges that the containment failure at Site-Ur was not unforeseen. Records indicate seven internal memoranda warning of this precise cascading sequence were filed in the preceding decade and marked non-actionable by the senior council. The civilization does not end. It is administered into ending. What follows will inherit our files and inherit our errors and will call themselves by a new name and will not check whether the name has been used before...*

The language is archaic. The bureaucratic character is not.

---

The thirteen files in the subsection alphabetized under *O* each bear a two-digit number. The photographs in each file match public Foundation records for current sitting O5 Council members, which is a sentence that requires a moment to be read correctly. Not former members. Not members from the archive's own civilizational context. Current members, sitting members, faces that appear in the internal directories of a Foundation that does not officially know this archive exists.

Each file contains a visitation log.

Each log is dated. The dates range from three to eleven years before the Foundation's official discovery of the anomaly.

Each log is signed.

The signatures match.

---

Dr. Osei's final log entry was filed to Site Director Vasquez and, through standard escalation procedures, to the O5 Council. The Council's response was to deny a subsequent related request. The entry itself was neither confirmed received nor confirmed unread. These are not the same thing.

The entry reads, in part:

*We went back. The door still glows at the edges when the lights go out. Inside, the section containing our own files has been partially cleared. My file is gone. Farrow's remains. She will not discuss this.*

The entities were present again, farther in, moving fast. They did not follow the entities. This was a decision, not a failure. The distinction is in the record. It may be that the record is what the distinction is for.

---

Outside, the textile mill resumed being a place where noise had gone to be forgotten. The door was inside it, flush with the wall in the dark, lit at its edges by something amber and patient. Farrow stood outside for a while with her file in her hand. She had carried it out of the archive without deciding to. Now she held it in the Łódź dark and looked at the mill's upper windows and did not say anything with the quality of an observation being filed and released.

After a while she said: "It's already written down in there. What happens next."

"We don't know that," Chu said.

"It had the date I was born on a piece of paper written before there was paper like that. It had things about me I've never written down." 

"We don't know what it has."

"I do," she said. "About mine. I know what it has."

She did not say what it had. Agent Chu did not ask. Dr. Osei made a note that the distinction between *not asking* and *already knowing* was one she could not resolve from available evidence, and that this was probably the point.

They drove back to the site facility through streets that were not yet light. The archive remained in the mill. The door remained in the archive's wall. The amber light continued its patient work at the edges, illuminating the gap between what is known to be there and what lies beyond, which is a gap with a specific name in the Foundation's documentation and no adequate name in any other vocabulary.

Inside, something was still working.

Whether it was working for them was a question the archive had almost certainly already answered.

The file was in Dr. Osei's bag. She did not look at it again that night.

In the morning she submitted the report.
