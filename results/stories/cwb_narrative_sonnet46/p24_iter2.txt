# SCP-XXXX: "The Preceding Record"

**Item #:** SCP-XXXX

**Object Class:** Keter

---

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-XXXX cannot be contained. Current protocol designates all known instances as Site-Adjacent Observation Points. Personnel discovering additional instances are to photograph the door exterior only, report GPS coordinates to O5 Command, and withdraw. Personnel are not to enter without authorization from a sitting O5 member.

This instruction has proven, in practice, unenforceable.

---

**Description:**

SCP-XXXX designates a recurring anomalous door type, currently catalogued across 847 locations worldwide: decommissioned factories, collapsed apartment blocks, warehouses with no record of construction permits, and at least one building whose municipality has no official record of its existence. The doors are identical—steel, painted a gray that is not quite any standardized gray, fitted with bar handles worn smooth at the grip. The wear pattern on each handle is consistent across all specimens: a thumb-depression on the upper surface, a polish along the right side where a palm would rest while pulling. No manufacturer's mark appears on any specimen. No seam, no hinge pin, no visible fastening mechanism. The doors appear to have been installed from the inside.

The anomaly of primary note is weathering inconsistency. In every instance, SCP-XXXX doors exhibit corrosion, oxidation, and material fatigue measurably older than the structures in which they appear. The oldest host building currently known was erected in 1887. Core samples from that installation's door suggest material age in excess of six hundred years. No explanation for this discrepancy has been produced.

The newest host building currently known was completed in 2019. It is a mixed-use commercial development in Łódź, Poland. Its SCP-XXXX instance exhibits the same degree of material fatigue as every other specimen. The building's concrete was still curing when the door was found.

This, too, has not been explained.

Each door opens onto the same interior.

The interior is an archive.

**[00:04]** The door opens inward. Dr. ████ notes no resistance—not the absence of friction she would expect from a well-maintained hinge, but an absence of any counterforce whatsoever, as if the door were always already open and the act of pushing is a formality the door accommodates out of courtesy. Air from the interior is cool and carries a smell she will later describe as *paper and beneath paper something older, organic, the smell of material at the edge of becoming dust.* Flashlights activate. The team steps through.

The ceiling, if there is one, is not visible.

Shelving units extend in every direction. They are freestanding—no anchoring to walls visible, no bracing against one another—yet stable. The aisles between them maintain a consistent width of approximately 1.2 meters, which Agent Reyes notes is wide enough for two people to pass only if one turns sideways. Dr. Osei observes that the shelving is not consistent in material throughout: some units appear wooden, darkened with age, with the specific patina of furniture that has been touched by many hands over many generations; some are metal, riveted, industrial; some are a pale composite he cannot identify and does not touch. The overall impression, he says, is of *accretion rather than construction*. As if the archive grew.

He means it architecturally. He will later wonder if he meant it biologically.

Each shelf holds folders, ledgers, boxed documents, and materials that do not immediately resolve into any recognizable format. No labels on the exterior of any container yet examined. No signs, no directory, no map at the entrance. Only the aisles, extending.

Agent Summers notes that their footsteps echo. He notes this because the echo is wrong—it returns from a direction inconsistent with the space directly ahead of them, as if the sound is bouncing off something much closer than the visible geometry suggests, or something much farther away.

He does not note any other sounds. The archive is otherwise silent in a way that feels considered.

---

**[00:31]** First document extraction. Dr. ████ selects a folder from the nearest accessible shelf. The folder has no exterior marking. Inside: a series of manifests, handwritten, in a script she tentatively identifies as proto-Uralic—a language family that should not have produced administrative documentation of this sophistication at any period for which written records exist in the conventional historical archive. The documents are arranged neatly, no water damage, no foxing, no deterioration. They are old in the way that objects preserved in a controlled environment are old: technically ancient, vitally immediate.

On the second page of the manifest, partially visible beneath a translated notation in the margin (translated by an unknown hand, in German, in ink that is itself not new), is a geometric diagram.

Dr. ████ stares at this diagram for a moment. Agent Summers looks over her shoulder. Neither speaks.

The diagram represents a containment geometry. Not unfamiliar. Not unfamiliar at all. The proportions, the internal segmentation, the notation system indicating approach vectors and exclusion radii—these are recognizable. Not identical to current Foundation methodology. Close enough to constitute a dialect rather than a different language.

Agent Summers photographs the page. He does not comment. Dr. ████ replaces the folder and they continue.

---

**[01:17]** Dr. Osei locates what functions as an index.

It is a system of cross-referenced ledgers, maintained with a precision that becomes more alarming the longer he examines it: location codes keying to a global coordinate system that predates any known standardized mapping convention; designation codes that operate on a logic he can parse without being able to explain how he can parse it; and, at the rear of the third ledger, a personnel registry.

He begins searching. He finds it not because he knows to look for it but because the index appears, in some structural sense, to be *organized around the expectation of being searched.* Tabs. Clear divisions. A rhythm of access that his hands follow before his mind has committed to looking.

He finds Dr. ████'s name.

He does not say anything for eleven seconds. He has counted. Then he calls her over.

The file is a standard folder, thin, buff-colored in a way that no color specification has ever been able to precisely replicate in the Foundation's own documentation. Her name, her surname redacted as it is redacted in all Foundation records and redacted here in the same format—the same number of obscuring marks, the same style of deletion. Her height: correct. Her corrected vision: correct, listed by a metric system whose unit notation she does not recognize but whose values correspond. Her psychological evaluation scores from her Foundation intake assessment, conducted eight years ago in a room she remembers because the chair by the window had one short leg and rocked slightly when she shifted her weight.

The file is dated 1761.

Not the folder—the underlying document. The paper itself, which she does not touch. The ink, in the handwriting of no one she knows, recording a life she has not yet finished living, in the year 1761.

Dr. ████ notes in her incident report that her first thought was procedural: *update the anomaly classification.* She describes this, in the same report, as a coping response. She also notes that after eleven seconds of her own silence she closed the folder, replaced it in the index, and turned to continue the survey. That she did this deliberately—chose procedure over response—and that she is including this notation precisely because she knows what it means that procedure was what became available to her.

What it means, she does not specify. The reader is expected to understand.

---

**[02:19]** The team has now established that the archive extends—to the best of their measurement—at least 400 meters in three directions from the entry point, with no wall encountered and no terminus to the shelving. Spatial measurements have ceased to be reliable. Agent Reyes notes that the entry door, visible on return traverse, appears closer than the distance walked should permit. He notes this once. No one contests it.

Dr. Osei has been working through sections of the index. Cross-referencing. He does not share what he is finding except to say that the personnel registry is not limited to current Foundation staff. That it appears to include individuals across a span of centuries. That the filing system does not distinguish between them by era, only by designation. That the designation system bears structural resemblance to the one currently used by the administrative hierarchy of the organization for which they work.

He says: *I want to be clear that this observation doesn't imply anything I'm prepared to imply.*

No one asks him to clarify.

**Recovered Document XXXX-D-14 (Translated from recovered interior text, script identified as a variant of Medieval Latin incorporating loan-structures from an unidentified administrative language)**

*...the Threshold Authority acknowledges that containment of Class-Sovereign anomalies is no longer viable at current institutional capacity. The cascading failure designated Event Lacuna has propagated beyond modeled parameters. Member-states of the Authority have ceased communication. Field operatives are no longer returning from assignments. We record this not as a warning—there is no one to warn—but as the final institutional act of a body that believed, until the end, that documentation was itself a form of survival. A form, at minimum, of witness.*

*The Authority's naming protocols, containment procedures, personnel hierarchies, and object classification schema are appended in full to this record. Should any successor institution arise—and the historical pattern suggests one will—they will not start from nothing.*

*They have not started from nothing.*

*They will not know this until it is too late for the knowledge to constitute a choice.*

---

**[02:44]** Movement in a far aisle. Agent Reyes reports figures—humanoid, suited in what appears to be hazmat equipment of white and yellow coloring, carrying document boxes in the direction of an exit point the team has not yet identified. He calls out. All movement stops. All figures turn, in unison, toward the sound. He cannot see faces through the visors—the visors are opaque, reflective, angled slightly downward in a way that suggests the wearer's field of vision is directed at the floor rather than ahead. He counts six figures.

He calls out again. They do not respond.

Then they move away. Not running—something more controlled than running, with the specific efficiency of people who have done this before and know exactly how much speed is safe and exactly where they are going. Within forty seconds they are no longer visible. He does not hear a door.

What remains in the aisle: three shelving units, cleared to bare metal. The contents of those units are unknown. Their location codes do not match anything in the index because the index no longer contains those location codes.

This is not redaction. Redaction leaves evidence of itself—the black bar, the whited-out text, the gap that announces its own presence. This is something else. The pages that should precede and follow the absent entries are intact, uninterrupted, their sequence logical. There is no gap to notice. The absence is complete in itself. Only the bare metal of the shelves remains as physical evidence that something was here, and even that might, Dr. ████ notes, be misread as empty shelving awaiting new material.

She notes that this distinction—between a gap in the record and an absence so complete that the record reformats around it—is the most troubling thing she has encountered in the archive so far. More troubling than her own file. She notes this and does not explain it further, because she believes the explanation is implicit, because she believes the reader of this report is a person who will understand what it means when an institution learns to make its own omissions invisible.

She believes this, or she is writing toward the possibility that such a reader exists, which she acknowledges may not be the same thing.

---

**[03:05]** Agent Summers locates a visitor log near what may function as the archive's central access point—a recessed lectern, wooden, carved with notation systems in at least a dozen scripts, the carvings worn to near-illegibility by contact with hands. The log is a leather-bound ledger, its cover unembossed, its pages cream-colored and dense. Entries span the length of the ledger. Earlier entries are in languages the team cannot identify on sight. The script shifts, across the ledger's length, through what might be a historical progression—though none of them are paleographers and the identification is tentative. The most recent entries are in English.

The most recent entry is dated fourteen months ago.

The handwriting does not belong to any researcher presently assigned to the XXXX investigation. It does not match any sample in the team's reference materials. It is precise, undecorated, the handwriting of someone who writes regularly and functionally, not for legibility but for record. Three designations: O5-1. O5-4. O5-9. Beside each, a single notation, written once and then not repeated for the other two because repetition is unnecessary: *reviewed and understood.*

Dr. ████ photographs the page. She photographs the pages before it. The entries go back twenty-two years. They are regular—not frequent, averaging four to six visits per year, sometimes clustered, sometimes spaced by months. Different handwriting for different designations, consistent across entries attributed to the same visitor. One set of entries, attributed to a designation she does not recognize in the format she expects—a variation on the O5 structure, a prefix she has not encountered—spans the earliest English-language section and continues into a period where the language around it is still transitional. This set of entries spans forty-one years and the handwriting does not change.

She photographs this section without comment. She closes the ledger. She replaces it on the lectern.

She does not speak for a moment in which several things occur to her simultaneously, and she chooses not to narrate any of them in this log because the log is a record, and she is no longer certain of the record's security, and she has learned something today about what it means when an institution has been curating what its own members are permitted to know.

Then she instructs the team to leave.

They walk back through the aisles. The door is where they left it. The air outside is warmer than she remembered, or she had grown accustomed to the cool of the archive so thoroughly that the normal temperature of a condemned textile facility in Gdańsk reads as warmth. They step through.

The door remains open behind them for a moment. Then it closes, without sound, with the same absence of resistance with which it opened—accommodating the closure, as it accommodated the entry, as a formality.

**Addendum XXXX-A: Investigative Status**

Requests for comment submitted to O5-1, O5-4, and O5-9, via three separate internal channels including two flagged as highest-priority secure, have received no response. The request pipeline routing to O5-1 has been flagged as *misdirected* on three separate occasions and automatically closed. Dr. ████ has been unable to determine where the misdirection flag originates.

Dr. ████'s Level 4 clearance was suspended for forty-eight hours following the submission of this report's preliminary draft. The suspension was reversed without explanation, without acknowledgment that the suspension had occurred, and without any notation in her personnel file. She knows this because she checked. She checked the personnel file made available to her, which is to say the personnel file this institution permits her to see, which is to say she does not know what she does not know about what notations may or may not exist.

She is aware of the recursion.

The doors remain. The archive remains. Personnel assigned to the XXXX investigation continue to report, as a post-deployment observation, the sensation upon exiting of having been expected. Not watched—expected. The distinction, several researchers have noted independently without apparent coordination, feels important. Watching implies surveillance. Expectation implies a prior knowledge of arrival. Expectation implies a record already prepared.

The doors are always older than the buildings they appear in. This was initially documented as the anomaly's primary characteristic. The working assumption was that the doors were placed retroactively—inserted into structures, for reasons unknown, after construction. This assumption has not been formally abandoned. It has become simply harder to maintain in the presence of certain implications.

Because the doors are older than the buildings. And the buildings were not old when the doors appeared in them. And the archive contains files dated in 1761 for researchers who were born in the twentieth century. And the Threshold Authority, whatever it was, believed that keeping the record intact was the only form of continuity available to a collapsing institution. And the archive is organized as if for discovery. And the visitor log runs twenty-two years without a gap.
