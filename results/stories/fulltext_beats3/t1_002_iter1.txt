**OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT — STATE OF OHIO v. ELEANOR MARIE VANCE**
**Case No. 97-CR-0441**
**Sutton County Court of Common Pleas**
**Trial Day 3 — Direct Examination of Defendant**
**October 14, 1997**
**Court Reporter: J. Hartley, CSR-4412**

---

THE COURT: We're back on the record. Ms. Vance, I'll remind you that you remain under oath. Mr. Pruitt, you may continue.

MR. PRUITT: Thank you, Your Honor. Ms. Vance, directing your attention to the period of March through June of 1996, you were the assigned caseworker for the Godwin household, is that correct?

THE DEFENDANT: That's correct.

MR. PRUITT: And in that capacity, how frequently did you visit the home?

THE DEFENDANT: Twice a month, per protocol. The first and third Wednesdays.

MR. PRUITT: Can you describe for us what a typical visit looked like?

THE DEFENDANT: I would arrive between nine and ten in the morning. I would speak with the foster mother, Mrs. Godwin, review the children's school reports if applicable, conduct a visual inspection of the home, and then spend twenty to thirty minutes with each child individually. Standard wellness assessments. I documented everything.

MR. PRUITT: You documented everything.

THE DEFENDANT: Yes.

MR. PRUITT: Ms. Vance, I'd like to direct your attention to People's Exhibit 14. Do you recognize this photograph?

THE DEFENDANT: Yes. That's the bedroom of the two older children, Tyler and Brianna.

MR. PRUITT: And the markings on the wall above the bed — can you identify those?

THE DEFENDANT: They're drawings. The children drew on their walls. A lot of children draw on their walls.

MR. PRUITT: These are children's drawings.

THE DEFENDANT: Yes.

MR. PRUITT: Ms. Vance, I'm going to show you a detail enlargement of the same photograph, which has been marked as People's Exhibit 14-A. The markings directly above the headboard. Would you say these are children's drawings as well?

THE DEFENDANT: I —

[Defendant pauses. Approximately four seconds of silence.]

THE DEFENDANT: Those are — the children had books. Activity books. They liked to copy things. Brianna was especially — she liked patterns. She was good at patterns.

MR. PRUITT: These are copied patterns.

THE DEFENDANT: From a book. From their activity books.

MR. PRUITT: Your Honor, with the Court's permission, I'd like to show the witness People's Exhibit 22.

THE COURT: You may proceed.

MR. PRUITT: Ms. Vance, Exhibit 22 is a photograph of the markings found beneath the carpet in the basement, after the carpet was pulled up by investigators on July 9th, 1996. These markings extend over an area of approximately nine by twelve feet and were made in a substance identified by the state crime lab as a mixture of animal fat and calciumite powder. Do these also appear to you to be copies from a children's activity book?

THE DEFENDANT: I didn't — that was under the carpet. I never saw what was under the carpet.

MR. PRUITT: But you documented everything.

MR. ALDISS: Objection. Argumentative.

THE COURT: Sustained.

MR. PRUITT: I'll rephrase. Ms. Vance, is it your testimony that in fifteen months of bimonthly visits to the Godwin home, you never observed the markings shown in Exhibit 22?

THE DEFENDANT: The carpet was down. It was always down.

MR. PRUITT: Let me direct your attention now to People's Exhibit 23, another enlargement. This is a detail of the basement markings. I'd like you to compare it to 14-A, the markings from the children's bedroom. Do you observe any similarity?

[Defendant does not respond. Approximately seven seconds of silence.]

MR. PRUITT: Ms. Vance?

THE DEFENDANT: [Unintelligible.]

MR. PRUITT: I'm sorry, could you —

THE DEFENDANT: [Unintelligible vocalization, approximately fifteen seconds. See Appendix C for phonetic transcription.]

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, I need you to —

THE DEFENDANT: [Vocalization continues, volume increasing. Court reporter notes defendant is gripping the arms of the witness chair. Duration: approximately thirty-two seconds before marshals intervene.]

THE COURT: We're going to take a short recess. Marshals, please assist Ms. Vance. Mr. Aldiss, I'd like to see you and Mr. Pruitt in chambers. We're off the record.

[RECESS — 10:47 a.m.]

**AUDIO DIARY — J. HARTLEY**
**October 14, 1997 — 11:42 p.m.**

[Tape noise. Sound of a chair, possibly a refrigerator compressor in background.]

This is Janet Hartley, October 14th, a little before midnight. I'm at home. I should be asleep but I want to get this down while the session's fresh.

Today was... not unusual, actually. This is what I want to say clearly. Triday three. Pruitt is doing what Pruitt does — he's methodical, he builds, each exhibit is a stepping stone. I've worked with him before. He puts on a clean case. The defendant, Eleanor Vance, was perfectly composed through most of direct examination. Social worker voice. That particular tone they have — warm, measured, like they're perpetually de-escalating. She had an answer for everything. The drawings were children's drawings. The basement symbols were something she'd never seen beneath the carpet. All plausible. All delivered in this very calm, very practiced cadence.

Then the photographs. Not the first set — the bedroom photographs didn't bother her, or if they did, she handled it. But when Pruitt introduced the enlargements and asked her to compare them — the basement markings to the bedroom markings — that's when it happened. She just... produced sound.

I've been doing this work for eleven years. I've had witnesses break down, I've had defendants shout obscenities at judges, I had a man in a custody case sing "Danny Boy" at the top of his lungs until the marshals physically removed him. None of that is difficult to transcribe. It's words. It might be disruptive speech, but it's speech.

What Eleanor Vance produced was not speech. Not exactly.

I don't mean it was screaming. It was structured. It had rhythm, it had what sounded like intonation contours — rising and falling, the kind of prosody you hear in actual language. It's just that it wasn't any language.

Or — let me be precise. It wasn't any language I recognized. Which doesn't mean it wasn't language. There are thousands of languages I don't recognize.

[Pause. Seven seconds.]

So I went home and I did what I would do with any difficult segment. I pulled the backup audio — I always take the backup, the little Marantz, belt and suspenders — and I ran through the vocalization slowly. I made the phonetic transcription you'd expect. Three segments. The first two resolved into identifiable phoneme clusters. The third didn't. The third is the one I've been sitting with.

I should say — my job is accuracy. My entire professional value is that what I produce is a verbatim record of proceedings. When a defendant produces three segments of vocalization and I can only transcribe two of them, that's a problem. That's my problem. That's a gap in the record that I am responsible for filling.

So I spent some time with segment three. I slowed the playback. I tried different EQ settings — the Marantz has a simple bass and treble, nothing sophisticated. I listened on headphones. I listened on the speaker. And then, almost as a kind of diagnostic — I've done this before with garbled audio, it sometimes helps you hear the consonant attacks differently — I played the segment in reverse.

[Pause. Four seconds.]

I got a result.

I'm not going to characterize it yet because I think there was an artifact. Probably tape hiss — the Marantz picks up its own motor noise, I've noticed this before, there's a low whine that sometimes resolves into what sounds like tonal information when you reverse the playback direction. It's a known issue with these recorders. I'm going to re-record the segment to a fresh tape, clean up the transfer, and play it again.

I want to be accurate. That's all this is.

[Pause. Two seconds.]

I do want to note — just for my own reference — that during the vocalization, I was watching Eleanor's face. Everyone was watching Eleanor's face. But I was also watching her hands. She was gripping the arms of the witness chair — the court record reflects this — but what the record doesn't reflect, because it's not the kind of thing that makes it into a record, is that her fingers were moving. Not randomly. Not the way fingers move when someone is gripping something in distress. They were... sequential. Left to right. Patterned.

I'm noting this because I'm thorough. I notice things.

The tape hiss. I'll re-record tomorrow and check the result.

[Sound of recorder being switched off. Click.]

**OFFICIAL TRANSCRIPT — STATE OF OHIO v. ELEANOR MARIE VANCE**
**Case No. 97-CR-0441**
**Trial Day 4 — Direct Examination of Defendant (Continued)**
**October 15, 1997**
**Court Reporter: J. Hartley, CSR-4412**

---

THE COURT: On the record. Mr. Pruitt.

MR. PRUITT: Ms. Vance, yesterday afternoon we were discussing the markings found at the Godwin home. I'd like to move in a somewhat different direction this morning. You've been a licensed social worker in the state of Ohio since 1988, correct?

THE DEFENDANT: Correct.

MR. PRUITT: And prior to your assignment to the Godwin household, you managed a caseload of how many families?

THE DEFENDANT: It varied. Fifteen to twenty-two at any given time.

MR. PRUITT: Any complaints filed against you?

THE DEFENDANT: No.

MR. PRUITT: Any adverse findings?

THE DEFENDANT: No.

MR. PRUITT: Any families where children in your care were subsequently found to be in danger?

MR. ALDISS: Objection, Your Honor. Relevance. Ms. Vance's prior caseload isn't at issue.

MR. PRUITT: It goes to the defendant's professional competence, Your Honor, and specifically to whether she would have recognized signs of ritualistic abuse if present.

THE COURT: I'll allow it. You may answer.

THE DEFENDANT: No. No adverse findings. I was — I am — good at my work.

MR. PRUITT: You are good at your work.

THE DEFENDANT: Yes.

MR. PRUITT: Then I'm going to ask you to look at People's Exhibit 31. This is a page from your own case notes, dated May 8th, 1996. Third line from the bottom. Can you read that aloud for the record?

THE DEFENDANT: "Brianna reports difficulty sleeping. States she hears 'the humming' at night. Recommended Mrs. Godwin consult pediatrician re: possible ear infection."

MR. PRUITT: "The humming." Brianna told you she heard humming.

THE DEFENDANT: Children hear things. They have ear infections, they have nightmares, they have active imaginations. An eight-year-old reporting a humming sound is not evidence of ritual abuse. It is evidence of being eight.

MR. PRUITT: And the recommended follow-up — did Mrs. Godwin take Brianna to a pediatrician?

THE DEFENDANT: I don't — I'd have to check the next visit's notes.

MR. PRUITT: I've reviewed them. There's no follow-up notation.

THE DEFENDANT: Then I should have followed up. That's on me. But a missed follow-up on what sounded like an ear infection is not —

MR. PRUITT: People's Exhibit 32, Your Honor. Ms. Vance, this is from your notes dated June 5th, 1996. Your last visit before the investigation began. Can you read the highlighted passage?

THE DEFENDANT: [Reading.] "Tyler, during individual session, drew a picture when asked about home activities. Drew himself and Brianna lying down in a dark room. When asked what they were doing, stated" — [Defendant pauses.] — "stated, 'Listening.'"

MR. PRUITT: "Listening." To what?

THE DEFENDANT: I asked him. He said he didn't know what it was. He said it was under the floor.

MR. PRUITT: Under the floor. You didn't find that notable?

THE DEFENDANT: I found it notable enough to write it down. I wrote it down.

MR. PRUITT: But you didn't —

THE DEFENDANT: I wrote it down. I documented it. I was one person with twenty-two families and I wrote down what a six-year-old said about a sound under the floor and I was going to follow up and then the investigation started before my next visit and they took the children and they took the house and they took me and that's — that's all there was. There was no time left.

[Defendant's voice elevated but controlled. No vocalization event.]

MR. PRUITT: Let me ask you about the sound Tyler described. This sound under the floor. Ms. Vance, is the basement of the Godwin home directly beneath the children's bedroom?

THE DEFENDANT: I — yes. The bedrooms are above the east half of the basement.

MR. PRUITT: The east half. Exhibit 22 — the floor markings — were located in the east half of the basement, beneath the carpet. Beneath the children's bedroom.

THE DEFENDANT: I see what you're —

MR. PRUITT: I'm not suggesting anything, Ms. Vance. I'm reading your own notes and a floor plan. Tyler said he and Brianna listened to a sound under the floor. Brianna reported humming. The markings were under the carpet, under the floor of their bedroom. You documented all of this. You are, by your own account, good at your work. And in fifteen months, you never put these together?

[Defendant does not respond. Approximately twelve seconds of silence.]
