# The Commissar's Confession

## I.

The dossiers were arranged by tribunal date, not alphabetical, which was the new method since Prairial and which had caused some confusion in the filing office because the older clerks still organized by surname and the newer ones by district of arrest, so that any given bundle of warrants might follow three competing logics at once, and it fell to the inspector to reconcile them before endorsement, which he did now, standing at the left side of the long table in the committee chamber on the second floor of the building that had been, eighteen months ago, a residence belonging to a family whose name had been chiseled from the lintel with such haste that the ghost of the capital _D_ remained if you knew where to look, which the inspector did, because it was his business to know where to look.

Citizen Robespierre turned a page.

The pen moved. The list was not long. It was the standard form — printed, not handwritten, since the tribunal's output had exceeded the capacity of clerks in Germinal and the Committee had requisitioned the presses at Rue Honoré and Rue du Temple and, after the arrest of the printer Delacôte and the seizure of his workshop, the one on Rue Marat that had been Rue Saint-Jacques before the renaming, all three now running warrants and tribunal findings at a rate of eleven hundred sheets per day, which was an improvement of three hundred over the previous quarter and which the inspector had noted in his efficiency report of the ninth because it was his function to note such things.

"The tribunal has confirmed the sentences for the following."

Robespierre read the names without inflection, in the order in which they had been printed, which was the order of tribunal confirmation, which was not the order of arrest nor the order of denunciation but a third sequence that followed the internal calendar of the Revolutionary Tribunal itself, a calendar the inspector had learned to read the way a farmer reads weather — for what it predicted.

Gravier. Condemned for correspondence with émigré elements. The inspector had recruited Gravier in the spring of Year One, in a courtyard behind the Church of Saint-Sulpice that was now a Temple of Reason. Gravier had been a clerk at the Ministry of the Interior. His handwriting tilted eleven degrees to the left, a peculiarity the inspector had used to authenticate six intercepted letters and which now served as evidence in the prosecution file that the inspector had helped compile. Gravier's wife had been arrested separately. Her case was in the next morning's docket, pending.

Mornay. Condemned for harboring a non-juring priest. The inspector had warned Mornay in December, through the bookseller Aubert, that a denunciation had been lodged. Mornay had refused to move the priest. The priest had been found in a cellar on the fourteenth of Nivôse, behind a wall that the inspector could have described to the Committee before the search because he had been in that cellar twice.

Delacôte-Vraux, née Girard. The printer's widow. Condemned for production and distribution of seditious material. Category: conspiracy against the sovereignty of the people.

The inspector looked at the wall behind Robespierre's left shoulder, where a water stain in the plaster described, if you traced it from the crown molding downward, a shape not unlike the map of the Vendée, which was appropriate or inappropriate depending on whether one believed, as the inspector had been trained to believe and still, at certain hours of the day, did believe, that the revolution's geography was also its destiny — that the shape of the Republic on a map was the shape of the Republic in fact and that what fell outside the borders was what fell outside the future.

He thought: the widow's handwriting on the proofsheets was not tilted at all. It was upright and small and she used a violet ink she mixed herself from logwood and alum and iron sulfate, and the color was not the official blue-black of government correspondence but something closer to the color of a bruise three days old, and she dotted her lowercase _i_ with a stroke so fine it could have been a printer's error, and she had once, in the margin of a pamphlet about the price of bread in the Faubourg Saint-Antoine, corrected the word _farine_ to _farines_, plural, because, she said —

"The executions are scheduled for eleven hours. You will supervise."

The inspector said: "Citizen, it will be done."

Robespierre did not look up. The pen moved to the next document. The interview, which had not been an interview but an assignment, which had not been an assignment but a transmission — the information traveling from the Committee to its instrument the way a charge travels from a battery through a wire to the apparatus that converts it into function — was concluded. The inspector turned, opened the door, and entered the corridor.

The corridor was eleven meters long. He knew this because he had measured it on his third visit to the building, as he measured all spaces in which he operated: dimensions, exits, sight lines, the number of paces between any given point and the nearest staircase down which a body could move unobserved. The flagstones were limestone, each roughly forty centimeters square, which meant approximately twenty-seven and a half flagstones between the committee chamber door and the top of the staircase. He counted them. He always counted them. Tonight the count was the same — twenty-seven full flagstones and the half-stone at the stair's edge, cracked across its diagonal since at least October, which no one had repaired because the maintenance of government buildings fell under the jurisdiction of the Commission of Public Works, which had been reorganized in Ventôse and again in Floréal, and the crack remained.

Twenty-seven and a half.

Eleven names on the list.

One he had recruited.

One he had warned.

One whose handwriting was violet and upright and who dotted her lowercase _i_ with a stroke so fine it could have been a printer's error but was not.

Three others in the network who were still alive because of deportation papers he had forged on the nineteenth of Messidor using blanks stolen from the Commune offices during the confusion surrounding the Festival of the Supreme Being, papers that listed occupations and destinations that did not exist, signed by an official who was himself dead, countersigned by the inspector under a name that was not his name and never had been.

Three forged documents. Three people still drawing breath in cities they had been instructed to reach by routes the inspector had memorized and then burned.

He reached the staircase. He descended.

One step for each name. He did not allow this thought to complete itself. He counted the steps instead — fourteen to the landing, fourteen to the ground floor — and noted, as he always noted, that the banister had been replaced on the left side with an inferior wood since the original mahogany had been requisitioned for the manufacture of pike handles in the autumn, and that the grain of the replacement wood ran vertically rather than diagonally, which meant it would split under lateral pressure more quickly, a fact of no strategic importance that he recorded nonetheless because recording was what he did, what he had always done, what he would do tomorrow while the blade fell and the crowd performed its function and the body of the printer's widow dropped into the basket that was emptied, he had been told, every four executions into the cart that carried the remains to the cemetery at Errancis, where the gravediggers worked by the piece and were paid, per the treasury records he had also read because it was his business to read such things, nine livres per day.

He stepped into the street. The evening air carried the smell of the river. He turned east toward his lodging and did not run.

## II.

The Conciergerie occupied the angle of the Île de la Cité the way a jaw occupies a face — the lower structure, the working part, the mechanism by which the island consumed what was fed to it. He had written that image in a report once — not a report for the Committee but for the network, in the early months, when he still wrote with something like style, before the handler told him that style in intelligence correspondence was a signature and a signature was a death sentence. After that he wrote in the prescribed format: date, location, names, actions observed, assessment of risk, recommended next steps. No metaphors. No jaw.

The handler.

He did not plan to think of the handler. The thought arrived the way they all did now, not as memory but as construction — the mind reaching for its drafting tools, the compasses and straightedges it could not put down, and assembling from fragments a scene it had no authority to reconstruct because he had not been there, had not seen it, had only received the report through Aubert through the bookseller's cousin through a woman in Angers whose name he had never been told and who was, by now, likely also dead.

But the mind built what it built.

The Conciergerie window — third from the east corner, second floor, barred — was lit from within by a light the color of tallow, which was the color of government-issue candles, which were made from a rendered fat whose animal origin the regulations did not specify, and the light moved as someone inside moved past it, occluding and revealing, occluding and revealing, and the rhythm of it caught in the inspector's chest like a gear engaging a tooth it had not meant to engage, and what the gear turned was the mechanism of three years ago, the rented room above the tannery on the Rue des Fossés-Saint-Marcel.

The room had measured four meters by three. The ceiling was low enough that the handler, who was tall — taller than the inspector by a full head, though he carried his height apologetically, the way some tall men do, shoulders rounded as if ducking beneath a lintel the rest of the world could not see — the ceiling was low enough that the handler stood with his head slightly inclined whenever he was near the window, which was where he stood when he talked, always near the window, the light from the courtyard falling across the left side of his face so that the right side remained in the shadow of the room and the face was therefore always half-lit, divided, which the inspector had cataloged at the time as a security liability (a man seen always in profile from the street) and which he now understood had been, without the handler's intention, a figure for everything the handler believed: that the revolution had illuminated half of an idea and left the other half in darkness, and that the work of the true republican was to turn the face until the whole of it was lit.

He might not have said that. The inspector was no longer certain which of the handler's arguments had been spoken in the room above the tannery and which had been composed afterward, in the months and years since, by a mind that needed the arguments to have been made, needed the recruitment to have been not coercion or convenience but *conviction* — a word the handler had used or that the inspector believed he had used or that the inspector required to have been used because without it the three years of forged documents and double reports and the steady daily manufacture of a self that did not exist were not sacrifice but pathology.

The handler's name was Vignon. Or had been Vignon. The inspector had never verified it. There were eleven Vignons in the Paris municipal rolls as of the Year One census, and he had traced none of them because tracing them would have violated the elementary protocol the handler himself had established: you do not search for what you cannot afford to find.

In the room above the tannery the stink of lye rose through the floorboards with such persistence that it became, within twenty minutes, invisible — the nose's only mercy — and in that chemically scrubbed air the handler sat at the table with a half-loaf and tore it. Not roughly. Precisely. Each piece separated along a line the handler appeared to visualize before his fingers applied pressure, so that the bread came apart in approximate squares, each one a mouthful's worth, arranged on the table's surface in a grid the inspector now realized had been, in its accidental geometry, not unlike the grid of a typesetter's case — each square of bread a letter, each row a line, the whole arrangement a text the handler was composing and consuming simultaneously.

"The republic," the handler said, or had said, or might have said, "is not a destination. It is an ongoing act of maintenance. The moment you believe it is *built* you have already begun to let it decay."

Or: "Justice is not a word. It is an infrastructure. It requires inspection."

Or: "What you are being asked to do is not to spy. It is to measure the distance between what the revolution says and what the revolution does, and to keep that measurement accurate, because accuracy is the last form of loyalty available to us."

These sentences existed in the inspector's memory with the clarity of type on a well-inked page, but he knew — had known for months, since the night he woke at three in the morning and realized he could no longer distinguish the handler's voice from his own internal monologue — that clarity was not proof. Memory under pressure did not decay; it *improved*. It filled its own gaps. It typeset itself.

Two months ago the report came through: Vignon — if Vignon — had been arrested in Nantes during the repressions following the Vendée campaign. The representative-on-mission, Carrier, had conducted summary trials at a rate that exceeded even Paris. The bodies were disposed of in mass graves west of the city. The location was approximate. The number was approximate. The only precision was in the method: the condemned were stripped of identifying documents before execution so that no individual record would remain.

The inspector had not been to Nantes. He would not go to Nantes. There was no operational reason to go to Nantes, and in the current environment any travel required papers that generated records that were reviewed by committees that reported to committees that reported, ultimately, to the room on the second floor where Robespierre did not look up.

But the mind built what it built.

A pit. Dimensions estimated from the reported count — between eighty and one hundred twenty bodies, which, assuming an average volume of seventy liters per body and allowing for the irregular packing of limbs, indicated a pit of approximately nine cubic meters minimum, roughly three meters by three meters by one meter deep at the shallowest estimate, though the actual depth would depend on the water table in the alluvial deposits west of Nantes, which the inspector did not know and could not verify, so he assumed one and a half meters and adjusted the surface dimensions accordingly: three meters by two meters, which was the size of the room above the tannery, which was a coincidence that meant nothing and from which the mind could not turn.

The handler's body in the pit. The tall frame folded at the waist because the pit's depth was less than the body's length. The shoulders rounded as they had always been rounded, as though ducking beneath a lintel. The head inclined. The face no longer half-lit but wholly dark. The mouth, which had spoken or had not spoken the sentences the inspector carried, open — because the dead, the inspector had observed in the course of his duties, did not close their mouths; the jaw relaxed; gravity or decay performed its function; the mouth opened — and the mouth filling, slowly, with the soil that was shoveled or pushed or simply fell from the pit's edge as the gravediggers moved to the next section.

Soil entering the mouth that said *accuracy is the last form of loyalty*.

Soil entering the mouth that tore bread into meticulous squares.

The handler's mouth a cavity of precise and calculable volume — approximately two hundred milliliters at maximal opening — filling at a rate determined by the soil's grain size and the angle of entry, which was a calculation the inspector could perform and now, standing on the Quai de l'Horloge with the Conciergerie's tallow light pulsing against the evening sky, did perform, not because the number mattered but because the mind had lost the ability to be in the presence of a void without measuring it.

Two hundred milliliters of soil.

The volume of a cup.

The handler had drunk from a cup in the room above the tannery — a tin cup, dented on the rim, filled with water from the tannery's well, which tasted of iron and lime and which the handler drank without complaint because complaint was not, he said, a republican faculty.

The inspector walked on. The river moved beneath him, black and approximate. He did not count the bridges.

## III.
