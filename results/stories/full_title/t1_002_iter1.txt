# THE SUTTON COUNTY TRANSCRIPTS

## A Compilation of Selected Documents Pertaining to the Matter of *The State of Texas v. Eleanor Ruth Vance*, 43rd Judicial District Court, Sutton County, Texas

---

**COMPILER'S NOTE**

The following materials have been assembled from my professional and personal files for the purpose of creating a complete and accessible record. I have endeavored to present them in chronological order. Where the sequence of documents might cause confusion, I have provided brief contextual notes, clearly marked and set apart from the primary materials.

I am a Certified Shorthand Reporter in the State of Texas, license number 04-7831, and I served as the official court reporter for the 43rd Judicial District Court, Sutton County, from September 1987 through the conclusion of the proceedings documented here. My duties included the stenographic recording and transcription of all courtroom proceedings, the maintenance of the official record, and the custody of exhibits during trial sessions.

This compilation includes excerpts from the official trial transcript, my professional annotations to that transcript, selected documents from county personnel and administrative files, and recordings from personal audio tapes I made during the trial period. I have included the personal recordings because they contain observations relevant to the record that could not, by their nature, be entered into the official transcript. I recognize that their inclusion is unorthodox. I ask only that the full body of materials be considered together, and in order, before any judgment is made regarding their relevance or my credibility.

The documents speak clearly enough. I have tried to let them.

Margaret Ann Halloway, CSR
Sonora, Texas

THE COURT: We are on the record in Cause Number 88-04-0347-CR, the State of Texas versus Eleanor Ruth Vance. Let the record reflect that the jury has been seated and sworn. Mr. Pruitt, you may proceed with your opening statement.

MR. PRUITT: Thank you, Your Honor.

Ladies and gentlemen of the jury, good morning. What you are going to hear over the next several days is not complicated, though it is deeply disturbing. You are going to hear the story of three children — Cody Wayne Driscoll, age four; his sister, Bethany Anne Driscoll, age six; and Michael James Roark, age three — who were placed in the care of the defendant, Eleanor Vance, through the foster system administered by the Texas Department of Human Services. You are going to hear that all three of these children died, in the same home, over a period of fourteen months, and that their deaths were attributed, in each case, to sudden and unexplained medical events.

You are going to hear from medical experts who will testify that the pattern of these deaths is inconsistent with natural causes and consistent with the deliberate and systematic administration of a toxic substance. You are going to hear from investigators with the Sutton County Sheriff's Department who will describe the items recovered from the defendant's residence — items including journals, diagrams, and texts relating to occult rituals, specifically rituals involving the sacrifice of children. You are going to hear from a child psychologist who evaluated a fourth child formerly in the defendant's care, a child who will not be named in these proceedings for her protection, a child who described to that psychologist, in terms no child should possess, ceremonies conducted in the defendant's home in the hours after dark.

You will hear that Eleanor Vance was employed as a caseworker by the Department of Human Services for eleven years. You will hear that throughout that employment, she had access to vulnerable children and occupied a position of trust that was, by its very nature, difficult to supervise or audit. And you will hear that she used that position not to protect children, but to select them.

This is not a case that requires you to believe in the occult. It is not a case about religion or superstition. It is a case about three dead children and the woman who was responsible for their safety. The State will prove, beyond a reasonable doubt, that Eleanor Ruth Vance caused their deaths. We ask that you listen to the evidence, and we are confident that when you have, you will have no difficulty reaching the just verdict.

Thank you.

THE COURT: Mr. Landis.

MR. LANDIS: Thank you, Your Honor.

[Transcript annotation — M.A.H.: Defense opening statement, 9:22 a.m. to 9:41 a.m. Full text follows in official record. Summary for purposes of this compilation: Mr. Landis stipulated to the deaths of the three children and to the presence of occult-related materials in the defendant's home. Defense theory presented as follows: deaths were attributable to an undiagnosed environmental contaminant (specifically, elevated carbon monoxide levels from a defective heating unit in the Vance residence, since replaced by the property owner); occult materials were related to the defendant's personal research into ritual abuse claims she encountered in her professional capacity as a social worker, not evidence of her own practice or belief; the prosecution's case was a product of community hysteria following the widely publicized ritual abuse investigations in Jordan, Minnesota, and Kern County, California, and the defendant was being scapegoated for deaths that were, in fact, accidental.]

THE COURT: Thank you, Mr. Landis. Mr. Pruitt, call your first witness.

MR. PRUITT: The State calls Dr. Allen Briggs.

[Transcript annotation — M.A.H.: Dr. Briggs is the Sutton County Medical Examiner. Direct examination by Mr. Pruitt covers autopsy findings for Cody Wayne Driscoll (DOD 3/12/87), Bethany Anne Driscoll (DOD 9/03/87), and Michael James Roark (DOD 5/19/88). Cause of death in each case originally certified as "undetermined" pending toxicology. Toxicology results for Driscoll children inconclusive due to tissue decomposition at time of exhumation — bodies were initially buried without autopsy per original determination of natural cause. Toxicology for Roark: elevated concentrations of ethylene glycol consistent with antifreeze ingestion, but Dr. Briggs concedes under later cross-examination that a single positive finding is "suggestive but not conclusive" absent corroborating evidence. This concession is not highlighted in the prosecution's examination.]

**EXCERPT — VOLUME I, DAY 1, p. 63**

MR. PRUITT: Dr. Briggs, in your professional opinion, is the pattern of three deaths in the same household over a fourteen-month period consistent with natural causes?

DR. BRIGGS: No, sir. In my professional opinion, the statistical likelihood of three unrelated sudden deaths in children of this age, in the same household, from unconnected natural causes, is extremely low.

MR. PRUITT: Can you quantify that for the jury?

DR. BRIGGS: Well, each event individually — a sudden unexplained death in a child under five — occurs at a rate of roughly one to two per thousand. For three such events to occur in the same household, unrelated to each other, you would be looking at a probability in the range of one in several hundred million. It is, for practical purposes, a medical impossibility that all three deaths were accidental and unconnected.

MR. LANDIS: Objection, Your Honor. "Medical impossibility" is an overstatement of the witness's own statistical framework, and the characterization is prejudicial.

THE COURT: Sustained. The jury will disregard the phrase "medical impossibility." Dr. Briggs, please confine your characterizations to what the statistics support.

DR. BRIGGS: Yes, Your Honor. I would say it is medically and statistically extraordinary, and that in my professional experience, a cluster of this nature warrants investigation of external causes.

MR. PRUITT: Thank you, Doctor. No further questions.

---

[Transcript annotation — M.A.H.: Cross-examination of Dr. Briggs by Mr. Landis, pp. 64–78. Mr. Landis establishes that Dr. Briggs has no specialized training in pediatric forensic pathology and that his statistical testimony regarding the probability of clustered deaths relies on a general population database rather than a database specific to foster care environments, where children may arrive with pre-existing medical vulnerabilities. Mr. Landis also elicits the concession regarding the ethylene glycol finding noted above. Effective cross. Jury appeared attentive. One juror — no. 4, second row, woman in blue cardigan — was taking notes.]

**EXCERPT — VOLUME II, DAY 2, pp. 31–33**

MR. PRUITT: Dr. Cahill, you evaluated the child we are referring to in these proceedings as "Jane Doe," is that correct?

DR. CAHILL: That is correct.

MR. PRUITT: And how many sessions did you conduct with Jane Doe?

DR. CAHILL: Six sessions over a period of approximately five weeks, between June and July of this year.

MR. PRUITT: And during those sessions, did Jane Doe describe events that occurred in the home of the defendant?

DR. CAHILL: She did.

MR. PRUITT: Can you summarize those descriptions for the jury?

DR. CAHILL: Jane Doe described — and I want to be careful to use her language as much as possible — she described times when Ms. Vance would "do the special nighttime" with her and the other children. She said this involved going into the basement of the house after the other children — meaning the Driscoll children and the Roark child — were "put to sleep," which she used to mean put to bed, and that Ms. Vance would light candles and speak in words Jane Doe did not understand. She described Ms. Vance drawing circles on the floor. She described —

THE DEFENDANT: No.

THE COURT: Ms. Vance —

THE DEFENDANT: She didn't say that. That is not what the child —

MR. LANDIS: Eleanor, please —

THE DEFENDANT: You are building this from NOTHING. You are taking a child's —

THE COURT: Ms. Vance, you will be silent or I will have you removed from this courtroom.

THE DEFENDANT: *Av eht gnidrocer. Av eht gnidrocer. Ssel-ev eht si gninetl ehs. Ssel-ev.*

[Transcript annotation — M.A.H.: Phonetic rendering of defendant's utterance. Approximately 4–5 seconds in duration. Delivered in a continuous, level tone markedly different from the agitated speech immediately preceding it. Judge Mooring called for order. Defendant was silent after this utterance. Defense counsel conferred with defendant briefly at the table.]

THE COURT: Mr. Landis, control your client.

MR. LANDIS: I apologize to the Court, Your Honor. It will not happen again.

THE COURT: If it does, I will clear the defendant from this courtroom and we will proceed in her absence. Dr. Cahill, please continue.

DR. CAHILL: The child described Ms. Vance drawing circles on the floor and placing objects inside the circles. She described some of these objects as "the animals that were sleeping," though she could not or would not elaborate on what that meant. She described Ms. Vance speaking to someone who was not in the room, addressing this person or — entity — in the same words she did not understand. And she described a feeling of being very cold, specifically in her hands and feet, during these ceremonies, even though she said the candles made the room warm.

MR. PRUITT: Did Jane Doe describe these experiences spontaneously, or only in response to specific questioning?

DR. CAHILL: The initial disclosure was spontaneous. During our second session, she was doing a free-drawing exercise and she drew a circle with smaller circles inside it, and when I asked her about the drawing, she began to describe the "special nighttime." Once the disclosure was initiated, I used open-ended, non-leading prompts to elicit further detail, consistent with established forensic interview protocols for children.

[Transcript annotation — M.A.H.: Phonetic string on p. 32 repeated almost identically from prior outburst by defendant on Day 1, p. 29, l. 8 (not included in this compilation — see full certified transcript, Vol. I). Consistency of phonetic content across two separate outbursts is unusual for disorganized or psychotic speech, which typically varies significantly between episodes. Noted for the record.]

**EXCERPT — VOLUME II, DAY 2, pp. 34–36**

MR. PRUITT: Dr. Cahill, in your professional opinion, were Jane Doe's disclosures consistent with the experience of actual events, or with fantasy, coaching, or confabulation?

DR. CAHILL: In my professional opinion, several characteristics of the disclosure are consistent with genuine experiential memory. The sensory detail — the cold in the hands and feet, the warmth of the candles — is the type of peripheral sensory information that is more typically associated with genuine memory than with coached or confabulated accounts. The child's affect during the disclosure was constricted, which is consistent with a traumatic memory. And the disclosure emerged through a natural associative pathway — the drawing — rather than in a rehearsed or coached-appearing narrative.

MR. PRUITT: And the elements that seem — to use a layperson's term — fantastical? The circles, the speaking to an unseen presence, the "animals that were sleeping"?

DR. CAHILL: Children interpret events through the cognitive frameworks available to them. A child of Jane Doe's age does not have the conceptual vocabulary to describe ritualistic practices in adult terms. She describes what she saw and felt. The interpretation of those descriptions is properly a matter for the investigators and for this Court, not for me as a clinician. What I can say is that the *form* of the disclosure — its consistency, its sensory grounding, its affective signature — is consistent with genuine memory, regardless of how the *content* is ultimately interpreted.

MR. LANDIS: Objection. Dr. Cahill is essentially testifying that the child's account is true while disclaiming that she is doing so.

THE COURT: Overruled. The witness has clearly stated the limits of her opinion. The jury will evaluate the testimony as they see fit. Continue, Mr. Pruitt.
