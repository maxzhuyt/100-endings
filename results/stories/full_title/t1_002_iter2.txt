**JURY SELECTION AND PRELIMINARY PROCEEDINGS**

[PROCEEDINGS COMMENCED AT 9:07 A.M.]

THE COURT: Good morning. We are on the record in Cause Number 87-CF-00441, State of Indiana versus Eleanor Ruth Vance. This matter is set for jury trial. Are both parties present and ready to proceed?

MR. LASSETER: State is ready, Your Honor.

MR. KESSLER: Defense is ready, Your Honor.

THE COURT: Very well. We have a panel of thirty-eight prospective jurors assembled. Before we begin voir dire, I want to address both counsel regarding the nature of the charges. The defendant is charged under Indiana Code 35-42-1-3 with five counts of battery resulting in serious bodily injury of a minor, one count of neglect of a dependent under 35-46-1-4, and one count of criminal confinement under 35-42-3-3. The State has indicated its intention to introduce testimony and evidence pertaining to what it characterizes as ritualistic practices. I remind both counsel that the scope of inquiry must remain within the evidentiary framework I outlined in my order on pretrial motions, filed March 12th of this year. We are not here to litigate theology. We are here to determine whether the defendant committed specific criminal acts against specific victims. Is that understood?

MR. LASSETER: Understood, Your Honor.

MR. KESSLER: Understood.

THE COURT: Let's bring in the panel.

[JURY PANEL ENTERS COURTROOM AT 9:14 A.M.]

THE COURT: Good morning, ladies and gentlemen. Thank you for your service. My name is Judge Walter Moynihan, and I'll be presiding over this trial. You've been called here as prospective jurors in a criminal matter. Before I turn things over to the attorneys, I want to say a few things about what your role is and what it is not. You are here to listen to evidence. You are here to apply the law as I instruct you. You are not here to bring your outside reading, your television viewing, or your personal beliefs about matters that may be discussed in the media into this courtroom. The facts of this case are what will be presented from that witness stand and admitted into evidence. Nothing else. Is there anyone on this panel who feels they cannot commit to that standard?

[NO RESPONSE]

THE COURT: Good. Mr. Lasseter, you may begin voir dire.

MR. LASSETER: Thank you, Your Honor. Good morning, members of the panel. I'd like to start with a simple question, and I'd ask you to be honest even if your answer feels uncomfortable. Has anyone in this room heard of the Eleanor Vance case prior to today?

[APPROXIMATELY FOURTEEN JURORS RAISE HANDS]

MR. LASSETER: All right. Let me be more specific. Juror number four — Mr. Garland, is it?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR GARLAND: That's right.

MR. LASSETER: Mr. Garland, what have you heard?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR GARLAND: Just what was on the news. That there was — I mean, what they said about the children, and the — the rituals, and — I guess what they're calling it now, I don't know the official term.

MR. LASSETER: Did what you heard cause you to form an opinion about the defendant's guilt or innocence?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR GARLAND: I don't think so. I mean, you hear things, but I know that's not the same as proof.

MR. LASSETER: That's exactly right. Thank you. Juror number eleven — Ms. Whelan?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR WHELAN: I saw the Geraldo special. About satanic cults. Not about this case specifically, but the general — phenomenon, I suppose.

MR. LASSETER: And did that program lead you to any conclusions about what may or may not have occurred in this case?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR WHELAN: No. I thought it was sensationalized, honestly.

MR. LASSETER: Do you believe it's possible for a person to commit acts of physical abuse against a child while employing ritualistic elements — symbols, chanting, what have you — that derive from occult or satanic belief systems?

MR. KESSLER: Objection, Your Honor. Counsel is asking the juror to prejudge the evidence.

THE COURT: Overruled. The question goes to the juror's capacity to consider the charges. You may answer, Ms. Whelan.

PROSPECTIVE JUROR WHELAN: I suppose anything is possible. But I would need to see the evidence.

MR. LASSETER: That's all the State would ever ask.

**TRIAL TRANSCRIPT — VOLUME II**
**OPENING STATEMENTS**

**DATE:** April 7, 1987

---

[PROCEEDINGS COMMENCED AT 9:03 A.M.]

THE COURT: Good morning. We are back on the record in State versus Vance. Both parties are present, the jury is seated. Mr. Lasseter, the State may make its opening statement.

MR. LASSETER: Thank you, Your Honor.

[COUNSEL APPROACHES JURY BOX]

MR. LASSETER: Ladies and gentlemen. Over the next several days, you're going to hear testimony that will be difficult to listen to. I want to prepare you for that. This case involves five children, ages four through seven, who were enrolled in the Shepherd's Gate Christian Day Care center in Denton, Indiana, between September 1985 and June 1986. The defendant, Eleanor Vance, was employed at Shepherd's Gate as a caretaker during that period. The evidence will show that Ms. Vance, acting alone and during periods when she had sole supervision of these children, committed repeated acts of physical abuse, including striking, burning, and confining these children in an unlit basement storage area for periods of up to three hours.

The evidence will further show that these acts of abuse were not random. They were not the product of frustration or poor temperament, which would be terrible enough. These acts were organized around a set of ritualistic practices. The children will describe — through their own testimony, through forensic interview recordings, and through the testimony of the therapists who have worked with them — a pattern of behavior that included the drawing of symbols, the recitation of phrases, and the use of specific objects in ways consistent with what experts on ritualistic abuse will describe for you as occult ceremonial practice.

I know what some of you may be thinking. You may have seen news reports about cases like this across the country. You may have heard people use the phrase "satanic panic." You may be wondering whether this is one of those cases — one of those hysterias, those moral panics, where a community convinces itself of things that never happened. I want to address that directly. This is not that. The State's evidence in this case is physical, medical, and testimonial. Doctors will tell you about burns. X-rays will show you fractures. Teachers will describe behavioral changes that correspond precisely to the periods of the defendant's access. This case is not about panic. It is not about satanism as a cultural bogeyman. This case is about what one woman did to five children in a basement, and the evidence will prove it beyond a reasonable doubt.

THE COURT: Thank you, Mr. Lasseter. Mr. Kessler?

MR. KESSLER: Thank you, Your Honor.

[COUNSEL APPROACHES JURY BOX]

MR. KESSLER: Members of the jury. Mr. Lasseter wants you to believe this case is simple. A woman, a basement, five children. He says this is not about panic. I suggest you keep that phrase in mind throughout this trial and judge for yourselves.

Eleanor Vance is thirty-one years old. She has no criminal record. She has no history of violence. She worked at Shepherd's Gate for fourteen months, during which time she received two positive performance evaluations and a written commendation from the center's director. The allegations against her emerged in November of 1986, five months after she left Shepherd's Gate to care for her ailing mother. They emerged after one child — one — made a statement during a therapy session that was then communicated to the parents of other children, who then arranged for their own children to be interviewed by a therapist whose methods the defense will demonstrate are leading, suggestive, and profoundly unreliable.

The defense will show that the physical evidence Mr. Lasseter describes is real but has been misattributed. Children fall down. Children burn themselves on stoves. The question is not whether these injuries exist, but who caused them and when. The defense will show that the so-called ritualistic elements — the symbols, the chanting, the objects — entered these children's accounts only after repeated suggestive interview sessions and are consistent with a pattern documented in dozens of cases nationwide in which investigators, intentionally or not, plant the very narrative they claim to be discovering.

Eleanor Vance did not hurt these children. What you're looking at is the anatomy of an accusation — how a community's fear can become a single woman's criminal charge. I ask you to watch carefully, listen carefully, and hold Mr. Lasseter to his burden. Thank you.

THE COURT: Thank you, Mr. Kessler. Mr. Lasseter, call your first witness.

MR. LASSETER: The State calls Dr. Richard Pemberton.

**TRIAL TRANSCRIPT — VOLUME III**
**TESTIMONY — DAY TWO**

**DATE:** April 8, 1987

---

[PROCEEDINGS COMMENCED AT 9:11 A.M.]

THE COURT: We're back on the record. Mr. Lasseter.

MR. LASSETER: The State calls Donna Harbaugh.

[WITNESS SWORN AND SEATED]

MR. LASSETER: Ms. Harbaugh, you are the mother of Kyle Harbaugh, one of the children identified in the indictment?

MS. HARBAUGH: Yes.

MR. LASSETER: How old is Kyle now?

MS. HARBAUGH: He just turned six.

MR. LASSETER: When did you first notice behavioral changes in your son?

MS. HARBAUGH: Spring of last year. 1986. March, maybe April. He started having nightmares. Not normal little-kid nightmares. He would wake up screaming and he wouldn't let us touch him. He would —

[WITNESS BECOMES EMOTIONAL — BRIEF PAUSE]

MS. HARBAUGH: He would push himself into the corner of his bed, against the wall, and put his hands over his face and just shake. Like he was trying to make himself smaller.

MR. LASSETER: Did Kyle ever tell you what the nightmares were about?

MS. HARBAUGH: Not at first. He wouldn't talk about it. But then in — I think it was June, after he was already out of Shepherd's Gate, he drew a picture at home. It was — you could tell it was a person standing over smaller figures, the smaller ones were lying down, and there were marks on the paper, like —

MR. KESSLER: Objection. The witness is characterizing a child's drawing without foundation and without the drawing in evidence.

MR. LASSETER: Your Honor, the drawing has been marked as State's Exhibit 14. I intended to introduce it through this witness.

THE COURT: You may introduce it now. Lay your foundation.

MR. LASSETER: Ms. Harbaugh, I'm showing you what's been marked as State's Exhibit 14. Do you recognize it?

MS. HARBAUGH: Yes. That's Kyle's drawing. I kept it.

MR. LASSETER: Move to admit State's Exhibit 14.

MR. KESSLER: Objection. A child's drawing is not evidence of a criminal act.

THE COURT: It's admissible as a statement of the child declarant under the residual hearsay exception, subject to the weight the jury cares to give it. Exhibit 14 is admitted.

MR. LASSETER: Ms. Harbaugh, you were describing the drawing. Please continue.

MS. HARBAUGH: There's a tall figure and smaller figures. And around them there are — shapes. Kyle later told his therapist they were the "special marks" the "dark lady" would draw on the floor before —

MR. KESSLER: Objection. Hearsay.

THE COURT: Sustained as to what the child told the therapist. Ms. Harbaugh, confine your testimony to what you directly observed or what Kyle said directly to you.

MS. HARBAUGH: He told *me* too. He told me the lady drew things on the floor. He said she talked in a voice that wasn't hers.

MR. LASSETER: Did Kyle identify the lady?

MS. HARBAUGH: He said it was Miss Eleanor.
