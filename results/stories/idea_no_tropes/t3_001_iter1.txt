## With Annotations

**DOCUMENT: Certificate of Dissolution, Howard County Voluntary Association No. 14 (Elk Falls Volunteer Fire Department). Filed January 9, 1960. Standard Form HC-12.**

**Reason for Dissolution (check one):**

☐ Insufficient membership
☐ Failure to meet annual reporting requirements
☐ Merger with another association
☒ Irreconcilable procedural differences

---

*Annotation, hand of D. Yarnell:*

The above is technically accurate in the way that describing the sinking of the *Titanic* as a "navigational procedural difference" would be technically accurate. Nobody has asked me for the actual cause, because everybody in Elk Falls already knows the actual cause, but I am writing it here in the margin because I have learned in eleven years as county clerk that memory is unreliable and margins are not.

The actual cause was Nadine Potoczak's tuna-noodle casserole.

Specifically: on October 3, 1959, at the Elk Falls Volunteer Fire Department Annual Fundraiser and Dish-to-Pass Supper, held as always in the auxiliary room of the Grange hall on Tecumseh Street, Nadine Potoczak entered her tuna-noodle casserole in the informal competition that has existed among the wives since approximately 1946. The casserole used cream-of-mushroom soup as a base, egg noodles (Mueller's, wide cut), canned tuna (Chicken of the Sea, two cans), and a topping of crushed Ritz crackers and melted butter. I know the recipe because Nadine submitted it to the *Elk Falls Courant* in 1957 and I have the clipping.

The informal ranking, which was performed by the men after the meal and which the wives tolerated in the way that wives in Elk Falls tolerate most things—with a silence that is not the same as agreement—placed Nadine's casserole sixth out of six entries. Last place. Below Carol Jessup's scalloped potatoes, below Irma Ott's baked beans, below Francine Halverson's deviled eggs, and below two other dishes I will not name because it is not relevant and also because one of them was mine.

Gerald Potoczak, who is Nadine's husband and who had at that point served as chief of the volunteer fire department for four years, took the ranking as a personal affront. He said so publicly. He said that the ranking reflected not an honest assessment of the casserole but a coordinated effort by Dale Jessup and his "faction" to undermine Gerald's authority as chief. Dale Jessup said that was horseshit. Gerald said it was not horseshit. Dale said a man who can't tell horseshit from tuna noodle has no business running a fire department. Gerald said a man who lets his wife put that much paprika on a scalloped potato has no business judging anything at all.

The alliances formed within the hour:

- With Gerald: Lloyd Ott, Harvey Breen
- With Dale: Vernon Speake, Mick Halverson
- Neutral (briefly): Russell Pym

Russell Pym sided with Dale by Thanksgiving, not because he agreed with Dale about the casserole but because Gerald had, in a separate but temporally adjacent dispute, told Russell that his method of splicing a fire hose was "amateurish," and Russell Pym has been splicing hoses since the Army and does not accept criticism on the subject.

By December, six men who had saved each other's barns, who had stood together in a relay line passing buckets when the Halverson grain dryer caught in '54, who had waded into the creek to pull Dale's truck out when the bridge washed and who had held a tarp over Gerald Potoczak's wife while she delivered their third child on the shoulder of Route 99 because the car wouldn't start and the snow was coming—these six men stopped speaking to each other.

I filed the dissolution on January 9, 1960. Gerald signed it. Dale signed it. The others signed it. Nobody looked at anybody else.

That was seven years ago. I note it here because the form asks for the reason and the form should have the reason, even if the form's checkbox is too small to hold it.

**DOCUMENT: USDA Form 674-B, Application for Crop Loss Adjustment, Howard County, Kansas. Filed September 22, 1967. Applicant: Gerald R. Potoczak, 1120 County Road 7, Elk Falls, KS.**

**Section III: Nature of Damage (describe fully; use additional pages if necessary)**

~~Hail~~
~~Not hail. Unknown~~
~~Something landed~~
~~There is a~~

Something is sitting in my north forty and it is *humming*.

---

*Annotation, hand of D. Yarnell:*

Gerald came in to file this himself, which he never does; he usually mails them. He was wearing clean overalls, which I note because Gerald does not generally come to town in clean overalls on a Tuesday. He stood at the counter and filled out the form in pencil, and I could see him writing and crossing out from where I sat, which is eight feet away but the office is quiet and I have good eyes. When he finished he folded it once, slid it across the counter, and said, "I don't know what to put."

I said, "You put whatever happened."

He said, "I put what happened."

I unfolded it and read it and I looked at Gerald and Gerald looked at the floor.

"It's humming," he said.

"What is humming, Gerald?"

"The thing."

"What thing."

"The thing in my north forty, Dottie. The thing that is sitting in my north forty and humming. I don't have a better word. If I had a better word I would have used the better word instead of crossing out four times like a person who doesn't have a better word."

I asked Gerald if he had called anyone. He said he had called the sheriff's office and they told him to call the county extension agent and the county extension agent told him to call the USDA office in Wichita and the USDA office in Wichita told him to fill out a 674-B and that is why he was standing in my office in clean overalls on a Tuesday.

I filed it. I file everything. That is what I do. If the Second Coming occurs within the unincorporated limits of Howard County, Kansas, I expect someone will bring me a form for it, and I will check the form for completeness, and I will date-stamp it, and I will put it in the appropriate drawer. The Lord can return at His convenience but He will need to provide a mailing address for correspondence purposes.

I note for the record that Gerald did not use the word "UFO." Gerald is not a man who uses words like that. Gerald is a man who grows soybeans and fixes his own equipment and files his crop-loss adjustments on time, and if he says something is in his field and it is humming, then something is in his field and it is humming. I have known Gerald Potoczak for twenty-three years and he has never filed a fraudulent claim.

Filed September 22, 1967. Drawer 7, Folder: Potoczak, G. Cross-referenced to Active Anomalies (new category, created today, because I did not previously have need of one).

**DOCUMENT: Letter to the Editor, *Elk Falls Courant*. Submitted September 29, 1967. Author: Gerald R. Potoczak. Published October 4, 1967, page 6, below the Farm Bureau meeting minutes.**

Dear Editor,

I am writing to request that the county or other appropriate authority send someone to inspect an anomalous agricultural obstruction on my property at 1120 County Road 7. The obstruction appeared on or about September 19 and has not moved. It is roughly the size of a grain truck, though not the same shape as a grain truck, or any other shape I am able to compare it to with confidence. It is situated in the north forty, approximately 300 yards from the tree line, in what was my best soybean acreage.

I have called the relevant offices and have been directed to file the relevant forms, which I have done. Nobody has come to look. I am writing to the *Courant* because I believe the community has a right to know about obstructions of this nature, and because someone from the county should come look at this before it becomes a bigger problem than it already is.

I am not calling it anything other than what it is, which is an obstruction. I would appreciate it if others would extend the same courtesy.

Respectfully,
Gerald R. Potoczak

---

*Annotation, hand of D. Yarnell:*

I have the clipping. Gerald's letter ran below a notice about the Farm Bureau's October sorghum-pricing meeting and above an advertisement for Dale Jessup's auto-body shop, which I imagine Gerald was not pleased about, though he did not say so.

I note the letter is careful. It is the letter of a man who knows how he will sound and is working very hard not to sound that way. Gerald graduated from Elk Falls High School in 1938, third in his class, and when he fills out forms his handwriting is legible and his spelling is correct, which is more than I can say for most of the men who come through this office. He chose "anomalous agricultural obstruction." Not "thing." Not what it is. He is managing his reputation, and he is doing it well, and it will not work because the thing in his field does not care about Gerald Potoczak's reputation.

**DOCUMENT: USDA Form 674-B (Supplemental), Filed October 6, 1967. Applicant: Gerald R. Potoczak.**

**Section III (continued): Updated Description of Damage**

Previous estimate of affected acreage was 5 acres. Revised estimate is 12 acres. The soybeans within approximately 200 yards of the obstruction are not dead. I want to be clear about that. They are growing faster than soybeans grow. I don't know how else to say it. I planted Harosoy 63 and they should be at R8 by now and ready for combining but they are still putting on new growth like it is the middle of July. The pods are the size of my thumb and they are the wrong color. They are producing light.

I am not going to cross things out this time, I am just going to write it: the soybeans are glowing. A pale blue. At night you can see the glow from my kitchen window. Nadine will not go in the kitchen after dark and I am not going to argue with her about it.

Revised crop loss estimate: I do not know how to calculate a loss when the crop is technically *more* than it was. Please advise.

*Annotation, hand of D. Yarnell, entered directly on the Blue Book form and returned:*

To whom it may concern:

I can confirm the presence of something on the Potoczak property. I have not personally visited the site but I have processed three USDA forms from the landowner and I have no reason to doubt his account. I cannot check "nocturnal light" or "daylight disc" because the object, to my understanding, is not aerial. It has not moved since it arrived. It is sitting in a soybean field. It is humming. I don't know what box to check for that, so I am checking "Other."

For Field 3, "Duration of Sighting": ongoing. It is still there. It has been there for three weeks. It does not appear to be going anywhere.

For Field 4, "Number of Witnesses": I am uncertain because nobody in Elk Falls is rushing to admit they've been out to look at it, but the humming is audible from George Fenton's Conoco station on Route 99, which is 1.7 miles from the Potoczak property, so functionally the number of witnesses is "everyone who has bought gasoline in the last week."

I will also note, since the form does not ask but should, that the soybeans in a 200-yard radius of the object are now growing at approximately nine times the normal rate and are producing visible light in the blue-white spectrum. This is not covered by existing USDA crop-loss categories. It is also not covered by your form. I am returning your form with my annotations because I do not have the authority to create Air Force categories, though if you would like to send me the appropriate template I would be happy to file a more complete report.

D. Yarnell
Howard County Clerk
Filed and copied. Drawer 7, Active Anomalies.

**DOCUMENT: Letter to the Editor, *Elk Falls Courant*. Submitted October 12, 1967. Author: Gerald R. Potoczak. Published October 18, page 4.**

Dear Editor,

I wrote to this paper two weeks ago about the situation on my property and since that time I have received no response from any authority at any level. In the meantime the following has occurred:

1. The affected area has expanded to approximately 20 acres.
2. The soybeans are now over seven feet tall and I can no longer walk among them safely because the stalks have thickened to the point where they will not bend.
3. The humming has increased in volume. My neighbor Vernon Speake called me on the telephone to complain about the noise, which I find Rich coming from a man who has not spoken to me in seven years over a matter I will not rehash here but which he knows very well.
4. The object itself has changed color twice.

I am a taxpaying citizen of Howard County. I vote in every election. I have never missed a filing deadline. I am asking, not for the first time and with DECREASING patience, for someone in a position of authority to come out to County Road 7 and LOOK AT THE THING IN MY FIELD.

Gerald R. Potoczak

---

*Annotation, hand of D. Yarnell:*

Note the capitalization. Gerald is not a man who capitalizes for emphasis. Gerald is a man who underlines, when he must, with a single clean stroke of a ruler. The capitals represent a departure from Gerald's established epistolary practice and I am entering it into the record as such.

I also note that Gerald mentioned Vernon Speake's phone call. This is the first direct communication between any two members of the former fire department since the dissolution, if you don't count the time Lloyd Ott nodded at Gerald in the hardware store in 1964, which I don't, because Lloyd nods at everybody. It is about a noise complaint. After seven years of silence, the first words Vernon Speake said to Gerald Potoczak were, I am told by Gerald himself, "Your field is keeping me up at night, you son of a bitch."

I don't have a form for that either but I note it here. Some things belong in the record even if there is no drawer for them.
