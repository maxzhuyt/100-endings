# The Moth, the Star, the Glass on the Counter

---

## I.

The radiator ticks. It ticks and ticks and then it does not tick and then it ticks. This is known. This is the first sound of the apartment after the outside sounds, which are not the apartment's sounds and therefore are only monitored, not trusted. A bus. Voices that rise. A dog— but the dog is far down and behind glass so the dog is only a sound-shape, not a dog.

The apartment is: the bed, which is for nighttime and also for the square of sun that moves across it between the time the keys come home and the time the keys leave again. The kitchen floor, which is cold and has the bowl. The bowl is white and has a chip on the side where the chip has always been. Next to the bowl is the water. The water is changed every morning after the sound of feet but before the sound of bag-lifting. This is known. The window ledge, which is wide enough for sitting and where the pigeons land. The pigeons are monitored. They do not know they are monitored. They walk with their heads moving forward and back, forward and back, and this is incorrect walking but they do not know this either. When a pigeon lands, the body goes still. The eyes go wide. The tail goes low. The teeth click behind closed mouth. The pigeon does not know any of this is happening. The pigeon leaves. The body releases. This is done many times.

The owner comes home the same way every time. First: the sound in the hall, which is keys but also feet, and the feet are known before the keys because the feet have a sound that is only those feet. The weight is particular. The speed is particular. Left foot louder than right foot, always, because the left shoe is older or the left leg is heavier or the left is simply louder, and it does not matter why because why is not a thing that needs knowing. The sound is the thing. The sound means: soon.

Then keys. Then the door moves. Then the smell comes in all at once: outside-smell and cold-smell and the blue fabric smell which is the uniform smell which is the particular smell of the owner when the owner is coming-home and not yet sleeping or sitting or eating. The bag drops. The bag always drops by the door, to the left of the door, and the bag smells of paper and other-people's-hands and sometimes rain.

Then: "Hey, buddy."

This is a sound the owner makes. It means the hand is coming. The hand comes to the top of the head and the hand moves in the way the hand moves, and the pressure is right, it is always right, it is exactly the weight and the speed of that hand on that head and this is known so deeply it is not even known, it is just *the way the apartment works.*

Can-opening. This sound is the best sound. The sound means food, and knowing this is a body-knowledge, it lives in the stomach and the legs and the ears all at once. The food goes in the bowl next to the chip. The eating happens. During the eating, the owner moves through the apartment doing owner-things: bag-to-chair, shoes-off (and the shoes are inspected later, the warm dark of them, the salt-smell), water-running, the small box that makes the owner's face glow (this is observed but not understood and not needed, the box is the owner's pigeon, the owner watches it the way it watches the window but the box is not interesting because nothing lands on it).

This is the apartment. This is every day and every day before this day and the days are the same the way the radiator ticking is the same, not because they are all one day but because the pattern holds, and when the pattern holds the body is soft and the eyes close halfway and the purr comes up from the chest without deciding.

Then on Tuesday the moth is on the bathroom ceiling.

It is there.

It is on the ceiling.

It is there and it is *on the ceiling.*

The body goes low. The eyes go wide. Everything else stops. The radiator ticks and the tick is not heard. The pigeon lands on the ledge and the pigeon is not seen. There is only the moth on the ceiling of the bathroom, pale brown, the span of it the width of two toe-beans, and its wings are flat and its body is a small dark line between the wings, and it is *absolutely still,* and this stillness is the worst thing and the best thing because still-things can become moving-things and when a still-thing becomes a moving-thing the catching happens, but the ceiling is high, the ceiling is a problem, the ceiling is up-there where no jumping reaches.

The chirp comes out. The stuttering throat-click that means: unreachable thing, desired thing, thing that exists perfectly in the space between possible and impossible.

The moth does not move.

This is Tuesday. This is the last time everything is right.

---

## II.

Nighttime is: the owner on the bed, on the back, the blanket up, and then the shift onto the side, always the right side, face toward the wall which is also toward the window. This is when the position is assumed. The position is: curled against the back of the owner's knees, or along the length of the owner's shin, and the warmth there is steady and the breathing is a rhythm that enters the body through the fur and the skin and becomes the same rhythm, so sleeping is not two things sleeping in one bed, sleeping is one thing made of one rhythm breathing.

The owner goes to bed at the time of going-to-bed. The owner lies down. The blanket goes up. The shift to the right side. The face toward the wall. The position is assumed. The warmth is there. The breathing starts its rhythm.

Then the breathing changes.

This is not the same as the breathing getting louder or softer, which are things that happen and are known and are nothing. This is the rhythm breaking. The breath comes in at the usual time but holds— holds past when it lets go— holds longer— and lets go with a sound that is not the usual letting-go sound. The new sound is lower. The new sound comes from deeper in the body, from a place the breathing does not usually come from. From behind the lungs. From the back-of-the-body place where the purring lives, except the owner does not purr, has never purred, and this sound is not a purr but it is from the purr-place and this is wrong.

The body stays still. When something is wrong and the what-is-wrong is not known, the body stays still. The ears go flat. The eyes open all the way in the dark. The dark is not dark. The dark is full of shapes that are the usual shapes— dresser, doorframe, the owner's shoulder rising and falling— but the shapes are coated in the new sound and the new sound makes the shapes less certain.

The owner's temperature changes. This is felt through the fur, through the skin beneath the fur, through the place where the body presses against the owner's leg, and the change is: warmer. Not sick-warm (sick-warm is known, sick-warm happened once, a winter, and the owner stayed in bed and the apartment smelled of medicine-orange and salt-sweat). This warm is different. This warm has a direction. It is moving through the owner's body from the top, from the head-end, downward, and as it moves the owner's muscles change, the leg that the body is pressed against goes rigid in a way that is not awake-rigid and not cold-rigid but something-else-rigid, a hardness that comes from inside, and the body pulls away from the leg because the leg is no longer the leg, the leg is something wearing the leg's shape and heat.

From the foot of the bed: watching. The owner's breathing is the broken rhythm. The owner's hands, on top of the blanket, are open. The fingers are spread. The fingers are spread too wide. The fingers do not spread that wide.

Then the sound. From the owner's mouth, but the mouth is not open the way a mouth opens for sounds. The mouth is barely open. The sound is coming through the teeth as though the teeth are not there, as though the sound does not need the mouth at all, the mouth is simply the nearest opening. The sound is— 

There is no comparison for the sound. There is no before-thing that matches this after-thing. The sound is low and it does not change in pitch, it is one flat tone, but it is *thick,* and the thickness grows, and the ears try to flatten more but they are already flat, and the sound enters not through the ears but through the bones of the skull the way the bus-rumble comes up through the floor, a vibration that is also a pressure, and the pressure says: *away.*

The body moves off the bed. The body goes to the doorway. From the doorway: watching.

The owner's eyes are half-open.

The owner is sleeping. The breathing says sleeping. The body-weight says sleeping. Everything says sleeping except the eyes, which are half-open and aimed at the ceiling but not *at* the ceiling. The eyes are aimed at the ceiling the way the eyes are aimed at the window when the eyes are looking at the street beyond the window. The ceiling is in the way of what the eyes are looking at. The eyes are looking past the ceiling, through the ceiling, at something that is above the ceiling, above the roof, above the building, above the city—

The tail goes thick. Every hair on the tail stands away from every other hair. This is not decided. This is the body's oldest knowledge. Something is here that should not be here. Something is in the room that has no shape and no smell and no sound except the sound that is coming through the owner's teeth, and it has come from above, from the far-above, from the place where the pigeons go when they leave the ledge and keep going up, past the telephone wires, past the other buildings, up to where watching cannot follow, and whatever the owner's half-open eyes are looking at lives there, in the un-followable place, and it is looking back.

Under the bed is where the body goes.

Under the bed is where the body stays until morning.

In the morning the owner wakes up and the feet hit the floor and the feet go to the kitchen and the water is changed and the can is opened and the hand comes down and the hand is right, the hand is exactly right, and the apartment is the apartment, and perhaps the night-thing was like the time the loud noise came from outside and the body hid in the bathtub for an hour and then the bathtub was not needed because the loud noise was gone and the apartment was the apartment again.

Yes. The night-thing was like that. The radiator ticks. The moth is still on the bathroom ceiling. The moth is still there. Good. Good.

---

## III.

The moth is on the ceiling. The moth is on the ceiling still. The moth has been on the ceiling for a number of days that is more than one but the exact number is not a thing that sorts properly, because days do not stack, days are a rhythm, and the moth has been on the ceiling for *several beats of the rhythm* and it is still there and this is fascinating and enraging and perfect.

The bathroom is small. The toilet is cold on the haunches but useful as a platform from which the distance to the ceiling can be measured (the distance is: too much). The edge of the bathtub is another platform (the distance from there is: also too much but from a different angle, which is important, because angles can make too-much into almost-enough, though not in this case, not yet). The preferred position is on the bath mat, directly below the moth, spine flat, head tilted entirely back so that the world is only the ceiling and the moth on it, and from this position the chirping happens.

The chirp is a specific sound. It is the sound the jaw makes when the jaw clicks rapidly and the throat pushes small sharp bursts of air through the click. It is the hunting-sound, the prey-is-seen-and-wanted sound, and it comes from a place that is not the deciding-place but the deeper-than-deciding place, the body's first room, where desire lives before it becomes action. The chirp says: *I see you. I want you. You are mine in the way that all moving things are mine, you are mine because I have seen you and my seeing has made you mine, and the only problem is the distance, the terrible distance, the gap between my wanting and your body.*

The moth does not move. The moth is perfect.

. . .

The bag comes home at the wrong time. The bag comes home when the light in the apartment is still the high-angle light, the light that means midday, and the bag has never come home when the light is this light. The feet come in the door and the feet go past the food bowl and the feet do not stop. The owner stands in the kitchen. The owner stands in the kitchen and does not open the can and does not run the water and does not touch the small glowing box. The owner stands in the kitchen with hands at sides and stares at the wall.

The wall is just a wall. The wall is known. There is nothing on the wall except a small mark that has always been there and a place where the paint is darker which has also always been there. The wall is *known* and there is nothing to see on it and the owner is looking at it the way the owner's sleeping-eyes looked at the ceiling: as though the wall is in the way.

The body does the ankle-rub. The body does the ankle-rub again. The owner does not look down. The owner does not look down. The sound comes out, the asking-sound, the short high sound that means *I am here and you should attend to this fact,* and the owner does not look down.

The glass on the counter. The glass on the counter has water in it. The glass on the counter has water in it and it is right there on the edge of the counter and the edge of the counter is where things sit when they are ready to not-sit, when they are ready to fall, and the paw reaches out and taps the glass. The glass moves a little. The glass does not fall. The paw taps the glass again. The glass moves more. This is satisfying. This is cause and then effect, this is the world working the way the world works, the paw pushes and the glass moves and if the paw pushes enough the glass will go over the edge and then the glass will fall and the falling will make a sound and the sound will be loud and correct and *the world will do what it is supposed to do.*

The owner's hand catches the glass.

The owner is looking down now. The owner is looking at the face looking up.

"Hey, buddy."

This is the sound the owner makes. This is the right sound. But the sound is— the sound comes out at the right speed and the right volume but the space between "hey" and "buddy" is longer than the space has ever been, a gap like the gap between the radiator-ticks, and in the gap there is the other sound, the barely-there sound, the frequency from the bad-sleeping-night, just a whisper of it under the owner's voice like a second voice in the same throat.

The hand comes down. The hand goes past the ears and the hand is— fine. The hand is fine. The hand scratches behind the left ear which is the correct ear and the hand's pressure is nearly right, it is close to right, and the purr starts because the purr always starts when the hand does this, the purr is automatic, and then the purr stops because the hand moves to the right ear but the transition between ears is wrong, the hand lifts too high, the hand comes down at an angle that is not the angle, and the purr stops the way a sound stops when a door closes.

The owner's hand stays on the head. The owner's eyes are on the face looking up. The owner's eyes are the owner's eyes. They are the right color. They are the right size. They are looking at the face looking up and they are seeing the face looking up and they are the owner's eyes.

But behind the eyes, far back, in the place where the looking comes from: something else is also looking. Something that is looking at the face looking up the way a thing looks at a thing it is learning. Not the way the owner looks.
